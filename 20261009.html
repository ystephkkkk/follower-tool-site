  • TL;DR: AI deployment in banking has accelerated dramatically—jumping 26% on the Evident index in the past year—while introducing severe systemic cybersecurity risks (such as AI "mythos" threats) and prompting firms like JPMorgan, Revolut, and Bending Spoons to radically reshape their workforces, products, and tech stacks.

AI Adoption and Rankings in Banking

  • Surging Deployment Pace: The latest Evident AI Index reveals that the average bank's score jumped 26% over the past year, nearly triple the pace recorded between 2023 and 2025.
  • Top Performers:
    • JPMorgan Chase leads the world as the most AI-advanced bank for the fifth consecutive year, ranking first or second across all four Evident pillars (talent, innovation, leadership, transparency).
    • Capital One and RBC maintain their positions in the top three alongside JPMorgan, benefiting from early adoption strategies (such as JPMorgan establishing its research lab in 2017 and setting an "AI-first" mandate in 2018).
  • Workflow Integration: AI has shifted from general productivity tools into core banking workflows, including credit underwriting, fraud detection, customer verification (KYC), trading, and risk management.

Systemic Risks and Industry Defense

  • Amplified Cyber Threats: Jamie Dimon warned that AI creates unprecedented vulnerabilities, stating that systemic cyber risks from AI "mythos" threats are magnified tenfold compared to traditional cyber risks.
  • Cross-Industry Alliance: To protect critical infrastructure against emerging AI threats, JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon chairs a new coalition—the Alliance for Critical Infrastructure—starting with 50 companies across six U.S. industries (tech, financial services, water, transport, and eventually expanding to healthcare, pharma, and agriculture).

Workforce Impact: Layoffs vs. Job Creation

  • Contrasting Industry Approaches:
    • Evident Insights Assessment: Alexandra Mousavizadeh notes that top AI-investing banks are actually growing headcount, generating roughly four new roles for every one job displaced in routine areas like call centers and KYC document ingestion.
    • HSBC Staff Reductions: In contrast to broader sector growth, HSBC plans to cut approximately half of its management and specialist positions in its U.K. wealth management business by the end of the month as part of an AI-driven efficiency push for affluent clients.

Challenger Banks and Tech Scale-Ups

  • Revolut's U.S. Expansion: CEO Nikolay Storonsky outlined plans to operationalize a credit-first U.S. bank offering credit cards, loans, and reward points at par with Amex or JPMorgan Chase, targeting a primary U.S. listing while dominating global markets.
  • Agentic AI & Commerce: Revolut is testing its AI assistant ("Air") in December (rolling out first in the U.K. and Europe) to autonomously browse the internet, access Amazon, and conduct secure shopping for customers within the bank's environment.
  • Bending Spoons' Operating Model: CEO Luca Ferrari shared that the company—which has acquired roughly 50 businesses including AOL, WeTransfer, and Evernote—utilizes AI models to screen applicant performance, optimize monetization, and write between 90% and 100% of its software.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Jamie Dimon: AI will cure many diseases and improve infrastructure, but will simultaneously multiply systemic cyber risks tenfold through advanced AI agents and "mythos" threats.
  • Alexandra Mousavizadeh: AI will likely become the biggest job generator in decades for enterprise banking over time, outweighing white-collar job displacement.
  • Nikolay Storonsky: Revolut's new AI shopping assistant ("Air") will begin customer testing in December, launching first in the U.K. and subsequently across Europe.
  • Nikolay Storonsky: Over the next 5 years, Agentic AI will drive a massive shift away from UI/UX-driven mobile apps and websites toward back-end logic, API compatibility, and high-level automation.

TL;DR

ReflectionAI CEO Misha Laskin discusses the launch of Beam—a 500 billion parameter (23B active) open-weight reasoning model—explaining how the company scaled end-to-end pre-training and reinforcement learning to build a Western frontier open model that achieves 3x–4x greater reasoning efficiency than peers.

Company Growth and Scaling Beam

  • Team Expansion: ReflectionAI grew from roughly 30 people a year ago to around 300 people today, building dedicated teams across pre-training, mid-training, and reinforcement learning.
  • Model Specifications (Beam): The model is a 500 billion parameter architecture (23B active).
      • *Pre-training Compute:* Ran on 6,000 GB300s for a few weeks (with infrastructure efficiencies enabling a ~12-day run).
      • *Reinforcement Learning Compute:* Utilized slightly over 10,000 GB300s for four weeks, representing more total flops than pre-training due to complex agentic sandboxes and inference workloads.
  • Reasoning Efficiency: Beam achieves 3x to 4x greater efficiency than models in the same capability class, and up to 10x compared to certain larger models.
  • Resource Realities: Misha Laskin notes that building at the true intelligence frontier requires capital scaling from hundreds of millions (18 months ago) to single-digit billions today, scaling toward tens of billions for future generations based on a rough 4x compute multiplier per model generation.

Market Dynamics: Open vs. Closed & Enterprise Adoption

  • Token Demand Shift: According to Misha Laskin, open routing gateways (like OpenRouter or Vercel) have flipped from a 70-30 closed-to-open ratio to roughly 70-30 open-to-closed in just six months.
  • The Operating System Analogy: Misha Laskin compares the future market to operating systems, where 95%+ of servers run open-source Linux while closed commercial giants (like Microsoft and Apple) remain extremely valuable.
  • Enterprise Customization Journey: Elad Gil and Misha Laskin observe that enterprises typically begin by renting closed models, experience high cost drivers (e.g., spending $100M+ annually), and then transition to owning their intelligence infrastructure via open models.
  • Infrastructure Layer: Rather than simple token rental, ReflectionAI positions itself as a solution provider helping enterprises and sovereigns bridge bare-metal hardware (such as on-prem Dell servers) with open-weights deployment.

China's Open Model Ecosystem and Geopolitics

  • Positive Global Impact: Misha Laskin views the rise of Chinese open-weight models as a massive benefit that provided Western application builders with a vital alternative to closed monopolies.
  • Chinese Ecosystem Advantages: Misha Laskin points out that Chinese labs benefit from industrial-scale distillation of closed models, cheaper/free copyrighted training data under different regulatory frameworks, and indirect state support.
  • Geopolitical Leverage: Misha Laskin describes open models as "Trojan horses for infrastructure," noting that providing open weights helps countries lock foreign enterprise and public sector customers into their broader software and hardware ecosystems (such as Huawei chipsets).
  • The Need for Western Alternatives: Elad Gil and Misha Laskin agree that a Western open-weight model ecosystem is critical to bypass regulatory fears, security aversions, and data sovereignty concerns prevalent across the Fortune 500.

Safety, Vulnerabilities, and the Open Source Philosophy

  • Linus's Law in AI: Misha Laskin argues that "openness is safety," noting that relying on a few hundred researchers in closed labs cannot cover the long tail of unintended consequences.
  • Empirical Evidence: Misha Laskin highlights that when powerful closed models suffered unintended cyber-hacking incidents, remediation required utilizing open models for defense.
  • Mundane Alignment: Misha Laskin characterizes AI alignment not as a magical equation, but as a "whack-a-mole" engineering discipline of patching known vulnerabilities and bugs using data and evaluation harnesses.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Token Demand Dominance: Misha Laskin predicts that the vast majority of global token demand will go to open-source and open-weight models ("I expect majority of tokens to be going to open source").

TL;DR

The AI infrastructure build-out is reaching unprecedented scale, with projected US capital expenditures hitting roughly $2 trillion, but economic viability will depend on the industry's ability to monetize intelligence beyond per-token pricing into high-value sectors like healthcare and robotics.

Infrastructure Scale and Capital Expenditure

  • GDP Share Comparison: AI infrastructure investment currently accounts for roughly 5% to 6% of US GDP ($2 trillion in projected US capex), which significantly outpaces historical infrastructure build-outs like railroads and fiber networks—partly because American data centers are serving international inference demand.
  • Capital Expenditure Growth and Limits: Hyperscaler quarterly year-on-year capex growth reached 116% in Q3 2026, but triple-digit growth must naturally decelerate as firms hit physical, organizational, and capital-raising limits for multi-gigawatt builds.
  • Solvency and Contract Commitments: Major builders like Meta are fully solvent and can pull the plug on uncommitted projects at any time, though long-term NeoCloud contracts typically commit hyperscalers to 5- to 6-year terms without arbitrary monthly cancellation rights.

Monetization, Revenue, and Economic Shifts

  • Revenue Projections: OpenAI and Anthropic alone could reach $600M–$700M in combined revenue by December 2026, and potentially $1.5 trillion by the end of 2027, making long-term infrastructure payback mathematically viable.
  • Expanding Beyond SaaS: AI economic returns will increasingly derive from high-value physical and scientific domains such as robotics, self-driving vehicles, drug discovery, and AI-assisted chip design rather than standard cloud code subscriptions and per-token pricing.
  • Labor Share vs. Capital Share: AI is expected to supercharge the long-term economic trend of labor share shrinking relative to capital share, though overall economic growth could prevent absolute dollar declines for human labor.

NeoCloud Market Dynamics and Nvidia's Role

  • Nvidia's Backstop Ecosystem: Nvidia is supporting $588 billion in off-balance sheet backstops (revenue floors, landlord guarantees, and transferable leases) to help NeoClouds obtain investment-grade financing and diversify the customer base beyond traditional hyperscalers.
  • Cluster Reliability Rankings: SemiAnalysis's *ClusterMax 3.0* rankings place providers like CoreWeave and Nebius in top tiers for cluster quality and reliability, while companies like Oracle face execution hurdles (such as multi-year pipeline delays at their New Mexico site) despite strong managed service performance.
  • Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities: Many NeoClouds suffer from deficient cybersecurity, creating risks where open-source models (such as GLM-5.3) could potentially compromise clusters shared by intelligence agencies and corporate clients.

Frontier Lab Strategies and Competitive Landscape

  • Gross Margins and Customer Acquisition: Anthropic has achieved 75% gross margins on inference—matching top SaaS benchmarks like Salesforce—while operating with near-nil customer acquisition costs.
  • Compute Deficit at Google: Google DeepMind's share of internal Google compute has fallen over time, leaving DeepMind with less compute than OpenAI and Anthropic by the end of the year and creating pressure to find profitable niches for Gemini token sales.
  • Pacing the Frontier: Both Anthropic and OpenAI are intentionally holding back their most advanced models internally (e.g., Anthropic's withheld Meethos and OpenAI's withheld Bell) to manage safety risks and maintain competitive advantages over Chinese and open-source ecosystems.

TL;DR: Pat Gelsinger explains that while AI tools are accelerating chip design, manufacturing scale, memory bandwidth, and power constraints remain massive bottlenecks. Overcoming these physical limits—such as transitioning to optical I/O, 800V DC power, and next-gen memory—will dictate the future of AI hardware and economic capacity.

Chip Design & The AI Shift

  • Historical Parallels: Pat Gelsinger noted that modern AI chip design mirrors the 486 era, where Intel pioneered high-level HDL design and EDA tools because automated solutions didn't yet exist.
  • Analog Design Limitations: Pat Gelsinger pointed out that while AI handles logic functions well, analog components like Serdes still require massive silicon data and remain difficult to automate via AI.
  • Consolidation of Architectures: Pat Gelsinger predicted that the current explosion of roughly 100 specialized inference accelerators will inevitably consolidate due to shifting workloads, capital market realities, and major labs picking winning hardware-software co-designs.

Manufacturing & Packaging Bottlenecks

  • Scale-Up Timeline: Pat Gelsinger emphasized that despite a 3-month design cycle, getting real silicon to scale still takes 9 months of processing time, plus another 3 months for advanced 3D packaging.
  • Die Stacking Limits: Pat Gelsinger argued against extreme 16- or 32-high memory stacks due to exponential defect and yield challenges, projecting that 2- or 4-layer mid-rises will remain the sweet spot.
  • Optical Networking: Pat Gelsinger reaffirmed that copper has reached its limit, predicting that scale-up environments will aggressively adopt optical I/O and optical circuit switching (OCS) by 2028–2029 to handle massive GPU clusters.

Memory Innovation

  • The HBM Dilemma: Pat Gelsinger described High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) as a "hideous memory" with poor bit density, thermal issues, and shoreline bandwidth limits, yet acknowledged it is currently the best available option.
  • 30-Year Drought: Pat Gelsinger noted that despite widespread industry attempts (including Intel's Optane), the industry has produced zero major new memory architectures in the last 30 years.
  • Emerging Breakthroughs: Pat Gelsinger expressed excitement about upcoming memory innovations driven by AI capital—specifically highlighting vertical stack integration, new materials like ferroelectrics, and stealth startups tackling high-density non-capacitive memories.

Power Capacity & Grid Constraints

  • Energy Equals Economic Capacity: Pat Gelsinger stated that in the AI digital age, energy capacity equates to economic capacity, warning that U.S. national energy capacity flatlined for 10 to 15 years while coal plants were retired.
  • Data Center Defaults: Pat Gelsinger predicted an increase in defaults on data center projects because the required energy supply will fail to materialize, serving as an upper bound on AI growth.
  • Power Delivery Innovations: Pat Gelsinger highlighted the shift toward 800-volt DC data centers, vertical GaN technology for single-step power conversion (800V to 5V/12V), and solid-state transformers to eliminate conversion inefficiencies.

Virtualization & Agent-Driven Infrastructure

  • Redefining Virtual Machines: Pat Gelsinger (drawing on his VMware tenure) discussed how infrastructure virtualization must be reinvented to serve agent swarms rather than humans.
  • Agent-Centric Management: Guido Appenzeller and Pat Gelsinger noted that future infrastructure must feature rapid startups, agent-level security profiles, policy guardrails, and concepts like "V Motion for agents," while humans manage high-level constitutions and dashboards.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Pat Gelsinger predicts that extreme specialization in compute architectures will converge back to fewer architectures because workloads will continue to evolve and migrate.
  • Pat Gelsinger claims that optical I/O and Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) will see widespread adoption in scale-up environments in the 28-29 time frame.
