TL;DR

17-year-old high school student Jonathan (Su Tinghao) independently conducted pre-training research and had his paper on Attention mechanisms accepted into the ICML 2026 Main Track. The discussion explores his self-taught AI journey, his perspective on the future of education, AI anxiety, and maintaining joy in an AI-driven world.

Background & AI Journey

  • Speaker Introduction:
    • Zhang Xiaojun (Host): Host of the *Zhang Xiaojun Podcast*, exploring the intersection of AI, education, and human society.
    • Jonathan (Su Tinghao): 17-year-old high school student (born in 2009, rising to Grade 12) at Chinese International School in Hong Kong, born in Shanghai, and self-taught AI researcher.
  • Spark of Interest:
    • Inspiration from OpenAI & Karpathy: Sparked by early OpenAI YouTube demos and Andrej Karpathy's 20-hour zero-to-hero video series on building GPT and Transformers.
    • 30-Day Reading Challenge: Challenged himself to read one machine learning paper every day for 30 days to build foundational research intuition.
  • Accepted ICML 2026 Paper:
    • Title: *Attention Projection Mixing with Exogenous Anchors*
    • Core Mechanism: Explores Value Residual Learning by applying RMSNorm on residuals, separating the dual functions of first-layer attention values by doubling projection width.
    • Resource Constraints: Burned 3,000 RMB and 1,500 RMB on failed un-saved checkpoints due to hyperparameter mistakes, scaling up models on a 0.5B parameter size using datasets like FineWeb-Edu (up to 20B datasets).

AI Industry & Market Perspectives

TL;DR

Whatnot has evolved from a niche collectibles marketplace into a global live shopping platform with over 100 categories, where users spend an average of 95 minutes per day. Co-founder Grant LaFontaine explains that the platform functions like a digital shopping mall—prioritizing discovery, entertainment, and community over traditional utilitarian e-commerce.

1. Origins & Evolution of Whatnot

  • Childhood Foundations: Grant LaFontaine's first online sale occurred in third or fourth grade when he sold a holographic Chansey Pokémon card on Yahoo Auctions for $10, which was paid for via a mail-in money order.
  • The Founding Story (December 2019): After brainstorming domain names while drinking in a Tokyo bar with co-founder Logan, they quit their jobs in December 2019 to launch an authenticated Funko Pop marketplace.
  • The Domain Acquisition: The initial domain name "Always Legit" was rejected by their first designer. Logan secured the domain whatnot.com for half a Bitcoin (valued at $5,000 at the time).
  • Multi-Category Expansion: Originally anchored in collectibles (Funko Pops, comic books, sports cards, and trading cards), Whatnot now spans 100+ categories, including fashion, fresh food and drink, and golf.
  • Global Footprint: The platform currently operates in 10 countries, covering North America (US and Canada), five European countries, Japan, and Australia, with nearly identical dynamics across regions.

2. The Demand Side: Entertainment & Discovery

  • Engagement Metrics: Users spend roughly 95 minutes a day on the platform, placing Whatnot's usage metrics on par with major social media and entertainment platforms.
  • Non-Transactional Browsing: On any given day, greater than 80% of users do not purchase anything; they are actively watching, chatting, and enjoying the experience.
  • Shopping Mall Analogy: LaFontaine compares the experience to visiting a physical shopping mall or retail store where consumers browse without a strict prior intent to buy, contrasting it with traditional e-commerce where users "have to know exactly what you're looking for."
  • Market Comparison: While live commerce accounts for 30% to 40% of all commerce in China (a $1 trillion+ market), the US market remains in single digits, presenting massive headroom for growth.

3. Empowering Small Businesses & Sellers

  • Economic Scale: In the previous year, Whatnot generated over $8 billion in sales, matching that same volume again in the current year while taking only a small fraction of the GMV.
  • Small Business Impact: The platform generates billions for small businesses ranging from one- and two-person operations up to teams of 200. The largest sellers on Whatnot generate upwards of $100 million in revenue with EBITDA margins of 20%, 30%, or 40%+.
  • The Retailer Value Proposition: Setting up a digital storefront on Whatnot costs $0, granting sellers immediate access to an unbounded global audience compared to expensive brick-and-mortar storefronts like Fifth Avenue.
  • Category Highlights:
    • Women's Fashion: Now the platform's largest category, operating similarly to a TJ Maxx or DSW model with off-season inventory and steep discounts.
    • Fresh Food & Drink: Early-stage category featuring direct-sourced fresh fish from San Diego docks and specialized barbecue.
    • Golf: Experiencing a surge, growing hundreds of percent year-over-year.

4. Trust, Safety & Operational Scale

  • Team Investment: Approximately 40% of Whatnot employees work on trust and safety, operating as a digital "police force and legal system" for a user base equivalent to "two New York Cities."
  • Automated Rules Engine: The platform utilizes an automated system that ingests billions of data points in sub-1-second intervals to detect bad behavior, harassment (via LLMs), delayed shipping, or high refund rates, executing automated warnings, suspensions, or bans.
  • Live Accountability: Because transactions and interactions happen via live video, bad actor behavior is inherently minimized compared to static e-commerce marketplaces.

5. Artificial Intelligence & Human Connection

  • Preserving the Human Element: Whatnot rejects AI avatars or tools that strip away human connection, emphasizing that buyers return specifically to interact with specific shop owners and community members.
  • Seller Efficiency Tools: AI is deployed in the background to make sellers more efficient—such as inferring metadata for live stream discovery systems, listing items, and providing business analytics—without replacing the human-centric experience.
