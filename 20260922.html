TL;DR

World Labs CEO Fei-Fei Li discussed the development of spatial intelligence and "world models" like the company's new Atlas model, emphasizing the need for robust ecosystems and human-centric governance to manage AI's dual-edged nature.

World Models vs. Large Language Models (LLMs)

  • Core Output Differences: Fei-Fei Li explained that unlike LLMs which output words, codes, and conversations, world models generate pixels, accurate 3D/4D geometric structures, and allow precise camera control and world construction.
  • Target Audience: The technology provides augmentative tools for creators, designers, and robotics developers.

Atlas: World Labs' Latest Technology

  • Product Status: Fei-Fei Li noted that World Labs recently shared a technical blog about Atlas and is actively working to release the underlying model as a product.
  • Technical Achievements: Atlas is the first of its kind in enabling camera control and pixel consistency with the real world while solving the computer vision challenge of combining pixel generation with intricate 3D structure reconstruction.
  • Performance: Fei-Fei Li stated that Atlas performs better than any state-of-the-art specialized model for these tasks.

Commercial Applications & Physical AI

  • Horizontal Use Cases: Fei-Fei Li stated applications span across creative industries, design, and robotics.
  • Robotics & Spatial Intelligence: World Labs operates as a spatial intelligence company, applying world models to design, creativity, robotics, healthcare, and education.
  • Training Bottlenecks: Fei-Fei Li explained that world models help unlock training and evaluation bottlenecks for physical AI by simulating realistic environments—particularly valuable in data-scarce sectors like pharmaceutical manufacturing and laboratory sciences.

Safety, Alignment, and AI Governance

  • Double-Edged Technology: Fei-Fei Li views AI safety as the same calculus applied to historical technologies like electricity, steam engines, and modern transportation, requiring a balance between human safety and civilizational advancement.
  • Ecosystem Collaboration: Fei-Fei Li argued against fatalism, advocating for a robust ecosystem that includes the public sector, open source, closed source, academia, government, and civil society.
  • Existential Threat Perspective: Fei-Fei Li stated that threats to human society stem from humans themselves—specifically how humans wield technology, govern it, and maintain individual and shared dignity, agency, and responsibility.
  • Benchmarking: Fei-Fei Li noted that while World Labs conducts internal technical benchmarks covering safety and model capability, proper evaluation requires ecosystem-level responsibility involving independent and public sector bodies like academia, alongside industry and government collaboration.

TL;DR

Blake Scholl, founder and CEO of Boom Supersonic, discusses the company's milestones in developing supersonic commercial flight and pivoting its engine core technology to power high-demand data centers.

Supersonic Flight and Regulatory Milestones

  • XB-1 Flight Success:
    • Boom Supersonic built the XB-1, the first privately developed jet to break the sound barrier, with a team of approximately 50 people.
    • The flight featured the first supersonic installation of Starlink, allowing live video streaming via an iPhone.
  • Boomless Cruise Technology:
    • Utilizes atmospheric refraction to redirect the sonic boom upwards ("mock cutoff"), allowing speeds up to Mach 1.3 (50% faster than current travel) without the boom touching the ground.
    • Full-speed supersonic flight (Mach 1.7) is reserved for over water.
  • Supersonic Legalization:
    • Following an executive order ending the US supersonic flight ban, Congress passed the Supersonic Legalization Act unanimously through the House and Senate.
    • A model of the Overture airliner currently sits in the Oval Office.

In-House Engine Development and Manufacturing

  • Pivoting from Outsourcing:
    • After parting ways with Rolls-Royce, Boom decided to build its own jet engine from scratch using advanced digital design and manufacturing tools down to the turbine blades.
    • The philosophy emphasizes that engineering and manufacturing must work together to re-industrialize America.
  • Engine Factory & Vertical Integration:
    • Building a vertically integrated jet turbine factory in an undisclosed location, manufacturing engines starting from raw materials.
    • The first vertically integrated jet engine core is scheduled for a test stand run next month.

Data Center Power Pivot

  • Surprising Ground Application:
    • By taking the supersonic engine core (designed to run full duration at high power in a hot environment at Mach 1.7), removing the front fan, and attaching a generator to the back, Boom created a behind-the-meter power generation product for data centers.
  • Deployment and Demand:
    • Packaged into a couple of trailers, the unit delivers 42 megawatts of power without needing water.
    • Blake Scholl reports tens of gigawatts of demand in his inbox and plans to auction off the first engine run next month.

Commercial Airline Economics and Future Vision

  • Overture Pricing and Timeline:
    • The break-even fare for a round-trip ticket across the Atlantic at Mach 1.7 (taking 3 hours each way) is about $3,500.
    • The target timeline to get passengers on the plane is approximately 4 years (around 2030), prioritizing speed over rigid predictability.
  • Long-Term Goal:
    • The ultimate vision is to make supersonic flight available to every class of passenger—from commercial airlines to private jets and government transport.

***

The Impetus for a New Educational Model

  • Mismatch of Traditional College: Traditional universities work well for scholars, but are a massive financial and time investment for 18-year-olds whose primary goal is to get a job or start a company.
  • Speed of Technological Evolution: The skill sets needed in technology are evolving too rapidly for large, legacy education systems to keep pace.
  • The AI Era Shift: Ben Horowitz compares the AI revolution to the industrial revolution, arguing that training built for the 20th-century corporate world will not map onto the AI future.
  • The Value Bundle: While standalone online courses fail to cut through, HA aims to deliver a complete "bundle"—combining learning with status, credentials, fun, residential community, and direct employer connections.

The Horowitz Andreessen Academy Experience

  • Learning by Doing: The curriculum prioritizes doing over studying—focusing on building real systems, writing songs, or creating products rather than listening to 5 hours of daily lectures.
  • San Francisco as the Campus: The school is residential in San Francisco, cohabitating students with active tech companies, builders, and co-ops to force real-world interactions and osmosis.
  • Corporate Integration: Students gain direct access to work and intern at iconic tech companies such as Databricks, Nvidia, and Stripe.
  • For-Profit Alignment: Operating as a for-profit entity ensures strict incentive alignment: the customer is the student, and success is measured purely by whether graduates go on to do great things.
  • Broad Definition of Building: The initial founding class will feature 50 student fellows who are hands-on builders across diverse fields, not limited strictly to software coding.

The Advantage of Being Young Today

  • Unprecedented Empowerment: Young people today enter a world using advanced tools where they can solve problems and be more capable than professionals with 30–40 years of experience.
  • Avoiding Mediocrity: Gen Z features high variance and fragmentation; Gagan Biyani and Ben Horowitz stress that students with "billion-dollar potential" should avoid settling for million-dollar ideas and instead explore hard problems.
  • Embracing Productive Failure: Drawing inspiration from Silicon Valley culture, the academy embraces failure as long as it produces deep learning and earns "secrets" through direct execution.

Developing People Skills & the Inner Game

  • Overcoming the Screen Addiction: While modern generations face a loneliness epidemic tied to screens, AI tools will free humans to focus on interpersonal dynamics, peer motivation, and leadership.
  • The Limits of Textbooks: People skills, negotiation, and handling the psychological stress of running a team cannot be learned from a textbook; they require real-world practice and high-stakes collaboration.
  • Cultural Osmosis: Cohabitation with the adult tech world forces students out of their comfort zones, requiring them to master professional communication and relationship-building organically.

TL;DR

Gabe Stengel, co-founder of Rogo, explains how his AI platform is automating financial workflows for institutions and dealmakers, arguing that specialized vertical platforms with deep enterprise infrastructure will outmaneuver generalist LLM labs in high finance.

Building Rogo: Evolution and Product Eras

  • Early Iterations: Gabe Stengel attempted two prior financial AI projects in high school and college before GPT-3, both of which failed due to immature underlying models.
  • Model-Driven Eras: Rogo's evolution mirrors base model capability leaps:
    • GPT-3 Era: Magical demos, but production reliability was poor.
    • O1 Pro Era: Provided sufficient reliability to serve as a baseline search and calculation tool.
    • Opus 4.5 Era (Late 2025 to Early 2026): Models reached a threshold capable of autonomously handling junior investment professional and banker workflows.
  • UX & Workflow Integration: Rogo succeeds by meeting users in their existing habits, such as letting a Managing Director email a markup of a pitch deck to an AI analyst over email and receiving a completed version in 20 minutes instead of two days.

The Dealmaker Core Market & Private Markets Plumbing

  • Ideal Customer Profile (ICP): While public equities have abundant data, Rogo's core market consists of dealmakers (buy-side and sell-side) coordinating mergers, acquisitions, and transactions.
  • Behind-the-Scenes Surface Area: Half of Rogo's product operates as the "underwater iceberg," automatically updating internal CRMs, portfolio monitoring systems, and LP distribution reports based on human deal activity.
  • Private vs. Public Markets: Private markets are attractive because workflows, coordination, and transacting are still heavily manual and lack standardized digital plumbing.

Beating OpenAI, Anthropic, and Generalist Labs

  • Perpendicular Building: Generalist labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are focused on reaching $1 trillion in revenue and view narrow financial workflows as low-priority tasks.
  • Unbuildable Enterprise Moats: Rogo builds critical last-mile infrastructure that labs will never touch, including strict MNPI compliance, pixel-perfect auditability, regulatory reporting, and secure data rooms.
  • Harness Over Intelligence: Borrowing from Claude Code versus ChatGPT, Stengel emphasizes that the software "harness"—the way models are prompted, routed, and chained—matters more than raw intelligence alone.

Internal Operations, Company Brain ("Shrek"), and Talent Strategy

  • Internal Transcription ("Shrek"): Rogo records 100% of internal conversations, feeding a company knowledge base that provides real-time proactive briefings for sales and deployment teams.
  • Talent Sourcing & Aggression: Rogo recruits high-ownership technical and domain talent, including former founders (having acquired six financial AI startups). Stengel emphasizes that building at this speed requires extreme operational aggression and an appetite for "chewing glass" (handling continuous hiring, retention, and product obsolescence lows).
  • Fundraising Adversity: During their Series A, Rogo was rejected by 40 top-tier VC firms (including Sequoia, Kleiner, Benchmark, and Thrive) before Keith Raboy invested, driven by SF investors' lack of familiarity with the financial software TAM.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Enterprise Value Shift: Gabe Stengel predicts that in 10 years, the world's best investment firms and banks will have 90% of their enterprise value in software, data, and autonomous systems rather than people.
  • Adoption Timeline: Gabe Stengel predicts that over the next 2 to 5 years, the world's best investors will spend their time figuring out how to integrate AI into every part of the investment life cycle.
