TL;DR

The White House Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) is overhauling federal science funding through its new "Science: A New Golden Age" initiative to eliminate administrative bloat, defund political mandates, and aggressively back high-impact breakthroughs.

Setting the Agenda and Reforming the Federal Science Enterprise

  • Michael Kratsios (White House OSTP Director): Outlined the OSTP’s primary mission to coordinate science and technology policy across federal agencies (such as NSF, Department of Energy, and DARPA) and implement the President's national S&T agenda.
  • Critique of Status Quo: Argued that the science community has become overly politicized, overly reliant on static funding increases without measuring outcomes, and stifled by institutional dogma.
  • Administrative Overhaul:
    • Criticized past grant allocations, noting that roughly 25% of National Science Foundation (NSF) grants under the Biden administration—equating to $8 billion ($2 billion annually for 4 years)—went toward DEI-related initiatives.
    • Highlighted administrative bloat at universities, where institutional overhead fees siphon money away from actual research.
    • Noted that the median age of an intramural researcher at the National Institutes of Health (NIH) is 71 years old.
  • Metascience and New Funding Models:
    • Creating metascience units at the NSF and NIH to run live experiments on funding efficiency (Eroom's Law shows research efficiency has fallen 80-fold since 1950, halving every 9 years).
    • Introducing "golden tickets" (modeled after tests in Denmark), allowing peer reviewers to unilaterally push through bold, high-risk grants outside committee consensus.
    • Moving away from rigid 18-month grant cycles to variable durations, including fast-track 3-to-6-month grants.

Climate Science, Energy, and Redefining Priorities

  • Michael Kratsios: Acknowledged that climate change is occurring and that burning fossil fuels adds CO2 to the atmosphere, but maintained that the data does not support the narrative of a "climate emergency."
  • RCP 8.5 Scenario: Pointed out that climate scientists recently pulled the extreme "RCP 8.5" scenario from national climate assessments and IPCC models because it lacked scientific integrity, despite being the basis for decades of alarmist media coverage and grant funding.
  • Energy Alternatives: Emphasized that forced economic penalties on carbon hurt the world's poorest populations (noting the UN reports 3 million annual deaths from dirty indoor cooking fuels like wood and dung) and that market-driven alternative energies and advanced nuclear tech are better solutions.

The Great Tech Race with China

  • Michael Kratsios: Highlighted the massive spending divergence: China increased annual R&D spending 19x from $33 billion in 2000 to $670 billion in 2021, compared to a roughly 3x increase in the US.
  • STEM Pipeline Deficits: Warned that 7 out of 10 STEM PhD candidates in the US are now non-American, a complete inversion of historical ratios where 70% were US-born.
  • Immigration and Talent: Acknowledged the need to fix legal immigration pathways for top foreign-born STEM talent while simultaneously reviving domestic K-12 advanced science and math programs (such as gifted and talented student tracks).

Big Bold Bets and Institutional Direction

  • Michael Kratsios: Outlined major federal moonshots designed to recapture American technological supremacy:
    • Space: Returning American boots to the moon by 2028, putting a nuclear reactor in space by 2028, and establishing a moon base by 2030.
    • Quantum Computing: Launching a National Quantum Initiative via Executive Order to build a scientifically relevant quantum computer by 2028.
    • Fusion Energy: Targeting commercial fusion energy by 2035.
    • The Genesis Mission: An all-of-government initiative launched late last year to double US scientific output by applying AI to materials science, chemistry, math, and physics.

COVID-19 and the Crisis of Scientific Trust

  • Michael Kratsios: Delivered a severe indictment of pandemic-era scientific leadership, stating that Dr. Anthony Fauci "did more disservice to science than anyone in modern history."
  • Epistemic Breakdown: Criticized the enforcement of uniform dogmas (such as school masking and contradictory protest rules) which destroyed public trust and weaponized the label "anti-science" against anyone exercising the fundamental scientific method of asking questions.

Predictions & Forward Calls

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer stated that recent trade talks with Canada collapsed because Canadian officials "wanted more" concessions despite the U.S. offering the world's most preferential market access. Greer emphasized that the U.S. is moving forward with targeted 5% tariffs, dismissing market concerns and political friction with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Background & Historical Trade Friction

  • Long-Standing Grievances: Greer stated the U.S. initiated talks after Canada maintained restrictions for over a year, specifically noting:
    • Wine & Spirits: Canada prohibited the sale of American wine and spirits in their stores.
    • Auto Limits: Canada placed limits on the number of U.S. autos entering the country.
    • Dairy Restrictions: Long-standing restrictions on the sale of U.S. dairy into Canada.
  • The Auto Pact Context: Greer explained that Canada’s auto industry originated from a 1960s agreement where Canada required companies to build locally if they wanted to sell into the Canadian market.
  • Current Tariffs: The U.S. proposed tailored tariffs covering roughly 5% of what Canada sends to the U.S., which amounts to 0.06% of overall U.S. economic consumption.

Breakdown of the U.S.-Canada Trade Negotiations

  • Canadian Outreach: Greer noted the U.S. did not initially ask to negotiate, but agreed in good faith after Canadian officials approached them about a month prior.
  • Near-Agreement & Collapse: Parties reached sufficient agreement by Tuesday night to announce a deal framework, but negotiations collapsed in the final hours when Canadian officials demanded broader concessions.
  • U.S. Concessions Offered: To accommodate Canadian sensitivities, the U.S. offered:
    • Steel and Aluminum: Cutting tariffs in half.
    • Automobiles & Lumber: Extensively reducing tariffs on autos and softwood lumber.
  • The Sticking Point on Trucks: Greer confirmed that negotiations stalled over expansions in scope, such as Canadian demands to ease tariffs on the heaviest medium- and heavy-duty trucks rather than just passenger cars, SUVs, and pickups.
