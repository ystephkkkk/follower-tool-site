TL;DR

Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey stated that AI could be either inflationary or disinflationary depending on whether demand shocks arrive before supply-side productivity gains. Speaking with Bloomberg's Lisa Abramowicz at Jackson Hole, Bailey also discussed UK inflation, defended a pluralistic committee approach, and emphasized that the BOE will make independent rate decisions regardless of Federal Reserve actions.

AI and Economic Shocks

  • Dual Economic Impacts: AI and robotics will generate both demand and supply shocks that affect price stability.
  • Sequencing Dictates Inflation Direction:
    • Upward Pressure: If demand shocks and related asset market effects outpace supply-side productivity gains, the net impact will be inflationary.
    • Downward Pressure: If productivity and supply-side efficiencies materialize first, the technology will prove disinflationary.
  • UK Productivity Need: Bailey noted that the UK specifically requires faster growth and improved productivity, making AI and robotics a critical source for long-term economic gains.

Monetary Policy Frameworks and Forward Guidance

  • Flaws of Forward Guidance: Bailey agreed with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh that forward guidance can become "antiquated," overly complicated, and tend toward inflexible, unconditional statements that complicate policy—noting the 2021 rate-hiking delay as an example of challenges during massive shocks like COVID-19.
  • Conditional Decision-Making: Monetary policy must look to the future due to the transmission lag of interest rates, but decisions remain inherently conditional amid ongoing uncertainty.
  • Decoupled Central Banks: The Bank of England will base its rate decisions exclusively on UK domestic economic conditions and inflation outlooks, regardless of whether the Federal Reserve hikes rates.

Committee Governance and Transparency

  • Pluralism and Deliberation: Bailey defended the Bank of England's nine-member Monetary Policy Committee as a deliberate body designed for diverse viewpoints and rigorous debate.
  • Enhanced Transparency Innovations: The BOE adopted recommendations from Ben Bernanke, introducing transparency measures such as paragraphs detailing individual members' views alongside a clear majority consensus statement.
  • Rejecting Binary Labels: Bailey declined to label himself a hawk or a dove, pointing out that second-round inflation effects in the UK remain relatively subdued due to a softening labor market.

Structural and Fiscal Challenges

  • Balance Sheet Management: Central bank balance sheet reduction is a complex global challenge; Bailey noted the importance of retaining future policy scope while reducing interest rate risk.
  • Long-Term Macro Pressures: Developed economies face interconnected pressures outside direct monetary control, including aging populations and the reversal of post-Cold War defense expenditure dividends, which are impacting fiscal policy and debt levels.

TL;DR

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee expresses concern that persistent inflation and a robust AI infrastructure build-out could be overheating the economy, while supporting a re-evaluation of Fed communication tools like the dot plot and forward guidance.

Inflation Drivers and the State of Monetary Policy

  • Current Policy Stance: Goolsbee notes that whether policy is restrictive depends on underlying inflation; with inflation running above 3%, real rates are lower than desired, leaving him nervous about inflation over the past six months.
  • Decomposing Inflation: Goolsbee attributes a significant portion of past inflation shocks to temporary factors like tariffs and oil price shocks, but warns that ongoing services inflation and recent warm readings mean these pressures cannot be perpetually dismissed as "transitory."
  • Supply Shocks & Uncertainty: The modern economy faces frequent supply shocks combined with demand-side pressures, creating unusual labor market dynamics such as low hiring and low firing, which requires central banks to incorporate higher baseline uncertainty into their frameworks.

The AI Boom and Overheating Risks

  • Data Center Build-out: High investment in AI infrastructure is creating localized resource crunches—such as shortages of land, construction contractors, and HVAC workers in Cedar Rapids, Iowa—competing with other sectors for labor and materials.
  • Aggregate vs. Sectoral Pressures: While AI investment is creating intense sectoral demand, Goolsbee notes that it only qualifies as macroeconomic overheating if the impulse broadens out to drive up wages and prices outside its immediate lane, potentially signaling that current interest rates are not restrictive enough.
  • Productivity Paradox: While successful AI-driven productivity growth could raise the neutral rate ($r^*$) and improve potential output long-term, recent data has shown six consecutive months of relatively weak productivity growth.

The Post-COVID Disinflation and Bond Market Signals

  • Immaculate Disinflation: Goolsbee credits aggressive Fed rate hikes with preserving the 2% inflation anchor—noted by TIPS inflation compensation remaining steadily anchored at 2.3% CPI—and preventing a catastrophic wage-price spiral during the post-COVID shock.
  • Bond Yields and Issuance: Rising long-term bond yields reflect a combination of inflation expectations, expectations of higher-for-longer Fed rates, and increased Treasury issuance, rather than a generalized freakout over U.S. credit default.

Re-evaluating Fed Communications and Frameworks

  • Skepticism of $r^*$: Goolsbee dismisses $r^*$, calling it the Fed's "Sasquatch" because it is completely unobservable and unhelpful for determining the next policy move.
  • Embracing Less Forward Guidance: Goolsbee supports reducing explicit forward guidance that ties policymakers' hands regarding future rate moves at specific meetings, arguing it adds unnecessary market volatility.
  • Rethinking the SEP and Dot Plot: Goolsbee is critical of the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) and dot plots, noting that they often damage Fed credibility when multi-year predictions fail and that the median dot does not represent a cohesive individual's reaction function.
  • Task Forces on Communication: All communication tools—including press conferences, meeting frequency, and dot plots—are on the table for review by the Fed's outside task forces, which Goolsbee views as a healthy post-zero-lower-bound rethink.
  • Reaction Function vs. Guidance: Goolsbee emphasizes that while pre-committing via forward guidance is unhelpful, communicating a transparent reaction function—how the Fed reads incoming data like wages and prices—remains vital.

TL;DR

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole address to outline a framework for monetary policy centered on humility, reduced forward guidance, and vigilance against persistent inflation. While praising the economy's resilience and AI-driven capital expenditure, Warsh stressed that current inflation readings remain too high and that the Fed's primary focus must remain firmly on price stability.

Artificial Intelligence and Economic Growth

  • Rapid Technological Progress: AI adoption has outpaced evangelist predictions, driven by scaling laws and significant capital deployment into related infrastructure.
  • Token Sales and Market Activity: Annualized token sales for the two leading labs exceed $100 billion, representing a 500% increase from 12 months prior.
  • Productivity and Job Task Force: The Fed has established a task force on productivity and jobs to study AI's long-term impacts on the economy, labor, and capital intensity.

Monetary Policy Principles and Forward Guidance

  • Abandoning Routine Forward Guidance: Warsh criticized routine forward guidance as having outlived its usefulness since the global financial crisis, arguing that overcommitting to future decisions inhibits the Fed's freedom to act.
  • Avoiding the "Hall of Mirrors": Warning against circular dependencies between the Fed and financial markets, Warsh emphasized that policymakers must rely on unfiltered market signals rather than letting markets trade primarily on Fed guidance.
  • Core Operating Principles: Warsh outlined foundational rules for the central bank, asserting that the 2% PCE inflation target is a firm, fixed mandate, that "money matters" in monetary policy, and that unconventional crisis tools should be used sparingly.

Current Economic Assessment

  • Robust Private Investment: Business CAPEX is rising rapidly, with the four-quarter change in investment for equipment and intangibles hitting 9% (its highest growth rate since 2021), more than half of which is attributable to AI infrastructure.
  • Corporate Profits and Equities: S&P company profits have grown by more than 20% over the past year with elevated profit margins, while equity volatility remains low.
  • Strong Private Domestic Purchases: Real consumer spending has increased by more than 2% over the past four quarters, and private domestic financial purchases have risen at a pace of 3% so far in the year.
  • Labor Market Stability: The unemployment rate stands at 4.1%, and unemployment claims on a four-week moving average are near multi-decade lows, indicating full employment alongside low turnover.

Inflation and Price Stability Concerns

  • Elevated Headline and Core Metrics: The 12-month change in the PCE price index stands at 3.7%, with the six-month change slightly above 4%, both remaining well above the Fed's 2% target.
  • Component-Level Disaggregation: Over the past 12 months, 54% of goods and services in the PCE basket showed price increases above 3%, down from post-pandemic highs of 77% but still elevated compared to historical averages.
  • Anchored Expectations: Medium-term inflation expectations and market-based compensation measures appear stable and well-anchored, though Warsh cautioned that these metrics can shift rapidly.

Facility Acquisition & Manufacturing Infrastructure

  • EDC Real Estate Deal: Sir Peter Beck noted that the Engine Development Center (EDC) building—formerly a Virgin Orbit asset valued at over $100M—was acquired out of bankruptcy for $16M.
  • Additive Manufacturing Shop: The facility houses a massive 3D printing operation run by approximately 10 people through full automation.
  • Printer Hardware & Capabilities: Features 12-laser metal 3D printers that sinter materials including titanium, Inconel superalloys, copper, and custom alloys developed in-house.
  • Production Scale:
    • The current line produces 1 Archimedes engine every 8 days.
    • The Rutherford line produces 1 engine every day.
    • Over 930 Rutherford engines have been sent to space.
  • Testing Operations: Rocket Lab maintains 2 engine test cells at Stennis Space Center in Mississippi running 20 hours a day, 7 days a week, alongside a Rutherford test cell in New Zealand.

Engine Architecture: Rutherford vs. Archimedes

  • Rutherford (Electron):
    • Design & Cycle: Powered by an electric pump cycle using kerosene and liquid oxygen (RP-1/LOX).
    • Scale & Usage: Single-use engine built for a 5-minute qualification test and a 190-second flight lifespan. 10 engines power the Electron first stage.
  • Archimedes (Neutron):
    • Design & Cycle: Stage combustion cycle using a methalox (methane and liquid oxygen) propellant combination.
    • Performance & Scale: Features a turbo pump operating at plus or minus 15,000 horsepower. Powered by 9 engines on the bottom and 1 on the top (configured for upper stage acceleration and terminal landing).
    • Longevity & Reusability: Designed for extreme reusability with a qualification burn time of 1 hour across 40 starts (compared to expendable standards).
    • Why Methane: Avoids the soot and carbon residue left by kerosene, enabling a quick-turn cadence and clean engine interiors.

Vertical Integration & Strategic Expansion

  • Complete In-House Build: Sir Peter Beck emphasized that every piece of hardware and software—including tanks, flight computers, reaction wheels, and solar panels—is built entirely by Rocket Lab.
  • Applications Layer Expansion: Following the acquisition of Iridium, Rocket Lab is entering the space applications layer to build a comprehensive com player ecosystem.
  • Strategic Philosophy: Sir Peter Beck stated that future dominant space companies require three pillars: their own rocket for launch access, in-house satellite manufacturing capability, and an applications layer.

  • Democracy of Foot Voting: Srinivasan argues that network states enable a transition from a two-party system to a thousand-city system via foot voting, allowing citizens to sort themselves based on preferences for governance, cleanliness, and biotech regulations.

Collective Mobility as Nonviolent Defense

  • Win-Lose vs. Win-Win: States that play a win-lose game by persecuting successful minority communities face the consequence of collective exit.
  • Economic Leverage: Collective mobility serves as a nonviolent alternative to a military, functioning as a communal effort where a mobile group with high earning power imposes an economic penalty on persecuting states (similar to historical precedents like Uganda).
  • Avoiding Immobility: Srinivasan contrasts traditional "blood and soil" nationalism with agile digital nomads who operate like a flock of birds, capable of relocating quickly with two suitcases.

Cryptography, AI, and Bitcoin's Role

  • AI as an Attack, Cryptography as a Defense: Srinivasan states that while AI can solve partial differential equations, it runs up against the hard wall of cryptographic boundaries, making cryptography the ultimate "Voight-Kampff test" for truth.
  • Fort Knox Obsolescence: In a low-trust era of AI-generated deepfakes, video evidence of gold reserves in Fort Knox is untrustworthy, making Bitcoin's transparent ledger essential for institutional solvency.
  • Three Regulatory Regimes: Srinivasan categorizes future regimes regarding Bitcoin into three groups:
      • Bitcoin Atheists: No-coiner regimes that ban or seize private Bitcoin ownership.
      • Bitcoin Monotheists: Bitcoin maximalists who accept only Bitcoin.
      • Bitcoin Polytheists: Jurisdictions that accept Bitcoin alongside other cryptocurrencies.

The Network School Model

  • Society as a Service (SAS): The Network School bundles physical infrastructure (gyms, housing, community events) to help digital nomads bypass setup friction and become productive quickly.
  • Education Innovation ("Pass an Exam, Get a Job"): The school aims to compress the 48-month, hundred-thousand-dollar college model into proctored, in-person examinations over short durations, promoting continuous lifelong learning rather than upfront schooling.
  • Lifestyle and Family-Friendly Infrastructure: Located in a 2-million-person city with spotlessly clean environments, the campus supports individuals, couples, families, and tech companies with outdoor activities, healthy dining, and biotech access.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Quantum-Safe Bitcoin Transactions (QSB): Srinivasan predicts QSB will be one of the most important developments in Bitcoin, noting that Ellie Ben-Sasson's open-source team figured out how to make Bitcoin transactions quantum-resistant using Script on top of existing Bitcoin without requiring a protocol change.

TL;DR

David Booth reflects on his career, the foundation of Dimensional Fund Advisors (DFA), and his new book *Stay Calm*, emphasizing that embracing uncertainty and trusting the science of efficient, factor-based markets are the keys to long-term investment success.

Academic Roots and the Birth of Quantitative Finance

  • The University of Chicago Influence: In the late 1960s and early 1970s, finance emerged as a science backed by CRSP market data starting from 1926, laying the groundwork for Eugene Fama’s Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH).
  • Early Career Pivot: Booth left his PhD studies at Chicago—earning an MBA on his way out—to join Mac MacQuown at Wells Fargo in San Francisco, where early institutional indexing and quantitative methods were being pioneered.
  • The Samsonite Account: Early quantitative experiments at Wells Fargo tested high-beta portfolios, while parallel trust department efforts led by MacQuown created early index funds that eventually evolved into multi-trillion-dollar industry giants like BlackRock.

Founding Dimensional Fund Advisors (DFA)

  • Small-Cap and Multi-Factor Genesis: Launched out of a Brooklyn Heights apartment in 1981, DFA initially focused on a small-cap strategy based on Fama's research and Ralph Banz’s dissertation on size quintiles.
  • Factor Evolution: Moving beyond single-factor beta, the firm incorporated Fama-French multi-factor models (size, value, and others) to build systematic, factor-based portfolios rather than simple market-cap weighting.
  • Trading as an Edge: Rather than trying to beat the market through stock-picking, DFA leverages execution mechanics and volatility to provide liquidity and achieve better pricing than rigid index funds that suffer from costly additions and deletions.

Philosophy of *Stay Calm*

  • Embracing Uncertainty: Uncertainty is not something to fear; it is the fundamental driver of possibility and opportunity in both life and investing.
  • First Principles and Human Ingenuity: When bad news hits (e.g., the 2020 pandemic or the 1998 financial crisis), markets drop because the bad news is already priced in; recovery is driven by human ingenuity, rewarding investors who stay calm and stay invested.
  • Plan, Don't Predict: Forecasting macroeconomic events or trying to time the market is a waste of time; investors should build a long-term plan, control what they can (risk allocation, savings), and tune out financial media noise.

Distribution, ETFs, and the Advisor Revolution

  • Fee-Only Advisors: Beginning in 1989 with Dan Wheeler, DFA partnered with fee-only financial advisors who acted as behavioral guardrails, preventing clients from abandoning their portfolios.
  • Late Entry to ETFs: DFA stayed out of ETFs for decades because institutional and advisory clients were satisfied with traditional mutual funds and dual-class structures that neutralized tax disadvantages.
  • Recent ETF Innovation: DFA launched its first ETF in 2020 and grew to become the largest active ETF issuer in the country, recently securing SEC approval to merge mutual fund and ETF pools of assets.

Philanthropy and Legacy

  • University of Chicago Booth School of Business: In November 2008, Booth gave a historic $300 million gift to the University of Chicago business school, leading to its renaming as the Chicago Booth School of Business.
  • University of Kansas Athletics: As a Lawrence native, Booth donated $300 million to KU athletics to support competitive programs and name, image, and likeness (NIL) student funding.
  • The Naismith Rules: In 2010, Booth purchased James Naismith's original 1891 typewritten rules of basketball for $4.5 million at auction (outbidding David Rubenstein) and returned them to Lawrence, Kansas.
  • MoMA Conservation Center: Serving on the board of the Museum of Modern Art for roughly 10 years, Booth endowed the conservation lab to advance the scientific preservation of modern art collections.

TL;DR

The bottleneck in AI has shifted from models to the physical infrastructure beneath them—spanning chips, memory, networking, power, and data centers. To address this unprecedented resource constraint, a16z is launching the Machine Age Fund to back hard technical problems across hardware and systems.

The AI Infrastructure Bottleneck and Surging Demand

  • Massive CapEx Expansion: Hyperscaler capital expenditure is exploding, with total spend projected to reach $700 billion this year and approximately $1 trillion next year collectively across major hyperscalers.
  • Supply Chain Depletion: Every part of the AI supply chain is under duress, with critical components and GPU capacity essentially fully booked out through 2027 and into 2028.
  • Exploding Token Consumption: The volume of tokens consumed is scaling by orders of magnitude due to the transition from simple chat applications to reasoning models, long-running agents, and multi-agent workflows.

Physical Limits and Rebuilding the Computing Stack

  • Power and Cooling Constraints: Rack power requirements are surging from roughly 5–10 kilowatts to 100–150 kilowatts, making traditional AC power obsolete and forcing a rapid transition to liquid cooling and DC power.
  • Data Center Obsolescence: Traditional data center designs are reaching physical limits regarding weight loads, acoustic output, water efficiency, and high-voltage electrical infrastructure (noting that only 2% of US electricians are certified on DC power).
  • First-Principles Innovation: Existing infrastructure was built for a different computing era, requiring founders to break down compute, memory, networking, and power from first principles and rebuild the stack.

The Shift in Venture Capital and Founder Dynamics

  • Surge in Hard Tech Founders: The share of top-tier founders pursuing complex hardware and systems problems has risen dramatically from roughly 3% historically to north of 20%–30%.
  • Capital-Driven Scaling: Unlike traditional software where throwing money at engineering fails due to the "mythical man-month," AI allows capital ($3B+ clusters) to directly drive intelligence and compress competitive leads.
  • Experienced Leadership: Complex supply chains and manufacturing requirements are drawing more experienced founders and systems architects into the hardware space alongside younger software talent.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Token Demand Growth: Demand for tokens will grow at close to 1,000% per year, far outpacing the physical rate at which supply can be expanded — Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, and Raghu Raghuram.
  • 5-to-10 Year Vision: In 5 to 10 years, America will win the infrastructure game, featuring an abundance of efficient, eco-friendly data centers, chips, memory, and power, securing technological leadership — Ben Horowitz.
