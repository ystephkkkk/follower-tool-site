TL;DR

Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey stated that AI could be either inflationary or disinflationary depending on whether demand shocks arrive before supply-side productivity gains. Speaking with Bloomberg's Lisa Abramowicz at Jackson Hole, Bailey also discussed UK inflation, defended a pluralistic committee approach, and emphasized that the BOE will make independent rate decisions regardless of Federal Reserve actions.

AI and Economic Shocks

  • Dual Economic Impacts: AI and robotics will generate both demand and supply shocks that affect price stability.
  • Sequencing Dictates Inflation Direction:
    • Upward Pressure: If demand shocks and related asset market effects outpace supply-side productivity gains, the net impact will be inflationary.
    • Downward Pressure: If productivity and supply-side efficiencies materialize first, the technology will prove disinflationary.
  • UK Productivity Need: Bailey noted that the UK specifically requires faster growth and improved productivity, making AI and robotics a critical source for long-term economic gains.

Monetary Policy Frameworks and Forward Guidance

  • Flaws of Forward Guidance: Bailey agreed with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh that forward guidance can become "antiquated," overly complicated, and tend toward inflexible, unconditional statements that complicate policy—noting the 2021 rate-hiking delay as an example of challenges during massive shocks like COVID-19.
  • Conditional Decision-Making: Monetary policy must look to the future due to the transmission lag of interest rates, but decisions remain inherently conditional amid ongoing uncertainty.
  • Decoupled Central Banks: The Bank of England will base its rate decisions exclusively on UK domestic economic conditions and inflation outlooks, regardless of whether the Federal Reserve hikes rates.

Committee Governance and Transparency

  • Pluralism and Deliberation: Bailey defended the Bank of England's nine-member Monetary Policy Committee as a deliberate body designed for diverse viewpoints and rigorous debate.
  • Enhanced Transparency Innovations: The BOE adopted recommendations from Ben Bernanke, introducing transparency measures such as paragraphs detailing individual members' views alongside a clear majority consensus statement.
  • Rejecting Binary Labels: Bailey declined to label himself a hawk or a dove, pointing out that second-round inflation effects in the UK remain relatively subdued due to a softening labor market.

Structural and Fiscal Challenges

  • Balance Sheet Management: Central bank balance sheet reduction is a complex global challenge; Bailey noted the importance of retaining future policy scope while reducing interest rate risk.
  • Long-Term Macro Pressures: Developed economies face interconnected pressures outside direct monetary control, including aging populations and the reversal of post-Cold War defense expenditure dividends, which are impacting fiscal policy and debt levels.

Sir Peter Beck gives a behind-the-scenes tour of Rocket Lab's Engine Development Center (EDC), detailing how vertical integration, advanced 3D printing, and methalox propulsion enable high-cadence production for the Electron and Neutron rockets.

Facility Acquisition & Manufacturing Infrastructure

  • EDC Real Estate Deal: Sir Peter Beck noted that the Engine Development Center (EDC) building—formerly a Virgin Orbit asset valued at over $100M—was acquired out of bankruptcy for $16M.
  • Additive Manufacturing Shop: The facility houses a massive 3D printing operation run by approximately 10 people through full automation.
  • Printer Hardware & Capabilities: Features 12-laser metal 3D printers that sinter materials including titanium, Inconel superalloys, copper, and custom alloys developed in-house.
  • Production Scale:
    • The current line produces 1 Archimedes engine every 8 days.
    • The Rutherford line produces 1 engine every day.
    • Over 930 Rutherford engines have been sent to space.
  • Testing Operations: Rocket Lab maintains 2 engine test cells at Stennis Space Center in Mississippi running 20 hours a day, 7 days a week, alongside a Rutherford test cell in New Zealand.

Engine Architecture: Rutherford vs. Archimedes

  • Rutherford (Electron):
    • Design & Cycle: Powered by an electric pump cycle using kerosene and liquid oxygen (RP-1/LOX).
    • Scale & Usage: Single-use engine built for a 5-minute qualification test and a 190-second flight lifespan. 10 engines power the Electron first stage.
  • Archimedes (Neutron):
    • Design & Cycle: Stage combustion cycle using a methalox (methane and liquid oxygen) propellant combination.
    • Performance & Scale: Features a turbo pump operating at plus or minus 15,000 horsepower. Powered by 9 engines on the bottom and 1 on the top (configured for upper stage acceleration and terminal landing).
    • Longevity & Reusability: Designed for extreme reusability with a qualification burn time of 1 hour across 40 starts (compared to expendable standards).
    • Why Methane: Avoids the soot and carbon residue left by kerosene, enabling a quick-turn cadence and clean engine interiors.

Vertical Integration & Strategic Expansion

  • Complete In-House Build: Sir Peter Beck emphasized that every piece of hardware and software—including tanks, flight computers, reaction wheels, and solar panels—is built entirely by Rocket Lab.
  • Applications Layer Expansion: Following the acquisition of Iridium, Rocket Lab is entering the space applications layer to build a comprehensive com player ecosystem.
  • Strategic Philosophy: Sir Peter Beck stated that future dominant space companies require three pillars: their own rocket for launch access, in-house satellite manufacturing capability, and an applications layer.

TL;DR

The bottleneck in AI has shifted from models to the physical infrastructure beneath them—spanning chips, memory, networking, power, and data centers. To address this unprecedented resource constraint, a16z is launching the Machine Age Fund to back hard technical problems across hardware and systems.

The AI Infrastructure Bottleneck and Surging Demand

  • Massive CapEx Expansion: Hyperscaler capital expenditure is exploding, with total spend projected to reach $700 billion this year and approximately $1 trillion next year collectively across major hyperscalers.
  • Supply Chain Depletion: Every part of the AI supply chain is under duress, with critical components and GPU capacity essentially fully booked out through 2027 and into 2028.
  • Exploding Token Consumption: The volume of tokens consumed is scaling by orders of magnitude due to the transition from simple chat applications to reasoning models, long-running agents, and multi-agent workflows.

Physical Limits and Rebuilding the Computing Stack

  • Power and Cooling Constraints: Rack power requirements are surging from roughly 5–10 kilowatts to 100–150 kilowatts, making traditional AC power obsolete and forcing a rapid transition to liquid cooling and DC power.
  • Data Center Obsolescence: Traditional data center designs are reaching physical limits regarding weight loads, acoustic output, water efficiency, and high-voltage electrical infrastructure (noting that only 2% of US electricians are certified on DC power).
  • First-Principles Innovation: Existing infrastructure was built for a different computing era, requiring founders to break down compute, memory, networking, and power from first principles and rebuild the stack.

The Shift in Venture Capital and Founder Dynamics

  • Surge in Hard Tech Founders: The share of top-tier founders pursuing complex hardware and systems problems has risen dramatically from roughly 3% historically to north of 20%–30%.
  • Capital-Driven Scaling: Unlike traditional software where throwing money at engineering fails due to the "mythical man-month," AI allows capital ($3B+ clusters) to directly drive intelligence and compress competitive leads.
  • Experienced Leadership: Complex supply chains and manufacturing requirements are drawing more experienced founders and systems architects into the hardware space alongside younger software talent.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Token Demand Growth: Demand for tokens will grow at close to 1,000% per year, far outpacing the physical rate at which supply can be expanded — Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, and Raghu Raghuram.
  • 5-to-10 Year Vision: In 5 to 10 years, America will win the infrastructure game, featuring an abundance of efficient, eco-friendly data centers, chips, memory, and power, securing technological leadership — Ben Horowitz.
