TL;DR

Frank Long of Coatue argues that AI agents will impact investing even more profoundly than coding, framing a hedge fund as a function that ingests external and internal context to output portfolio selection, sizing, and timing. Realizing this potential requires breaking down complex investment workflows into granular steps, selecting optimal execution runners—code, statistics, LLMs, or humans—and systematically tuning an enormous space of system hyper-parameters to maximize absolute quality rather than automating for its own sake.

The Hedge Fund as a Function

  • Core Investment Framework: Frank Long frames a hedge fund as a function that ingests external and internal context and outputs a simple tuple of selection, size, and timing (what stock, how much, and when).
  • External Context Inputs:
    • News, market prices, and regulatory filings must be directly accessible via API.
    • Long emphasizes that restricting an AI agent with a computer lacking logins to 75% of services severely limits its utility, drawing the analogy of hiring the world's best investors but withholding necessary tools.
  • Internal Context Inputs:
    • Firm-specific data including current positions, liquidity and risk requirements, and historical case studies or management team interactions.
    • Long notes that firms like KKR have spent decades building the technological infrastructure and rigorous cultural habits—such as filing notes on every trade and decision—to successfully expose their operations to AI.

Workflow Decomposition and the News Case Study

  • Pipeline Breakdown: To manage complexity, the hedge fund function is broken into distinct steps:
    • Gather: Pulling and filtering raw information to separate material market signals from irrelevant noise (e.g., distinguishing local data center permitting news from Korean NBA fandom).
    • Analyze: Evaluating gathered information to form public and private market views.
    • Decide: Translating views into actionable portfolio selections, sizes, and timing parameters.
  • Case Study on Korean Semiconductor News:
    • Analyzing Korean-language media is crucial for AI and semiconductor themes, effectively augmenting non-Korean-speaking investment staff.
    • The pipeline involves data collection (via MCP, CLI, or licensed feeds), data cleaning, chunking strategies (handling text versus images), filtration, translation, extraction, and cross-referencing against internal notes and meeting recordings.

Four Kinds of Execution Runners

  • Deterministic Code: Fast, cheap, and reliable; ideal for explicit rules (e.g., executing when a price-to-earnings ratio is under 15).
  • Statistics and Classical Machine Learning: Effective for fitting regression lines and modeling complex numerical patterns beyond simple if-statements.
  • LLMs and AI Agents: The state-of-the-art choice for complex qualitative tasks like summarization and natural language interpretation.
  • People: Indispensable runners for high-judgment tasks that require navigating the real world, such as meeting management teams (noting that robots or LLMs are unsuited for direct executive engagement).
  • Quality-Driven Selection: Long stresses that there is no glory in automating for its own sake; the objective is to deploy the optimal runner for every specific sub-task to maximize absolute quality.

Hyper-Parameter Search Space and Complexity

  • Multi-Runner Reality: Most practical steps rely on combinations of deterministic code, statistical methods, LLMs, and humans operating in conjunction.
  • Combinatorial Hyper-Parameters: The system features massive degrees of freedom across control flows, model selection (big, medium, or small models), thinking effort levels (e.g., extra-high, high, medium), harnesses, loaded skills, and execution hooks.
  • Hill Climbing and North Stars: With an infinite search space across a massively complex graph of nodes, teams must establish clear evaluation metrics and compartmentalized components to hill climb against, avoiding aimless tinkering.

The Rolls-Royce Breakup & In-House Pivot

  • Outsourcing Disaster: Blake Scholl reflects that relying on an established legacy engine maker was a mistake, noting that Rolls-Royce walked away publicly via the press in August 2022 after realizing their next-gen platform had been scrapped and could not meet Boom's supersonic requirements.
  • Political Fallout: The abrupt breakup left Boom temporarily "unfundable" and the center of aerospace trade press drama amid executive leadership changes at Rolls-Royce.
  • The Brian Chesky Advice: Drawing inspiration from Brian Chesky's advice on leading through crisis by returning to core identity, Scholl decided to build the engine in-house, partnering with a Florida-based contract engineering team composed of former Pratt & Whitney supersonic engine designers.

Solving Supersonic & Tapping the AI Energy Boom

  • Boomless Cruise Discovery: Developing their own engine allowed Boom to optimize transonic performance, accidentally solving sonic boom propagation six months prior to the XB-1 test aircraft breaking the sound barrier in early 2025.
  • AI Power Shortage Opportunity: Recognizing that supersonic jet engines function very similarly to continuous full-power turbines, Boom adapted their core design into a 42-megawatt natural gas turbine specifically sized for modular AI data center deployment.
  • Commercial Breakthrough: Backed by early investor Sam Altman, this pivot created a high-margin revenue stream that transformed Boom from a paycheck-to-paycheck startup into a self-funded enterprise.

Rethinking the Passenger Experience & Aerospace Stagnation

  • Lovable Cabin Design: Total engine control allowed Boom to redesign the fuselage and cabin to fix traditional passenger pain points:
    • Seating & Boarding: No middle seats anywhere, with a targeted 5-minute boarding and 5-minute deplaning process.
    • Integrated Technology: Built-in Starlink antenna hardware with no exterior humps or drag.
  • The "Missing Generation": Scholl critiques the stagnation of legacy aerospace and energy firms like Boeing—which hasn't designed a new commercial airplane since 2004—arguing that heavy regulation, corporate welfare, and short-term Wall Street thinking hollowed out the industry.
  • Resilient Defense Pivot: Boom's digitally engineered, highly flexible manufacturing lines are deliberately built to pivot rapidly to support defense industrial needs in a crisis if required.

Predictions & Forward Calls

The Agent-Driven Internet & 1,000x Search Scale

  • The Shift from Human to Agent Traffic: Parag Agrawal explains that the web is evolving from being consumed primarily by humans in browsers to being heavily driven by AI agents acting on users' behalf.
  • Overcoming Human Constraints: Human web consumption is fundamentally limited by time, attention, and alternative activities (like short-form video or Netflix), whereas AI agents are limited only by economically valuable work and willingness to pay for compute.
  • The 1,000x Volume Hypothesis: Parallel was founded on the thesis that agents will use the web 1,000 times more than humans ever did, requiring entirely new generations of search infrastructure to handle billions of automated queries.

Rethinking Search Engines for AI Agents

  • Differences in Human vs. Agent Search: Human search relies on under-specified keywords, quick impatience with slow load times, and a preference for information above the fold (e.g., finding media articles instead of 200-page SEC PDFs).
  • Optimizing for Context and Latency: An agent-optimized search engine bypasses human presentation flaws by extracting precise paragraphs (such as page 77 of a PDF) and feeding them directly into the agent's context window.
  • Token Efficiency: Traditional search engines force agents to waste valuable tokens by fumbling around the web pretending to be humans, whereas specialized agent search engines deliver faster, more efficient results.

Media Business Models & The "Rebel Alliance"

  • The Core Business Dilemma: The traditional web relies on ads and monthly subscriptions; without new economic models, AI agents scraping content without paying will force publishers to block them, which Agrawal considers value-destructive.
  • Differential Pricing for Agents: Agrawal envisions a scalable pricing mechanic where information usage is priced based on consequence—charging a high-frequency trader's agent significantly more for a data point than a casual reader's agent.
  • The Rebel Alliance: Parallel is positioning itself as a partner to publishers (such as *The Atlantic* and *Fortune*) to ensure media companies get paid in proportion to the GPUs and tokens consumed by agents, providing data transparency that big AI labs currently withhold.

Reflections on Twitter, X, and Social Platforms

Salesforce executive John Somorjai and a16z General Partner Alex Immerman analyze Salesforce's nearly $100 billion M&A strategy—highlighting major acquisitions like Slack, MuleSoft, and AI-native startups like Finn and Listen Labs—while examining venture capital valuations, AI-driven outcome pricing, and startup diligence pitfalls.

M&A Strategy & Acquisition Playbook

  • Core Acquisition Framework: Salesforce evaluates potential deals using a strict internal three-part criteria:
    • Strategic Fit & Customer Success: Assessing customer demand, product gaps, and the value the asset can immediately drive.
    • Business Acceleration & Stack Integration: Determining how fast the technology can integrate into the existing stack, evaluate technical feasibility, and review cultural alignment and talent.
    • Financial & Shareholder Value: Balancing reasonable valuations and timelines for value accretion against current market conditions.
  • The Distribution Advantage: Leveraging Salesforce's massive sales engine:
    • Salesforce maintains 15,000 sales reps and an ecosystem of 8,000+ ISV partners.
    • Acquisitions like Listen Labs gain instant access to global enterprise, mid-market, and small business distribution that would take a startup years to build independently.
  • Build-versus-Buy Calculus: Speed to market dictates the decision:
    • Software development has never been faster or cheaper, but hiring scarce AI engineers remains extremely difficult.
    • End-to-end M&A integration typically takes 6 to 9 months, serving as the benchmark for whether Salesforce can build solutions internally faster than buying them.

AI, Agents, & Outcome-Based Pricing

  • The Rise of Outcome-Based Pricing: Shifting from per-seat models to resolution-based value:
    • Products like Finn pioneer pricing based on successful resolutions rather than software seats because time-to-value is exceptionally fast (16 days average externally, 14 days internal pilot).
    • AI agents capture a larger share of the enterprise labor budget by automating entire workflows across customer support and sales.
  • High-Performance AI Deployments: Quantifiable metrics from recent acquisitions:
    • Finn (Service Cloud AI): Handles over 40% of traffic and delivers 65% resolutions without human escalation on help.salesforce.com.
    • Qualified (Piper Agent): Replaces traditional sales development rep (SDR) pipelines by handling website traffic, answering product questions via lifelike avatars, and automatically booking meetings.

Major Platform Products & Cultural Impact

  • Slack's Evolution: Transforming from collaboration tool to enterprise AI interface:
    • Acquired in 2020, Slack revenue is now nearly 3x what it was at acquisition and stands as Salesforce's fastest-growing product.
    • Serves as the ultimate conversational interface for AI agents and the central corpus mapping corporate decisions and organizational context.
  • Velocity and Enterprise Verticals: Injecting industry-specific go-to-market strategies:
    • Led by David Schmire, the Velocity acquisition expanded Salesforce from a horizontal CRM into 13 dedicated industry verticals.
    • Acquired founders have risen to top leadership internally, with former founders stepping into Chief Product Officer roles four times in company history.

Salesforce Ventures & Global Investment Mandate

  • Venture Portfolio Scale: Investing across 750+ companies with 29 total acquisitions:
    • Early ecosystem investments include DocuSign, Eninova, Twilio, and Snowflake.
    • Created a $1 billion AI fund in early 2023 to back foundational model leaders like Anthropic (invested across 6 rounds), Mistral, and Cohere.
  • Expansion into Frontier Technologies: Diverging beyond traditional enterprise software:
    • Backing voice (11 Labs), video (Runway), and cybersecurity (Wiz).
    • Venturing aggressively into physical AI and robotics with investments in Figure AI, Physical Intelligence, Dina Robotics, Skilled, drone delivery via Zipline, and Japanese logistics robotics company Mugen.

Startup Diligence & Deal Killers

  • Common Diligence Red Flags: Why deals collapse during deep review:
    • Hidden Attrition: Founders relying solely on ARR metrics often mask underlying customer churn, product quality issues, or unhappy users.
    • Security Vulnerabilities: Security is the single function that can issue an immediate deal-ending red flag; Salesforce penetration tests have occasionally exposed flaws allowing unauthorized access to source code and employee laptops.
    • Financial Controls: Lack of strong controls over financials and disorganized customer contract data slow down or kill otherwise promising transactions.

TL;DR

Physical AI is significantly more difficult than text-based LLMs due to the absence of a pre-existing physical data record and the complex, bidirectional coupling of the real world. Overcoming this requires hybrid training architectures that combine conventional physics simulators, neural world models, and high-performance hardware.

  • Isaac Lab & Newton Updates: Spencer Huang announced updates to Isaac Lab integrating the Newton physics framework (moving away from PhysX) to provide developer quality-of-life improvements and better scaling capabilities.
  • DGX Station Blueprints: Released as a starting point for training robotic policies directly on desk-ready workstations.
  • Agile One SN Reference Platform: Built alongside Agile, featuring out-of-the-box integration with Thor, optimized firmware/software stacks, pre-configured Groot, and Uji hands to eliminate weeks or months of integration friction.

Why Physical AI Is Fundamentally Harder Than LLMs

  • Lack of Historical Data: Spencer Huang noted that while LLMs benefited from centuries of written and recorded human history, no comparable dataset encapsulates the physics and interactions of the physical world.
  • Bidirectional Coupling: Unlike text, physical robots manipulate the world and the world manipulates back, creating complex deformations and forces that require robust physics solvers.
  • Teleop and Data Collection Bottlenecks: Teleoperation is uniquely difficult due to a lack of force feedback (comparable to numbing one's hands), though recent advancements leverage egocentric and human-pose data alongside imitation learning.

The Ideal Data Diet and Hybrid Simulation

  • Conventional vs. Neural Simulators: Spencer Huang explained that conventional simulators provide grounded, reliable physics but are tedious to build and lack diversity. Neural simulators (world models like Cosmos) offer vast diversity and multi-modality (tactile, acoustics, force feedback) but can hallucinate physics if not properly anchored.
  • Automatic Domain Randomization: Using neural simulators to randomize materials, lighting, and deformations while feeding them through a conventional physics-backed environment creates a hybridized training diet.
  • Sensitivity Analysis and Failure-Based Learning: Policies are tested inside Isaac Lab arenas, where tasks are decomposed into subtasks to isolate failures (such as collisions or lighting issues) and enable iterative, human-like failure recovery.

Whole-Body Control and Small-Scale Models

  • Sonic Model (42M Parameters): Spencer Huang highlighted Sonic as an open-weight, open-access model that achieves whole-body control for tracking, walking, crawling, and dancing across different body morphologies using only 42 million parameters.
  • System Hierarchy: Low-level models like Sonic handle rapid "System Zero" reactions (staying upright, rapid stepping), while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models handle long-horizon reasoning.
  • Whole-Body Maneuvers: Whole-body control bridges the gap between lower and upper body actions, allowing robots to walk and manipulate simultaneously (such as walking while carrying a heavy box or holding a cup).

The Sim-to-Real Gap and Real-World Adaptation

  • The "Doomed to Succeed" Phenomenon: Spencer Huang warned that developers are often doomed to succeed in simulation because parameters can always be tuned to fit a virtual environment without guaranteeing zero-shot real-world transfer.
  • End-to-End Nature of the Gap: The sim-to-real gap is not a single phase but spans data collection, kinematic mapping (retargeting human data to robot joints), training configurations, and deployment evaluation.
  • Emergent Failure Recovery: With sufficient training and simulation across envelopes, models exhibit emergent behaviors—such as robot dogs or humanoids adapting to walk on three working legs or a slightly broken ankle joint.

Silicon Valley venture capital is experiencing unprecedented velocity and multi-billion-dollar funding rounds driven by the explosive growth of AI, where startups now target trillion-dollar outcomes and face massive capital demands primarily driven by compute. Abstract Ventures Managing Partner Ramtin Naimi and host Patrick discuss how this market differs from 2021 through structural shifts in valuations, venture fund deployment pacing, and liquidity crunches.

Deal-Level Dynamics & The Venture Frenzy

  • Insane Deal Velocity: Rounds and follow-on financings are happening faster than ever, with top-tier founders commanding intense investor demand and preferential pricing.
  • The "Cuck Tranche": When investors lose out on a primary round, instead of waiting for a later stage, many are paying 3x to 5x higher valuations in tandem with the primary round to secure secondary access.
  • Revenue Nuances: While companies report 10x to 20x year-over-year growth, revenues often carry caveats such as low or negative margins, high customer concentration, annualized figures from short-term data sales, or usage-based consumer models rather than pure SaaS metrics.
  • Margin Expansion: Conversely, foundation model providers and portfolio companies are seeing rapid margin improvements; Anthropic reported 85% gross margins in the first half of the year, while some portfolio companies are scaling revenue 200% to 700% while margins shift from -15% to +35%.

Market Comparison: Today vs. 2021

  • Similarities: Both eras feature rapid capital deployment, intense FOMO, and late-stage public market crossover investors pushing aggressively into venture deals. Abstract Ventures observed a recent uptick in term sheets being renegotiated or pulled after being offered, reminiscent of late 2021.
  • Differences in Quality & Pace: Unlike 2021—where companies had constrained TAMs, modest 3x–5x SAS growth, and required a 7-year timeline to reach $100M ARR—today's companies are finding product-market fit at record speeds and driving tangible value that everyday consumers and families actively integrate into daily life.

The Venture Fund Capital Crunch & LP Liquidity

  • Accelerated Deployment: Mega platform funds modeled for 18-to-24-month deployment windows are returning to market 3x faster because they severely underestimated the capital required to secure target ownership percentages at higher company valuations.
  • Example Fund Pacing: One fund raised $2.5 billion, started deploying in July, and scheduled a return to market by January; another fund deployed $3 billion over 9 months and returned seeking $5 billion.
  • Funding Delays: For the first time in this cycle, VCs issuing term sheets (e.g., a $150M commitment or a $40M-out-of-$50M commitment) are asking founders to wait several months for funding while they close new funds and set up capital call facilities.
  • LP Liquidity Bottlenecks: Traditional LPs (endowments and foundations) expected much higher liquidity by now. Meaningful liquidity is heavily concentrated in just a few massive names—SpaceX, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Databricks—meaning LPs lacking exposure to these specific companies are facing cash constraints.

Compute Demands and Financing Challenges

  • The NeoCloud Landscape: While hyperscalers and frontier model labs drive the bulk of compute demand, roughly 80 to 90 NeoClouds have raised $1 billion to $5 billion.   • Benchmark Maxing vs. Commercial Viability: It is increasingly difficult to discern whether new model launches are genuinely groundbreaking at scale or simply "benchmark-maxed" to perform well on standardized tests without immediate commercial viability.
  • Alternative Financing Structures: Companies like General Catalyst offer customer value funds, and some VCs are exploring financing startups with compute resources instead of cash. However, equity dilution remains preferred due to the complexities of compute longevity and the avoidance of restrictive debt covenants.

Seed Strategy: Proactive vs. Reactive Investing

  • Goalpost Shift: Terminal outcome expectations have shifted from $10 billion (2016) to $100 billion (2021) to $1-trillion outcomes today, led by OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX (with Naimi predicting 2 to 3 more, not 40).
  • Proactive Alpha: Reactive investors who chased obvious consensus assets (like 100x SaaS multiples in 2021) underperformed. Proactive investors who back exceptional founders working on non-obvious markets generate outsized alpha.
  • Case Study — Conduit: Seeded 9 months ago in Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI) via a non-invasive head-worn device achieving 80% thought accuracy with small training data. After hitting 30,000 hours of training data, BCI became a hot category, leading to a heavily oversubscribed round with 13 term sheets.
  • Case Study — Etched: Despite widespread VC skepticism that a startup could out-compete chip fabrication incumbents, Sequoia's $10 billion valuation validation proved founders can run circles around incumbents before markets recognize the trend.

TL;DR

Despite widespread access to AI tools, only a small fraction of enterprises see positive financial returns because intelligence has become cheap; the true scarce advantage is organizational judgment. Leading firms capture this advantage by designing the right problems, building shared capabilities, and compounding their operational learnings.

The State of AI Adoption & The Scarcity of Judgment

  • Disappointing Enterprise Returns: Recent studies show only 37% of surveyed enterprises report a positive contribution to EBIT from AI, and just 6% qualify as high performers (yielding about a 5% contribution to EBIT). While 66% report productivity increases, only 40% see cost reductions and 20% achieve revenue growth.
  • Abundant Intelligence vs. Scarce Judgment: With intelligence becoming cheap and abundant across software, analytics, recommendations, and reports, mere answers no longer generate value. Sid Pednekar notes that the scarce resource is judgment—the creativity, taste, and trade-off decision-making needed to choose what matters and how to act when there is no single obvious answer.

Pillar 1: Design the Right Problem

  • Focus Over Tools: Laggards (Company B) start with the tool or co-pilot (e.g., passing earnings transcripts into a summarization firehose), which merely acts as a "faster horse" that loses context through information reduction. Leaders (Company A) obsess over the right problem, such as identifying why material signals arrive too late or locating workflow bottlenecks.
  • Deep Investment vs. Innovation Theater: High-performing firms avoid scattering document bots and retrieval tools across random pilots ("productivity theater"). Instead, they spend more than 80% of their investments on high-value, end-to-end use cases that transform key business functions.
  • Workflow Redesign: Winners do not just automate existing processes; they redesign how work gets done. High-performing cohorts are 75% more likely to redesign workflows (compared to 25% of the lagging cohort), shifting analysts from simple summarization to defining investment theses, gathering refuting evidence, and monitoring exceptions.

Pillar 2: Build Shared Capabilities

  • Breaking Down Silos: Technology and business are converging, with forward-deployed engineers, product managers, and portfolio managers working together as large enterprises increasingly view their technology arms as business growth functions.
  • Standardizing the Core and Personalizing the Edges: Leading teams build shared cores—such as standardized data sets, semantic layers, and captured trade-offs for data scientists—so that each new hire operates as a plug-and-play module standing on the shoulders of predecessors.
  • Enterprise Modernization for Agents: Because traditional enterprises were built for single humans interacting with single applications, agents that cross workflows and applications require infrastructure modernization. 71% of organizations are modernizing their core infrastructure to support AI, with 23% investing 6% to 10% of their annual revenue into core system modernization to overcome data quality and reuse challenges.
  • Tiered Agent Deployment: Organizations deploy different agent types, including subject-matter expert agents, workflow agents (e.g., Slack bots opening and closing tickets), and chief-of-staff agents that monitor organizational state.

Pillar 3: Compound the Advantage

  • Systemic Feedback Loops: Leading companies ensure that individual learnings, successes, and failures do not stay isolated. When a correction is needed or an error occurs, that knowledge is systematically fed back into the organization through updated tools, context, or training.
  • Preserving Evidence: Compounding requires preserving the underlying evidence alongside operational learnings to create a continuous flywheel that frees up human capacity and reinvests it into key monitored metrics.
