**TL;DR**
GPT-5.5 (codename “Spud”) represents OpenAI’s shift from language-only models to proactive AI agents capable of autonomous computer use, signaling a transition toward a "compute-powered economy" where intelligence is the primary resource for solving complex real-world problems.

## GPT-5.5: From Language Model to Computer-Use Agent
*   **New Intelligence Class:** Greg Brockman describes GPT-5.5 as a "new class of intelligence" that focuses on end-to-end problem solving rather than just predicting text.
*   **Functional Computer Use:** The model is optimized for interacting with standard software, including creating slides, managing spreadsheets, and navigating web browsers.
*   **Proactive Task Execution:** 
    *   Unlike previous models that require step-by-step instructions, "Spud" is designed to be more intuitive, understanding high-level goals with less "prompt engineering."
    *   OpenAI researchers are already using the model as a "research partner," allowing it to run variations of experiments overnight without human intervention in the terminal or code.
*   **Shift in Focus:** OpenAI has moved from optimizing for academic benchmarks to focusing on "real-world application" in sectors like finance, sales, and marketing.

## The Economic Moat: "The Machine That Makes the Machine"
*   **Defensibility:** Brockman argues that OpenAI’s moat is not just the model weights, but the "end-to-end co-design" and the repeatable team process used to build them.
*   **Pricing and Jevons Paradox:** 
    *   While GPT-5.5 is priced at double the cost of GPT-5.4, Brockman notes that historical trends show intelligence costs dropping by orders of magnitude over time.
    *   He cites the **Jevons Paradox**, suggesting that as the cost of intelligence decreases, demand and total activity will skyrocket, outstripping supply.
*   **Operating Margins:** OpenAI’s core business model is defined as renting, building, or buying compute and reselling it as intelligence at a positive operating margin.

## Deployment Strategy and Cybersecurity Risks
*   **OpenAI vs. Anthropic:** Brockman addresses the difference between OpenAI’s "iterative deployment" (broad public access) and Anthropic’s decision to keep its "Mythos" model private. 
    *   He argues that neither "centralized control" nor "unfiltered release" is the perfect solution, but emphasizes "democratic access" as a core value.
*   **Cyber Safeguards:**
    *   OpenAI utilizes a **Preparedness Framework** to evaluate risks in cyber and bio-intelligence.
    *   The model is tested by "defenders" (security professionals) before release to ensure ecosystem resilience.
    *   Brockman asserts that GPT-5.5 is not "cyber permissive," meaning it has built-in safeguards to prevent it from being used for malicious attacks.

## The Future of Autonomous "Fleet" Agents
*   **The Super App:** OpenAI is evolving its "Codex" harness into a "Super App" for general computer work, shifting the user’s role from "worker" to "CEO" of a fleet of agents.
*   **Workspace Governance:** 
    *   OpenAI recently released "Workspace Agents," which allow enterprises to host Codex harnesses in the cloud.
    *   **Governance Features:** To manage the "law of large numbers" risk, IT organizations can now oversee all agent conversations and set specific guardrails within the enterprise environment.
*   **Compute Scarcity:** Brockman predicts a future world defined by "compute scarcity," where the scale of available hardware determines the speed at which humanity can solve massive problems, such as drug discovery or curing Alzheimer’s.
