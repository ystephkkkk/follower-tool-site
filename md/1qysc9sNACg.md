## TL;DR
Neros Technologies operates a new 250,000-square-foot "Millennium One" facility designed to scale production to one million drones per year, currently manufacturing over 250 drones daily under one roof. Co-Founder & CEO Soren Monroe-Anderson and host Molly O'Shea tour the factory floors, detailing end-to-end engineering, automated tracking, and aggressive in-house vertical integration.

## Facility Overview & Scaling
* **Millennium One Facility:** 
  * **Scale and Footprint:** A 250,000-square-foot factory designed to eliminate multiple-site logistics and reach a target capacity of one million drones per year — a massive upgrade from their previous 15,000-square-foot facility with 100 people.
  * **Unified Operations:** Houses engineering, testing, supply chain, manufacturing, and future component expansion under one roof to ensure tight, cross-functional collaboration.
* **Current Production Metrics:** 
  * **Output Volume:** Manufacturing a little over 250 drones per day at normal operating rates.
  * **Facility Highlights:** Features a massive 30x60-foot American flag (noted as the largest in the South Bay) and an in-house powerlifting gym for employees.

## Engineering Labs & R&D
* **Flight Test & Prototyping Teams:** 
  * **Development Testing:** A 10-person flight test team operates out in the desert roughly two hours away, typically running two teams in separate locations every single day to push prototypes to failure.
  * **Testing Hardware:** Racks of engineering development drones and survivability-tested Archer AI drones are constantly pushed to their limits.
* **Bandit Interceptor Development:** 
  * **Performance Progress:** V1 Bandit interceptors reached about 300 km/h (roughly 40 km/h faster than earlier iterations) after just a couple of months of development.
  * **Future Benchmark:** Targeting roughly 400 km/h to effectively intercept faster jet-powered Shahed variants using bigger motors, larger batteries, and improved aerodynamics.
* **Design & Manufacturing Philosophy:** 
  * **Airframe Construction:** Uses 2D-cut carbon plates for cheap, lightweight mass manufacturing, pairing aluminum bottom plates with thermal interface material to act as a motherboard heat sink.
  * **Rejection of Battlefield 3D Printing:** Dismisses battlefield 3D printing for core components (circuit boards, motors, radios), noting that true mass manufacturing requires efficient, centralized factories rather than field printing.

## Assembly Lines & Manufacturing Software
* **Production Line Layout:** 
  * **Station Design:** Features side-by-side manual assembly lines for drones and ground stations, equipped with preloaded part bins and preset-torque drivers to ease operator burden.
  * **Software Tracking:** Manufacturing software deployed across tablets tracks serial numbers of critical components and logs station completion times to identify bottlenecks.
* **Automation Strategy:** 
  * **Manual vs. Automated:** Current drone versions are not designed around automation, making manual stations ideal for rapid iteration; full automation will require a ground-up product redesign.

## Testing & New Product Introduction (NPI)
* **100% End-of-Line Flight Testing:** 
  * **Stress Testing:** Every single manufactured drone undergoes 100% flight testing with a payload to simulate high-stress conditions.
  * **Data Collection:** Automated pre-test stations screen for issues, with long-term data collection aimed at eventually reducing flight testing to sample testing.
* **New Product Introduction (NPI) Line:** 
  * **Archer AI Line:** A dedicated NPI space larger than their entire old production line is currently used to prep the Archer AI product for mass production later this year.
  * **In-House Expansion:** Massive open factory space is reserved for bringing component manufacturing in-house to achieve complete vertical integration.
