## The AI Boom & Its Potential Achilles' Heel
* **Nvidia's New Financing Deal:** Nvidia is partnering with six large asset managers on a **$500 billion** financing push to treat compute infrastructure like commercial real estate toll roads or borrowable assets (likely involving securitizations). This could improve hyperscaler free cash flow and boost revenue for participating banks and private credit companies.
* **The LLM Dependency Risk (The Achilles' Heel):** 
  * While hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google) appear financially secure on the surface, their AI revenue is heavily concentrated: **70% of hyperscaler AI revenue** and **25% to 35% of total cloud revenue** comes from Anthropic and OpenAI.
  * Oracle has a **$600 billion backlog**, with half originating from OpenAI alone.
  * Both Anthropic and OpenAI currently lose billions and rely on raising capital to survive.
* **Competitive Pressures on LLMs:** 
  * There are virtually no moats around LLMs, as users switch models constantly.
  * Chinese LLMs are open weight models that are much cheaper, and enterprises are using them increasingly. Overcharging for tokens will be difficult if Chinese AI is "good enough," creating the potential for a destructive price war.
  * Should Anthropic and OpenAI fail, hyperscaler margins will compress, growth will slow, and the entire AI chain could go into reverse.

## Upstarts vs. Established Incumbents
* **The Entertainment Industry Parable:** 
  * Netflix began as an upstart mailing DVDs, disrupting Blockbuster (which declared bankruptcy in September 2010). 
  * Incumbent entertainment companies greedily sold their old syndicated show rights to Netflix for 100% margin "found money," choosing short-term quarterly margins over long-term survival. Netflix used this base to build merchandise ownership, launch streaming (launched Jan 2007), and eventually produce original content that crushed the incumbents. 
  * Today, Netflix's market cap exceeds **$300 billion**, compared to Disney at **$178 billion**, Warner Bros at **$69 billion**, and Paramount at **$10 billion**. However, Netflix's growth is now slowing, pushing its stock down **21%** year-to-date.
* **Circle & Stablecoin Challenges:** 
  * Circle went public on June 4, 2025, at **$30**, rapidly climbing to **$240** on June 20th before falling to **$71**. 
  * Investors assumed Circle would easily conquer the payment space, but Eisman warns this is a "hopeless cause." Visa and Mastercard fiercely dominate payments and have successfully fought off every disintermediating upstart. Circle (market cap **$18 billion**) faces a brutal battle and should consider selling.
* **Corporate Boards:** Conceptual checks on CEOs are largely window dressing; CEOs handpick compliant board members (such as professors or former government officials) who lack complex financial services expertise and are motivated by catered meetings, status, and compensation.
