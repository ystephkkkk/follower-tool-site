**TL;DR**
Former Fed Vice Chair Richard Clarida suggests that a potential "Kevin Warsh Fed" would likely pivot toward a new Fed-Treasury accord focused on balance sheet composition rather than yield caps, while potentially guiding interest rates down to 3%.

## The Proposed Fed-Treasury Accord and Fed Independence
* **Historical Context:** Clarida references the 1951 Accord, which established Fed independence by ending the practice of capping Treasury yields to support WWII debt.
* **Modern Interpretation:** A new accord under Kevin Warsh would likely focus on the **composition of the Fed’s portfolio** rather than rate caps.
    * **MBS Holdings:** Discussions may focus on whether the Fed should continue holding $2 trillion in mortgage-backed securities.
    * **Portfolio Duration:** A shift toward shorter-term T-bills and away from 10- and 30-year Treasuries is a primary topic of interest.
* **Feasibility:** While Clarida considers these discussions appropriate and ongoing since before the pandemic, he believes a formal "accord" is still a long-term prospect rather than an immediate change.

## Leadership Transition and the "Warsh Fed"
* **Interest Rate Path:** Clarida expects Warsh to persuade the committee to continue rate cuts down to approximately **3%**, provided economic data aligns with current expectations.
* **Jay Powell’s Departure:** Historically, Fed Chairs "ride off into the sunset." Clarida views it as unlikely that Powell will remain as a governor through the end of his term in 2028, despite the precedent set by Marriner Eccles.
* **Culture of Dissent:** Clarida argues that the Fed should not fear Bank of England-style "close votes" (e.g., 5-4 decisions). He notes that while the Fed historically prizes consensus, thoughtful dissent should be viewed as a feature of robust policy.
* **Confirmation Outlook:** Warsh is expected to be confirmed due to high qualifications, though the hearing process may be delayed.

## Monetary Theory: Models vs. Reality
* **Skepticism of Models:** Warsh has a "voluminous paper trail" expressing skepticism toward flawed, backward-looking academic models.
* **The Clarida/Powell Defense:** Clarida contends the Powell Fed was never "handcuffed" to models.
    * **2019 Precedent:** The Fed cut rates in 2019 with unemployment at a 50-year low because they recognized growth potential beyond what the models predicted.
    * **"The Doom Loop":** Clarida acknowledges recent theories (such as those by Eswar Prasad) suggesting the global economy may be entering a "non-linear" period of instability that standard linear macro models cannot capture.

## Economic Outlook: Tariffs and Labor
* **Muted Tariff Impact:** Contrary to early inflationary fears, the 2025 outlook remains stable. Clarida attributes this to three factors:
    * **Negotiation Strategy:** Actual tariffs collected (approx. 10%) are significantly lower than "liberation day" threats (35%).
    * **Corporate Absorption:** High profit margins allowed U.S. companies to absorb tariff costs rather than passing them to consumers.
    * **Offsetting Growth:** Buoyant tech capex and the "wealth effect" from a strong stock market have countered potential headwinds.
* **Labor Market Cooling:** The Fed is transitioning from an inflation-only focus to a dual-mandate balance. Clarida notes that while unemployment is at a healthy level, payroll gains are modest and could even show negative revisions for the second half of the year.

## Market Pricing and Volatility (PIMCO Perspective)
* **Yield Ranges:** Ten-year Treasury yields have remained in a relatively tight band between 3.75% and 4.75% for over two years.
* **Structural Shifts:** The market is pricing in a steeper yield curve than pre-pandemic eras, with higher underlying real rates. 
* **Volatility:** Clarida expects "somewhat elevated volatility" compared to the decade of suppressed rates and negative-yielding sovereign debt that preceded the pandemic.
