## World Models vs. Large Language Models (LLMs)
* **The LLM Limitation:** 
  * **Text-Based Proxies:** LLMs learn entirely from human-written books and text on the internet—like someone born in a room who reads books for centuries without ever going outside.
  * **Missing Traits:** Result in experts with zero direct experience of the real world, lacking common sense or the ability to handle unfamiliar situations.
* **The World Model Approach:** 
  * **Direct Learning:** Models learn directly from the real world—video, audio, sensory data, and physical interaction—starting from scratch like a baby without language.
  * **Data & Modalities:** Utilizes massive amounts of video data (the easiest to source) and touch/sensory data gathered via robotics.
* **LLM vs. World Model Division:**
  * **LLM Strengths:** Unbeatable for language-first, discrete, low-dimensional sequence problems (mathematics, programming, text).
  * **World Model Strengths:** Vastly superior for high-dimensional, noisy, long-horizon problems.
* **Flaws of Current Approaches:**
  * **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:** Described by LeBrun as a "very bad hack"—using an LLM as a hammer for everything, resulting in inaccurate, slow, and expensive systems ($100 of compute per 24 hours for a home robot).

## Advice for Founders & Ambition
* **Founder Strengths:** Founders strong in go-to-market and sales should wait for mature markets, whereas engineering-led founders excel at taking very early tech and building initial products.
* **The Ambition Framework:** 
  * **Narrow Problem, Large Vision:** Founders should pick an extremely narrow problem (one thing for one industry or set of customers) while holding an extremely large vision for the space.
  * **European Ambition:** European founders often think too small and avoid taking risks; big ambitions require risking failure to achieve breakthrough results.
