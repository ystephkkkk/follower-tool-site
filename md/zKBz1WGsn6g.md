**TL;DR**
The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at 3.5%–3.75% amid "misbehaving" inflation and geopolitical uncertainty, while Chair Jerome Powell announced he will remain on the Board as a Governor after his chairmanship ends to protect the institution from unprecedented political and legal attacks.

## Monetary Policy and Inflation Outlook
* **Interest Rate Decision:** The FOMC maintained the federal funds rate at 3.5% to 3.75%, characterizing the current stance as "appropriately restrictive" or at the "high end of neutral."
* **Elevated Inflation:** Total PCE price inflation rose to 3.5% through March, driven largely by a spike in global energy prices. 
    * **Core Inflation:** Core PCE (excluding food/energy) stands at 3.2%, which Powell attributed primarily to the effects of tariffs on goods.
    * **Target Timing:** The Fed expects tariff-related price increases to subside over the next two quarters; however, rate cuts are unlikely until there is clear progress on both tariffs and energy costs.
* **Internal Dissent:** The Committee saw three dissents regarding policy language. While the majority maintained an "easing bias," a growing minority favored a neutral stance (making a rate hike as likely as a cut) due to stalled progress on inflation.

## Economic Resilience and Labor Market Dynamics
* **Solid Growth:** GDP growth remains at or above 2%, bolstered by resilient consumer spending and "insatiable demand" for business investment in data centers.
* **Labor Market Cooling:** The unemployment rate held steady at 4.3% in March.
    * **Softened Demand:** Job gains have remained low, reflecting a decline in labor force growth due to lower immigration and participation rates.
    * **Low Turnover:** Powell described the market as being in an "uncomfortable balance" where hiring and quits are low, making it difficult for new workers to enter the workforce.
* **External Shocks:** The U.S. economy is currently navigating several simultaneous supply shocks: the tail-end of the pandemic, the invasion of Ukraine, trade tariffs, and the current Middle East conflict.

## Institutional Independence and Leadership Transition
* **Succession Plan:** Powell congratulated Kevin Warsh on advancing through the Senate Banking Committee and confirmed Warsh is expected to be sworn in as the next Chair following the end of Powell's term on May 15.
* **Powell’s Continued Tenure:** Powell announced he will remain on the Board as a Governor for an undetermined period to serve as a "constructive participant" and protect the Fed’s autonomy.
    * **Legal Challenges:** Powell cited "unprecedented" legal assaults from the administration as his primary reason for staying, noting these attacks threaten the Fed’s ability to conduct non-partisan monetary policy.
    * **Low Profile:** As a Governor, Powell intends to avoid being a "shadow chair," stating he will support the new Chair's consensus-building efforts.
* **Fed Independence at Risk:** Powell warned that the institution is being "batterred" by political interference, emphasizing that central bank independence is the "institutional architecture" that separates successful economies from unsuccessful ones.

## Impact of the Middle East Conflict
* **Energy Costs:** Brent crude is approaching $120 per barrel. While the U.S. is a major energy exporter—making it more resilient than Europe or Asia—prolonged high prices will eventually act as a "tax" on domestic consumer spending.
* **Shipping Corridors:** Powell noted little progress in reopening key energy trade corridors, which has contributed to rising near-term inflation expectations.
* **Policy Reaction:** The Fed typically "looks through" energy shocks as temporary, but Powell indicated they will be "very cautious" given that inflation has already been above the 2% target for several years.
