**TL;DR** 
The House Armed Services Committee convened to review the Trump administration’s historic $1.5 trillion FY 2027 defense budget request, focusing on revitalizing the defense industrial base, while facing intense bipartisan debate over the strategic, financial, and personnel costs of the ongoing 60-day war with Iran (Operation Epic Fury).

## The $1.5 Trillion FY 2027 Budget & Industrial Base Revitalization
* **Historical Underinvestment:** Chairman Mike Rogers highlighted that since World War II, defense spending as a percentage of GDP has steadily fallen, leaving the US defense industrial base (DIB) atrophied. China currently builds 47% of the world’s ships, while the US builds just 0.1%.
* **Budget Breakdown:** The historic $1.5 trillion budget request (consisting of $1.15 trillion in discretionary funding and $350 billion in mandatory reconciliation) aims to return defense spending to approximately 4.5% to 5% of GDP. 
    * **Readiness & Personnel:** Includes a 24% increase in Operation and Maintenance (O&M), a 115% increase in funding to repair military barracks, a 44,000 increase in troop end-strength, and a 7% pay raise for lower-enlisted service members.
    * **Procurement & R&D:** Proposes an unprecedented 76% increase in procurement and a 64% increase in Research and Development (R&D), including $54 billion to $74 billion for American-made drone dominance and counter-UAS systems, and $18 billion for the "Golden Dome" missile defense system.
* **Transition to a Business Model:** Secretary of War Pete Hegseth testified that the Pentagon has shifted from a bureaucratic model to an outcomes-driven business model.
    * **Private Capital Infusion:** The department has stimulated over 250 private investment deals worth more than $50 billion across 39 states, resulting in 280 new or expanded manufacturing facilities and 70,000 new defense-sector jobs.
    * **Clean Audit Goal:** The Department of War is executing a joint task force initiative to achieve its first clean audit by 2028.

## Strategic and Economic Debates Over the War with Iran
* **Operation Epic Fury:** Bipartisan members acknowledged the tactical proficiency and bravery of US troops during recent operations (Rough Rider, Midnight Hammer, Southern Spear, Absolute Resolve, and Epic Fury), including the high-profile rescue of two downed F-15E pilots.
* **The Cost of Conflict:** 
    * **DoD Estimates:** Acting CFO Jay Hurst estimated the cost of Operation Epic Fury at approximately $25 billion to date, primarily driven by munitions replacement and O&M costs. 
    * **Economic Blowback:** Ranking Member Adam Smith and other committee members criticized the war’s broader economic damage. Global crude oil prices have risen to nearly $120 a barrel, driving US gas prices up by over a dollar to a national average of $4.20 to $4.30 per gallon. 
* **Nuclear Deterrence vs. Escalation:** 
    * **Administration Defense:** Secretary Hegseth argued that the 60-day conflict has successfully degraded Iran’s conventional navy and air defense shield, placing the US in a position of strength to negotiate a permanent denuclearization deal.
    * **Strategic Skepticism:** Critics argued that the war has failed to halt Iran's nuclear program. Opponents pointed out that Iran has actually increased its stockpile of highly enriched uranium from zero to 10 pounds since the conflict began, while the Strait of Hormuz remains heavily disrupted.

## Personnel Controversies and Leadership "Purges"
* **Dismissal of Senior Officers:** Committee members raised sharp concerns regarding the sudden removals of top military leaders, including Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George, and four of the six service secretaries. 
    * **Secretary’s Rationale:** Secretary Hegseth defended the decisions, stating that "personnel is policy" and that changes were necessary to dismantle "social engineering" and return the military to a merit-based system focused solely on lethality and readiness.
* **Timothy Parlatore Appointment:** Representative Jason Crow questioned Hegseth over the appointment of his private defense attorney, Timothy Parlatore, as a senior advisor. Crow highlighted that Parlatore was directly commissioned as a Navy Reserve Commander in March 2025, bypassing Senate confirmation and the White House Presidential Personnel Office vetting process.
* **Labor & Health Policy Shifts:** 
    * **Collective Bargaining:** Hegseth defended his April 9th memo terminating collective bargaining agreements for DoD civilian employees, arguing that labor restrictions in shipyards and factories limited operational efficiency.
    * **Optional Vaccines:** The department defended its decision to make the annual flu vaccine optional for service members as part of a broader effort to restore individual freedoms to the force.
