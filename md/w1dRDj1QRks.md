## State of the AI Boom & Four Market Scenarios

*   **Defining the Tech:**
    *   **Large Language Models (LLMs):** Sophisticated word prediction machines (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude) distinct from artificial general intelligence (AGI) and agentic AI (which can execute tasks on a user's behalf).
    *   **Data Center Economics:** A single large AI data center capable of supporting a gigawatt of power demand costs around **$30 billion** to build and equip.
*   **The Four Scenarios (Probabilities Assigned by Steve Eisman):**
    *   **Bull Case (30% probability):** AI transforms the world; Anthropic, OpenAI, and Nvidia drive the stock market to continuous record highs as multi-year backlogs convert into massive profits.
    *   **Bear Case (30% probability):** AI is overhyped; capital required for data centers and expensive programming fails to yield adequate returns, pressured by Chinese open-weight models, leading to a full economic recession and market crash.
    *   **Intermediate Case (40% probability):** Too much CAPEX chasing demand; data center capacity outpaces demand (e.g., Brad Safflo of PAA Research estimates US data center capacity will double demand by the end of 2027), leading to a slow unwinding of AI gains and a bear market.
    *   **Doomsday Scenario (0% probability):** Sci-fi extinction scenarios (Terminator-style takeovers) are nonsense peddled by model developers to scare governments into regulating competition out of existence.

## Software Industry Disruption & The SaaS-pocalypse

*   **Software Vulnerability:**
    *   AI has collapsed the cost of creating software, threatening existing SaaS business models ("SaaS-pocalypse").
    *   High switching costs protect deeply embedded enterprise software like ServiceNow and Salesforce, but vulnerable companies like Adobe have seen massive corrections (Adobe stock dropped **60%** from its February 2024 high of $630 to around $245).
*   **Private Equity & Private Credit Exposure:**
    *   Between 2017–2022, private equity bought many SaaS companies using **50% debt and 50% equity** structures backed by private credit.
    *   Assuming a conservative **50% decline** in private software company values, much of this private equity is wiped out, posing localized risks when loans refinance.

## Five Major Economic Risks to the AI Story

*   **Issue 1: From Capital Light to Capital Heavy Financing**
    *   **Google:** Transitioned from capital-light cash-flow generation (previously generating over **$80** in annual free cash flow and avoiding equity raises since 2005) to raising **$85 billion** in equity capital in 2026.
    *   **Oracle:** Total debt increased from **$94 billion to $155 billion** over two years; S&P downgraded Oracle to **BBB-** (one level above junk). Its RPO explosion from OpenAI dependency caused severe stock volatility (spiking to **$330** then dropping to around **$130**, down **~70%** from its peak).
    *   **Off-Balance Sheet Shenanigans:** Meta created a **$27 billion** special purpose vehicle (SPV) in Louisiana named *Benyaye Investor LLC* (partnered with Blue Owl and Pimco; Meta owns **20%**) to keep debt off its balance sheet. Ernst & Young flagged this as a **"critical audit matter"** in Meta's 2025 10-K.
    *   **Circular Financing:** Nvidia invests in or lends to tech tech companies that use those funds to purchase Nvidia chips.
*   **Issue 2: Power and Data Center Constraints**
    *   Permit delays and public NIMBY backlash are slowing data center construction.
    *   Grid stress has driven demand for gas turbines (benefiting GE Vernova), nuclear power, and alternative gas turbine tech (Bloom Energy).
*   **Issue 3: Extreme Concentration Risk**
    *   **Nvidia:** **70%** of Nvidia's accounts receivable comes from just **5 customers** (hyperscalers or adjacent).
    *   **Hyperscalers:** Roughly **70%** of hyperscaler AI revenue comes solely from Anthropic and OpenAI (equating to **25% to 35%** of total hyperscalar cloud revenue). Oracle's backlog is **50%** OpenAI; CoreWeave's contracted order book is over **20%** OpenAI.
*   **Issue 4: OpenAI's Vulnerability & High-Level Departures**
    *   **Executive Departures:** Denise Dresser (CRO, resigned August 2026 after 8 months); Brad Lightcap (former COO, resigned August 2026 after 8 years); Caitlyn Kalinowski (Head of Robotics, resigned March 2026); Kate Rouch (CMO) and Kevin Weil (Science Research Officer) departed in April 2026.
    *   **Financials:** WSJ reported OpenAI's June quarter revenue reached **$6.7 billion** (up only **18%** vs. March quarter), while costs surged to **$12.3 billion** (up **$3 billion** vs. March quarter, outpacing revenue growth). Conversely, Anthropic's June quarter revenue exceeded **$11 billion** (up over **100%** vs. March).
    *   **Burn Rate:** Commentator Ed Zitron estimates OpenAI must raise **$100 billion per year** for the next several years to survive.
*   **Issue 5: Absence of Long-Term Moats & Pricing Pressures**
    *   Switching costs between LLM models are extremely low or zero.
    *   **Token Economics:** Enterprise "token maxing" (unrestricted usage) ended as costs soared (e.g., Uber blew through its entire AI budget in months).
    *   **Open-Weight Competitors:** Chinese open-weight models charge **$0.20 per 1 million tokens**, compared to **$2 to $10 per 1 million tokens** for flagship Anthropic/OpenAI models, creating severe risks of an industry price war.
