**TL;DR**
Skild AI is developing "Skild Brain," a foundational, general-purpose AI model designed to power any robot form factor—from humanoid to industrial arms—using a shared "Omni-bodied" intelligence that scales through a multi-layered data flywheel.

## The Shift to Horizontal Robotics
*   **Omni-bodied Intelligence:** Skild is moving away from "vertical" robotics (where software is custom-built for one specific task, like welding) toward a horizontal platform.
    *   **General-purpose Brain:** Similar to how Large Language Models (LLMs) provide a base for various text applications, Skild Brain serves as a universal controller that can be fine-tuned for specific industrial or consumer needs.
*   **Addressing the "Corner Case" Problem:** Traditional robotics often hits a performance wall at 90% because of unpredictable real-world variables. 
    *   By using a shared brain, a "corner case" in one industry (e.g., a misplaced box in a warehouse) becomes a "central case" the AI has already learned to handle in another environment.
*   **Learning vs. Programming:** The company is shifting the engineering focus from "how a robot should move" to "how a robot can learn from data," allowing for deployment in unmeasured, unstructured environments like homes and hospitals.

## The Three-Layer Data Strategy
Because there is no "internet of robot data," Skild utilizes a tiered approach to train its models:
*   **Video Pre-training (Diversity):** Leveraging billions of real-world videos to teach the model general physics and task concepts. While scalable, this data lacks "richness" (specific force and motor command data).
*   **Simulation (Scale):** Using simulators to collect trillions of data points.
    *   **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Simulation provides the "practice" needed to make behaviors robust, though it requires algorithmic bridging to work in the physical world.
*   **Real-world Post-training (Precision):** Collecting high-quality teleoperation data from real robots. This is the most expensive data to gather but is essential for "post-training" the model to perform specific, high-precision tasks in factories or warehouses.

## The Data Flywheel & Deployment Roadmap
*   **Continuous Improvement:** Every robot deployed contributes data back to the central brain. As the fleet grows, the amount of data required to teach the brain a new task decreases.
*   **Deployment Phases:**
    *   **Phase 1 (Current):** Structured/Semi-structured industrial environments (factories and warehouses) where tasks are repeatable but require AI for adaptability.
    *   **Phase 2 (Near-term):** Unstructured service environments, including hospitals, hotels, and grocery stores.
    *   **Phase 3 (Long-term):** Unstructured consumer environments (home robots).
*   **Deployment as a Technical Hurdle:** Unlike software, physical AI deployment is a distinct technical challenge. Skild focuses on "specializing" the general model for new partners in a matter of days rather than months.

## NVIDIA Partnership and Infrastructure
*   **Simulation and Solvers:** Skild uses NVIDIA Isaac Sim and is co-developing advanced physics solvers (Newton) to improve simulation accuracy.
*   **Generative AI for Augmentation:** The company leverages NVIDIA Cosmos and other video models for data augmentation, creating multiple variations of a single data point to increase model robustness.
*   **Edge Computing:** Because robots require near-instantaneous reaction times to prevent falling or accidents, Skild partners with NVIDIA to utilize high-performance on-device edge compute rather than relying on cloud processing.
