**TL;DR**
As AI capabilities outpace traditional bot detection, World (formerly Worldcoin) proposes iris-based biometric verification as the only scalable, privacy-preserving method to distinguish unique humans from autonomous agents in an increasingly deepfaked digital economy.

## The Escalating Crisis of Digital Personhood
*   **The "1% Glimpse":** Alex Blania (CEO of World) warns that current bot activity is less than 1% of what is coming; as the cost of intelligence drops, AI agents will become superhuman in their ability to manipulate and "program" humans.
*   **Identity Categories:** The internet is shifting from a binary (human/bot) to a three-tier interaction model:
    *   **Humans:** Unique biological individuals.
    *   **Human-Directed Agents:** AI acting with specific permissions on behalf of a human.
    *   **Autonomous Agents:** Purely AI-driven entities with their own agendas.
*   **The Failure of Digital Signals:** Previous "Web of Trust" models (relying on GitHub history or social media longevity) are now obsolete. AI can easily forge years of digital history and "attest" to the humanity of other AI accounts, creating self-validating bot networks.

## Why Conventional Solutions Fall Short
*   **The Entropy Problem:** Standard biometrics like FaceID or fingerprints work for 1-to-1 authentication (checking if you are the same person who owns the phone). However, they lack the mathematical entropy required for 1-to-N uniqueness (checking if you are a unique human not already in the system). FaceID "hits a wall" after tens of millions of users, whereas the iris provides enough information to scale to billions.
*   **Government ID Limitations:** Relying on state-issued IDs is dismissed due to:
    *   **Privacy Risks:** Instant loss of anonymity and the danger of centralized government surveillance.
    *   **Fragmentation:** Government systems are not globally interoperable; a "perfect" system in Singapore does not help a platform like Meta manage 3 billion global users.
*   **Replay Attacks:** Digital-only verification is vulnerable to deepfakes injected directly into camera streams. World argues that custom hardware (the Orb) is necessary to verify physical presence through multi-spectral sensors that can distinguish a real eye from a high-resolution screen.

## World’s Technical and Privacy Architecture
*   **Privacy-Preserving Verification:** To avoid a "centralized database of eyeballs," World utilizes two primary cryptographic techniques:
    *   **Multi-Party Computation (MPC):** The iris code is broken into fragments and distributed across multiple independent computers. No single entity ever possesses the full biometric template.
    *   **Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKP):** Users hold a "secret" on their phones. They can prove to a third-party platform (like Reddit or X) that they are a unique human verified by the MPC system without ever revealing their biometric data or real-world identity.
*   **Hardware Evolution:** The Orb has transitioned from a "wild" futuristic concept to a necessary utility. Blania notes that iris scanning is becoming normalized through devices like the Apple Vision Pro.

## Macro Applications: From Dating to Democracy
*   **Economic Integrity:** Ben Horowitz notes that $400 billion was stolen during COVID stimulus programs due to identity fraud. Cryptographic "proof of human" is essential for the efficient distribution of Universal Basic Income (UBI) or social services.
*   **Protecting Democracy:** Current systems (like mail-in ballots and Social Security numbers) are vulnerable to AI-scale fraud. Without proof of human, high-scale impersonation could render the "will of the people" impossible to determine.
*   **Consumer Use Cases:** 
    *   **Dating:** Tinder is testing World ID in Japan to ensure users are interacting with authentic humans.
    *   **Video Conferencing:** As deepfakes become real-time and photorealistic, high-value users (e.g., fund managers) will need to cryptographically verify their identity on calls.
    *   **Gaming:** Preventing AI from "destroying" human players in competitive environments where money or status is at stake.
