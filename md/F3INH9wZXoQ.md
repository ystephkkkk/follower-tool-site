## TL;DR
Eric Landau, Co-CEO of Encord, explains how his company evolved from a computer vision tool into the data layer for physical AI, emphasizing the compounding nature of product-market fit and the importance of riding the emotional rollercoaster of startup life.

## Background & Origin
* **Academic & Professional Trajectory:** 
  * **Particle Physics & Quant Career:** Eric Landau spent a decade as a high-frequency trading quant following a background in big data particle physics.
  * **The "Bitter Lesson":** Early AI relied on heavy feature engineering and domain expertise (mirroring his early career in physics and quant trading), whereas modern AI relies strictly on scaling data and compute.
* **The Decision to Quit:** 
  * Left a lucrative quant career during COVID at the exact moment his trading desk experienced its most profitable month ever (including the day oil traded negative).
  * Spent early days fighting Python dependencies on a couch, experiencing immediate contrast with the existential dread of his previous career.

## Product-Market Fit & Market Evolution
* **The "Desert" Years (2021–Release):** 
  * Spent two years building before achieving breakout adoption, driven by long-term conviction in AI as a generational paradigm shift.
  * **ChatGPT Catalyst:** ChatGPT didn't create an overnight binary switch for Encord, but it made the market receptive to conversations about AI infrastructure.
* **Compounding PMF:** 
  * PMF was not a single launch event, but a slow, daily compounding process where the market moved toward their continuously improving product.
  * Milestone of realization: Closing sales via Gong without knowing what the customer's company actually did.
* **Data Scale & Operations:** 
  * Deals with multiple petabytes of multimodal data (video streams, sensor data, audio, language).
  * Processes more data than was used to train GPT-4.
  * Maintains a Bay Area operations facility equipped as a "film set" for robot data collection, alongside headquarters in London.

## Pivot to Physical AI
* **The 80% Economy:** 
  * Started in vision because it was the hardest modality, anticipating multimodal systems.
  * Shifted focus as the market moved toward physical AI, noting that 80% of economic activity involves manipulating or moving things in the real world.
* **Key Use Cases:** 
  * Autonomous driving (nearing mass-scale production).
  * Robotics (humanoids, consumer robotics, manufacturing), with robotics insiders predicting robots will outnumber humans in 5 to 10 years.
  * Quirky applications include facial recognition for cows to track health.
* **Product Stack:** 
  * Beyond data collection for pre-training, Encord provides data management, curation ("finding a million needles in a billion haystacks"), annotation/enrichment, and evaluations.

## Team Building & Sales Evolution
* **Building the Sales Org:** 
  * Built entirely through trial and error, requiring three successive sales teams before finding what worked.
  * Highlighted the danger of ignoring YC advice (e.g., keeping a bad hire for 9 months due to a lack of a single forced reason to fire them).
* **Geographic Strategy:** 
  * Co-founder is in San Francisco; Eric is based in London.
  * Strategy is to be where important people are (customers and talent, then investors). Most customers are in the US/Bay Area, while London offers a comparative advantage for AI and engineering talent with less intense competition.
  * Competes against scaled players and companies like Mercor, welcoming competition to drive company improvement.
