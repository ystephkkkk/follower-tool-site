## ## The $1.535 Trillion Defense Budget & Modernization Priorities

*   **FY 2027 Budget Request:** The Trump administration is seeking $1.535 trillion for the Department of War, representing a nearly 50% increase from the FY 2026 topline of $1 trillion.
*   **Reconciliation Funding Deployment:** 
    *   **Obligation Rate:** Out of the $154 billion allocated in last year's Reconciliation 1.0, the Pentagon has only put $26 billion on contract so far. 
    *   **Delays:** Secretary Hegseth attributed the delay to a new funding vehicle process and sluggish fund transfers from the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) but promised the "floodgates" are about to open.
*   **Strategic Tech and Munitions Allocations:**
    *   **Drone Dominance:** $54 billion is dedicated to unmanned aerial systems (UAS), counter-UAS, and the newly established Autonomous Warfare Group.
    *   **Nuclear Triad & NC3:** $71 billion is designated to modernize the nuclear triad and ensure secure command, control, and communication systems.
    *   **"Golden Dome" Air Defense:** A $17 billion request for FY 2027 is aimed at building out a layered homeland missile defense system.
    *   **Munitions Rebuilding:** Hegseth cited a $331 billion requirement for long-range fires, Tomahawks, and hypersonics to replenish stocks depleted by operations in Iran and previous drawdowns for Ukraine.
*   **B-21 Raider Scaling:** The current program of record plans for 100 B-21 Raiders, but Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman General Dan Cane agreed to review requirements to potentially scale procurement to 200 stealth bombers to counter pacing threats in China.
*   **Civilian vs. Military Pay:** While the budget guarantees a 7% pay raise for junior enlisted troops and eliminates failing barracks, it contains no guaranteed base pay raise for the DoD's 800,000 civilian employees, relying instead on a 4.2% bonus pool.

## ## The Strategic and Economic Debates Over the Iran War

*   **Operation Epic Fury & Midnight Hammer:** Hegseth labeled the 60-day-old military operations against Iran a "stunningly effective" success that decimated the regime's conventional missile shield and neutralized their ability to execute nuclear blackmail.
*   **The Strategic Stalemate:** Ranking Member Jack Reed and critics argued the war is a strategic quagmire.
    *   **Missile and Drone Retention:** Unclassified intelligence assessments indicate Iran still retains 40% of its drone arsenal and 60% of its ballistic missile launchers.
    *   **Human Costs:** The conflict has resulted in 13 to 14 U.S. service member deaths, over 400 wounded, and significant damage to regional U.S. military bases.
*   **Economic Consequences:** Lawmakers pressed the panel on the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, which has spiked global gasoline prices (with Michigan prices reaching $4.99/gallon), inflated fertilizer costs, and strained the global economy.
*   **War Expenditures:** Acting Comptroller Jay Hurst confirmed that Operation Epic Fury has cost approximately $25 billion to date. Critics like Senator Richard Blumenthal argued this dramatically undercounts long-term equipment replacement and military construction costs.
*   **War Powers Resolution Compliance:** Senator Tim Kaine noted the 60-day statutory limit for unauthorized presidential military action is expiring. Hegseth replied that the administration’s legal counsel views an active ceasefire as "pausing" the statutory clock, a position Kaine heavily disputed.

## ## Global Contingencies: Ukraine, Venezuela, and Southern Sphere

*   **Zeroing Out Ukraine Aid:** The FY 2027 budget contains $0 in U.S. financial support for Ukraine. Hegseth argued that rich European nations, with a combined $20 trillion economy, should step up and completely fund the conflict.
*   **Delayed $400 Million Funding:** Senator Jeanne Shaheen grilled the witnesses on why $400 million in security assistance enacted by Congress in January has not reached Ukraine. Comptroller Hurst confirmed the funds were only recently released and are being tied to the Prioritized Ukraine Requirements List (PURL) to ensure European burden-sharing.
*   **Operation Southern Sphere:** Lawmakers scrutinized the ongoing maritime interdiction campaign against suspected drug boats in the Caribbean, which has resulted in nearly 200 fatalities. 
    *   **Legal Basis:** Senator Kaine questioned the legality of striking boats in international waters without explicit evidence of narcotics. General Cane defended the actions, noting that the targets are classified, designated transnational terrorist organizations.
*   **Operation Absolute Resolve:** General Cane commended the Joint Force for successfully executing the Venezuelan intervention, which removed an aspiring dictator and stabilized the nation.

## ## AI Ethics, Financial Scandals, and Rhetoric Concerns

*   **Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards:** Under questioning from Senators Jacky Rosen and Kirsten Gillibrand, Hegseth and General Cane reconfirmed that the U.S. military strictly adheres to DoD Directive 3000.09, mandating that humans always make lethal targeting decisions.
*   **Anthropic Blacklisting:** Hegseth defended blacklisting AI safety developer Anthropic, comparing their refusal to allow fully autonomous targeting to Boeing delivering planes but telling the military who they can shoot at.
*   **Insider Trading Allegations:** Senator Elizabeth Warren pressed Hegseth on reports that his Morgan Stanley broker attempted to buy BlackRock defense fund stocks immediately prior to Trump's public tweets about the Iran war. Hegseth denied this, calling the allegations a "big, fat negative."
*   **Controversial Rhetoric:** Senator Rosen challenged Hegseth's public labeling of journalists and political critics as "Pharisees," pointing out the term's historically antisemitic weaponization. Hegseth stood by the term, stating it accurately describes hypocrites who ignore the historic military success against Iran's nuclear program.
