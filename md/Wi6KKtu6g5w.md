## Meta's $98B Equity Raise & AI Infrastructure
* **Structured Capital Raising:** Meta is executing a multifaceted nearly $98 billion equity offering to finance AI buildouts without relying on debt.
  * **Tranche Breakdown:** Includes a $25 billion common stock sale, a $12.5 billion preferred share offering, and a $60 billion "At-the-Market" (ATM) Class C non-voting share sale program.
  * **Oversubscription:** The initial $25 billion portion of the stock sale finished 5 times oversubscribed, showing robust market demand.
* **Vendor Participation:** Key chipmakers and infrastructure partners are participating in the equity raise, including Nvidia ($3 billion), AMD ($1 billion), and Broadcom ($1 billion).
  * **Circular Financing Context:** Analyst Mandeep Singh noted that while these vendor investments represent a small fraction (5–6%) of the total raise, the optics of circular financing are present given Meta holds warrants against AMD shares, though overwhelming external demand overshadows it.
* **Hardware & Silicon Integration:** Meta has installed AMD’s Helios rack-scale AI systems for testing ahead of a broader rollout. Meta already utilizes AMD CPUs for its Muse AI agent and AMD GPUs for AI training, ranking, and recommendation systems.
* **Governance and CEO Liquidity:** The Class C non-voting share structure provides CEO Mark Zuckerberg with liquidity to cover potential tax liabilities from a proposed California billionaire tax, drawing comparisons to Alphabet’s dual-class share structure.

* **Staggering Growth and Losses:** Shirin Ghaffary reported on Anthropic’s S-1 filing for its upcoming IPO, which could be one of the largest in history:

## Anduril's Bluechip: Rebuilding the US Computer Supply Chain
* **New Venture with Qualcomm:** Anduril founder Palmer Luckey announced "Bluechip," a partnership with Qualcomm to build a computer and laptop supply chain entirely within the US and allied nations.
  * **Manufacturing Arsenal:** Circuit board assembly is planned at Anduril’s Arsenal 1 factory in Ohio, utilizing Qualcomm Snapdragon CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs manufactured in Arizona.
  * **Target Audience:** Initial production focuses on ruggedized military requirements, public safety, first responders, and critical national infrastructure (defense and energy companies handling classified or grid-critical data).
  * **Supply Chain Roadmap:** Current prototypes are approximately 90% US and allied-sourced (with glass made by an American company and local display assembly), targeting a 100% US technical roadmap with display manufacturing announcements expected in 2027.
* **Strategic Autonomy:** Luckey emphasized that Anduril's independence from Chinese supply chains stems from being sanctioned by the Chinese Communist Party, giving the defense tech firm unique expertise in scaling domestic advanced systems without foreign adversaries.
