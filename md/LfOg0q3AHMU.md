## TL;DR
IBM CEO Arvind Krishna addressed the company's lowered full-year sales forecast—driven by a temporary CapEx slowdown and mainframe sales dip among Fortune 100 clients—while reaffirming free cash flow targets and highlighting strong enterprise demand for AI, Red Hat software, and quantum computing.

## Mainframe Performance and Capacity Growth
- **Z17 Capacity Surge:** The current Z17 machine, launched in May 2025, has achieved **130% capacity growth** compared to the prior generation.
- **Client Adoption:** **85% of mainframe clients** are actively increasing their capacity, while 15% are optimizing.
- **Structural Value Proposition:** Addressing investor comparisons to the 1990s mainframe shift, Krishna noted that unit costs for protected, resilient, and burst-capacity workloads on the mainframe remain **5 to 15 times cheaper** than alternatives, making it an architecturally superior platform rather than a displaced technology.

## Financial Guidance, Software, and Cost Management
- **Full-Year Revenue Guidance:** IBM narrowed its full-year sales forecast to **4% to 5% growth**, depending on whether CapEx headwinds persist through September.
- **Software Portfolio Split:** **80% of the software business** operates as an annuity, consumption, and OPEX-based model growing at approximately **8%**, featuring products like Red Hat, Confluent, and HashiCorp. The remaining **20%** is CapEx-sensitive.
- **Cash Flow and EBITDA:** Over the past four years, cash flow growth has outpaced revenue at **7% to 8% to 9%** annually (driven by adjusted EBITDA), supported by productivity levers and maintaining the dividend.
- **Supply Chain Backlog:** IBM exited the second quarter with **half a billion dollars of backlog** in distributed infrastructure, storage, and Unix systems due to component demand.
- **Third-Party Spend:** Headcount has remained flat over recent years; additional efficiencies are being targeted via third-party spend rather than widespread workforce reductions.
- **Starbucks Client Relationship:** Regarding Starbucks replacing an aging 10-year-old Tririga real estate lease management tool ($2M/year client), Krishna noted that interaction-based software is easily displaced by AI agents, but expects Starbucks to remain a larger overall client next year via other IBM portfolios like HashiCorp and security.

## Macroeconomic Environment and Component Pricing
- **Global GDP and Tech Spending:** IBM relies on a projected global GDP growth rate of **2% to 3%** for the year, with technology spending tracking **3 to 4 points higher** (putting the overall tech market at **5% to 6% to 7% growth**). Consulting is expected to grow at **1% to 2% to 3%**.
- **Underlying Component Inflation:** Over the past 18 months, semiconductor memory pricing is up **3 to 4 times**, networking infrastructure is up **60% to 80%**, and fiber and connectors have also risen significantly.

## Talent and Hiring
- **College Hiring Surge:** IBM hired **3 times as many college graduates** this year compared to last year.
- **Talent Pool:** The applicant database contains roughly **20 million resumes**, shifting the internal focus from talent acquisition to career pathing and ensuring employees effectively utilize AI productivity tools.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Enterprise Tech Spend Share (Arvind Krishna):** Technology spend will grow from historical averages of 3% to **5% to 6% on average today**, and will reach **10% of every enterprise's budget by 2035**.
- **Quantum Computing Timeline & Scale (Arvind Krishna):** Quantum computing has transitioned from science to engineering; IBM will deliver a large-scale, fault-tolerant quantum computer capable of **100 million computations by 2029**.
