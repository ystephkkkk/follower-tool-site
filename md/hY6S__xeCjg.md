## Compute, Energy, and Infrastructure Constraints
* **The 2030 Bottleneck:** A hyperscaler infrastructure leader noted that nothing will move the needle for power scale before 2030, raising severe concerns about natural gas availability and grid capacity for data centers.
* **Regulatory and Alignment Hurdles:** The primary limits to building abundant cheap energy (such as nuclear base load) and massive data centers are regulatory and societal alignment (convincing local populations to permit construction), rather than technical or entrepreneurial shortcomings.
* **Compute Independence:** Rebuilding the US industrial base via automation requires massive compute and energy inputs. Guo compares this to energy independence, warning that critical vulnerabilities in the global hardware supply chain (like TSMC's manufacturing concentration) necessitate domestic and allied capacity building across data centers, robotics labor, nuclear energy, and alternative chip architectures.

## Open Source and Global Competitiveness
* **The Cat is Out of the Bag:** Open-source models from international fronts (including China, Europe, and the US) are already powerful, highly competitive, and used everywhere.
* **Economic Necessity for Enterprises:** Enterprises and downstream users rely on open-source models because frontier provider APIs are often too expensive, slow, or sensitive for specific workflows. 
* **The Folly of US Restrictions:** Guo argues that restricting open-source models in the United States would only handcuff law-abiding American businesses while failing to stop bad actors who operate outside domestic jurisdictions. Instead, the US should focus on rigorous safety testing and understanding model capabilities.
