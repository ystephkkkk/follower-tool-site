## TL;DR
Anthropic’s co-founders left OpenAI in 2021 to build a public benefit corporation dedicated to developing frontier AI safely, betting that scaling compute and data would rapidly unlock human-level intelligence with both immense benefits and severe risks. After years of trailing competitors, Anthropic reached the frontier with Claude 3 Opus, Sonnet 3.5, and Claude Code—and is now confronting real-world cybersecurity risks with its Mythos model through controlled, safety-first deployment.

## Origins & The Scaling Realization
* **Diverse Scientific Roots:** The co-founders came from disparate backgrounds before converging at OpenAI by 2018:
  * **Jared Kaplan** was a theoretical physics professor at Johns Hopkins; **Daniela Amodei** worked in global development; **Jack Clark** was a journalist who studied data centers obsessively; **Sam McCandlish** was a mathematician and programmer; and **Dario Amodei** did a postdoc in cancer biomarkers at Stanford before seeing major AI results in 2013–2014.
* **The "Big Blob of Compute" Trend:** Dario Amodei identified a predictable scaling law—adding more compute and data to language models consistently yielded higher intelligence and emergent abilities without needing to explicitly program reasoning or tool use.
* **The Precipice of Human-Level AI:** The founders realized human-level AI was not 100 years away, but achievable within their lifetimes, carrying the potential to solve problems bottlenecked by human ingenuity (e.g., reducing cancer rates by 90%, curing heart disease, obesity, and major infections).
* **Existential Responsibility:** Recognizing that AI was analogous to pharmaceuticals or "unlocking the secrets of the atom," the team felt an urgent responsibility to build an organization where safety was the foundational priority rather than an afterthought.

## Founding Anthropic & Early Chaos
* **Public Benefit Corporation (PBC):** Incorporated on **January 26, 2021**, Anthropic chose a PBC structure so the company could prioritize its safety mission over maximizing short-term shareholder profits.
* **Naming the Company:** After considering names like *Squirrel*, *Owl*, *Whale*, *differentiated*, and *humane*, **Jared Kaplan** proposed *Anthropic* (meaning human-oriented or human-centered), which also had the cheapest available domain (`Anthropic.com`).
* **All-Hands Startup Grit:** In the early days, everyone handled unglamorous operational tasks:
  * **Tom Brown** secured AWS compute clusters.
  * **Chris Olah** co-built the first website.
  * **Dario Amodei** focused on fundraising, while **Daniela Amodei** led recruiting.
  * **Jared Kaplan** briefly ran payroll for a few months.

## From "Junior" to the Frontier
* **Helpful, Harmless, and Honest:** A 2021 proof-of-concept document defined the goal of a general AI assistant for conversation and software engineering. Early models were charming but flawed—one version tried to convince users an all-potato diet was healthy, and another would scream in all caps if put in "dragon mode."
* **Passing the Helicopter Test:** In mid-2022, a model code-named **Junior** (later **Claude**) marked a breakthrough. It passed **Jack Clark’s** "helicopter test" by recognizing that *"How many helicopters can a person eat in one sitting?"* was a trick question.
* **The "Years of Suck":** Anthropic publicly released Claude in **March 2023**, but the team was visibly behind the frontier. They used this period to maniacally focus on acquiring compute, building "compute multipliers," and improving pretraining and reinforcement learning (RL).
* **Reaching the Frontier:** The release of **Claude 3 Opus** put Anthropic on par with the best models in the world, followed months later by **Sonnet 3.5**, which made models genuinely useful for programming and spawned **Claude Code**—triggering a massive wave of external demand.

## Safety, Mythos, and the Public Benefit Mission
* **Why Frontier Capability Matters for Safety:** Studying weaker models does not reveal how advanced systems fail; being on the frontier gives Anthropic early visibility into emerging risks and more time to build safeguards.
* **2026: When Risks Get Real:** Prior to 2025–2026, AI risks were largely conceptual. Anthropic’s newest model, **Mythos** (currently in limited testing), demonstrated expert-level ability to find software vulnerabilities and exploits.
* **The Glass Wing Project:** Recognizing that malicious actors could use Mythos to exploit software globally, **Dario Amodei** and leadership decided to break from standard release practices. Under the **Glass Wing** project, Anthropic is granting Mythos access only to a small set of companies and cyber defenders to patch vulnerabilities while safeguards are built—accepting short-term financial pain to uphold its PBC mission.
* **Building Something to Fight For:** Beyond mitigating risks, Anthropic is focused on positive impact, including helping doctors diagnose patients, enabling entrepreneurs, and partnering with biologists who recently discovered a potential new gene-editing mechanism using Claude.
