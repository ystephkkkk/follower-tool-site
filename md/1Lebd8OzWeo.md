## Leadership & Origins
- **Co-CEO Structure:** Waymo is jointly led by Tekedra Mawakana, who oversees commercialization and market strategy, and Dmitri Dolgov, who leads hardware and software engineering teams.
- **Background & Influence:** Mawakana grew up in Mississippi with family ties to farming, which instilled an understanding of self-sustaining, productive systems. Her career spans law in the mid-1990s and Internet 1.0 leadership roles at AOL, Yahoo, and eBay before joining Waymo.
- **Foundational Lineage:** The company's origins trace back to Google’s original self-driving project and a Stanford engineering team that won the 2005 DARPA Grand Challenge by navigating 132 desert miles.

## Safety Record & Public Scrutiny
- **Data vs. Perception:** According to an Insurance Institute for Highway Safety study across four cities, Waymo vehicles were involved in **68% fewer crashes** than human drivers and achieved a **91% lower rate** of rear-end collisions.
- **Public Hesitancy:** A **2026 Pew Research poll** revealed that **71% of adults** report feeling uncomfortable in a driverless car. Viral videos of minor operational issues—such as cars blocking traffic or honking at one another—frequently fuel public skepticism.
- **Safety Philosophy & Human Error:** Mawakana emphasizes humility regarding potential future incidents, arguing that society must weigh the imperfect status quo of human-caused traffic fatalities against autonomous safety improvements.
- **Remote Assistance Clarification:** Responding to Senate hearing disclosures, Mawakana clarified that remote operators based in locations like the Philippines provide guidance during edge cases (such as blocked emergency routes) but **do not actively drive** the vehicles remotely.

## Technology & Manufacturing
- **Vehicle Fleet & Hardware:** Waymo's current scaling relies on the electric minivan Ojai (built by Chinese automaker Zeeker and outfitted with the Waymo driver in Phoenix, Arizona) following the discontinuation of Jaguar's I-PACE model.
- **Multi-Sensor Tech Stack:** Each vehicle is equipped with:
  - **13 cameras:** Capture visual details like lane markings and traffic light colors.
  - **6 radar sensors:** Measure distance and velocity when cameras are less reliable.
  - **4 LiDAR sensors (Light Detection and Ranging):** Emit laser pulses to build a 3D picture of shapes and distances up to **three football fields away** in a 360-degree radius.
- **Manufacturing Efficiency:** Amanda Bell leads Waymo's Phoenix manufacturing site, where vehicles are fully outfitted with the sensor suite and hardware in a single work shift (one day).
- **In-House vs. Competitor Approach:** Waymo designs its entire hardware and software stack in-house, contrasting with competitors like Tesla, which relies primarily on cameras and AI trained on real-world miles while rejecting LiDAR as costly and unnecessary.

## Business Model, Competition, & Pricing
- **Trip Volume & Cost:** Waymo surpassed **500,000 trips per week** earlier this year. Rides average **$9.58 per mile**, compared to **$6.88 for Uber** and **$6.41 for Lyft**. Mawakana noted that price is not the primary immediate priority, as dynamic pricing adjusts during peak commuter times for fleet consistency.
- **Complex Partnership Dynamics:** Waymo maintains a nuanced relationship with Uber—acting as investor, rival, and partner in markets like Austin and Atlanta, even as both companies prepare to enter new markets and face labor union pushback over driver displacement.
- **Surveillance and Law Enforcement:** Waymo maintains that it does not run continuous background tape for general tracking or police monitoring; data is only shared with law enforcement under strict, down-scoped lawful processes.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Future Fleet Evolution:** Mawakana predicts that over the next 5, 10, or 20 years, a generalizable Waymo driver deployed across multiple business lines will become the standard expectation for consumers seeking safe transit from point A to point B.
- **Steering Wheel Timeline:** Mawakana claims that steering wheels will disappear "over time, absolutely," with pedals and driver seats potentially following, though Waymo has delayed this to maintain accessibility for first responders and tow truck operators.
- **Licensing Strategy:** Mawakana states that Waymo will expand beyond ride-hailing into other business lines, including licensing its autonomous technology to traditional automotive companies for retail sale.
