## TL;DR
As frontier AI labs experience 10x annual revenue growth while compute capacity only scales 3x year-over-year, soaring demand and limited hardware supply threaten to drive up compute prices drastically.

## Revenue vs. Compute Growth Mismatch
* **Anthropic's Exponential Revenue Growth:**
  * **Historic and projected metrics:** Anthropic's revenue has 10x'd year-over-year for the last three consecutive years and is likely to do so again. After ending last year with **$9 billion** in revenue, they are projected to end the current year between **$100 billion and $150 billion**.
  * **Staggering future scale:** For this trend to continue, Anthropic would need to reach **$1 trillion** in revenue by the end of next year, though this ultimately depends on whether AI capabilities reach that level of usefulness.
* **The Compute Bottleneck:**
  * **Limited scaling:** Lab compute only 3x's year-over-year, creating a massive gap against 10x revenue growth.
  * **Three potential pressure valves:** To bridge this gap, labs must rely on some combination of:
    1. Increasing lab margins.
    2. Increasing the price of compute.
    3. Increasing the percentage of compute spent on inference rather than training.
  * **Current market adjustments:** All three mechanisms are already underway:
    * **Margins:** Anthropic's inference margins reportedly rose from **40%** in the middle of last year to upwards of **80%** currently for Fable.
    * **Spot prices:** Compute spot prices are more than **40% higher** than the February trough earlier this year.
    * **Inference vs. training share:** According to Epoch, OpenAI spent just **one-quarter** of its compute on inference in 2024, a figure that is now likely **50% or higher**.
  * **Lab incentives against inference heavy allocation:** Labs prefer not to spend the majority of compute on inference because doing so signals that AI progress has stalled, turning them into standard cloud providers rather than builders of AGI.

## The Economics of Compute and Pricing Pressures
* **Frontier Lab Compute Acquisition:**
  * **Scale and security needs:** Frontier labs cannot rely solely on spot instances; they require massive scale for efficiency/flexibility, alongside strict security for their own weights and customer information.
  * **SpaceX rental case study:** Google and Anthropic rent compute from SpaceX. Google pays **$900 million a month** for **110,000 GPUs** (a blend of **GB200s and GB300s**), a price that is **2x** the spot price per hour.
* **Monetization and the Value of Intelligence:**
  * **H100 value equivalent:** If a true human-level software engineer could run on an **H100 equivalent**, at today's software engineer wages, that H100 should rent for over **$250K a year** (**15x** the current spot price), even before accounting for working nights and weekends.
  * **Labor supply shock vs. economics:** Standard economic theory (drawing parallels to high-skill immigration and the lump of labor fallacy) suggests the marginal value of compute will stay astonishingly high rather than depressing wages.
* **Economic Effects on Competition and Efficiency:**
  * **Competitive exclusion:** As top labs get better at monetizing compute and costs rise, competing against them becomes increasingly difficult.
  * **Alchian-Allen effect:** When H100 rentals cost **$20 an hour**, using weaker, less efficient models becomes extremely costly in wasted tokens, allowing labs with efficient models to charge much higher premiums.
  * **Pricing out popular applications:** As AI matches top human capabilities, tech giants will outbid consumer use cases to automate AI research rather than support casual conversational apps ("AI slop talk").

## Limits to Compute Scaling
* **Inelasticity Compared to Commodities:**
  * **Simon-Ehrlich bet analogy:** Unlike metal extraction, the supply of compute is far less elastic, less capable of absorbing large demand shocks, and less adaptable via substitutes.
  * **Breakdown of the 3x year-over-year scaling:** The current 3x annual compute capacity growth is difficult to sustain due to strict physical and supply chain limits:
    * **1.4x** comes from Moore's Law (which will be a miracle to maintain for a few more years).
    * **1.2x** comes from building new fabs, bottlenecked up to **2030** and beyond by the availability of ASML EUV machines.
    * **1.8x** comes from AI absorbing wafer allocations previously reserved for smartphones and PCs. This channel will hit a wall by the end of next year when AI wafer consumption reaches **86%** (up from **60%**) at TSMC's leading-edge N3 nodes.

## Post-Singularity Outlook and Economies of Scale
* **Pre-Singularity vs. Post-Singularity Regimes:**
  * **Compute cost recovery:** Compute will eventually become cheap again post-singularity once robots convert silica sand and copper mines directly into chips, pricing compute strictly at the cost of raw inputs and processing tools.
* **Concentration of Power:**
  * **Economies of scale in models:** Unlike human labor (where each instance must be retrained from scratch), model training is a one-time fixed cost shared across all users. This creates exceptionally strong economies of scale for intelligence, raising valid concerns about power concentration.
