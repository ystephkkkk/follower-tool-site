**TL;DR**
Technological supremacy, particularly in AI and production scale, has become the primary driver of geopolitical power, requiring the U.S. to abandon laissez-faire economics for a modern industrial strategy to counter China’s state-directed model and navigate increasingly volatile global flashpoints.

## Technology as the New Currency of Power
* **The Tech High Ground:** Technological capability is currently the "vital input" to the global power equation, shaping economic, military, and diplomatic influence more rapidly than in previous decades.
    * **AI Centrality:** While quantum and biotech are significant, the "explosion of capability" in Artificial Intelligence is the primary driver of the current geopolitical shift.
* **China’s Theory of Power:** China has shifted from "chasing innovation" to a strategy centered on production, scale, and control of critical inputs.
    * **Learning by Doing:** Sullivan emphasizes that China leverages production capacity to drive innovation, a model the U.S. must adapt—not by copying China's top-down system, but by using public investment to "crowd in" private capital.
* **Global Tech Offerings:** To prevent a global "race to the bottom" led by low-cost Chinese technology (e.g., Huawei 5G), the U.S. must provide concessional financing to help other nations adopt the American technology stack.

## U.S.-China Strategic Competition
* **Export Controls:** Sullivan defends the Biden administration’s "small yard, high fence" approach to advanced semiconductors.
    * **The "Addiction" Argument:** He rejects the notion that the U.S. should have kept China "addicted" to Western chips, noting that Beijing began prioritizing "indigenization" of its chip stack as early as 2015.
    * **The Frontier Advantage:** U.S. chip controls are designed to prevent China from "leapfrogging" at the frontier of AI compute, even if China maintains a "fast-follow" capability.
* **Taiwan Outlook:** Sullivan assesses the risk of military action in the Taiwan Strait as "quite low" for the next two years.
    * **The 2028 Pivot:** The year 2028 is identified as a critical juncture due to the convergence of the next Taiwanese presidential election and the end of the current U.S. administration.
* **The Stability Paradox:** Sullivan observes that because the U.S. is currently viewed as a "disruptor," China is skillfully positioning itself as a "guardian of the order" to gain diplomatic leverage with the Global South and Europe.

## Middle East Crisis and the Iranian "Hormuz Card"
* **Deterrence Shift:** The recent war has demonstrated that Iran’s ability to close the Strait of Hormuz is an "actual capability" rather than a theoretical risk, fundamentally altering the regional deterrence equation.
* **Nuclear Diplomacy:** Sullivan argues that any viable deal must resemble the fundamental architecture of the JCPOA (limited enrichment and verifiable inspections in exchange for sanctions relief).
    * **The "Atmospherics" Obstacle:** Sullivan notes a clash in negotiating styles: Iran requires "face-saving" language, whereas Donald Trump prefers "spike the football" rhetoric that emphasizes total submission.
* **U.S.-Israel Relations:** Reflecting on the "quiet Middle East" assessment from 2023, Sullivan admits the U.S. should have applied more pressure on the Israeli government earlier.
    * **Security Assistance:** He advocates for treating Israel as a "normal ally" where military assistance is contingent on policy alignment, specifically regarding the West Bank and Gaza objectives.

## Global Risks and the Changing World Order
* **The Russia-Ukraine Grind:** Russia is currently benefiting from elevated oil prices. Sullivan warns that Vladimir Putin has little incentive to end the war, as doing so would bring hundreds of thousands of disaffected soldiers back to Russia and require an explanation for the conflict's costs.
* **Nuclear Proliferation:** The risk of U.S. allies seeking their own nuclear deterrents (in Europe and Asia) has "gone up materially" due to rising regional threats and perceived U.S. unpredictability.
* **The "Long Tail" of Political Volatility:** Sullivan warns that a second Trump term would force a global recalculation of the U.S. relationship that will persist for decades, regardless of who follows him in office.
