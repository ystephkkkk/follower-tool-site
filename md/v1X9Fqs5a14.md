## TL;DR
Former notorious jewelry thief Larry Lawton—who robbed over 25 stores across the East Coast for the Gambino crime family before serving 12 years in federal prison—breaks down the realities of high-stakes heists, critiques the upcoming *Grand Theft Auto VI*, and details how he turned his life around to reform policing and keep at-risk youth out of jail.

## GTA VI, Casing & The Reality of Heists
*   **GTA VI Trailer Critique:** Lawton, whose criminal heyday took place in late-1980s and 1990s South Florida, noted that Rockstar's *GTA VI* trailer misses key operational realities—such as letting strangers walk past armed guards or putting calls on speakerphone in front of a crowd. Real crews rely on strict secrecy and tight circles of trust.
*   **The Art of Casing:** Lawton cased stores for 2 to 6 weeks (and cased over 1,000 stores in his career) before striking. He tracked mailmen, FedEx/UPS deliveries, trash pickups, neighboring businesses, and sun angles (hitting east-facing stores in the morning so glare blocked outsiders from seeing in).
*   **Robbing People, Not Tech:** Despite modern surveillance like Flock cameras, Lawton emphasized that "you're not robbing technology, you're robbing people," and people are habit-driven. He always chose strip malls anchored by supermarkets like Publix or Winn-Dixie so he could blend in while observing.
*   **Target Selection & Fencing:** Robbing convenience stores like 7-Eleven is "for morons." Lawton targeted wholesale jewelers because diamonds offer maximum value-to-weight (e.g., the $140M Carlton Hotel heist in France fit in a briefcase).
    *   He paid $10,000 under the table to attend the Gemological Institute of America (GIA) to learn diamond grading.
    *   He used a single trusted fence, getting 25–30% on jewelry and a flat $2,000 per standard Rolex (which retailed for ~$4,000). Gold was melted and signature pieces dismantled within 24 hours.

## Organized Crime, Capture & Code of Conduct
*   **Gambino Family Ties:** After a Coast Guard injury left him on medical leave in New York, Lawton began bouncing at underground card games in Queens, became a bookie under a major layoff guy named "Mac," and ran loan-sharking operations (charging 3 points, or $600/week on $20,000, eventually taking 50% ownership of bars).
*   **First Heist & Rules:** His first robbery (~1988–1989) was a mob-arranged insurance setup in South Florida that netted him $150,000. He was forbidden from robbing in New York because stores paid mob protection. He maintained a strict rule of never hurting "civilians," reserving violence only for people "in the business."
*   **The FBI Takedown:** Following a 6-year investigation led by FBI agent Matt Mullen, Lawton was arrested on December 2, 1996. A store in Fairless Hills, Pennsylvania, had recorded his license plate, allowing the FBI to pull convenience store surveillance footage.
*   **Refusing to Snitch:** Facing life in prison, Lawton turned down a federal offer of 2 years in prison and keeping $6M–$6.1M in exchange for testifying against his associates. He served 12 years (from age 34 to 46) and was the only person ever convicted under the RICO Act alone after refusing to implicate his brother.

## Prison Survival, Corruption & System Reform
*   **USP Atlanta & Prison Economy:** Lawton served time at USP Atlanta alongside 2,000 inmates—880 of whom had life sentences (including ~200 he described as true psychopaths). After cigarettes were banned, postage stamps became the currency ($6/book, valued at $5 or 20 quarters; later rising to $10). Lawton ran a prison bookmaking operation holding thousands of books of stamps.
*   **Solitary Confinement ("The Hole"):** Lawton spent 11 straight months in solitary confinement on "investigation" after defending his cellmate over 10 lbs of stolen pasta and throwing a paper shredder at unit manager Tubbs. He endured regular beatings and four-point restraints, nearly committing suicide before a midnight-shift guard talked him through the cell door.
*   **Suing the BOP:** Lawton successfully sued Attorney General Alberto Gonzales, BOP Director Harley Lappin, and prison staff over being thrown in the hole for masturbating in his cell, forcing a policy change.
*   **Fixing the System:** Lawton argued that only 7% of prisoners are incarcerated for truly violent crimes and proposed a zero-cost way to end prison abuse:
    *   Replace fake American Correctional Association (ACA) inspections—which give 2 weeks' advance notice—with unannounced, surprise inspection teams (comprising a former inmate, guard, warden, medical worker, and administrator) empowered to fire anyone who blocks their entry.
    *   **High-Profile Deaths:** Lawton believes Jeffrey Epstein was murdered (citing disabled cameras, sleeping guards, and his removal from suicide watch) and noted that Whitey Bulger was killed within 12 hours of hitting the yard at USP Hazelton.

## Coast Guard Service, Policing & Redemption
*   **Coast Guard Veteran:** Lawton was on the first boat on scene for the 1980 Marielito boat lift, pulling bodies from the water and facing down a Cuban gunboat 15 miles off Cuba. He also boarded Russian fishing vessels in Alaska during the Cold War and later crushed his spine in a 30-foot fall into a fish hold.
*   **Community Policing:** Now an honorary police officer in Lake St. Louis, Missouri, Lawton teaches at police academies and has spoken to the International Association of Chiefs of Police (IACP) ethics committee in Dallas. He urges officers to act as part of the community rather than creating an "us-against-them" mentality through ticket "money grabs."
*   **Reality Check Program:** After a judge saw his 10-slide PowerPoint and began sentencing kids to his "program," Lawton built the Reality Check Program. According to an Eastern Florida State College study, the program has achieved a 90% success rate in keeping kids out of jail, a 70% improvement in parental attitudes, a 43% increase in grades, and a 31% increase in school attendance.
