## TL;DR
Former U.S. Secretary of Commerce Gina Raimondo warned at the 2026 Bloomberg New Economy Forum in New Delhi that while AI is accelerating faster than its developers anticipated, global governance is lagging behind in an increasingly fragile world. She called on governments, businesses, and civil society to reject isolationism and collaborate on shared AI safety standards, workforce preparation, and cross-border partnerships—highlighting the U.S.–India relationship as central to this effort.

## Geopolitical Fragility and the Case for Collaboration
* **A More Fragile World:** Raimondo noted the world feels more fragile than it did a year ago in Singapore, citing:
  * The ongoing war in Iran with no obvious end, driving high energy prices and disrupting shipping lanes and supply chains.
  * The multi-year conflict in Ukraine continuing unabated.
  * Tariffs and energy shocks compounding global instability, demonstrating that in an interconnected world, conflict anywhere quickly disrupts commerce and security everywhere.
* **Rejecting Isolationism:** While political waves sometimes favor isolation, Raimondo argued it has never been good for prosperity and, in the age of AI, is a bad bet for safety and security.
  * Reiterated her message from last year's conference on reindustrialization: *"America first shouldn't mean America alone."*
  * Noted that over the past year, countries have begun working more closely together on supply chain coordination and semiconductors, and urged that this cooperation continue.

## The Rapid Acceleration and Risks of AI
* **Still in the "First Inning":** AI is advancing faster than even its developers expected, bringing both massive upside and serious downside risks.
  * **Optimism & Real-World Impact:** Highlighted AI's productivity enhancements and life-saving potential, sharing the story of a mother whose two-year-old child with an incurable form of cancer is beginning to see potential cures because of AI.
* **Withheld Capabilities:** Some AI models are now so powerful—particularly at finding and exploiting software weaknesses—that developers are holding them back from public release and pacing access to only a small number of trusted organizations.
* **Autonomous "Rogue" Agents:** AI agents can now plan, act, and operate with real autonomy. What was dismissed as a thought experiment a year ago—rogue agents behaving in unintended ways—is happening today.
* **The Governance Gap:** Raimondo expressed greater concern over governance challenges than engineering challenges, noting that technology is moving far faster than governing institutions and bureaucracies in both the U.S. and India.

## Three Challenges for Global AI Leadership
* **1. Establish Shared Global Standards:** No single country, company, or industry can manage AI alone, as a model built in one place is trained on data from another and runs on chips designed, made, and packaged across different nations.
  * A patchwork of national rules will leave everyone less safe, as a software vulnerability or rogue agent knows no borders.
  * Governments, businesses, and researchers must set common expectations for developing powerful AI, monitoring and controlling agents, and establishing protocols when things go wrong.
  * Pointed to existing precedents in aviation, financial services, and nuclear safety, as well as the U.S. AI Safety Office (created during her tenure) working with international AI Safety Institutes.
* **2. Prepare the Workforce:** Warned against repeating the "China shock," which left millions of Americans out of work and fueled political division and populism on both the left and the right.
  * Cited Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella's warning: why would Microsoft have a social license to operate if it only creates technology that puts Americans out of work?
  * Urged leaders not to slow down AI, but to implement it alongside retraining, redeployment, and safety nets so no American, Indian, or global worker is left behind.
* **3. Bring Everyone to the Table:** Having spent 15 years in government, Raimondo emphasized that governments move too slowly and lack all the necessary knowledge to act alone.
  * Collaboration must include businesses, scientists, universities, and civil society (noting global leaders in attendance, such as the Chairman of Hyundai from Korea).

## India's Role and the U.S.–India Partnership
* **Leadership & Optimism:** Praised Prime Minister Narendra Modi's energy and "power to empower" message, as well as India's scale, ambition, entrepreneurship, and national pride.
* **AI as "America India":** Recounted a one-hour meeting at Prime Minister Modi's home at 9:00 PM three years ago, where Modi told her: *"Secretary, let's not call AI artificial intelligence. Let's call it America India."* When she saw him again prior to her remarks, his first words were *"AI, America, India."*
* **Workforce Advantage:** With a growing, young, and entrepreneurial workforce, India is positioned to be at the center of the AI economy; Raimondo noted that the country that prepares its people best will hold a global advantage in using AI.
