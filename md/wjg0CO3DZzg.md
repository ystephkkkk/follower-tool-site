**TL;DR**
Former National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan warns that the Trump administration’s "war of choice" against Iran lacks clear strategic objectives and a viable "off-ramp," arguing that tactical military successes have not made the U.S. safer or permanently neutralized the nuclear threat.

## Failure of Strategic Planning
* **Unanswered Questions:** Sullivan asserts the administration has failed to answer two critical "Situation Room" questions: what the specific objectives are and how the U.S. will counteract the enemy's inevitable response.
* **Definition of Success:** He criticizes White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt’s definition of "unconditional surrender" as "word games," suggesting the administration is using vague rhetoric because they lack a clear exit strategy.
* **Predictable Escalations:** Despite it being "obvious" that Iran would target the Strait of Hormuz to drive up global energy prices, the administration appears "befuddled" by these developments two weeks into the conflict.

## Tactical Success vs. Strategic Failure
* **Conventional Degradation:** Sullivan acknowledges that U.S. forces have performed "remarkably," deeply setting back Iran's navy, air force, and ballistic missile/drone capabilities.
* **Reconstitution Risks:** He warns these gains are incremental and easily reversed, as Iran retains the capacity to rebuild its conventional military infrastructure within a few years.
* **The Nuclear Conundrum:** While past operations (e.g., Operation Midnight Hammer) damaged facilities, Iran still possesses highly enriched uranium and the scientific expertise to build a bomb. Sullivan views proposed special operations to extract nuclear material as "incredibly risky" with no guarantee of success.

## The Case for Diplomatic Off-Ramps
* **Prioritizing the Nuclear Threat:** Sullivan advises a "sequenced" approach to diplomacy, focusing first on the nuclear stockpile (dilution or removal) before addressing proxies or ballistic missiles.
* **The Necessity of Verification:** He maintains that only diplomacy can provide the "inspectors on the ground" required to verify that Iran is not pursuing a weapon.
* **Quid Pro Quo Requirements:** Sullivan notes that successful diplomacy requires incentives; the U.S. must be prepared to offer metered sanctions relief in exchange for compliance, as "maximalist" demands often lead to dead ends.

## Regional Instability and Economic Costs
* **Iranian Resilience:** Recent reports indicate Iran is not a "decapitated regime," having launched strikes against Tel Aviv, Dubai International Airport, and regional financial institutions.
* **Global Leadership:** Sullivan argues that launching a "war of choice" without an imminent threat or informed public consent degrades America’s capacity for global leadership and strains relationships with Gulf allies.
* **Domestic Impact:** Beyond the immediate risk of asymmetric attacks (cyber or terror), the conflict is creating significant economic strain for Americans "at the pumps."
