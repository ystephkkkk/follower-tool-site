### TL;DR
Bill Ackman identifies the artificial intelligence revolution as the definitive economic driver of 2026–2028, remaining highly bullish on compute demand and capital sustainability while noting that high debt levels will keep interest rates persistently high. Richard Ditizio moderates the conversation at the Milken Institute Global Investors' Symposium in Mexico City.

## The Macro Landscape: AI Infrastructure, Debt, and Rates
- **AI as the Primary Growth Engine:** Bill Ackman highlights that the global economy is changing rapidly via AI, approaching super intelligence as the cost of intelligence falls and democratizes.
- **Sustainability of Infrastructure Spending:** Acknowledges that the largest tech companies will spend $650 billion to $750 billion on AI infrastructure using borrowed money, but argues this is entirely sustainable because the return on investment (ROI) on compute far outstrips the cost of capital.
- **Hyperscalers vs. Neo-Cloud Risk:** Ackman expresses confidence in major hyperscalers due to their creditworthiness and financial resources, while warning that pure-play neo-cloud companies resemble highly leveraged real estate developers and carry significantly higher risk.
- **Sovereign Capital Competition:** Notes that companies like SpaceX (with Elon Musk projecting massive gigawatt data center buildouts) may need to borrow up to $1 trillion, competing directly with sovereign levels for debt and keeping rates persistently high.
- **Geopolitical Opportunities:** Points to potential resolutions in Middle Eastern and Russian conflicts as possible catalysts for a global peace dividend and lower energy prices that could reduce inflationary pressures.
- **Global Political Risks:** Highlights the socialist state model as unsustainable over the long term, citing France spending 59% of GDP on social programs alongside ongoing civil unrest as a major threat to global prosperity.

## Portfolio Strategy, Public Equities, and Mexico
- **Stock Selection and Valuation Anomalies:** Notes that Pershing Square owns companies like Uber and Amazon, pointing out that Uber trades at 16 times earnings with projected earnings growth of 35% over the next year and 25% per annum over the next five years.
- **Autonomous Vehicle Competition:** Rejects the market fear that Tesla’s cyber cab will monopolize autonomous transport, predicting instead roughly a dozen autonomous vehicle companies competing to offer consumers the cheapest ride.
- **Internet Bubble Comparison:** Distinguishes the current AI wave from the 1999 internet bubble by noting that foundational AI companies like Anthropic, Cognition, and Databricks are scaling at unprecedented rates with massive, profitable business models unlike "pets.com".
- **Mexican Market Potential:** Acknowledges that the Mexican stock market is cheap and contains interesting gems—mentioning a special situation pitched to him recently—but explains that Pershing Square rarely invests locally because family-controlled companies limit public float and liquidity for multi-billion-dollar investments.

## Philanthropy and the Hackman Oxman Institute
- **Catalyst for Medical Innovation:** Following his daughter Lucy's survival of a severe brain hemorrhage with massive swelling, Ackman and his family committed $400 million (now nearing $800 million) to establish the Hackman Oxman Institute.
- **Incentive Flaws in Healthcare:** Points out that healthcare systems heavily incentivize high-margin surgeries ($40,000 for neurosurgery in minimal square footage) while severely underfunding rehabilitation, which requires large space and poor reimbursement rates.
- **Institute Scope and Vision:** Led by Mount Sinai neurosurgeon Chris Kellner, the institute acquired a 3.4-acre site in New York City with a brand-new biotech building to eventually scale 2 to 2.5 million square feet.
- **Cross-Disciplinary Approach:** Combines neurosurgery, brain rehabilitation, longevity, and technology development—recruiting top-tier neuroscientists, partnering with Formula 1 response trainers, and deploying elite athletes and Navy SEALs alongside patients.
- **Brain-Computer Interfaces:** Reveals that the institute has already deployed nearly $100 million into brain-computer interface and device development, signaling an underinvested space poised for rapid growth.
