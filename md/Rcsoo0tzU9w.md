**TL;DR**
The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) maintained the federal funds rate at 3.5% to 3.75%, opting for a "wait-and-see" approach as the Fed balances persistent inflation—driven by tariffs and Middle East energy shocks—against a softening labor market that shows signs of zero net job creation in the private sector.

## Monetary Policy and Interest Rate Outlook
*   **Policy Decision:** The FOMC voted to keep the target range for the federal funds rate at 3.5% to 3.75%, characterizing the current stance as "mildly restrictive" or at the "high end of neutral."
*   **Rate Cut Projections:** Despite elevated inflation readings, the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) indicates a median expectation of a 3.4% rate by year-end (implying modest cuts), though Chair Jerome Powell noted a "meaningful movement" among some officials toward fewer cuts compared to December forecasts.
*   **Meeting-by-Meeting Approach:** Powell emphasized that policy is not on a preset course. Decisions will be based on incoming data, the evolving outlook, and the balance of risks.
*   **Leadership Continuity:** Powell confirmed he would serve as "Chair Pro Tem" if a successor is not confirmed by the end of his term (May 15), and he has no intention of leaving the Board until an ongoing internal investigation is finalized.

## Inflation Drivers and the "2% Target"
*   **Current Indicators:** Headline PCE inflation rose 2.8% over the last 12 months, while core PCE (excluding food and energy) stands at 3.0%.
*   **The Tariff Factor:** Powell attributed a significant portion of current core inflation (between 0.5% and 0.75%) to tariffs. He characterized these as "one-time" price increases rather than ongoing inflation, expecting their impact to fade by mid-year.
*   **Energy Shocks:** Supply disruptions in the Middle East have driven up oil prices, leading to higher near-term inflation expectations. 
    *   **"Looking Through" Shocks:** While the Fed typically ignores temporary energy spikes, Powell noted that doing so is contingent on inflation expectations remaining "well-anchored" at 2%.
*   **Service Sector Persistence:** Non-housing services have moved "sideways" for a year. Powell expressed frustration that this sector has not yet responded to a cooling labor market.

## Economic Growth and Labor Market Dynamics
*   **GDP Revisions:** The median projection for real GDP growth was revised upward to 2.4% for this year, reflecting growing confidence in productivity gains.
*   **Labor Market Softening:** The unemployment rate remains stable at 4.4%, but other indicators show clear softening.
    *   **Zero Net Job Creation:** Powell noted that when adjusting for "overcounting," private sector job creation is effectively at zero. 
    *   **Equilibrium:** The Chair described the current state as a "zero employment growth equilibrium," largely driven by changes in immigration policy and lower labor force participation.
*   **Downside Risks:** While inflation risks are currently to the upside, Powell highlighted that labor market risks are shifting to the downside, creating a "difficult situation" for the dual mandate.

## Global Uncertainty and Productivity
*   **Middle East Volatility:** Powell repeatedly stated that the "size and duration" of the conflict's economic impact are unknown, making current forecasts subject to high uncertainty.
*   **Stagflation Dismissal:** Powell rejected the term "stagflation," noting that current conditions (low unemployment and ~3% inflation) bear no resemblance to the double-digit misery of the 1970s.
*   **Productivity and AI:** 
    *   **Recent Gains:** Powell attributed high recent productivity to pandemic-era economizing.
    *   **AI Impact:** While generative AI could expand potential output long-term, Powell warned that in the short term, the massive construction of data centers might actually put upward pressure on inflation and the neutral interest rate.
