## TL;DR
Legora has grown from $1 million to $100 million in ARR since October 2024, with over 3% of the world’s lawyers actively using the platform. Co-founder and CEO Max Junestrand shares how an intense focus on product execution, scrappy early customer development in Sweden, and an uncompromising winning culture fueled one of the fastest enterprise software trajectories in history.

## Origin Story and Early Legal Market Entry
- **The Founding Team:** Started by four co-founders in Sweden (a lawyer, a physicist, an engineer, and a psychologist) who observed law students spending internships summarizing court cases using early NLP models.
- **Cold Emailing Lawyers:** **Max Junestrand** notes that the founders initially had zero domain expertise, so they cold-emailed Swedish lawyers on LinkedIn, offering to pay their hourly consulting fees to take them to lunch and learn about their practice areas.
- **Moving Into a Law Firm:** **Max Junestrand** explains that the team eventually moved into a windowless conference room at Manheimer Swartling (the largest law firm in the Nordics) where the AC turned off at 5:00 PM, working side-by-side with lawyers to build practical workflows.

## Y Combinator Rejection and Acceleration
- **The First Interview Rejection:** **Max Junestrand** recounts applying in May 2023 with a pitch to query legal documents using LLMs; when YC partners asked what type of lawyers they served, the founders naively asked, *"What do you mean, are there different types of lawyers?"*—prompting open laughter from partner Tom Bloomfield.
- **Reapplying and Acceptance:** **Max Junestrand** states that the team returned to building immediately, reapplied two months later under a new name and platform, and successfully entered the batch, during which they scaled from zero to $1 million in ARR.
- **Global Operations Strain:** **Max Junestrand** shares that during YC, he worked sales calls from a San Francisco Airbnb between 1:00 AM and 10:00 AM daily using an influencer ring light to maintain his European customer base.

## Fundraising, The 6-Month Sales Freeze, and Product Focus
- **Benchmark Investment:** **Max Junestrand** recalls scheduling 80 investor meetings in 10 days, ultimately partnering with Benchmark (partner Chae An), who invested $9.51 million, followed by Redpoint's Series A three weeks later.
- **The Bank Dilemma:** **Max Junestrand** points out that with $35 million in the bank and a 10-person team, there was a month where the company made more money from interest rates on its cash pile than from customers.
- **Freezing Sales:** **Max Junestrand** highlights the critical decision to freeze their sales motion for six months to completely rebuild the product, moving away from democratic team voting on features to a disciplined product manifesto that established scalable agentic workflows.

## High-Performance Culture and Talent Strategy
- **Unlearning Resume Bias:** **Max Junestrand** explains that the company learned to avoid hiring based on fancy resume logos ("y-intercept"), preferring high-trajectory, intensely driven young talent—such as their top seller, a 23-year-old university dropout with no sales background who has sold over $10 million in Legora contracts.
- **LFG Values:** **Max Junestrand** outlines the company's three core cultural values: **Lean in**, **Fight for excellence**, and **Grow together** (abbreviated as LFG).
- **The "Taste of Blood" Intensity:** **Max Junestrand** discusses how they imported a Swedish phrase meaning waking up with a taste of blood from excitement, turning it into the internal slogan *"blue smoke"* to maintain relentless execution speed.
- **Jude Law Campaign:** **Max Junestrand** details how persistent nagging convinced actor Jude Law to front their ad campaign—conditioned on using his own SNL script director and the cinematographer from *Oppenheimer*—which successfully catapulted the brand into mainstream recognition.
