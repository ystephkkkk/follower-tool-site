**TL;DR**
The U.S. is executing a "whole-of-government" strategy to break China’s dominance in critical minerals while transitioning from multilateral trade agreements to high-stakes bilateral deals aimed at reducing the trade deficit and boosting domestic manufacturing.

## Critical Minerals Strategy and China Decoupling
* **Strategic Partnerships:** The U.S. is aligning with the EU, Japan, and Mexico to diversify supply chains for minerals vital to national security, such as rare earths, graphite, and neodymium.
* **Economic Safeguards:** The administration is negotiating mineral-specific **price floors** to protect domestic and allied producers from non-market Chinese subsidies.
* **Domestic Production:** Efforts are underway to accelerate environmental approvals and mining in the U.S.
    * **Utah:** A significant deposit of "heavy" rare earths—similar to those found in China—is being fast-tracked.
    * **South Carolina:** Production of permanent magnets (used in electric motors) has resumed for the first time in 25 years.
* **National Security:** Greer emphasized that these minerals are indispensable for missiles, satellites, and radiation-hardened semiconductors, making reliance on China an unacceptable risk.

## Shifting Global Trade Frameworks
* **Bilateral Focus:** The administration is abandoning broad multilateralism in favor of "America First" bilateral deals. Recent agreements include Argentina, Bangladesh, India, Guatemala, and El Salvador.
* **The India Pivot:** A major interim trade framework is in motion, with a broader deal expected by March.
    * **Energy Shift:** India is winding down Russian oil purchases in favor of U.S. energy.
    * **Economic Commitment:** India is expected to purchase up to $500 billion in U.S. energy, aircraft, and technology over the next five years.
* **Upcoming Indonesia Deal:** Greer identified Indonesia (250 million people) as the next major trade target, with a deal expected to be finalized within weeks to open the market to U.S. exports.

## North American Trade Resets (USMCA)
* **Bilateral Negotiations:** The U.S. is decoupling its approach to Mexico and Canada. Greer noted that Mexico is being "pragmatic," while negotiations with Canada remain "challenging."
* **Canada Friction:** Specific disputes are stalling a reset with Canada:
    * **Gordie Howe International Bridge:** President Trump is threatening to block the opening of this Canadian-owned bridge until the U.S. is compensated for revenue and American content is addressed.
    * **Trade Barriers:** Greer cited Canada’s refusal to sell U.S. wine and spirits as a persistent obstacle.
* **Review Process:** The administration is utilizing the built-in USMCA review period to adjust rules of origin and external tariff policies.

## Economic Impact and Tariff Leverage
* **Manufacturing Gains:** Greer reported that U.S. manufacturing productivity surged 5% higher than expectations last quarter, accompanied by rising real wages for workers.
* **Tariffs as a Tool:** Tariffs are being used as leverage to force supply chain shifts and reduce the trade deficit.
* **Supreme Court Ruling:** The administration is awaiting a SCOTUS decision (expected around Feb. 20) regarding tariff authority.
    * **Contingency Planning:** If the court rules against the administration, Greer stated they will use "other tools" to maintain continuity in tariff levels and address unfair trade surpluses.
