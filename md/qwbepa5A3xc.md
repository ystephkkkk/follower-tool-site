## TL;DR
Sam Parr and Shaan Puri explore hidden-in-plain-sight business models—such as lab-testing monkeys and soybean oil collateral audits—while discussing how entrepreneurs can find low-competition opportunities through childhood passions, curiosity, and the art of noticing.

## Hidden-in-Plain-Sight Businesses & High-Margin Niches
* **Lab Testing Monkeys:** 
  * More than 20% of all drugs are tested on monkeys, utilizing roughly tens of thousands of imported monkeys annually (specifically long-tailed cynomolgus macaques).
  * Monkey prices surged from roughly $2,000 to between $20,000 and $50,000 per monkey following COVID-19 and China halting exports.
  * **Charles River Labs:** A major publicly traded research testing-as-a-service provider working with ~80% of biotech companies, doing $4 billion a year in revenue; they purchased a monkey breeding supply business for $500 million to control supply chains.
* **The Soybean Oil Crisis & Audit Arbitrage:** 
  * Warren Express/American Express history: A massive financial scandal erupted when a fraudster took multi-million-dollar loans using non-existent soybean oil inventory (tanks filled with seawater) backed by American Express verification guarantees.
  * The scale of such hidden service industries demonstrates that massive multi-hundred-million-dollar businesses operate in obscure nooks and crannies far from traditional software and tech.
* **Dental Back-Office Automation:**
  * **Daydream Dental:** Founded by a Stanford grad/dropout whose mother was a dentist, the company uses AI and human teams to handle dental insurance billing codes and claims.
  * They collect 15% to 20% more revenue for dentists that would otherwise be left on the table, scaling to close to $10 million a year in revenue by year two.
* **Industrial Generator Servicing:** 
  * Companies servicing and installing industrial-sized generators for nursing homes and hospitals represent vital, low-competition, high-defensibility infrastructure businesses.

## The Art of Notice & Entrepreneurial Advantage
* **Studying Creativity over Hustle:** 
  * Shaan Puri notes that studying creative icons (like Rick Rubin or top comedians) reveals that great insights come from **noticing** everyday struggles rather than brute-force scaling.
  * **Jerry Seinfeld's Daily Habit:** Seinfeld has spent the first 2 hours of every day for 40 years writing half-jokes on legal pads, compiling them into his book *Is This Anything?*.
* **Paul Graham's "How to Do Great Work" Framework:**
  * **Motor and Rudder:** Follow the path of most excitement because genuine interest provides both work endurance and differentiation.
  * **The Frontier:** Travel to the absolute edge of any field or industry to discover unaddressed gaps and tedious problems that smart people normally ignore.
* **Playing in Low-Competition Pools:** 
  * Avoid crowded, high-status fields (like building standard to-do list apps or entering hyper-competitive gaming championships) and instead target obscure categories with weak competition.

## Childhood Roots & Shameless Enthusiasm
* **Knowing Thy 8-Year-Old Self:**
  * Childhood free time and early obsessions provide a blueprint for genuine adult interests (e.g., Dan Brown's father giving him treasure maps for Christmas, leading directly to writing *The Da Vinci Code*).
* **Embrace Shamelessness:**
  * **Elan Lee (Exploding Kittens):** Creator of multiple top-10 board games globally, building massive commercial success by leaning fully into childhood toys, LEGO building, and escape rooms without filtering out his "grown man kid" interests.
  * **Enthusiasm as a Superpower:** While smart people often feel self-conscious or low-status being enthusiastic, genuine enthusiasm is a magnetic honeypot that attracts like-minded collaborators and provides the resilience needed for hard work.

## Awe, San Francisco, and Obsession
