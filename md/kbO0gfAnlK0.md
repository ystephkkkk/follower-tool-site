## Q3 2026 Sector Performance & Narrative Shifts
* **S&P 500 Performance:** The index rose 2% in Q3 and 12% for the first nine months of the year, though performance was highly mixed with only 47% of Russell 1000 stocks above their 200-day moving average.
* **Energy (Best Performer):** Surged 16.5% for the quarter and 37% for the year, driven by higher oil prices stemming from the war in Iran; however, it only accounts for 3.4% of the S&P 500, contributing 1.3% to the year's total index performance. Large energy companies like Marathon and Phillips gained over 50% for the quarter.
* **Information Technology:** Gained 7% for the quarter and 28% for the year, representing 40% of the S&P 500. 
  * **SaaSpocalypse Narrative Flip:** The dominant prior narrative that AI would eliminate software flipped; software and tech consulting stocks rallied hard in Q3, including Palantir (**+60%**), Workday (**+56%**), Cognizant (**+48%**), Accenture (**+47%**), and Salesforce (**+46%**).
  * **Hardware Reallocation:** Conversely, previous hardware high-flyers dropped as investors reallocated; Western Digital fell 29% in Q3, and FICO plummeted 50% as the worst-performing tech stock of the quarter.
* **Financials:** Declined 50 basis points for the quarter and 2.5% for the year. Large and regional banks dropped over 10% from their July highs due to recession and credit-cost fears, while safe-haven financial exchanges (Nasdaq, CME, ICE) rose 17% to 24%. PayPal gained 22% after receiving an unsolicited takeover bid at $60 that management rejected, with the stock subsequently falling into the low 50s.
* **Consumer Discretionary:** Fell 5% for the quarter and 6% for the year, hit by locked housing markets and soaring rates. Home Depot dropped 19%, Deckers Outdoor fell 21%, and Norwegian Cruise Line dropped 31%.
* **Industrials:** Dropped 10% for the quarter but gained 8% for the year. AI data center infrastructure plays took a breather, with Generac and Invertive falling nearly 30%.
* **Utilities (Worst Performer):** Plunged 13% for the quarter and 8% for the year. Despite spending roughly $250 billion in AI-related capital expenditures, the sector suffered from rising long-term rates eroding dividend appeal and a wave of ratepayer backlash causing Fitch to downgrade North American utilities to deteriorating. Stocks like NRG Energy, Edison, and PG&E dropped over 25% in Q3.

* **The AI Monoculture:** Infotech (40%), tech-adjacent giants like Google and Amazon (pushing past 50%), utilities (3%), AI industrial construction/power plays (Eaton, GE Vernova, Quanta), and AI-financing financials push total AI exposure in the S&P 500 to **north of 65%**. Non-AI sectors are largely limited to Staples and Healthcare (14% combined).
* **Bond Market Limitations:** Approximately 15% of existing corporate bonds and over 50% of current-year debt issuance are AI-related, limiting traditional fixed-income diversification. Eisman suggests short-term Treasuries as an AI-immune parking place for cash.
