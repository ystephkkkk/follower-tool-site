U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio reaffirmed the "unbreakable" bond of Western civilization between the U.S. and Europe while calling for a strategic shift away from globalist "delusions" toward an alliance rooted in national sovereignty, reindustrialization, and border security.

## Re-evaluating the Post-Cold War Order
* **The "End of History" Fallacy:** Rubio argued that the post-Cold War belief in a borderless, rules-based global order was a "dangerous delusion" that ignored human nature and 5,000 years of history.
* **Economic Consequences:** He criticized decades of "unfettered trade" and deindustrialization, noting that the U.S. and Europe systematically undercut their own economies by:
    * Shipping middle-class jobs overseas.
    * Handing control of critical supply chains to adversaries.
    * Outsourcing sovereignty to international institutions while neglecting national defense.
* **Energy and Climate:** Rubio characterized current energy policies as "impoverishing" Western populations to appease a "climate cult," while competitors exploit fossil fuels for economic leverage.

## The Vision for a "New Western Century"
* **Civilizational Unity:** The Secretary emphasized that the U.S. is a "child of Europe," bound by shared Christian faith, culture, and history rather than just military or economic interests.
* **Economic Renewal:** A call for a "Western supply chain" for critical minerals and investment in "new frontiers" to compete with the Global South, specifically:
    * Commercial space travel and Artificial Intelligence (AI).
    * Industrial automation and flexible manufacturing.
* **Border Security:** Rubio labeled mass migration as a "crisis" destabilizing Western societies. He asserted that controlling borders is a fundamental act of national sovereignty, not an expression of xenophobia.
* **Reciprocity and Strength:** He clarified that President Trump’s administration demands "seriousness and reciprocity" from allies, stating that a weak Europe makes the United States weaker.

## Reforming International Institutions and Conflict Resolution
* **The United Nations (UN):** Rubio argued the UN has been "powerless" to solve major crises, contrasting its inaction with decisive American leadership in:
    * Negotiating the release of captives in Gaza.
    * Bringing Ukraine and Russia to the negotiating table.
    * Using "precision bombs" to constrain Iran’s nuclear program.
    * Capturing a "narco-terrorist dictator" in Venezuela.
* **The Conflict in Ukraine:** 
    * **Status:** Rubio noted that while the issues have "narrowed," they are now focused on the hardest questions of territory and terms. 
    * **Strategy:** The U.S. is "testing" whether Russia is serious about a settlement while continuing sanctions and the "PERL" program for weaponry sales.
* **China Relations:**
    * **Pragmatic Engagement:** Rubio described it as "geopolitical malpractice" not to communicate with China but maintained that the U.S. will prioritize national interests.
    * **Expectations:** He expressed skepticism regarding a "deal," noting that the U.S. and China have fundamental, long-term national interest misalignments that must be managed to avoid conflict.
