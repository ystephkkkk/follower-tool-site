**TL;DR**
Kevin Warsh is expected to transition from a historical "inflation hawk" to a pragmatic, productivity-focused Fed Chair who prioritizes market signals and private-sector data over traditional economic models while seeking closer "soft coordination" with the Treasury on balance sheet management.

## Shift in Monetary Policy Philosophy
* **From Hawk to "Productivity Dove":** While Warsh was historically more concerned with inflation than labor (2006–2011), his current outlook is shaped by a belief in a "positive supply shock" driven by AI.
    * **Supply vs. Demand:** Warsh argues that current inflation risks are low because the economy is seeing supply-side improvements rather than the money-financed fiscal expansion seen during the pandemic.
* **The "Greenspan-Druckenmiller" Model:** Krishna Guha describes Warsh’s ideal central banker as a 50/50 split between Alan Greenspan (obsessive detail in obscure data) and Stan Druckenmiller (forward-looking market signals).
    * **Model Skepticism:** Warsh is openly "scornful" of backward-looking models like the Fed’s FRB-US, preferring micro-level corporate data and market-derived insights.
* **Data Dependency:** He intends to move away from "backward-looking data dependency" (last month's CPI/jobs reports) toward a more nuanced, forward-looking assessment of the economic outlook.

## Balance Sheet Management and "Soft Coordination"
* **"Smaller, Cleaner, Humbler":** A consistent theme in Warsh’s career is the belief that a large Fed balance sheet constitutes "big government" and encourages fiscal deficits.
    * **Current Status:** The balance sheet has already dropped from a $9 trillion peak to approximately $6.5 trillion, unwinding the pandemic expansion.
* **The Fed-Treasury Accord:** Warsh proposes closer cooperation between the Fed and the Treasury to ensure balance sheet plans are consistent with debt management.
    * **The "Soft Veto":** Guha suggests this accord effectively gives Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent a "soft veto" over aggressive Quantitative Tightening (QT) to prevent spikes in bond yields and mortgage rates.
* **Avoidance of "Taper Tantrums":** Despite his desire for a smaller footprint, Warsh is expected to be pragmatic. An immediate, aggressive restart of QT is deemed "laughably improbable" due to the risk of market destabilization.

## Institutional "Regime Change" and Internal Relations
* **The "Repair Job":** Warsh has been highly critical of the Fed’s culture, using phrases like "breaking heads" and "regime change." To be effective, he will need to win over the existing Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC).
    * **Leadership Style:** Despite his rhetoric, Warsh is noted for high "EQ" and networking skills, suggesting he may be more conciliatory behind closed doors.
* **Jerome Powell’s Tenure:** Political attacks and DOJ subpoenas from the Trump administration have ironically made it *more* likely that Powell will stay until the end of his term (May 2026) to avoid the appearance of leaving under political pressure.
* **Consensus Building:** Unlike the Bank of England’s individualistic voting, the Fed typically operates through a "structured negotiation" led by the Chair. Warsh’s success depends on his ability to craft a central view the committee can support.

## Deregulation as a Monetary Tool
* **Linking Regulation to Reserves:** The size of the Fed's balance sheet is currently dictated by the "ample reserves" system (where banks hold high levels of reserves at the Fed).
* **Supply-Side Reform:** Warsh, in concert with the Treasury, is expected to push for significant banking deregulation.
    * **Goal:** By making it cheaper (in capital/liquidity terms) for banks to hold Treasury bills instead of Fed reserves, the Fed can naturally shrink its balance sheet without causing money market stress.
