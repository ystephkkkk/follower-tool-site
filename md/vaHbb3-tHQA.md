### TL;DR
Warren Buffett revealed he initiated Berkshire Hathaway's multi-billion-dollar stake in Alphabet, which now exceeds $31 billion including a recent $10 billion private placement. While acknowledging that tech giants are now playing a massive capital-spending game on AI, Buffett defended the investment based on Alphabet's fundamental earnings power and returns on capital.

## Alphabet Investment & Portfolio Scale
* **Alphabet Position Size:** Berkshire Hathaway holds a stake in Alphabet exceeding **$31 billion**.
  * The position began building in the **third quarter of 2025** and accelerated after **Greg Abel** took over as CEO of Berkshire.
  * A **$10 billion private stock purchase** of Alphabet shares was added just last month.
* **Portfolio Ranking:** Alphabet ranks as roughly the **number 5 or 6** holding in Berkshire's portfolio (or **number 3** if fully counting marketable securities and the $10 billion private placement).
  * It sits behind Apple and American Express among equity holdings, though fully-owned subsidiaries like Burlington Northern Railroad carry higher standalone values.
  * It ranks ahead of Bank of America and Coca-Cola (which Buffett noted has been owned for **45 years**).

## Philosophy on "Good Businesses" & Capital Returns
* **Definition of a Good Business:** Buffett defines a superior business as one that consistently earns high returns on capital over an extended period, significantly outperforming riskless government treasuries yielding **$20, 30, or $40 billion a year**.
* **American Express Benchmark:** While typical banks earn **13% to 14%** on capital, American Express earns **30% plus** on capital without incurring higher relative risk.
* **The Charlie Munger Principle:** Longtime partner Charlie Munger insisted that businesses must generate real cash rather than just engage in "sexy things," possessing the ability to either distribute that cash or profitably reemploy it.
* **Flaws of Wall Street:** Buffett criticized Wall Street analysts for focusing on short-term quarterly results and selling hype rather than analyzing internal rates of return.

## Tech Stocks, CAPEX, & The AI Game
* **Shift on Technology:** Despite historically avoiding tech stocks and labeling himself as someone who doesn't understand them, Buffett defended the Alphabet stake, noting he also previously built Berkshire's massive Apple position (which he categorized as a consumer company).
* **Massive Capital Expenditures:** Unlike the asset-light software era that Buffett previously avoided, today's tech giants and hyperscalers (including Alphabet, Amazon, and Microsoft) are deploying hundreds of billions in heavy CAPEX for AI.
  * Buffett compared this to old industrial spending, noting that companies like the railroad or IBM faced industry-shifting disruptions when competitors arrived to better serve customers.
* **Customer Satisfaction vs. Market Hype:** Buffett emphasized that satisfying customers is paramount over time, contrasting the pragmatic approach of a grocery store owner (like his experience in his grandfather's grocery store operating since **1869**) with the speculative dreams of Wall Street.

## Historical Perspective on Investing
* **Historical Precedents:** Buffett pointed to John D. Rockefeller as potentially the most successful long-term investor, compounding wealth steadily through the oil and gas industry over **150 to 200 years**.
* **Early Tech Venture:** Buffett recalled his early association with technology in **1958**, when he and friends started a company called **Data Documents** after reading that IBM had settled an antitrust suit by divesting 50% of its best business capacity.
