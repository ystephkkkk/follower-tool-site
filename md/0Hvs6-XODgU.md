## TL;DR
Ramp has scaled to a $60 billion valuation and over $2 billion in annual revenue by aligning its business model with customer savings, and is now using internal AI harnesses like **Glass** and **Inspect** to turn every employee into a "100x" operator—driving >25% faster growth and 7,000 non-engineer pull requests in Q2 alone.

## From Paribus to Ramp: Aligning Incentives
* **Paribus Origin & Early Traction (YC S15):** Eric Glyman and co-founder Karim launched Paribus after Eric noticed a flight dropped $100 in price and recovered nearly $1,000 for friends under a 24-hour airline refund policy.
  * Built initially as a VBA script, Paribus saved money for 1 in 20 users within minutes of signing up.
  * Applied to YC twice before getting into Summer 2015; acquired early users via the "Beer Money" subreddit and targeted 10%+ weekly user growth.
  * Overcame "too good to be true" skepticism by introducing a two-sided referral program that lowered their 25% fee on savings to 20% for both referrer and referee, scaling to nearly 1 million daily users within a year of graduating YC.
* **The Capital One Anti-Lessons:** After selling Paribus to Capital One and spending over 2.5 years there, Eric saw how institutional incentives in credit cards—pushing purchase volume and devaluing points—misaligned with customers who simply wanted more money in their bank accounts.
* **The Ramp Thesis (Actionable Intelligence):** Paribus could only email customer service to request refunds across hundreds of millions of emails; Ramp embedded intelligence directly into the movement of money to *stop* wasteful spend (e.g., unused subscriptions) before it happens.
  * **Zero-to-One Strategy:** As Garry Tan noted, selling to startups first rather than enterprises paid off—Ramp served companies like **Cursor** and **Cognition** when they were just a handful of people and grew alongside them.

## Ramp’s Next Chapter: From Cost-Cutting to Revenue Growth
* **Chapter One (The Mesh Layer):** Over seven years, Ramp consolidated fragmented finance tools (cards, expenses, bills, procurement, travel) into a single box. Today, Ramp serves tens of thousands of businesses, generates over $2 billion in annual revenue, and helps the median customer cut expenses by 7.5% per year (up from ~5% a year ago—significant given the average U.S. company has an 8% profit margin).
* **Chapter Two (Growth & Productivity):** Ramp is expanding beyond cost-cutting to help customers grow revenue faster, launching **Accounts Receivable** alongside AI tools **Glass** and **Inspect**.
* **Defying Scale Gravity:** Ramp is growing more than 100% per year and is growing over 25% faster now than it was a year ago, shipping major product releases every month for the last five months (up from 1–2 per year).

## The 100x Organization: Solving Uneven AI Adoption
* **The 1% vs. 99% Divide:** Eric noted that 1% of AI spenders account for 80% of AI token spend. Inside Ramp, engineers became 10x–100x more productive while other functions lagged (e.g., designers at 2x, PMs at 5x), breaking traditional 6–8:1:1 org ratios.
* **Forcing the "Aha" Moment:** Earlier this year, despite growing >100% YoY, Ramp paused all hiring for an entire quarter so teams would treat AI as a core competency rather than a 5% side project.
* **Removing IT Friction:** Ramp eliminated the "wall" between AI and company data by building authentication and integrations directly into its tools, letting employees use whatever frontier models or tools work best for them (Ramp was the first alpha customer for Anthropic's **Claude Code**, the first customer for Cognition's **Devin**, and an early **Cursor** customer).
* **The ROI Era of AI:** Eric argued that flat AI budgets ($100 or $1,000 caps) are silly; Ramp focuses on semantically tying token spend to business outcomes (e.g., spending $5 on a landing page that converts 3% better to generate $5,000/month).

## Company Brains, AI Harnesses, and Macro Shifts
* **Inspect (Coding Agent):** Accounts for >85% of all pull requests at Ramp, with PRs per engineer still growing ~40% per month. In Q2, non-engineers (mostly customer experience) created 7,000 PRs—up from functionally zero two years ago—allowing bugs to be fixed in 15 minutes instead of 2–3 months.
* **Glass (The Company Brain):** Out of 1,930 employees at Ramp, 1,925 used Glass in the last 24 hours. It connects to data warehouses, analytics, and Zendesk with built-in permissions, shared skill files, and smart model routing (e.g., trading 10% longer reasoning for a 50% price cut on background coding agents). Ramp began onboarding external customers onto Glass this week.
* **Policy Agent:** Reviews expenses against company policy with 99% accuracy, learning from overrides and updating policies automatically.
* **System of Record vs. AI Harness:** Eric agreed with Garry Tan’s thesis that companies must either die as a system of record or evolve into an AI harness. Ramp’s **MCP** usage has grown ~10% per week over the past two years.
* **Macro AI & Jevons Paradox:**
  * Eric observed that over the last two months, **Jevons paradox broke**—discounting the price of intelligence by 10% no longer triggered a >10% surge in consumption, likely because the 1% of power users hit a saturation point while the 99% remain blocked by internal politics and fiefdoms.
  * Both Eric and Garry bet on **hyper-specialization** over a single-firm AI monopoly: as intelligence costs drop toward zero, thousands of specialized billion-dollar companies will emerge.

## Hiring & Founder Lessons
* **Sutton’s Law for Hiring:** Rather than relying on resumes, Eric looks for proof of self-driven building—such as teenagers running businesses on **Minecraft** private servers or winning at **IMO**, **IOI**, or **MathCounts**.
* **Advice to Younger Self:** Eric emphasized that the most powerful phrase a founder can say is *"I need your help,"* noting that YC office hours taught him to confront problems directly by asking three questions: *How much did you grow this week? What’s your biggest problem? What are you going to do about it?*
