## TL;DR
David Gross reflects on his 25-year career scaling Bain Capital in Asia—including executing the $18 billion Kioxia turnaround—and shares insights from his transition in January to serving as the firm's first sole managing partner since Mitt Romney.

## Background & Early Career in Japan
* **Upbringing:** **David Gross** grew up in suburban and rural parts of Ohio and upstate New York as a twin in a family of four children.
* **Wharton & Japan Inspiration:** Attended Wharton in the late 1980s during the height of the "Japan is number one" economic era; took a mentor's suggestion to visit Japan through a rural homestay program and a Mitsubishi research stint.
* **Corporate Japan Realities (1992):** Joined NEC Corporation in Tokyo just as the economic bubble burst, working on the 40th floor of a 60-floor headquarters.
    * **Observations:** Witnessed the dichotomy between a highly flat, bottom-up manufacturing floor utilizing *kaizen* and continuous improvement, and a rigid, slow-moving white-collar management structure that struggled with software, the internet, and knowledge-worker efficiency.

## Building Bain Capital Asia
* **Entering the Asian Market (2000–2006):** Joined Bain Capital in 2000; opened the Tokyo office in 2006 when most Western investors had abandoned Japan.
    * **Strategic Pivot:** Shifted away from complex, globalized tech giants facing macro headwinds (like NEC and Toyota) to domestic service, consumer, and food businesses with high defensive moats and turnaround potential.
* **Regional Differentiation:** Adapted the U.S. buyout model across vastly different Asian ecosystems:
    * **China:** Initially a minority growth-oriented market.
    * **Japan:** Focused on control-oriented buyouts.
    * **India:** Partnered with businesses seeking global scale and operational best practices.
* **Fund Evolution:** Raised the first Asia fund at **$1 billion** in 2007, later expanding the platform into credit, special situations, and life sciences.

## The Kioxia (Toshiba Memory) Saga
* **Navigating High-Stakes Complexity:** Executed the defining deal of his career by beating out KKR, Western Digital, and Foxconn for Toshiba's crown-jewel memory business.
* **Key Hurdles Overcome:** Kept the Japanese government and Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI) comfortable, survived seven months of Chinese antitrust review during a U.S.–China trade war, and resolved ongoing litigation with joint-venture partner Western Digital by bringing in Apple, Dell, and Seagate as part of a consortium.
* **Financial Outcome:** Survived years of cyclical downturns where the business generated zero EBITDA before capitalizing on the AI and hyperscaler boom; Bain fully exited the deal this summer with over **$15 billion** in returns and **$45 billion** in net income/EBITDA generated.
* **Cultural Payoff:** Maintained trust with Japanese banks by prioritizing debt recovery on earlier, tougher deals, which unlocked flexible financing and support during Kioxia's darkest periods.

## Leadership at Bain Capital & Firm Governance
* **Taking the Helm:** Became the sole managing partner of Bain Capital in January, the first person to run the firm alone since Mitt Romney after spending 25 years working alongside the founding generation.
* **Private vs. Public Status:** Emphasized that Bain Capital will remain private to protect its unique partnership culture, maintain alignment with long-term principal investors, and avoid public market pressures for short-term fee revenue.
* **AI Integration:** Implementing an internal AI model trained on Bain's historical deal data to overcome investor blind spots, challenge pattern recognition biases, and improve valuation frameworks.
