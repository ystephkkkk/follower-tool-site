## US Chip Restrictions & The Mega Speed Investigation
*   **New US Export Controls:** Moderating the discussion, Associated Press Executive Editor Julie Pace noted the Trump administration's move earlier that day to restrict remote access to advanced US computing, chips, and servers, alongside new licensing requirements for chip sales in Singapore.
*   **Singapore’s Response:** Minister Josephine Teo stated Singapore is engaging affected businesses and US counterparts to clarify the rules and ensure "trusted access" to AI infrastructure for legitimate businesses.
    *   Teo emphasized Singapore's commitment to the rule of law, noting the country hosts roughly 6,000 US companies.
    *   Despite the new rules, Singapore's AI momentum remains strong, with OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind all announcing new activities there in the last couple of months.
*   **The Mega Speed Probe:** Teo detailed Singapore's independent investigation into Mega Speed, which began in February 2025.
    *   In June 2025, Singapore learned the US Bureau of Industry and Standards (BIS) was also interested in Mega Speed for post-shipment verification checks.
    *   Singapore requested US assistance regarding Mega Speed’s server movement declarations, specifically seeking cooperation from Super Micro, from whom they received "very little support and information."
    *   Teo met with US Deputy Secretary of State Kurt Campbell, and both agreed to keep lines of communication open as the investigation continues.

## Sierra’s Singapore Expansion & Enterprise Traction
*   **Why Singapore:** Sierra CEO Clay Bavor explained that his company chose Singapore for its Asia headquarters due to its business-friendly environment, investment incentives, ministry access, and major local enterprises—including DBS, Singapore Airlines, and Singtel (the latter two are Sierra customers).
    *   Sierra co-founder Bret Taylor was in Singapore at the time of the event launching the company's next phase.
*   **Enterprise Scale:** Bavor shared concrete metrics on Sierra's US footprint, noting the company serves 50% of the Fortune 50 and 40% of the Fortune 100.
    *   **Financial Services:** Sierra works with Rocket Mortgage to originate over $1 billion in new mortgages every month.
    *   **Healthcare:** Sierra partners with 4 out of the 5 largest US healthcare payers, using AI agents with "superhuman memory" to help patients navigate complex procedure eligibility.
*   **Agent Alignment:** Addressing Pace's question on whether AI agents serve the company or the consumer, Bavor argued the two are aligned: "You do right by customers, you do right by your business."
*   **Leadership & Nvidia:** When asked about his recent comments on singular leaders like Nvidia’s Jensen Huang and succession planning, Bavor deferred to Nvidia’s board of directors, calling it "above my paygrade."

## Data Center Pushback, Sustainability & Workforce Disruption
*   **A Destabilizing Force:** Responding to US mid-term election pushback against data centers and the pace of AI, Bavor acknowledged it is more than a communications issue. He noted that software engineers no longer write code—now called "hand coding"—a shift that seemed wild just 18 months ago.
*   **Regional Data Center Pushback:** Teo noted that objections to data centers are also occurring in neighboring Thailand and Malaysia, outlining Singapore’s three-part strategy:
    *   Recognize that unlimited data center growth is incompatible with climate commitments.
    *   Focus on greener data centers by using less energy or greener energy.
    *   Invest serious R&D funding into "resource-efficient AI" to optimize operations and how computing is performed.
*   **Workforce Strategy:** Teo advocated for looking at the workforce in totality—attracting new growth industries like Sierra while fostering a culture of "shared responsibility" among individuals, companies showing solidarity with employees, and government resource support.
