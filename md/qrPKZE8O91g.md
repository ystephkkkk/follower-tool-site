## TL;DR
Anthropic made history on the Nasdaq (ticker: ANTH) as the largest IPO of all time and the first pure-play Frontier AI lab to go public, raising $107.3 billion at $83 a share before opening at $111.50—a ~34% premium valuing the company at roughly $2 trillion. Investors and executives framed the listing not as a finish line, but as "day one" for deploying a massive $232 billion cash war chest into enterprise AI diffusion and next-generation compute.

## The Record-Breaking Debut & Market Backdrop
*   **Historic Scale & Pricing:** Host Ed Ludlow detailed that Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share, raising $107.3 billion (including a $26 billion private placement largely backed by hyperscalers and strategics). Lead underwriters Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley orchestrated the opening trade, which walked up from $102–$103 indications to open at $111.50 per share (~$2 trillion fully diluted valuation) before settling around $106–$108.
    *   The offering was roughly 7x oversubscribed, with about 13% allocated to retail investors (compared to ~25% for SpaceX).
*   **Nasdaq’s Banner Year:** Nasdaq President Nelson Griggs noted that Anthropic caps a historic five-month stretch featuring the three largest IPOs in history: SpaceX (June), SK Hynix, and Anthropic. 
    *   Nasdaq has captured 91% of 2026 operating company IPO proceeds to date and added over $4 trillion in market cap via transfers, including GM this week.
    *   Griggs noted that the average 2026 IPO is up ~16% versus the broader market's 15%, with value stocks outperforming growth stocks this year.
*   **Mixed Broader Markets:** Bloomberg’s Isabelle Lee reported volatile trading alongside the debut. The Nasdaq swung from +2.5% pre-market into negative territory before hovering up 0.1% to 0.7%, while the S&P 500 flirted with a six-day losing streak (its longest since April 2024).
    *   **Semiconductors:** The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.5%–2%. Nvidia gained 1.5% (snapping a five-day losing streak following strong Tuesday earnings), Broadcom rose 1.5%, and AMD fell 6% (after dropping as much as 11%) due to a short-seller report.
    *   Investors remain watchful of AI capex ROI and potential regulatory headwinds for data center buildouts following a "blue sweep" in the recent midterm elections.

## Anthropic’s Financial Engine & Compute Strategy
*   **Hyper-Growth Trajectory:** Anthropic CFO Krishna Rao highlighted that the company is not yet six years old (founded 2021) and earned its first dollar of revenue in Q1 2023, yet surpassed $81 billion in run-rate revenue last month.
    *   Altimeter Partner Pauline Yang noted that since the start of 2025, Anthropic's revenue is up 45x while compute costs are up only 10x. Compute fell from 10x revenue in 2023 to 55% of revenue in H1 2026, tracking toward a long-term steady-state target of 45%–50%.
*   **Fortress Balance Sheet:** Rao confirmed Anthropic entered the IPO with over $130 billion in cash and now holds roughly $232 billion in cash and short-term investments.
*   **Multi-Silicon & "Anthropic Direct":** To manage what Rao called the "cone of uncertainty" around future model capabilities and demand, Anthropic uses a diversified compute strategy across four different accelerator platforms. Beyond buying from cloud partners, its "Anthropic Direct" strategy involves leasing data centers and chips separately to maintain fungibility and resilience.

## Investor Perspectives: Growth, Diffusion & Enterprise Focus
*   **From Passing to Doubling Down:** Altimeter’s Pauline Yang revealed the firm passed on Anthropic’s early 2023 ($4 billion) and subsequent rounds due to negative gross margins (2023–2024) and questions over its commercialization DNA. Altimeter first invested 18 months ago in the $180 billion round, co-led its $65 billion raise in May, and secured a public-fund allocation in the IPO.
    *   Yang attributed recent revenue surges to specific model releases: Opus 4.5 (late 2025) turned "vibe coding into production code," and Opus 55 added $10 billion in ARR in October alone.
*   **"Bigger, Faster" vs. "Private for Longer":** Baillie Gifford Head of Private Companies Peter Singlehurst—whose firm first invested in August 2025 across Series F, G, and H—contrasted SpaceX (24 years to IPO) with Anthropic (6 years to IPO), noting Anthropic created two decades' worth of value in six months.
*   **The Enterprise & Diffusion Thesis:** Both investors emphasized that Anthropic’s focus on enterprise (coding, law, healthcare, biotech, robotics) offers far higher ROI and pricing power than consumer markets, where free models suffice.
    *   Singlehurst highlighted **diffusion** as a massive secondary growth driver alongside new model capabilities: Fortune 500 aggregate spend on Anthropic currently represents just 0.1% of their total spend, leaving vast room for expansion.

## Governance, Safety & The Public-Market Balancing Act
*   **Public Benefit Corp (PBC) Dynamics:** Both Rao and Singlehurst pushed back on the idea of an inherent tension between Anthropic’s AI safety mission and shareholder returns. Singlehurst argued that pursuing safe AI is "completely compatible with maximizing long-term shareholder value."
*   **Political & Commercial Conviction:** At the opening bell, CEO Dario Amodei stated Anthropic "will not bend to business or political pressure." Yang praised management's conviction—citing their willingness to hold their ground during earlier friction with the Department of War—and noted that all seven co-founders remain at the company with industry-leading retention.
*   **Expected Volatility:** Yang cautioned that Anthropic will likely be a volatile stock quarter-to-quarter because revenue spikes around major model releases while compute capacity comes online in non-linear chunks.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
