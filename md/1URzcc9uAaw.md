## TL;DR
Two years after launching ChatGPT, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman predicts that 10-year AI advancements will give every person more leverage than today's most impactful individuals, while acknowledging that the economic transition for knowledge workers will likely be painful.

## The State of AI & The "AGI" Milestone
- **Evolution and Timeline:** ChatGPT launched 2 years and a quarter ago, driving exponential capability gains.
- **Deep Research Launch:** Introduced roughly a week prior to the interview; Altman estimates this single feature handles about **5% of all tasks in the economy today** (e.g., summarizing research, baby crib shopping, consulting reports, complex financial analysis) by saving days or weeks of human work.
- **Defining AGI:** Altman tried to ban himself from using the term "AGI" due to poor definitions, but gave up because it is too sticky; internally, OpenAI uses **five levels of AI progress** rather than a single AGI threshold.
- **Democratizing Genius:** Envisions AGI as perfect collaboration providing as much genius-level intelligence as needed across every area.

## Infrastructure, Investments, and Competition
- **DeepSeek Moment:** Admits it was surprising that the capability dropped on *that specific day*, praising their nice chain-of-thought product work and massive free-tier availability, though noting it was not a massive research update for OpenAI.
- **Cost Scaling Law:** Observes that the price for a given level of intelligence falls by about **10x per year** (a 100x drop over the last few years).
- **Stargate Project:** A massive **$500 million project** to build a very large training and inference system (noting future clusters may reach the trillion-dollar scale).
- **Big Tech Spending:** The top seven tech companies are investing roughly **$560 billion** into new AI data centers and infrastructure this and last year.
- **Nvidia's Dominance:** Describes Nvidia as an incredible company and a "real powerhouse," noting it is easier for commentators to predict their downfall than for competitors to actually take their business.

## Societal Transition & Knowledge Work Impact
- **Knowledge Worker Disruption:** The transition will be messy and "pretty painful" for knowledge workers, who will feel the impact well before physical work (though robotics work is ramping up behind the scenes).
- **Work and Meaning:** Rejects the idea of a miserable UBI-driven existence of video games and drugs, arguing humans are hardwired to be useful and create; societal labor market transitions can be absorbed over generations, but the compression into a 5-to-10-year window creates friction.
- **Democratic vs. Authoritarian AI:** Cautions that the biggest unresolved fork in the road is heading toward authoritarian vs. democratic AI; democratic AI empowers individuals to an unprecedented degree but comes with societal growing pains.

## Geopolitics, Regulation, and Government Relations
- **Global Standards:** Suggests looking to the IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency) for nuclear material—despite its imperfections—as a model for an international regulatory body with global safety standards, rather than using a rigid "arms race" framework with China.
- **Europe and Regulation:** Notes that strict European regulations (e.g., a 3-to-5-month testing requirement for models) risk leaving European deployment perpetually out of date compared to 3-month improvement cycles.
- **US Political Climate:** Welcomes a vibe shift under President Trump, praising his focus on infrastructure, manufacturing, permitting, and energy that enables domestic mega-projects like Stargate, contrasting it with the Biden administration's friction on infrastructure.
- **Military Collaborations:** Explains that OpenAI is exploring work with the US military (such as a deal with Anduril) because it is critical that the US government understands AI and its impacts.
