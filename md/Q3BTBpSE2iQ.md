## TL;DR
Stephen Miran argues that current US inflation data is distorted by statistical measurement errors rather than true consumer price pressures, meaning the Federal Reserve has no economic justification to hike interest rates. Furthermore, he contends that the Fed should maintain a singular focus on monetary policy mandates rather than trying to police fiscal spending or congressional behavior through higher rates.

## Inflation Data and Measurement Errors
* **Core CPI Normalcy:** Core CPI is running at approximately **2.5%**, which is historically completely normal and sits about 40 basis points above PCE due to methodology differences.
* **PCE Distortion via Portfolio Management:** Core PCE is currently being elevated by about **45 basis points** year-over-year because portfolio management services mechanically track stock market gains rather than true consumer prices (accounting for roughly half of the month-on-month increase in core PCE).
* **Software Quality Conflation:** Software measurement errors are incorrectly counting product upgrades (such as LLMs added to Excel) as price inflation rather than quality improvements; combined, these two error categories account for nearly **70 basis points** of distortion.
* **Upcoming Data Revisions:** The Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) recognizes these measurement errors and will begin rolling out methodological revisions in slightly over a month, which will result in significant downward revisions to core PCE.

## Federal Reserve Policy and Mandates
* **Inconsistent Reaction Function:** Holding rates steady in June, July, and September while subsequently receiving better inflation data makes hiking rates now completely irrational under any sound reaction function.
* **Avoiding Statistical Hikes:** Hiking interest rates to compensate for statistical noise effectively asks Americans to lose their jobs over measurement errors.
* **Separation of Fiscal and Monetary Policy:** Central bank independence requires the Fed to "stay in its lane"—focusing strictly on its dual mandate of maximum employment and stable prices—rather than meddling in climate policy, racial politics, or attempting to discipline congressional fiscal profligacy via higher rates.
* **Forward-Looking Lags:** Monetary policy operates with a **12 to 18-month lag**, meaning rates must be set for where inflation is projected to be in the second half of **2027**, by which point transient shocks like the Iran energy spike and measurement errors will have dissipated.

## Treasury Market Liquidity and Federal Communication
* **Treasury Buyback Impact:** Treasury's program to increase buybacks at the long end of the yield curve (moving from **$2 billion to $4 billion**) targets the most illiquid part of the market, enhancing overall market liquidity.
* **Market Signals vs. Distortions:** Increased liquidity provides clearer market signals for central bank observation, representing a small flow within a **$30 trillion+** Treasury market rather than a destructive government monopoly on buying.
* **Shift Away from Forward Guidance:** Incoming Chairman Kevin Walsh is expected to continue his longstanding opposition to heavy forward guidance, resulting in higher short-term bond volatility in exchange for healthier, market-driven price discovery.
