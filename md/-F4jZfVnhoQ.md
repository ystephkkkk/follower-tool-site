## Meta's $98 Billion Equity Raise & Chip Partnerships
* **Oversubscribed Equity Offering:** Meta has boosted its planned equity offering to almost $98 billion, with the initial $25 billion stock sale tranche experiencing five times oversubscription.
* **Transaction Structure:** The raise includes $12.5 billion in preferred shares and a $60 billion at-the-market share sale program using Class C non-voting shares.
* **Shareholder Resistance:** A lawsuit has been filed against Meta and CEO Mark Zuckerberg, with critics labeling him a "boardroom bully" over the Class C stock mechanism, which critics argue provides Zuckerberg with liquidity to cover potential tax liabilities from the proposed California billionaire tax.
* **Semiconductor Integration:** Major chipmakers are participating in the equity raise—NVIDIA is investing $3 billion, while AMD and Broadcom are each contributing $1 billion. Additionally, Meta has installed AMD’s rack-scale AI systems and is testing them ahead of a broader rollout.

## Anthropic's IPO Filing & The Economics of AI Compute
* **Staggering S-1 Metrics:** Anthropic's S-1 filing reveals revenue growth exceeding 1,000% year-over-year in 2025, driven heavily by the adoption of its automated coding agents, alongside an $8.7 billion net loss on $16.3 billion in revenue for the first six months of 2026.
* **Balance Sheet and Cash Reserves:** Anthropic is sitting on $130 billion in cash and is expected to target a raise matching or exceeding SpaceX's valuation.
* **Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs):** Broadcom is arranging a $63 billion deal via a new SPV with committed financing from multiple firms to purchase chips and lease them back to Anthropic, acting as an alternative funding mechanism to traditional unsecured bonds and equity.
* **Compute Cost Pressures:** Compute costs account for 55% of Anthropic's revenue, and the company carries contractual commitments scaling from one year out to 20 years.

## Anduril's "Blue Chip" U.S. Manufacturing Initiative
* **Supply Chain Overhaul:** Anduril is partnering with Qualcomm to build "Blue Chip," a rugged computer and laptop line designed for the military, public sector, first responders, and critical infrastructure (such as medical facilities and power plants).
* **Domestic Sourcing Milestones:** The laptop will be designed and assembled in the U.S., with circuit board assembly at Anduril's Ohio factory and Qualcomm chips manufactured in Arizona; the supply chain is currently about 90% U.S. and allied, with a roadmap to reach 100%.
* **Software Integration:** Anduril maintains more software engineers than hardware engineers combined, utilizing its *Lattice* software platform to ensure compatibility with legacy government systems.
