## TL;DR
At the 2026 New York Times DealBook Summit, Anduril Industries founder Palmer Luckey argued that the U.S. must adopt a posture of unambiguous deterrence—from renaming the Pentagon to the Department of War to resuming nuclear effects testing—while warning that AI-driven cyberattacks are outpacing legacy defenses. Throughout his conversation with Andrew Ross Sorkin, Luckey maintained that tech executives should remain "dogs on a chain" rather than dictate foreign policy, even as he defends his own deep involvement in Pentagon strategy and his recent decision to repartner with Meta's Mark Zuckerberg on military tech.

## Cyber Warfare, AI Risks, and Civilian Preparedness
* **AI-Accelerated Cyberattacks:** Luckey noted that advanced AI tools are enabling rapid exploit creation, with organizations—particularly in North Korea—leveraging the mismatch between fast-moving attackers and outdated defensive systems for financial gain (citing a concurrent PlayStation outage).
* **Uncontrollable AI Models:** Addressing Pentagon scrutiny of Anthropic and OpenAI, Luckey stated empirically that AI models cannot be controlled at the use level, as "every major model has been jailbroken to some degree in a matter of months."
  * He emphasized that the real threat is "really bad, evil people with pretty good AI," rather than a sentient superintelligence, noting that AI currently lacks physical agency and data centers can still be shut down.
* **Autonomous Weapons vs. LLMs:** Luckey distinguished between general-purpose LLMs (which hallucinate) and specialized military AI that processes radar, thermal imagery, and electromagnetic emanations.
  * He argued that smart, autonomous weapons are far more reliable and cause fewer civilian casualties than older analog systems or "dumb weapons," comparing public skepticism to the "self-driving car problem."
* **Basic Preparedness:** Despite owning a decommissioned missile base 200 feet underground, Luckey dismissed it as an apocalypse shelter (noting a Home Depot Sawzall can cut the hinges off in 10 minutes). Instead, he advised every citizen to keep 3 days of water, 3 days of dry food, and to refill prescriptions 72 hours before running out.

## The "Department of War," Deterrence, and Geopolitics
* **Renaming the Pentagon:** Luckey took partial credit for pushing the Trump administration (including transition head Howard Lutnick) to rename the Department of Defense to the Department of War, citing five reasons:
  * **Deterrence & Clarity:** "If you want peace, prepare for war"—the name signals clear war-fighting intent rather than allowing endless, obfuscated spending on tangential issues like climate change.
  * **Morale & Talent Filtering:** The "War Department" won wars, whereas the "Department of Defense" lost them; the name also acts as "objector repellent" to filter out employees uncomfortable with the military's core mission.
* **China, Iran, and Stockpiles:** Responding to Sorkin's citation of former MI6 chief Richard Moore—who warned Xi Jinping might miscalculate on Taiwan after watching U.S. outcomes in Iran and Ukraine—Luckey noted that China's equipment failed in Venezuela and that Xi's recent purge of his top generals buys the U.S. time.
  * Luckey defended President Trump's claim that the U.S. can fight indefinitely despite stockpile concerns, comparing it to Nixon's "madman theory" as a necessary strategic deterrent.
* **Ukraine and European Readiness:** Luckey pushed back on claims that Trump is soft on Russia, arguing Trump was handed an impossible situation by European nations that ignored his first-term warnings about Russian oil—noting Germany has sent more money to Russia than Ukraine since the war began.

## Corporate Power vs. Government Responsibility
* **Keeping Tech Founders on a Leash:** Luckey asserted that foreign and military policy should be decided by the republic, not corporate executives, calling himself "a dog on a chain" who would go to prison if he unilaterally chose which countries to sell weapons to.
* **Defending Elon Musk on Starlink:** Addressing controversies over Elon Musk limiting Starlink speeds in Ukraine, Luckey offered a "steel man" defense: if Russia views Starlink as offensive military infrastructure, it could legally justify destroying the entire global constellation, which U.S. forces also rely on.
  * Luckey argued it is untenable for the U.S. government to outsource that risk to Musk when the Pentagon itself refuses to grant Ukraine access to its own military-grade **Star Shield** network out of fear Russia would destroy it.
* **Project Meridian & Conflict of Interest:** Sorkin read a critique from retired Air Force Master Sergeant Wes Bryant, who called Project Meridian (the Pentagon's 120-day study on warfare in 2050, co-led by Luckey and Elon Musk) indicative of "decay and oligarchy."
  * Luckey countered that over 20 people—including respected generals and admirals—sit on the MITRE-run panel, and argued the critique is partisan, noting Sorkin would never have leveled the same accusation at Eric Schmidt on the Biden-era Defense Innovation Board.

## Project Meridian, Nuclear Testing, and Future Tech
* **Unchampioned Ideas for 2050:** Following a Project Meridian meeting at Cheyenne Mountain, Luckey said the panel is focusing on concepts that lack existing Pentagon champions—such as **weather control** (e.g., altering ocean precipitation to clear views of targets)—rather than obvious trends like "killer robots."
* **Resuming Nuclear Testing:** Luckey advocated for restarting nuclear weapons testing, not just to maintain the psychological credibility of Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD), but to test broadband electromagnetic pulse (EMP) effects on modern electronics.
  * Current rad-hardening guidelines are based on decades-old simulations for transistors thousands of times larger than those in modern smartphones, leaving the U.S. blind to whether an adversary's space-based nuclear air burst could disable an entire region's autonomous weapons and critical infrastructure without killing anyone.
* **Transparency in Violence:** Luckey defended his support for televised public executions as a libertarian check on government power, arguing that medicalizing and hiding capital punishment trivializes it and removes public accountability—a principle he extended to real-time media coverage of war.

## Nvidia, Offshore Infrastructure, and Repartnering with Meta
* **Defending Jensen Huang:** Luckey explained his comment that "China wants Jensen out, so I want him in." While Luckey opposes exporting chips to China, he noted that 600,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs are currently sitting in a port because Beijing refuses import permits in order to force domestic silicon development.
  * Luckey argued that ousting Huang—which knocked $1 trillion off Nvidia's market cap—weakens America, as no interim CEO can replace Huang's personal relationships and founder-led execution.
* **Offshore Platforms:** Luckey clarified that he has been working for 10 years on floating offshore platforms for regulated or "NIMBY" industries—such as metal ore processing, oil refining, desalination, and potentially AI data centers—to bypass state and local pushback.
* **Facebook Firing & Repartnering with Zuckerberg:** Luckey recalled being fired from Facebook three years after selling Oculus for $2 billion in 2014—despite "greatly exceeded expectations" reviews—after donating $9,000 to a pro-Trump group in 2016.
  * While he still resents the lieutenants he made rich who "stabbed [him] in the back," he exonerated Mark Zuckerberg, noting Zuckerberg was simply acting on signals from deputies. Consequently, Luckey repartnered with Meta when Anduril took over the Army's **IVAS** (Infantry Vision Augmentation System) program, choosing to put soldier safety above personal grievances.

## Predictions & Forward Calls

I think people are going to want to control their own data. I am actually somewhat skeptical of the mass scale data center as the way AI proliferates." — *Palmer Luckey*
