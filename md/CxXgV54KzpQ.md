Google Chief Scientist Jeff Dean and YC’s Diana Hu discuss AI progress, hardware breakthroughs, inference scaling, and why small teams can win by focusing on context engineering and niche problem domains.

## Hardware, Energy, and the "Inference Moment"
- **The 2001 Search Analogy:** In 2001, Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat realized Google's search index fit in RAM and shipped a RAM-based search version in days. The 2026 equivalent is high-performance, low-energy **inference hardware** that drastically cuts latency (e.g., 50x better).
- **The TPU Origin Story (2013 Napkin Math):** 
  - Realizing that 3 minutes of daily voice recognition per Google user would double the server fleet, Dean and Sanjay built the TPU—specialized for low-precision dense linear algebra.
  - The resulting chip was **30 to 80 times more energy-efficient** and **20 to 30 times lower latency** than contemporary CPUs and GPUs.
- **The Data Movement Energy Gap:** Moving data from accelerator memory (HBM) to the processor costs **1,000x more energy** than performing the actual computation. This hardware reality dictates why batching is necessary.

## Context Engineering & Long-Running Agents
- **Context vs. Training Data:** The model is only one piece of an overall system. Context engineering (retrieval, tool use, memory) provides clear information directly to the model, unlike trillions of training tokens stirred into a parameter soup.
- **Long-Running Agents:** Agents can now run for days or weeks on complex tasks (e.g., rewriting software in a new programming language for better safety or performance).
- **Overcoming Agent Rail Failures:** 
  - Multi-agent systems use inference-time compute to search through multiple potential solutions and evaluate the most promising paths.
  - Providing explicit guidelines, skills, and performance hints (such as Dean and Sanjay's published 30-page "Performance Hints" document) helps keep models on successful execution paths.

## Startup Opportunities & Where 2-3 People Can Win
- **Targeting 0% Success Rates:** Founders should look for problems where current general models succeed 0% to 1% of the time, rather than 20% (which indicates general capabilities are rapidly encroaching).
- **Privileged Data & Niche Models:** 
  - Build around personal/private data that general models cannot access.
  - Build specialized niche models (similar to AlphaFold for protein folding) for domains like material science or chip design.
- **The Power of Good Taste:** As AI automates code writing, the scarce, high-value skill is having great taste and wisdom regarding *what* problem to work on.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
