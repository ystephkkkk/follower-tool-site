**TL;DR:** Neuralink clinical trial participant Billy, who lives with tetraplegia, demonstrated the ability to play *Grand Theft Auto VI* entirely telepathically—controlling 2 joysticks and 12 buttons without a physical controller—while also using a robotic arm to hold his microphone during an internal livestream.

## Telepathic Gameplay & Control Mechanics
* **Full Controller Emulation:** Billy operated *GTA VI* using 2 joysticks and 12 buttons simultaneously via his brain implant, completing missions involving boating, stealth, combat, flying a sea plane, and driving.
  * **12-Button Layout:** Included 4 face buttons on the right (X, Square, Triangle, Circle), 4 D-pad buttons on the left, both triggers, and both bumpers.
  * **10 Fingers + Foot Mapping:** Beyond his 10 fingers, Billy mapped 2 additional buttons to his right foot—pushing down for one action and pulling/lifting up for another—to help manage complex controls like flying.
* **Specific Action Mappings:**
  * **Face & Shoulder Buttons:** Triangle is mapped to his pinky, Square to his right ring finger, and L1 (weapon wheel) to his left middle finger.
  * **Camera & Movement:** Both joysticks on his left hand control camera panning and forward movement, while the right joystick is mapped to his whole hand (imagined like sliding a mouse left or right).
  * **Flight Controls:** Pulling the nose of the sea plane up requires imagining sliding his right hand forward (like gripping and pushing a mouse) combined with rolling his wrist.
* **Aiming & Learning Curve:** Aiming is aided by in-game aim assist and is not overly difficult, though Billy noted the controls are brand new to him and require practice to build muscle memory.

## Mental Mapping & Signal Nuance
* **Keyboard & Piano Analogy:** Billy described the mental sensation of pressing buttons as imagining typing on a keyboard or playing piano keys—imagining the physical press even though nothing moves.
* **Varying Signal Strength:**
  * **Dominant & Strongest Inputs:** His right thumb requires barely a flex to register, and his index, middle, and ring fingers were the first three clicks he mastered.
  * **Weaker Inputs:** Because his right hand is dominant, his left hand is "a little sticky" and weaker, requiring him to imagine pushing down harder on the fingertip to register a click.

## Hardware & Assistive Tech Integration
* **Robotic Arm Control:** During the livestream, Billy used a robotic arm to hold his microphone, noting he can control it "very, very well."
* **Pre-Implant Baseline:** Prior to receiving the Neuralink chip, Billy relied on a mouth stick to peck at an iPad ("like a bird"), which caused jaw soreness and severely limited the games he could play.
