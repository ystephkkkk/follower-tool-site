## TL;DR
Kansas City Fed President Jeffrey Schmid discusses the 49th Jackson Hole symposium, focusing on financial innovation in payments, demographic labor shifts, and navigating above-target inflation under new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. Schmid expresses optimism about the economy's durability while warning that the Fed must continue addressing inflation currently running in the mid-3s.

## Jackson Hole Symposium & The Future of Payments
* **Focus on Payments Innovation:** The Kansas City Fed economic symposium centers on financial innovation in payments, moving toward "atomic settlement" where money transfers and reconciliations occur instantaneously.
* **Operational Goals:** The Federal Reserve ultimately aims to make payments boring while moving $5 trillion to $10 trillion daily through multiple payment pipes.
* **New Systemic Demands:** Instantaneous payments require proven liquidity and a focus on the duration of assets to back immediate settlements securely.

## Labor Force Dynamics & Demographic Shifts
* **Baby Boomer Retirements:** The labor force is undergoing a major structural change with approximately 4 million baby boomers retiring per year.
* **Intellectual Capital Risk:** While retirements create new opportunities, Schmid notes the primary risk is losing institutional knowledge and intellectual muscle.
* **AI Knowledge Transfer:** Banks and Fed economists are actively using artificial intelligence to transfer institutional knowledge from 65-year-old retirees to younger 25-, 35-, and 45-year-old analysts.

## Economic Growth & AI-Driven Commodity Demand
* **Trend State Growth:** Schmid views the economy as growing nicely between 2% and 3.5%, expressing optimism about the durability of the current economic expansion.
* **Data Center and AI Boom:** The build-out of data centers and artificial intelligence is creating heavy commercial credit demand and directly competing with other industries for resources.
* **Commodity and Supply Pressures:** Surging demand is impacting machinery, steel, copper, and even grain futures markets, which complicates the Fed's path back to its 2% inflation target.

## Monetary Policy, Yields, and the Mandate
* **Inflation Concerns:** Schmid emphasizes that the Fed has not finished its job on inflation, noting a recent surge into the mid-3s that must be addressed to fulfill the dual mandate.
* **Price of Money and Yields:** The 10-year Treasury yield and other long-term rates reflect supply and demand dynamics, credit competition between the commercial and public sectors, and a fairly normalized yield curve.
* **Accommodative Financial Conditions:** Schmid assesses current policy rates as relatively accommodative, noting that short-term rates primarily influence immediate decisions like inventory pre-buying, while long-term yields impact decade-long capital projects.

## FOMC Governance & The Warsh Era
* **Open Debate Culture:** Praising both former Chair J. Powell and current Chair Kevin Warsh, Schmid welcomes Warsh's emphasis on transparency and a willingness to accept robust debate rather than top-down mandates.
* **Role of Dissents:** Dissents serve as a mechanism to signal that an FOMC member weighs the risks between full employment and price stability differently than the majority.
* **Task Forces and Communication:** Newly formed task forces are evaluating economic data sets and communication policies, though regional Fed presidents have not yet engaged in direct dialogue with them pending the release of their formal research.
