**TL;DR**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent presented a $11.5 billion FY2027 budget request, representing a 12% reduction aimed at streamlining the IRS through technology and implementing the "Working Families Tax Cuts Act," while facing scrutiny from Democrats over reduced tax enforcement and sanctions efficacy.

## FY2027 Budget Strategy and IRS Modernization
* **Budget Reduction:** Secretary Bessent proposed an $11.5 billion budget, a $1.5 billion (12%) decrease from the previous year. He characterized this as a shift toward efficiency and "outcomes over outlays."
* **IRS Headcount and Efficiency:** 
    * The IRS has reduced its workforce by approximately 28,000 employees since the start of the administration, with a further 4,000-person reduction planned. 
    * Bessent argued that technology—including AI and digital-first filing—has allowed for a 12% increase in enforcement recoveries ($41 billion total) despite lower staffing levels.
* **Service Metrics:** Treasury is introducing new performance metrics, such as "Assistor Service Rate" and "Enterprise Service Completion Rate," to better track how digital and automated systems serve taxpayers compared to traditional phone support.
* **Closing Assistance Centers:** Senator Susan Collins (R-ME) expressed concern over the closure of Taxpayer Assistance Centers in rural Maine, noting that some residents now face a 10-hour round trip for in-person help. Bessent committed to reviewing the "10-hour problem."

## Tax Policy and the Working Families Tax Cuts Act
* **Implementation Progress:** The Treasury is currently implementing guidance for the "Working Families Tax Cuts Act," which includes signature policies such as "No Tax on Tips" and "No Tax on Overtime."
* **Refund Data:** Bessent reported that average tax refunds for the current filing season are up 11–13% over the prior year.
    * **No Tax on Tips:** Over 7 million filers claimed an average deduction of $7,000.
    * **No Tax on Overtime:** 28 million filers claimed an average deduction of over $3,100.
    * **Seniors:** 34 million seniors claimed an enhanced deduction averaging $7,500.
* **Trump Accounts:** Nearly 5.5 million "Trump Accounts" have been opened to foster long-term financial security, with 1.3 million children eligible for a $1,000 pilot program contribution.
* **Direct File Shutdown:** Bessent defended the decision to terminate the "Direct File" program, arguing it cost $138 per person to administer ($72 million total for 300,000 users) and that the public-private "Free File" partnership is more cost-effective.

## Digital Assets and the "Genius Act"
* **Regulatory Framework:** Treasury is prioritizing the implementation of the "Genius Act," which establishes the first U.S. regulatory framework for stablecoins.
* **Strategic Funding:** The budget requests $1.8 million for departmental offices and $3 million for FinCEN to drive innovation and expertise in digital assets.
* **Global Leadership:** Bessent emphasized that providing regulatory clarity for digital assets is essential to maintaining the U.S. dollar’s status as the world’s reserve currency and ensuring the U.S. leads in payment technology.

## National Security and Geopolitical Finance
* **Sanctions Discrepancy:** Senator Chris Coons (D-DE) challenged the Secretary on reports that Iran and Russia have gained significant revenue via sanctions relief. Bessent disputed the $14 billion figure cited by Coons as a "myth," arguing that limited relief was necessary to prevent global oil prices from spiking to $150/barrel.
* **UAE Swap Line:** Bessent confirmed that the UAE and other Gulf allies have requested "swap lines" through the Exchange Stabilization Fund to maintain order in dollar funding markets, though he denied any linkage to the President's private business ventures.
* **Illicit Activity in Maine:** Senator Collins highlighted over 200 illegal marijuana grow houses in Maine linked to Chinese transnational criminal organizations. Bessent pledged that IRS Criminal Investigations and FinCEN would prioritize dismantling the associated money laundering operations.

## Contractor Accountability
* **Booz Allen Hamilton:** Chairman Hagerty and Secretary Bessent discussed the cancellation of contracts with Booz Allen Hamilton following a massive data breach of taxpayer information.
* **Vetting Failure:** Bessent described the breach as "premeditated" and "egregious," stating the firm failed to properly screen employees who weaponized sensitive data. He signaled a broader Treasury-wide loss of confidence in the contractor’s ability to safeguard information.
