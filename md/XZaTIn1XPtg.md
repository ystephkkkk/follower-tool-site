**TL;DR**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent presented a $11.5 billion FY 2027 budget request featuring a 12% ($1.5 billion) spending reduction, aiming to pivot the Treasury toward a "digital-first" agency while defending significant cuts to IRS enforcement and the termination of the Direct File program.

## FY 2027 Budget Priorities and IRS Modernization
* **Fiscal Reduction:** The budget proposes an 11.7% decrease in funding compared to the previous year, which Secretary Bessent characterized as a "thoughtful effort to balance responsible stewardship" with government efficiency.
* **IRS Workforce and Funding:** The request slashes IRS funding by $1.4 billion and anticipates a reduction of 4,000 employees. 
    * **Enforcement Debate:** Ranking Member Jack Reed criticized these cuts, noting that every $1 spent on enforcement yields $11 in revenue. 
    * **Outcome-Based Defense:** Bessent argued that despite previous budget cuts, enforcement recoveries rose 12% last year to $41 billion, claiming technology and better targeting are more effective than high-headcount "bloat."
* **Technology Transition:** The Treasury plans to exhaust one-time Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) funds by FY 2028, moving back to regular appropriations.
    * **Digital-First Strategy:** 98% of returns are now filed electronically. The IRS is utilizing AI to alert taxpayers of likely audit risks before they file, allowing for pre-emptive corrections.
    * **Customer Service:** Bessent reported improved "assistor service rates" and claimed that while call wait times changed, taxpayers are now serviced immediately upon connection.

## Implementation of the Working Families Tax Cuts Act
* **Taxpayer Impact:** Bessent reported that 60 million filers claimed at least one signature tax cut this season.
    * **Average Refunds:** Total refunds are up an estimated 11-13% over the prior year.
    * **Key Provisions:** Benefits included exemptions for tips (averaging $7,000 deduction), overtime ($3,100), and enhanced deductions for seniors ($7,500).
* **Trump Accounts:** The administration reported that 5.5 million "Trump Accounts" have been opened, with 1.3 million children eligible for a $1,000 pilot program contribution to foster long-term financial security.
* **Direct File Termination:** Secretary Bessent defended the decision to shut down the IRS "Direct File" program, citing a high cost to the government ($138 per person) and a 60% incompletion rate among users.

## Digital Assets and the GENIUS Act
* **Regulatory Framework:** The Treasury is prioritizing the implementation of the GENIUS Act, which provides the first regulatory framework for stablecoins.
* **Resource Allocation:** 
    * **Departmental Offices:** Requesting $1.8 million and six full-time employees for rulemaking and governance.
    * **FinCEN:** Seeking $3 million and 10 full-time employees to establish national leadership in digital asset innovation and anti-money laundering.
* **Global Leadership:** Secretary Bessent emphasized that providing regulatory certainty is essential for the U.S. to maintain its position as the world's reserve currency provider.

## International Trade, Sanctions, and National Security
* **Sanctions Policy:** Senator Coons challenged the Treasury on sanctions relief for Russia and Iran. 
    * **Bessent's Rebuttal:** The Secretary argued that "sanctions relief" was a strategic move to prevent global oil prices from spiking to $150/barrel, which benefited American consumers and prevented Russia from earning higher revenues on lower volumes.
* **Contractor Accountability:** Bessent confirmed the cancellation of contracts with Booz Allen Hamilton following a massive data breach of taxpayer information. He stated the firm failed to properly vet an employee who "premeditated" the leak.
* **Global Trade:** The administration continues to push "America-first" trade policies to address persistent trade deficits and dependence on competitors like China for critical goods.
