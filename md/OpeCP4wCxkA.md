## The Modern AI Chip Landscape & ASIC Ecosystem
* **The "Zoo" of Options:** The current chip market features a diverse spectrum of options, though many AI ASICs remain relatively identical in underlying architecture.
* **Hyperscaler Efforts:** Major players rely on proprietary internal chips developed alongside back-end delivery houses like Broadcom (a $2 trillion company):
    * **Google:** TPU (pioneered over 10 years ago)
    * **Meta:** MTIA
    * **Microsoft:** Maya
    * **OpenAI:** Jalapeno
* **Common Architectural Threads:** Most existing GPUs (NVIDIA, AMD) and ASICs utilize HBM (high-bandwidth DRAM memory), tensor cores for matrix multiplications, and TSMC advanced packaging.
* **The Abstraction/Handoff Model:** Traditional chip design involves architects conceiving circuits (like tensor cores for matrix math), front-end designers writing RTL code, and Broadcom/foundries performing synthesis and physical layout (GDS2 file formatting, metal layers, and transistor placement).

## Fractile's Technical Bets & Full-Stack Agility
* **Full-Stack Integration:** Unlike outsourced models, Fractile maintains an in-house team spanning workload understanding, front-end design, physical design, backend implementation, and advanced packaging.
* **The SRAM-to-DRAM Evolution:** 
    * **Initial Approach:** Built SRAM-based chips (similar to Groq or Cerebras) leveraging on-chip high-bandwidth memory for thousands of tokens per second.
    * **Scaling Concerns:** Faced scalability limits regarding growing context lengths in late 2023 and 2024.
    * **DRAM "Skunk Works":** Moved toward working with memory vendors to achieve extremely high bandwidth access to higher capacity, lower-cost DRAM memories. A platform uniting DRAM scalability with Groq/Cerebras-style speed is set to ramp in the second half of next year.
* **Workload-Driven Design:** Fractile collaborates with frontier players to anticipate scaling vector trends and align chip development with rapidly changing model architectures.

## Compressing Design Cycles & Semiconductor Realities
* **AI-Forward Chip Design:** Using AI internally shortens front-end design workflows, but fundamental physical and financial constraints remain.
* **Industry Bottlenecks:** 
    * **Fab Cycle Times:** 3 to 5 months to get chips back from foundry partners (TSMC/Samsung) even in super-hot lot scenarios.
    * **Payoff Periods:** Chips require a 3 to 5-year amortization window to remain financially viable, limiting the feasibility of shipping fundamentally new silicon every few weeks.
* **Strategic Value:** The primary competitive advantage is compressing the latency between observing a workload shift and realizing that bet in volume, aiming for a 3 to 6-month competitive window over rivals.
