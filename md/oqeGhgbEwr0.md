**TL;DR**
Fed Chair Jerome Powell reaffirmed the FOMC’s commitment to a 2% inflation target despite new pressures from tariffs and energy shocks, while emphasizing that the U.S. financial system is significantly more resilient than it was prior to the 2008 crisis.

## FOMC Governance and Policy Dissent
* **Value of Disagreement:** Powell argues that internal dissent is a strength, not a weakness, for the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC).
    * **Stress-Testing Ideas:** He compares the process to private equity, noting that he wants "the smartest people" to try to take a deal apart before committing to it.
    * **The Current Tension:** He acknowledges the difficulty of the current environment, where downside risks to the labor market suggest lower rates while upside risks to inflation suggest the opposite.
* **Institutional Independence:** Powell emphasizes that the Fed must "stick to its knitting" and avoid mission creep into political or social areas not assigned by Congress.

## Inflation Outlook and Economic Shocks
* **Target Commitment:** The FOMC remains strictly committed to returning inflation to its 2% target on a sustained basis.
* **The "Soft Landing" of 2024:** Powell notes that despite widespread recession predictions, the economy grew at 2.5% in 2024 with full employment and inflation nearing target levels.
* **New Price Pressures:**
    * **Tariff Inflation:** Powell estimates that recent tariffs are currently adding between 0.5 and 1.0 percentage points to inflation, though he views this as a one-time price increase.
    * **Energy Shocks:** Events in the Middle East are impacting gas prices; however, the Fed typically "looks through" supply shocks unless they begin to shift long-term inflation expectations.
* **Policy Stance:** Powell believes current policy is "in a good place" to wait and see how these external shocks evolve.

## Financial Stability and the Balance Sheet
* **The $6 Trillion Balance Sheet:** While the balance sheet has decreased, it remains over $6 trillion. Powell defends the efficacy of Quantitative Easing (QE) for restoring market function during the 2008 and 2020 crises.
* **Resilience Over Prevention:** Powell describes the Fed's role as "levee building" rather than "hurricane prevention."
    * **Capital Requirements:** Higher capital and liquidity requirements post-Dodd-Frank have "hardened" the system against the types of credit losses seen in 2008.
    * **Monitoring Risks:** The Fed is closely watching private credit, commercial real estate, and potential cyber-attacks, though Powell currently sees no "broader systemic event" in private credit.
* **National Debt:** Powell warned that the U.S. federal debt is on an "unsustainable" path, growing faster than the economy, though he noted that fiscal policy is not the Fed's mandate.

## AI and the Future of the Labor Market
* **Technology and Productivity:** Powell is optimistic about the medium-to-long-term impact of AI on the U.S. economy.
    * **Complementarity vs. Substitution:** While AI may automate certain middle-management and back-office roles, Powell believes it will ultimately make workers more productive and raise living standards.
    * **Economic Dynamism:** He points out that U.S. productivity has grown at twice the speed of Europe since WWII, driven by a flexible economy that constantly reinvents itself.
* **Advice for New Entrants:** Despite low job creation in the current cycle, Powell advises students to master new technologies, as "the marginal benefit of a new employee" who can use AI effectively remains high.
