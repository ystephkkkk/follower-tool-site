## TL;DR
Krishna Guha and Steve Eisman break down the rise of the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, attributing it to a combination of AI debt issuance crowding out Treasuries, war-driven oil prices, a massive 6% GDP deficit, and Kevin Warsh’s removal of Fed forward guidance guardrails.

## Kevin Warsh's Shift in Fed Policy
* **New Leadership Approach:** 
  * **Supply-Side Ideology:** Krishna Guha notes that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh views himself as a supply-side change agent, moving away from the 20-year consensus of demand-side New Keynesian economics championed by his predecessors (Ben Bernanke, Janet Yellen, and Jay Powell).
  * **Anti-QE Stance:** Warsh believes quantitative easing (QE) was massively overdone and bloated the central bank balance sheet, arguing that QE should be restricted strictly to market seizure emergencies like the Global Financial Crisis or the pandemic.
* **Balance Sheet Reduction Plans:** 
  * **Current Scale:** The Fed's balance sheet sits between **$6.5 trillion and $7 trillion**, down from a peak of **$9 trillion**.
  * **Tactical Caution:** Warsh has established an external expert task force on the balance sheet, but Guha emphasizes he must proceed carefully to avoid triggering a spike in long-term interest rates by dumping bonds prematurely.

## The Perfect Storm Driving the 10-Year Yield to 5%+
* **Competing Borrowers & AI Boom:** 
  * **Hyper-Issuance:** A surge of debt issuance by AI hyperscalers is competing directly with U.S. Treasuries.
  * **Structural Shift:** Bond investors are pricing in a multi-year AI investment boom that has transitioned the global economy from a world of excess savings to one of high investment demand chasing a limited pool of capital, alongside a federal deficit running at **6% of GDP** (within a **$40 trillion** market).
* **Energy Shocks:** 
  * **Refined Products:** While Brent and WTI crude fluctuate, refined products like diesel and jet fuel have surged drastically—with diesel prices essentially doubling in a short window—creating a lasting energy cost impulse.
* **Removal of Guardrails:** 
  * **Death of Forward Guidance:** Warsh has largely abandoned broad strategic communication and the Fed's "reaction function playbook," returning the central bank toward early-Greenspan opacity. Guha argues this removal of market guardrails has left bond investors to navigate uncertainty alone, causing yields to spike.

## Scott Bessent's Bond Buyback Program
* **Operation Twist Execution:** 
  * **Scale Disconnect:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent initiated a buyback program initially set at **$4 billion per operation**, later scaled to **$6 billion**, aimed at retiring long-term high-yield bonds while financing them by issuing short-dated Treasury bills.
  * **Butter Knife to a Gunfight:** Given the **$40 trillion** Treasury market size, Guha characterizes the initiative as bringing a butter knife to a gunfight, noting that Bessent ultimately backed off after biting off more than he could chew.
* **Credibility & Regulatory Shifts:** 
  * **Loss of Market Confidence:** Intervening in the debt market made the U.S. government look like a struggling emerging market, damaging credibility.
  * **Regulatory Reforms:** The administration and the Fed are exploring reforms to Liquidity Coverage Ratios (LCR) and banking regulations to encourage major banks to hold fewer Fed reserves and more Treasury debt.

## Economic Strains & The Rubicon for Rates
* **Two-Speed Economy:** 
  * **AI Resilience:** AI investment is largely interest-rate insensitive because projected returns (e.g., **500% to 10,000%**) dwarf 6% to 9% borrowing costs. High-end consumer wealth effects driven by AI stock gains are currently propping up consumer spending.
  * **Traditional Strain:** Ground zero for rate pain is the housing market, where 3D-impact mortgage rates sit around **7%**, while mom-and-pop "old economy" businesses face severe crowding out.
* **The Stock Market Threshold:** Guha warns that while a **5-handle** on the 10-year yield strains non-AI sectors, the ultimate breaking point to watch is whether the stock market can withstand current yields, as a sell-off would trigger a devastating negative wealth effect for consumers.
