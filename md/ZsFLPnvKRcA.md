## TL;DR
Global economic growth is proving resilient at around 3%, supported by positive AI demand and emerging market strength, but faces persistent risks from sticky inflation, rising debt-servicing costs, and AI-driven financial stability concerns.

## Global Economic Outlook & Shocks
- **Growth Resiliency:** The global economy is hovering around 3% GDP growth, a massive achievement despite ongoing shocks, though projections will be revised to reflect high ongoing risks.
- **Opposing Forces:** Two main drivers are pushing in opposite directions:
  - A negative energy supply shock holding the economy back.
  - A positive AI investment demand shock lifting the economy up.
- **Emerging Market Strength:** Emerging markets and developing economies have built strong institutional buffers and monetary policy frameworks that match or exceed advanced economies.

## Inflation, Debt, and Fiscal Pressures
- **Persistent Inflation:** Inflation is stable but not resolving quickly, requiring central banks (including in Europe and the US) to maintain or increase tightening.
- **Climbing Debt-Servicing Costs:** Higher interest rates have increased debt-servicing burdens, making fiscal consolidation essential.
- **US Fiscal Deficits:** US debt levels have climbed to over $40 trillion, and while relying on growth to reduce debt is a strategy, the IMF advocates for gradually bringing deficits down.
- **Low-Income Country Debt:** The IMF is engaging in debt restructuring discussions—notably using a new playbook for Senegal—and utilizing the Global Sovereign Debt Roundtable to coordinate traditional, new, and private creditors.
- **Argentina's Transition:** Argentina has successfully moved from deficit to surplus and brought inflation down; the focus must now shift to translating macroeconomic stability into growth and employment.

## AI Risks and Energy Security
- **Financial Stability Front-Loading:** High expectations for AI create risks of front-loaded financial instability, circular financing, and leverage vulnerabilities if expectations are disappointed.
- **Global Value Chain Exposure:** US-centric AI concentration impacts integrated value chains globally, with visible stock market vulnerabilities in Korea and critical production in the Netherlands, Malaysia, Thailand, and Singapore.
- **Surging Energy Consumption:** AI is already consuming 3% of energy in Europe, and consumption is projected to triple by the end of the decade, making energy security critical.
- **Energy Shock Mitigations:** The 2025 energy shock was sustained through rapid responses:
  - Nigeria and India expanded refinery capacity quickly.
  - The US and Norway increased production.
  - Saudi Arabia and the UAE provided alternative routes around the Strait of Hormuz.
  - European renewables and reduced energy intensity helped absorb the shock.
