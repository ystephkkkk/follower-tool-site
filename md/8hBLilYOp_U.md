Despite recent geopolitical volatility, market leadership has rapidly reverted to AI, semiconductors, and power-related industrials, suggesting a potential 1999-style "melt-up" rather than a fundamental regime change.

## Market Sentiment and Price Action
*   **Headlines vs. Price Action:** Much like the spring of the previous year, the market recently experienced a brief "swoon" (down 7-9%) only to recover rapidly. Leadership has returned to the same themes: AI, tech, power, and banks.

## The Semiconductor Takeover vs. Software’s Decline
*   **Semiconductor Concentration:** Semiconductors now represent **17% of the S&P 500**, up from just 2% a decade ago. 
    *   **Industry Evolution:** The group is becoming as dominant as Energy was in the 2000s or Tech in 1999. Micron is now larger than Johnson & Johnson; Nvidia and Broadcom are major index drivers.
*   **Software Neglect:** Software’s weight in the index has dropped from the teens to single digits (approx. 7-8%).
    *   **Indiscriminate Selling:** Software stocks are being sold regardless of performance. For example, ServiceNow reported strong 22% growth but saw its stock drop 14% on the news.
    *   **Generational Oversold Conditions:** Software is deeply "oversold," but Chris Verrone notes that a bottom likely won’t form until semiconductors finally crack.

## Financials and the Credit Landscape
*   **Bank Resilience:** Traditional banks remain strong. Citigroup is currently viewed as a "canary in the coal mine"; its continued strength suggests the market is not yet facing a systemic credit event.
*   **Private Equity (PE) Risks:** Unlike banks, PE firms and alternative asset managers are under pressure due to their heavy exposure to the struggling software sector and private credit.
    *   **Apollo Exception:** Apollo has acted better than peers largely due to its lower exposure to software lending.
*   **Credit Conditions:** While credit spreads have widened slightly since January, they have not "blown out." The lack of deterioration in consumer or commercial credit data suggests a major financial crisis is not imminent.

## Industrials, Power, and Energy
*   **The "Power" Trade:** Industrials associated with the electrical grid and AI infrastructure are thriving.
    *   **GE Vernova (GEV):** This spin-off is now larger than GE Aerospace, highlighting the massive demand for gas turbines (a market controlled by only three global players: GEV, Mitsubishi, and Siemens).
    *   **Other Leaders:** Eaton, Vertiv, and Quanta Services continue to show strength.
*   **Transportation as a Recession Gauge:** Transports (truckers and rails) are reaching all-time highs. This outperformance makes it difficult to maintain a "recession" base case, as transports usually decline ahead of economic downturns.
*   **Energy Outlook:** Energy remains in a long-term uptrend that began in late 2023. Recent corrections are viewed as opportunities to increase exposure rather than exit positions.
