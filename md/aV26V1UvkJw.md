### TL;DR
Within the next few years, OpenAI and Anthropic are on track to control most of the world's usable AI compute and effective labor through massive revenue-per-megawatt generation and compute outbidding, raising profound questions about economic centralization, interest rates, and national debt crises.

## Lab Economics & The Shift from Inference to Training
* **Gross Margin Evolution:** Frontier AI labs have transitioned from venture-funded losses and negative gross margins on models like GPT-4 (run on Nvidia Hopper GPUs) to high profitability. 
* **Revenue per Megawatt:** While baseline compute costs approximately $10M to $15M per megawatt, labs like Anthropic now generate up to $50M in revenue per megawatt (with projections pointing toward $100M+).
* **The Internal Allocation Tradeoff:** Because internal R&D (such as recursive self-improvement and automated research) yields higher long-term returns than external inference sales, labs increasingly allocate marginal compute away from revenue-generating external tokens and back into training fleets.

## Compute Centralization & Supply Dynamics
* **Accelerating Centralization:** At the start of the year, OpenAI operated ~2 gigawatts and Anthropic under 2 gigawatts. By year-end, both exceeded 5 gigawatts, capturing roughly 30% of global incremental compute. 
* **The 2028 Horizon:** By the end of 2028, leading labs are projected to command roughly 100 gigawatts combined (reaching ~50 gigawatts per lab), effectively consuming 70% to 80% of the world's incremental compute.
* **New Infrastructure Entrants:** Cloud hardware supply chains are expanding, with new major players like SpaceX building massive compute clusters and leasing them to the highest bidders (OpenAI and Anthropic) at steep markups (e.g., $25M to $50M+ per megawatt).

## Macroeconomic Fallout & Potential Sovereign Debt Crisis
* **CapEx Projections:** Total global AI capital expenditures are projected to exceed $2 trillion annually by 2028, with cumulative CapEx reaching $11 trillion between 2024 and 2029 ($6 trillion funded by cash flow and $5 trillion by debt).
* **Interest Rate Pressures:** Hyperscaler and supply-chain debt issuance will crowd out traditional lending, driving up interest rates across the broader economy.
* **Global Sovereign Defaults:** While the US economy can theoretically tax domestic data center infrastructure to service rising interest payments, developing nations with high debt loads and weak tax bases (such as Pakistan and Nigeria) face severe bankruptcy risks akin to a second Volcker shock.
* **Equity Market Bifurcation:** Higher discount rates will crush valuations for non-AI equities (such as stable, long-term cash-flow stocks like utilities, consumer goods, and financials), forcing a massive capital reallocation toward AI-driven enterprises.

## Geopolitics & Compute Inequality
* **China's Domestic Lag:** China currently accounts for sub-10% of global incremental AI data center compute due to US export controls, relying heavily on smuggled chips and limited domestic production (from SMIC and CXMT).
* **Future Convergence Timeline:** While leading US labs will possess massive compute advantages by 2028, China is projected to "hockey stick" in domestic chip production by 2028–2029, potentially reaching 50 incremental gigawatts by 2029, though constrained by lower chip performance relative to Western hardware.
