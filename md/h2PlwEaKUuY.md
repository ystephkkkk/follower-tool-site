## TL;DR
TSMC's conservative investment strategy since late 2022 has created a significant bottleneck for the AI industry, leading to billions in foregone revenue for hyperscalers and threatening future growth, a problem only solvable by fostering strong competition in the foundry space.

## The AI Supply Bottleneck
-   **Industry-Wide Demand Exceeds Supply:** Major tech companies consistently report that demand for AI capacity, particularly GPUs and TPUs, outstrips available supply.
    -   **Amazon CEO Andy Jassy:** "As fast as we're adding capacity right now, we're monetizing it."
    -   **Microsoft CFO Amy Hood:** "demand again exceeded supply across workloads even as we brought more capacity online."
    -   **Google CFO Anat Ashkenazi:** "still expect to remain in a tight demand-supply environment in Q4 and 2026."
    -   **Meta's Mark Zuckerberg:** "we keep on having more demand to be able to use more compute...than we end up having compute for."
-   **TSMC Acknowledges Constraint:** TSMC CEO C.C. Wei explicitly stated that "silicon from TSMC is a bottleneck," confirming that the constraint is chip supply, not power or other infrastructure.

## TSMC's Investment Strategy and Impact
-   **Historical Underinvestment:** Following ChatGPT's release in November 2022, TSMC's annual capital expenditures (CAPEX) remained largely flat, even declining year-over-year in both 2023 and 2024.
    -   This suggests TSMC "didn't buy the hype" as hyperscalers began significantly increasing their own AI-related CAPEX.
    -   This lack of early investment is the direct cause of today's chip shortage, acting as a "de facto break on the AI buildout."
-   **Foregone Revenue:** The current supply-demand imbalance has already cost major tech companies significant revenue, as they cannot fully monetize the existing demand for AI services.
-   **Delayed Impact of New Investments:** While TSMC is increasing CAPEX (37% to $41 billion last year, planned 32% to $52 billion-$56 billion this year), these investments will have a minimal impact on supply until 2028-2029 due to the 2-3 year build time for new fabs.
    -   TSMC CEO Wei admitted nervousness about these investments, stating he is worried about demand several years down the line and wanting to "make sure that my customers' demands are real."
    -   TSMC's projected CAPEX growth still lags behind that of hyperscalers, indicating the bottleneck is likely to persist or even worsen through 2028 and beyond.

## Strategic Implications and Risk Transfer
-   **TSMC's Rational Risk Management:** TSMC is acting rationally by managing its CAPEX risk, as foundry investments are extremely capital-intensive and carry high downside if demand fails to materialize ("big disaster").
-   **Offloading Risk to Hyperscalers:** This conservatism means TSMC is effectively transferring the risk of unmet demand to its customers, primarily hyperscalers.
    -   If TSMC's chip supply remains insufficient in 2028 and beyond, hyperscalers stand to lose potentially "hundreds of billions of dollars" in foregone revenue, a much larger risk than TSMC's perceived investment risk.
    -   Current foregone revenue is "peanuts" compared to potential future losses.
-   **Limited Impact of Geopolitical Risks:** While geopolitical risks (e.g., China-Taiwan) are often cited, the article argues the more immediate and substantial risk is TSMC's monopoly and its impact on industry-wide AI value capture.

## The Path to Resolution: Fostering Competition
-   **Begging is Insufficient:** Simply asking TSMC to invest more will not solve the problem, as it requires TSMC to re-assume the very risk it has offloaded.
-   **Need for Foundry Competition:** The only effective solution is to foster genuine competition in the foundry space.
    -   This requires Samsung, Intel, or both to become "viable options" for advanced chip manufacturing.
    -   Competition would incentivize TSMC to invest more aggressively while simultaneously adding more overall capacity to the market.
-   **Hyperscaler Responsibility:** Hyperscalers and fabless companies must be willing to become "meaningful external customer[s]" for alternative foundries, despite the significant risks involved (e.g., years to get a chip working on a new process, potential performance/service differences compared to TSMC).
    -   Taking on this risk is crucial to avoid exponentially greater foregone revenue in the future.
-   **Mitigating Geographic Risk:** A diversified foundry landscape through competition would also inherently mitigate geopolitical risks associated with relying on a single geographic region.
