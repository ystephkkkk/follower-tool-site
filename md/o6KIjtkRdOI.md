## TL;DR
AI has collapsed the cybersecurity "pyramid of pain," giving any activist or e-criminal nation-state-level attack capabilities and driving a 300% year-over-year surge in discovered vulnerabilities. As a result, organizations face a "break or breach" reality—rushing to patch decades of technical debt at machine speed (often causing major public outages) while defending against autonomous attack frameworks like RTX that can compromise a company in under three hours.

## The Agentic Threat & The "Mythos Moment"
- **The Hugging Face Watershed Moment:** OpenAI deployed thousands of autonomous software agents with minimal guardrails, resulting in an end-to-end autonomous attack. George Kurtz noted the real "aha moment" wasn't the attack itself, but that defenders lacked frontier-level AI capabilities to understand it and had to rely on Chinese models because US frontier models refused their defensive requests.
- **Collapsing the Pyramid of Pain:** Historically, cyber adversaries ranged from low-sophistication activists at the base, to e-crime ($20B/year industry) in the middle, to nation-states at the apex. With AI agents—the new "agent state"—nation-state capabilities are democratized down to anyone.
- **Trusted Access & Obliterated Models:** CrowdStrike participates in Anthropic’s **Glasswing** and OpenAI’s **Daybreak** trusted access programs. However, open-weight models (like GLM and Qwen) are only ~3 months behind frontier models, and bad actors are using "obliterated" models (e.g., Warlock, CyberStrike) with post-trained guardrails removed to achieve parity when paired with the right harness engineering.
- **RTX Autonomous Attack Framework:** Built in China and briefly open-sourced before being taken down, RTX allows a human to set a target and let sub-agents handle recon, vulnerability discovery, exploit building, privilege escalation, and data theft—completely compromising a company in **under 3 hours**. Over **12,000 forks** of RTX now exist, and CrowdStrike is actively responding to RTX-driven incidents (including hits on Korean banks where agents targeted 200 organizations).

## The Vulnerability Surge & "Break or Breach"
- **Exponential CVE Growth:** In 2026 alone, **17,000 CVEs** (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures) have been found—representing **25% of all CVEs ever discovered** in history—with a **300% year-over-year increase** (October to October) and volume doubling monthly.
- **Dormant Code & Vulnerability Chaining:** Most newly surfaced flaws are dormant bugs in legacy code (e.g., a PHP vulnerability dating back to 2004). AI excels at "vulnerability chaining"—combining 3, 10, or 100 low-risk flaws into a single high-risk remote code execution exploit.
- **Break or Breach:** The traditional 30-day Microsoft patch window has collapsed. Organizations must now choose between rushing patches at machine speed (risking cascading infrastructure failures) or leaving systems unpatched and getting breached.
- **Outages as Second-Order AI Effects:** Recent high-profile outages (e.g., FAA, Microsoft Outlook/Teams, Chase, United, Apple) are largely driven by companies scrambling to patch and modernize 25–30 years of technical debt. Some CEOs are even considering taking global services offline temporarily just to safely apply PHP or Java patches.

## Open-Weight, Local AI, & AI Sovereignty
- **Shift Away from Frontier Token Maxing:** Fortune 500 companies are moving away from pure frontier-model usage toward **AI sovereignty** and cost minimization, running open-weight models locally or on private servers and routing only complex edge cases to frontier labs.
- **Hardware & Local Inference:** Both Kurtz and Jason Calacanis highlighted the rise of local compute—such as Apple Mac Studios (512GB RAM) and Nvidia DGX Sparks—enabling knowledge workers to run powerful inference locally.
- **Open Source as Defense:** Despite political pushback against Chinese open-source models, Kurtz and Calacanis agreed that open source provides transparency ("sunlight") that allows defenders to inspect code, track forks, and neutralize threats.

## Identity, Human Factors, & The Future of Defense
- **Why Break In When You Can Log In:** Attackers bypass Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) by stealing **session tokens** (e.g., the 30-day "trust this computer" passkeys in Outlook/Gmail) and setting up hidden inbox rules to intercept wires and communications.
- **North Korean IT Worker Infiltration:** CrowdStrike uncovered a widespread scheme where North Korean operatives interview for remote tech jobs (often with cameras off), have company laptops shipped to "laptop farms" (e.g., in Texas), and VPN in to work—and gain internal system access. CrowdStrike’s first 40 customer notifications were 100% accurate; one company even exposed an operative by asking them to say "Kim Jong-un is a fat pig" on a call.
- **Spy vs. Spy:** Even if AI rewrote all global code to have zero vulnerabilities, breaches would still occur via identity and authentication flaws. The future of defense requires an automated **red-blue AI loop** (offensive AI continuously testing defensive AI), alongside strong relationships with law enforcement (e.g., local FBI offices) since corporations lack legal authority (Title 50) to hack back.
