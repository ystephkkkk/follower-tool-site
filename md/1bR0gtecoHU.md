## Databricks' Origin & Contrarian Bets
- **UC Berkeley Roots:** Founded in 2013 out of UC Berkeley's AMPlab (~20 researchers, the largest AI lab at the time), where Ali Ghodsi and his team worked on the 2008–2009 Netflix Prize ($1M for a 10% improvement in movie recommendations). "AMP" stood for Algorithms, Machine Learning, and People—reflecting an early belief in human-in-the-loop AI.
- **Three Contrarian Bets:** At a time when AI was dismissed as niche ad-tech, Databricks bet everything on:
  - **Cloud:** Separating compute and storage (an analyst firm bet Ali it would be a "strategic blunder" that would sink the company).
  - **AI Use Cases:** Building for enterprise data science teams.
  - **Open Source:** Making the core technology open.
- **Scale & Growth:** Grew from $1.5M in revenue in 2015 to a $7B+ run rate by August 2026, growing 70–80% YoY (with Ali aiming for >100% growth) across a 12,000-person organization.

## AGI Is Here, But Enterprise AI Is Behind
- **AGI vs. Superintelligence:** Ghodsi believes AGI—as defined by AMPlab in 2009–2010—was achieved last year, though researchers have moved the goalpost. By contrast, **Superintelligence** (Nick Bostrom's 2014 definition: doing 1,000 years of human thought in a minute) remains the obsession of frontier labs.
- **Current Enterprise Reality:** Global 2000 and Fortune 500 companies mostly use AI as a "glorified Google search" (chatbots) and for coding (seeing 20–30% speedups), rather than running thousands of autonomous agents.
- **Four Bottlenecks Holding Enterprises Back:**
  - **The Context Problem:** AI is useless if it isn't in the room. One major bank CEO told Ghodsi they will "never" record meetings due to liability.
  - **Cybersecurity & Surface Area:** Connecting agents to all corporate data multiplies attack surface area by 1,000x+ and creates insider threat risks.
  - **Exponential Token Costs:** Token spend rises exponentially without a matching exponential lift in revenue, prompting CFOs to push back on OpEx and COGS.
  - **Data & Intelligence Sovereignty:** Companies fear IP leakage into foundation model providers (e.g., Anthropic, OpenAI) that release competing features every two weeks.
- **Digital Natives Lead:** Younger companies (10–15 years old), such as Jack Dorsey's Block/Square (building a "Company World Model"), are far ahead of 20–50-year-old legacy enterprises.

## Live Demo: "Genie" & Management Moneyball
- **The Company Ontology:** Databricks' **Genie** product constructs a live, 24/7 graph of the entire company—mapping org charts, emails, Slack, docs, spreadsheets, and meeting transcripts—to cut latency across hierarchical org charts.
- **HR Survey & Causality:** Out of 10,000+ employee responses, executive leadership scored 88%, work-life balance 60%, and "action" (feeling heard) ~33%. When the CFO hypothesized that poor work-life blend caused low action, Genie disproved causality (e.g., surfacing a manager with 44% work-life blend and 77% action) and sliced root causes by department (PM: too many stakeholders; Engineering: tools/systems; Sales Ops: career growth).
- **Engineering Productivity (The L-Curve):** Plotting weekly pull requests across 2,000 engineers revealed an L-shape where the top 10% ship vastly more code by running "automated factories." Overlaying token cost showed top shippers weren't necessarily spending more because they route simpler tasks to open-source/cheaper models.
- **Eliminating Human Blockers:** Genie tracks "human blockers" (legal, finance, sibling orgs) and audits whether regional offices (e.g., Japan vs. EMEA vs. LatAm) adhere to core cultural principles. Jason Calacanis noted he built a similar internal bot ("Major Decisions") to track Slack, Notion, and email for decision-making speed.

## Staying Private vs. Going Public
- **The Case for Going Public:** Provides access to public capital, massive quarterly marketing events, and liquid M&A currency (though Databricks has already executed $1B+ acquisitions privately).
- **Why Databricks Is Staying Private (For Now):**
  - **Avoiding the Public Rollercoaster:** Ghodsi wants to shield employees from public market sentiment swings during the AI transition, where a sudden stock drop feels like a pay cut and triggers attrition.
  - **Vibrant Secondary Liquidity:** Databricks has raised $20B–$30B in annual tender offers. Employees rarely use up the full tender allocation because they prefer to hold onto 70–80% compounding growth.

## RSI, Cyber Risk & The Doom Debate
- **Why We Don't Have RSI:** Writing 98% of code with AI does not equal Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI). Ghodsi outlines **four unmet conditions** required for an RSI flywheel:
  1. *Faster Training:* Next-gen models must train super-linearly faster (currently, pre-training takes longer and follows scaling laws requiring exponentially more hardware).
  2. *Fewer Resources:* Training must use less compute, not 10x more hardware each generation.
  3. *Generalized Accuracy:* Accuracy must improve at least linearly across all domains, not just in narrow RL gyms or math.
  4. *Continuous Loop:* All three must compound continuously (e.g., training dropping from 3 months → 1 month → 2 weeks → 1 week, and cost dropping from $100M → $50M → $1M).
- **Cyber Risk Is Real, But Finite:** Cyber is asymmetric—attackers only need 1 success out of thousands of agents, while defenders must be 100% secure. Databricks addresses this with **Lakewatch**. However, code vulnerabilities are finite; as AI scans new code and rewrites/shadow-patches legacy code, software will reach a more secure steady state.
- **Tangible AI Wins:** Regeneron used Databricks to identify the genome behind chronic liver disease; Merck and Databricks trained **Teddy** (Transformer Enabled Drug Discovery) on gene regulatory networks (GRN); Crisis Textline uses LLMs to detect self-harm; and Zipline uses Databricks for drone delivery operations.
