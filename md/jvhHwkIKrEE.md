**TL;DR**
At the San Francisco Fed’s Productivity and Jobs Task Force Conference, Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh, tech CEOs, and leading economists agreed that superintelligence (SI) is driving unprecedented, exponential gains in software and research productivity, even as macro-level economic data has yet to show a definitive breakout. While frontier firms are already seeing massive returns, policymakers face deep uncertainty over whether SI will broadly augment human labor, displace white-collar workers, or trigger a capital-investment bust.

## The Fed’s Framework & Macro Contingencies
* **Opening Context:** San Francisco Fed President **Mary Daly** noted that since OpenAI’s November 2022 release of ChatGPT, AI has evolved faster and felt more transformative than the 1996 internet boom, requiring policymakers to track its real-time impact on growth, productivity, inflation, and labor.
* **Automating Idea Generation:** Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** explained that while research efforts have increased more than 20x since the 1930s, research productivity has fallen by an even larger amount. Unlike past general-purpose technologies (electricity, internal combustion, transistors), SI could supercharge and automate the creation of new ideas, pushing token prices down ~50% per quarter.
* **Three Illustrative Scenarios:** Warsh outlined three non-predictive contingencies developed by Fed Board staff to guide monetary policy:
  * **Broadly Shared Gains:** SI boosts productivity across both labor and capital owners, keeping real GDP persistently above its long-term average and maintaining near-full employment.
  * **Labor Market Disruption:** GDP rises above its long-term average, but widespread automation and labor frictions push the jobless rate above full-employment levels.
  * **SI Disappointment:** Following massive capital investment and equity gains, expected returns turn down, valuations fall, and the economy slows with a notable rise in unemployment before recovering without productivity gains.

## Inside the Frontier: Adoption, Productivity & Startups
* **Affirm’s Engineering & Underwriting Gains:** CEO **Max Levchin** reported that AI has made Affirm’s engineering ~5x more productive (with cost per feature expected to drop to 1/10th). Using LLMs to design a new transformer-based underwriting model yielded an 80% performance increase over the non-LLM baseline—translating to ~$2 billion more per year in loans—with **zero layoffs** due to AI.
* **Stripe’s Startup Surge:** CEO **Patrick Collison** noted that Stripe’s G&A hiring has slowed as a share of revenue, while R&D and sales hiring are accelerating due to higher ROI. Crucially, the 2026 cohort of startups on Stripe is massively outperforming prior years, with new business launches up ~2x year-over-year (unprecedented in Stripe’s 15-year history).
* **Enterprise Bottlenecks vs. Consumer Scale:** Palo Alto Networks CEO **Nikesh Arora** emphasized that while AI democratizes intelligence for 6 billion people, enterprise adoption is slowed by edge cases. Citing Google’s driverless car project (from a 2009 Lexus to Waymo in 2026), he noted it took 17 years and >$30 billion to replace one human job, and that AI still requires human judgment for the critical 20% edge cases.

## Cybersecurity, US Leadership & Market Structure
* **Asymmetric Cyber Threats:** **Nikesh Arora** warned that open-source models like "Mythos" (released 6 months ago) allow even unskilled hackers to find latent vulnerabilities in 50-year-old human-written code, creating a 2-to-3-year "technology rebuild moment" for critical infrastructure like banking. **Patrick Collison** echoed this, noting that even the heavily scrutinized Linux TCP stack is riddled with exploitable bugs.
* **US vs. Rest of World:** **Patrick Collison** and **Nikesh Arora** argued the US holds a decisive lead in both model creation and enterprise diffusion, widening the growth gap with Europe. **Max Levchin** added that while the EU is still debating regulatory frameworks, the US and China are aggressively investing in compute and energy.
* **Who Captures the Surplus?** **Patrick Collison** argued that for the foreseeable future, the vast majority of surplus will acrue to *users* of AI rather than just the frontier labs. **Max Levchin** cautioned that unlike past revolutions, cash-rich Fortune 100 incumbents are adopting AI immediately, risking an oligopolistic squeeze on mid-tier firms even as solo entrepreneurs thrive.

## Labor Markets, Inflation & Higher Education
* **The Productivity Inflection Point:** Stanford economist **Nick Bloom** shared Atlanta Fed CFO survey data showing that over the past 3 years, 90% of firms saw zero employment impact from AI and only +0.1% to +0.2% annual productivity growth (sales per employee). Looking forward 3 years, however, CFOs project +0.8% annual productivity growth, pointing to a major inflection in 2027–2028 alongside high labor churn.
* **The White-Collar "China Shock" & Human Capital:** Former CEA Chair **Cecilia Rouse** warned that unlike the China shock (which hit trade/manual workers), AI targets knowledge workers—particularly administrative roles in monopsiny towns (e.g., college towns, state capitals). Her "nightmare" scenario is that overreliance on LLMs erodes entry-level human capital and creativity.
* **Services Inflation & Relational Jobs:** **John Levin** noted that healthcare and education are "relational" sectors where humans may resist automation, potentially limiting disinflationary pressure, though **Nick Bloom** and **Dan Huttenlocher** saw strong disinflationary potential in back-office services and small businesses as token costs plummet.
* **Reinventing Universities & Accountability:** **John Levin** detailed how universities are adapting to AI shortcuts (e.g., dropping problem-set grades, using daily quizzes, blue-book writing, and oral exams). **Dan Huttenlocher** stressed that machines cannot take accountability, calling for formal verification ("automated reasoning") and new organizational mental models for human-AI teams.
* **Data Infrastructure Gaps:** **Cecilia Rouse** and **Nick Bloom** urged the Fed and statistical agencies (BLS, BEA, Census) to modernize data collection, noting falling survey response rates, 1-month lags, and a lack of hard data on corporate AI spending.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Productivity Inflection:** US corporate productivity growth (sales per employee) will accelerate to +0.8% per year over the next 3 years, with the macro inflection hitting in 2027–2028 — *Nick Bloom (citing Atlanta Fed CFO Survey)*.
* **Drop-in Remote Workers:** Many in the AI sector expect drop-in remote AI workers (capable of joining Slack/Zoom and doing any keyboard-and-monitor job) within the next 12 months — *Patrick Collison*.
* **Energy & Compute:** Two influential AI leaders have a standing bet on whether global energy use will double in a single month in some year over the next 10 years — *Patrick Collison*.
* **Cyber Rebuild:** The next 2 to 3 years will be a "technology rebuild moment" as enterprises refactor legacy code to fix AI-discovered vulnerabilities — *Nikesh Arora*.
* **US Growth Divergence:** The economic growth gap between the US and Europe will continue to widen due to faster US AI diffusion — *Patrick Collison*.
