**TL;DR**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent defended a proposed 12% reduction in the department’s FY2027 budget, prioritizing President Trump’s "Working Families" tax cuts and a digital-first IRS modernization while facing intense questioning over inflation, IRS enforcement cuts, and oil sanctions relief for Russia and Iran.

## FY2027 Budget Strategy and Tax Relief
*   **Budget Overview:** The Treasury is requesting $11.5 billion for FY2027, representing a $1.5 billion (12%) decrease from enacted levels.
*   **Tax Cut Initiatives:** Bessent highlighted the "Working Families Tax Cut Act," which includes "no tax on tips," "no tax on overtime," and enhanced deductions for seniors.
    *   **Impact:** Over 7 million filers claimed the "no tax on tips" benefit (average $7,000 deduction), and 28 million claimed the overtime benefit (average $3,100 deduction).
*   **Trump Accounts:** Nearly 5.5 million long-term savings accounts have been opened to build financial security for future generations, including a $1,000 pilot program contribution for 1.3 million children.
*   **Paid Family Leave:** Senator Fischer emphasized the importance of the Paid Family and Medical Leave tax credit; Bessent committed to prioritizing updated IRS guidance to ensure employers can accurately claim these credits.

## IRS Modernization and Enforcement Controversies
*   **Digital-First Agency:** The IRS aims for a 98% online filing rate. Bessent reported that paper processing costs have dropped from $45 million to $20 million through automation.
*   **Staffing and Budget Cuts:** Ranking Member Jack Reed criticized the proposed $1.4 billion cut to the IRS, noting the agency has already lost 28,000 employees under the current administration and faces 4,000 more cuts.
*   **High-Income Enforcement:** Reed highlighted a proposed 50% decrease in exams for high-income individuals for FY2026.
    *   **Counter-Argument:** Bessent argued that new technology allows the IRS to flag potential audits to taxpayers in advance, increasing voluntary compliance and revenue. He noted enforcement actions are up 12% this year, totaling over $40 billion.
*   **Direct File Termination:** Bessent defended the decision to shut down the "Direct File" program, claiming it cost taxpayers $138 per person to administer compared to "Free File" public-private partnerships.

## Sanctions, National Security, and Foreign Policy
*   **Global Oil Markets:** Bessent engaged in a heated exchange over sanctions relief for Russia and Iran. He argued that allowing Russian oil on the market (more than 250 million barrels) kept global prices at $100/barrel rather than $150/barrel.
*   **30-Day Extensions:** Bessent admitted to extending Russian sanctions relief for 30 days following pleas from "vulnerable and poorest countries" during World Bank/IMF week.
*   **Transnational Crime:** Senator Collins (Maine) raised alarms regarding 200+ illegal marijuana grow houses in Maine linked to PRC criminal syndicates. Bessent committed to using IRS Criminal Investigations and FinCEN to dismantle these money-laundering operations.
*   **Digital Assets:** The budget includes investments to implement the **Genius Act**, a regulatory framework for stablecoins intended to preserve US leadership in fintech.

## Community Development and Rural Infrastructure
*   **CDFI Funding Cuts:** The budget proposes a 60% cut to Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs).
    *   **Rationale:** Bessent argued the program had become "loaded with a partisan wish list" and cited instances of fraud in auto-lending.
    *   **Rural Focus:** The Secretary highlighted a new $100 million allocation specifically targeted for rural areas in states like Nebraska and Maine.
*   **Taxpayer Assistance Centers (TAC):** Senators expressed concern over the closure of physical IRS centers in rural Maine, forcing 10-hour round trips for in-person service. Bessent promised to "remedy this 10-hour problem."
