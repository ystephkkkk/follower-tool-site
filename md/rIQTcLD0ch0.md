**TL;DR**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Bjorn Lomborg argue that the IMF and World Bank must abandon "elite-driven" climate fixations and return to their core missions of fostering economic growth, financial stability, and poverty reduction through energy abundance and cost-benefit analysis.

## Refocusing Multilateral Institutions
* **Core Missions:** Secretary Scott Bessent emphasized that the IMF must return to focusing on **global financial stability** and correcting unsustainable economic paths, while the World Bank’s primary mandate should be **lifting people out of poverty**.
* **Critique of "Mission Creep":** Both speakers criticized the shift toward "Western elite issues" such as gender, social topics, and climate change.
    * **Climate Fixation:** Bjorn Lomborg noted that 48% of World Bank financing is now dedicated to climate finance, potentially at the expense of basic needs like healthcare and education.
    * **Short-termism:** Bessent argued that institutional policies are often based on "short-term" scientific trends or "luxury beliefs" that do not reflect the priorities of the world’s poorest populations.
* **Institutional Efficiency:** Bessent suggested symbolic and practical reforms, such as the IMF focusing on **global trade imbalances** (specifically mentioning China’s trillion-dollar surplus) rather than maintaining peripheral assets like its Maryland golf course.

## Energy Abundance and Economic Growth
* **"All-of-the-Above" Energy Strategy:** Bessent praised the World Bank’s recent pivot toward supporting **nuclear energy**, arguing that affordable and reliable energy is a prerequisite for growth.
* **Innovation over Restriction:** The speakers advocated for solving climate issues through **technological innovation** rather than carbon mandates.
    * **Historical Precedents:** They cited the "Green Revolution" in agriculture as a model for how innovation, not austerity, solves global crises.
    * **Energy Realities:** Lomborg highlighted that despite trillions spent, Germany and China remain heavily dependent on fossil fuels (79% and 87% respectively), arguing that forcing renewables on poor nations is "hypocritical."
* **U.S. Energy Role:** Bessent noted the U.S. transition from energy scarcity to holding larger reserves than Saudi Arabia due to fracking, framing energy independence as a pillar of financial stability.

## Development Priorities and Trade-offs
* **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Lomborg argued for a return to rigorous cost-benefit assessments to ensure "the most bang for our buck."
    * **Resilience through Prosperity:** He noted that a hurricane kills hundreds in poor Haiti but virtually no one in rich Florida, asserting that **prosperity is the best protection** against environmental risks.
* **Local Priorities:** According to Afrobarometer surveys, climate change ranks 31st out of 34 concerns for Africans, far behind unemployment, health, and corruption.
* **Capitalism and Infrastructure:** Tim Adams and Bessent identified foundational drivers of development:
    * **Property Rights:** Essential for farmers and SMEs to secure capital.
    * **Capital Access:** "Capitalism without capital is just another ism."
    * **Infrastructure:** Basic needs like clean water, electricity, and roads must precede higher-level social goals.

## Global Economic Stability and Geopolitics
* **The "Argentina Success":** Bessent lauded the IMF’s recent work with Javier Milei’s administration, noting that Argentina is accumulating reserves and receiving support from its poorest citizens who desire economic stability.
* **Debt Transparency:** A key concern for the multilateral banks should be addressing the "loan-to-own" programs and undisclosed debt arrangements—specifically targeting tolling arrangements and "downward spirals" in developing nations.
* **International Cooperation:** Bessent stated that "America First does not mean America alone," emphasizing that the U.S. intends to lead the G20 and G7 by focusing on tools that hinder growth, such as unsustainable debt and poor infrastructure.
