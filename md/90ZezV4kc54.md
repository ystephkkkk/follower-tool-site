**TL;DR**
Following the close of AMD's $8.2 billion acquisition of World Labs, newly appointed AMD Chief Scientist Fei-Fei Li outlines how spatial intelligence and "world models" represent the next frontier of AI beyond language, while warning that universities are being priced out of AI research and calling for science-based governance.

## The $8.2B AMD Acquisition & Chip Ecosystem
* **Deal & New Role:** World Labs, which Li spent two and a half years building, announced an $8.2 billion deal to sell to AMD in September; the deal closed on Friday, making this interview day two of Li's tenure as Chief Scientist at AMD.
* **Strategic Rationale:** Li and AMD CEO Lisa Su view the partnership as "1+1 bigger than 3," extending far beyond simple access to compute.
  * **Co-Design:** The focus is on platform, infrastructure, and the co-evolution between AI models, software, and hardware, alongside expanding AMD's open ecosystem.
* **Multi-Chip Ecosystem:** Asked by moderator Andrew Edgecliffe-Johnson whether Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia is good for AMD or damaging to the broader ecosystem, Li declined to comment directly, citing it being her second day on the job.
  * She emphasized that a healthy, multi-chip ecosystem—spanning Nvidia, AMD, TPUs, and companies like Broadcom—alongside great labs, academia, and the public sector, benefits everyone.

## Spatial Intelligence, World Models & Products
* **Evolutionary Roots:** Li, who began laying the foundation for modern computer vision 20 years ago, noted that spatial intelligence—the loop between seeing, sensing, and interacting—is more than 540 million years old, dating back to ancient underwater animals.
* **World Models Defined:** World models connect pixels, 3D worlds, and 4D worlds with deep reasoning, generation, and understanding capabilities, moving AI beyond language.
* **Product Releases & Users:**
  * **Marble & Atlas:** World Labs released its first model, *Marble*, last year, and launched *Atlas*—a more advanced version of Marble—last week.
  * **Early Adopters:** Current users include avant-garde game designers, VFX artists, independent studios, architects, and robotics companies.
* **Market Opportunity:** Beyond established industries like VFX, gaming, design, and movies, Li sees world models transforming healthcare, education, manufacturing, and agriculture, with the market cap for robotics being "truly unbounded."
* **Competition:** Asked how World Labs differentiates from spatial intelligence efforts by Jeff Bezos (Prometheus) and Yann LeCun (AMI), Li expressed respect for both leaders but stated she has no insight into their work.

## The Public Sector Compute Crisis
* **Priced Out:** Having recently stepped down as co-director of the Stanford Human-Centered AI Institute (HAI), Li warned that universities and the public sector are not keeping up with commercial AI because they are "priced out" of tokens and compute.
* **10-Year Advocacy:** Li has advocated for public-sector AI resourcing for roughly 10 years, warning it would be "one of the biggest failures of our time" if open democratic societies fail to fund researchers, students, and educators.
  * Public-sector compute is essential for discovering cures for cancer, teaching the next generation, and maintaining an independent voice for benchmarking and accountability.

## AI Governance & Global Robotics Competition
* **Science-Based Policy:** Li, who co-authored a paper on world model governance this summer, urged Washington D.C. to adopt a balanced, pragmatic, and science-based (rather than science-fiction-based) governance framework.
  * Key priorities include model evaluation, incident reporting, addressing cybersecurity dangers, and investing in the public sector.
* **US-China Robotics Race:** Pressed on whether the U.S. can win in physical AI given China's lead in robotics, Li framed robotics as a "civilizational technology" where the goal should be for humanity to win against poverty, sickness, and ignorance (e.g., through disaster relief, elder care, and scientific discovery).
  * Squaring this with geopolitical competition requires "concentric rings" of responsible AI use: individual, institutional, industry-wide, societal, and multinational collaboration.
