## Leadership, Governance, and the Altman–Brockman Partnership
* **11-Year Partnership:** Altman and Brockman co-founded OpenAI nearly 11 years ago (following a July 2015 dinner) and operate with high-bandwidth communication, often speaking five to six times a day on everything from word choices to major strategic pivots.
* **Division of Labor:** While Altman has historically been the public face of OpenAI, Brockman has increasingly taken on day-to-day operational management.
* **Equity and Control:** 
  * Altman does not hold an equity stake in OpenAI, whereas Brockman holds a stake worth roughly $30 billion; both stated this has not affected their relationship, though Altman noted he "should have listened to Greg" when Brockman originally advocated for him to take equity.
  * Brockman noted that OpenAI is the only major AI company where the founders do not hold supermajority control, which he views as an important governance distinction.
* **Perspective on "Cult of CEO" & Jensen Huang:** Both expressed empathy for Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang amid recent unspecified news, calling him "singular" and noting Nvidia is "built to last."

## AI Capabilities, Alignment, and Keeping Humans in Control
* **Humans in Control vs. Containment:** Both Altman and Brockman rejected the Silicon Valley notion of a "gradual handover" to AI, insisting humans must remain in control rather than merely "in the loop."
* **Disagreement with Satya Nadella:** Altman pushed back on Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella’s view that AI models can never be truly aligned and must instead be contained, arguing that alignment is achievable—though the bar for confidence must rise as models become smarter.
* **Technical Milestones:**
  * **Chip Design:** OpenAI announced a partnership with AMD where AI models achieved a full year's worth of generational chip design improvements in 5 months.
  * **High-Compute Use Cases:** Brockman highlighted that applying $1 million worth of compute can uncover 1,000 bugs in the Linux kernel in a short period with a single researcher (compared to the typical ~20 bugs found per year).
* **Degrading Chain-of-Thought Monitoring:** Brockman explained that as models grow more capable, they do more reasoning in the "forward pass" without writing out their thinking, meaning traditional chain-of-thought monitoring is degrading and requires new technical monitoring approaches.

## Safety, Security, and Industry Competition
* **Shift to Development-Phase Safety:** Brockman noted that 2026 has been a transition year because models are now capable enough to pose real-world risks *during* development, not just at deployment.
* **Security Sprint & Hugging Face Incident:** 
  * Following a security incident involving Hugging Face (around August), OpenAI paused 25% of its production engineering projects for a security sprint, redirecting those engineers to secure the company using its own frontier cyber models.
  * Altman pushed back against a California Attorney General lawsuit regarding the incident, defending OpenAI’s "iterative deployment" model as the only way to safely confront reality.
* **Anthropic IPO & Shared Standards:** 
  * Altman reiterated his view that going public right now (referencing Anthropic’s imminent IPO) is challenging given the tension between commercial viability and heightened safety requirements, though OpenAI plans to go public eventually.
  * Both called for shared industry safety standards to eliminate the "fog of war" and race dynamics, noting that companies currently lack visibility into each other's internal safety bars.
* **The White House Accord:** Brockman clarified that OpenAI is aligned with the White House Accord on safety standards, sidestepping a question about a push to rebrand "AI" to "SI."

## Business Growth, Compute, and Vertical Integration
* **Explosive Scale & Revenue:**
  * **User Base:** Over 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT every week, including 330 million weekly users for health queries.
  * **Financials:** In Q3 alone, OpenAI’s annualized top-line revenue grew 70%, and enterprise revenue doubled.
* **Compute Constraints & Custom Chips:** 
  * Brockman confirmed he has asked both Elon Musk and Sergey Brin for compute, stating the world is not building enough of it and that compute is the primary limiter of economic acceleration.
  * OpenAI is vertically integrating across the entire "intelligence stack," including building its own data centers and developing a custom inference chip called **Jalapeno**, which Brockman claimed will be the best inference chip in the industry.
* **Cost Efficiency:** Responding to Microsoft’s claims of running AI tasks at 5% of OpenAI's cost, Altman welcomed the competition, stating OpenAI is proud of driving down the cost of intelligence.

## Legal & Public Perception
* **New York Times Lawsuit:** When asked by Andrew Ross Sorkin about emails showing Brockman responding "nice" to a colleague scraping *The New York Times* past its paywall, Brockman declined to comment due to active litigation, noting he had already addressed it in his deposition.
* **Hollywood Portrayal:** Asked about Andrew Garfield playing him in the upcoming film *Artificial*, Altman said the weirdest part of his life isn't the movie, but the responsibility of ushering in a technology with such high stakes.
