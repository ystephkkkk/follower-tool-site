## TL;DR
The open public internet as a global platform is dying due to AI-driven security vulnerabilities, bot-filled content slop, and hyper-competition, forcing a return to smaller, private, and trusted spaces like group chats and niche subscription models.

## The End of Blogging and the Rise of Subscriptions
* **Early Blogosphere Origins:** Early blogging waves in the early 2000s were dominated by political and niche content creators (e.g., Talking Points Memo, Andrew Sullivan, Boing Boing, Instapundit, Matt Yglesias, Daily Kos, Daring Fireball, AVC).
* **The Google Aggregation Trap:** While distribution was free, Google captured discovery and most ad revenue through AdSense, eventually leading to intrusive ads and shrinking payouts as content supply became limitless.
* **The Subscription Pivot:** 
  * Andrew Sullivan proved the viability of reader-supported models, earning nearly $1 million a year over his final two years through loyal readers.
  * Ben Thompson noted that going small and niche—limiting audience size to serve customers who truly care—is a more sustainable business model than milking ad pennies from everyone.

## The Evolution of Social Media into Group Chats
* **The Decay of Public Platforms:** Social media followed the 99-1 rule (1% speak, 9% comment, 90% lurk), eventually prioritizing engagement metrics and transforming public networks into personal entertainment sites like TikTok with zero social component.
* **The Group Chat Renaissance:** 
  * Public social media has hollowed out, but social networking itself is stronger than ever inside private, high-trust group chats.
  * The modern winning scale is not an ever-larger audience, but the proliferation of infinite finite groups where users feel safe to communicate.

## The Collapse of the Public Internet and Software Security
* **Current Security Vulnerabilities:** Recent security failures include critical PHP remote code execution bugs (patched automatically on Shurtugal via Pressable), compromised intermediate CA private keys, and major vulnerabilities in node.js, GitLab, Java, Cisco products, and LLM supply chains.
* **The AI Threat Multiplier:** 
  * Software has historically been built on creaky, human-authored foundations where the primary gate was functionality rather than security.
  * AI possesses limitless time and the ability to substitute iteration for cleverness, exposing vulnerabilities faster than human defenders can patch them.
* **The Death of DIY Hosting:** Setting up your own public website, server, or app from scratch has become a dangerous proposition in the age of AI, turning the public internet into a battle zone and wasteland.

## The Shift to No-Code and Bespoke Software
* **Private and Internal Apps:** Instead of building public apps for the App Store (which will be flooded with AI slop), creators are building internal software for themselves and small, trusted teams (e.g., Ben Thompson's Shady Acres apps for podcast production, task tracking, and calendar management).
* **Democratized Creation:** AI eliminates the high upfront costs traditionally required to justify large audiences, making it possible to build customized software for a single person or team.

## Cognitive Capitalism and Economic Transformation
* **Venkatesh Rao’s "Cognitive Mercantilism":** Draws a parallel between the agricultural revolution—which liberated humans from subsistence farming to create industrial markets—and the AI revolution, which frees human minds from "subsistence cognition."
* **Competing Views on AI Intelligence:**
  * *AI Malthusian:* Sees an oversupply of intelligence that outruns humanity, creating economically redundant minds.
  * *Cognitive Capitalist:* Sees an expansion of demand from minds liberated to pursue previously unfulfillable cognitive desires.
* **The Power of Association:** Citing Alexis de Tocqueville’s *Democracy in America*, the future points toward infinite local markets and voluntary associations rather than monolithic global platforms, creating diverse opportunities to meet new human needs.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
