## The Macro Context: A "Fourth Turning" Debt Cycle

    *   **Sovereign Debt Crisis:** This period is defined by high debt-to-GDP ratios and the exhaustion of traditional monetary tools (interest rates at zero).

*   **Shift to Public Debt:** We have moved from the "private debt" bubble (2008) to a "sovereign debt" bubble. Because governments rarely default nominally, the primary outcome is currency devaluation and purchasing power debasement.
*   **Demographic & Tech Pressures:** Unlike previous cycles, this turning includes shrinking global demographics and the "real-time chaos" of social media, which accelerates the memetic downfall of institutional norms.

## Fed Independence and the 1940s Parallel
*   **The Powell-Trump Clash:** Alden identifies Jerome Powell’s recent public resistance to executive pressure as the most direct clash between the Fed and the White House since the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord.
    *   **Soft Capture:** While the Fed is more independent than it was during WWII (when it was a literal arm of the Treasury), high fiscal dominance is leading to a "soft capture" where political influence dictates rate policy.
*   **Kevin Warsh as Nominee:** Alden analyzes the potential impact of Trump’s Fed Chair nominee, Kevin Warsh.
    *   **Policy Divergence:** She expects Warsh to be **dovish on interest rates** (using AI-driven productivity gains as a justification for cuts) but potentially **hawkish on the balance sheet** (attempting to limit QE).
    *   **Populist Rhetoric:** Warsh’s criticism of the Fed’s "Wall Street-centric" policies aligns with the populist movement to refocus monetary policy on "Main Street."
*   **The "Gradual Print":** Alden’s base case is not a massive "money printer" event, but a steady, gradual increase in the Fed’s balance sheet as they reach the floor of quantitative tightening.
