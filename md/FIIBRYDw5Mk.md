## TL;DR
North Texas has experienced a doubling of wealth over the past decade, driven by a massive influx of people, corporate headquarters, and a resilient entrepreneurial culture, even as wealth managers navigate the human complexities of the great wealth transfer and AI integration.

## The North Texas Wealth Boom & Influx
- **Population & Economic Inflow:** Approximately 900,000 people have moved to Texas in the last five years, contributing to a diverse economy spanning energy, tech, healthcare, commercial real estate, and finance (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo; William Thompson, UBS; Clinton Warren, Citi).
- **Corporate Relocations:** Major companies have established global headquarters in the region, such as Charles Schwab near Westlake and Fidelity Investments, alongside a massive wave of real estate development in western suburbs (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Cross-Industry Collaboration:** New arrivals are networking and doing deals across traditional and emerging sectors, bolstered by networks and venture capital initiatives built by figures like Ross Perot and Aaron Pierce (Clinton Warren, Citi).

## Alternative Investments, Real Estate, and AI Exposure
- **Private Markets & Democratization:** Ultra-high-net-worth investors and family offices remain heavily committed to private markets, looking for liquidity and deal opportunities without any slowdown in commitment (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Private Credit Compression:** An influx of capital chasing private credit deals has caused expected yields to compress, with interest rates dropping from 15% down to 9% or 10% in some cases (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Data Center & Land Transactions:** Massive valuations and eye-watering offers—driven by AI infrastructure and data center development (including projects tied to Elon Musk)—are prompting multi-generational ranch-owning families to sell land they never expected to part with (William Thompson, UBS).
- **AI Portfolio Diligence:** While major IPOs like Anthropic draw interest, advisors stress that investors must audit their portfolios to understand their actual AI exposure and avoid repeating historical valuation bubbles like the .com era (Clinton Warren, Citi; William Thompson, UBS).

## Generational Wealth Transfer & Family Dynamics
- **Misaligned Inheritances:** According to wealth studies, 67% of business-owning parents do not anticipate leaving their businesses to their children, creating a disconnect with kids who assume they will inherit them (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **Entrepreneurial Next-Gen:** Millennials and Gen Z heirs are showing high entrepreneurial spirits, often seeking capital to start new businesses or repurpose legacy assets—such as converting ranch lands into new ventures (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **The Role of Human Advisors:** As multi-generational family goals splinter and asset legacy meanings shift, the core value of wealth managers increasingly relies on navigating complex family psychology and human "sticky muck" rather than commoditized portfolio management (Clinton Warren, Citi; Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).

## Technology & Election Impact on Wealth Management
- **AI in Investment Committees:** Some large family offices are experimenting with unconventional governance, such as replacing a human investment committee member with an AI agent to vote on cash flows, capital calls, and exposures (Clinton Warren, Citi).
- **The Human Element in Advisory:** Despite clients experimenting with AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude for financial questions, zero advisors reported clients challenging them without human advisory backing; firms are leaning into high-touch human elements (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **Election and Policy Sentiments:** While clients frequently inquire about portfolio hedging during election cycles, wholesale liquidation is rare due to tax consequences; investors are primarily focused on practical risk management, interest rate trajectories, and potential regulatory shifts concerning AI and energy (Clinton Warren, Citi; William Thompson, UBS; Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
