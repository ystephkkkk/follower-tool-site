## 1. The Scale and Impact of the Norwegian Oil Fund

* **Importance to the State Budget:**
  * **25% of the national budget** comes from the fund (every fourth krone).
  * Equates to every fourth nursing home placement, police position, school, and road project.

* **The Spending Rule:**
  * Regulated by the *handlingsregelen*, which limits spending to **3% annually**—the estimated real return on the fund.

## 2. Historical Precedents of Fallen Fortunes
* **National Wealth Loss:** Tangen notes that no large national fortune has ever survived permanently over time.
  * **Arendal (1886):** Once Norway's richest city built on sailing ships; went bankrupt after its local bank collapsed, wiping out fortunes and jobs.
  * **Spain (15th–16th Centuries):** Amassed massive wealth from American colonization, leading to high inflation, laziness, foreign reliance, and heavy war debts ("became poor because they had too much money").
  * **Great Britain (19th Century to WWI):** The world's leading financial superpower and creditor until WWI in **1914** forced it to liquidate assets and borrow heavily from the USA.
  * **Argentina (Early 20th Century):** Once one of the world's richest countries via agricultural exports; now ranks **76th** due to political instability, protectionism, and debt crises.
  * **The Netherlands (1959):** Discovered North Sea gas, funding a generous welfare state, but left with high obligations when gas revenues dropped ("Dutch disease").
* **Norway's Prior Attempt:** 
  * *Statens reservefond* was established in **1904** with first-class European bonds as a constitutional safeguard, but was liquidated by **1925** after heavy losses during World War I.
