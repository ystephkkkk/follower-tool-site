## Market Correction & The "Jensen Unwind"
* **Market Action:** From Friday through Thursday (Nov 19), the NASDAQ fell ~4%. The market declined Monday and Tuesday, staged a small rally Wednesday, and saw only a brief, fading pop on Thursday following the Anthropic IPO.
* **Political & Leadership Catalysts:** A Democratic sweep of both houses in the midterms introduced the risk of an AI moratorium or data center construction slowdown. Combined with Jensen Huang’s resignation, these events dwarf all other market news.
* **S&P 500 AI Concentration:** 
  * Infotech represents 40% of the S&P 500; adding tech-adjacent giants like Google and Amazon brings it to 50%.
  * Including utilities, large banks, asset managers/alternative asset managers funding data centers, and industrials providing construction or power (e.g., Eaton, Quanta, GE Vernova) pushes AI-exposed companies to 65% of the S&P 500.
* **Circular Financing & Dissolution:** Under Huang, Nvidia operated as a "virtual vertical holding company" by taking equity stakes or issuing loans to customers who used that capital to buy Nvidia chips—including Anthropic ($20B), OpenAI ($30B), SpaceX ($20B), CoreWeave ($2.5B), and Nebius ($2B).
  * Huang's resignation dissolves this structure. While tech leaders want him back, Treasury Secretary Bessent has appointed himself watchdog, making a front-door return to Nvidia unlikely.
  * Banks, private credit, private equity, and shareholders must now evaluate these AI companies on their own standalone merits (cash flow, balance sheets, leadership, and financing).

## How to Think About Quarterly Earnings
* **Earnings Cadence:** Begins ~15 days after quarter-end, opening with the banks and closing ~6 weeks later with Nvidia.
* **Shifting Sector Metrics:**
  * *Banks:* Loan growth, net interest margin (NIM) changes, operating efficiency, and credit quality.
  * *Tech/AI:* Shifted from revenue, earnings, and backlog to CapEx, cash flow, and whether CapEx is funded internally or via capital markets.
  * *Software (SaaS):* Shifted from seat growth and pricing trends to any signs that AI is slowing growth rates.
* **Thesis Checking & Guidance:** Earnings serve as a data-point check (e.g., 16% vs. 14% growth against a 15% thesis). Because companies "under-promise and over-deliver" and can massage accounting within limits, missing guidance signals a potential fundamental issue.
* **The "Steve Eisman Drama Scale" (1–10, Nvidia at 11):** For low-drama stocks like Coca-Cola, backward-looking numbers suffice. When sector narratives shift rapidly (like AI today, or Cisco’s recent strong report), backward-looking earnings tell investors very little about future quarters.

## The Anthropic IPO & Mailbag
* **Anthropic IPO:** Went public Thursday (Nov 19) to an anti-climactic reception. Eisman finds the stock uncompelling and is uninvolved due to:
  * *Slowing Growth:* Sequential revenue growth decelerated from 145% in Q2 to 45% in Q3, attributed to the end of "token maxing."

  * *Balance Sheet & Cost Risks:* Anthropic has $900B in commitments, meaning it will have to raise expensive debt (a junk credit rating is possible) while spending heavily to keep its frontier models competitive.
* **Mailbag 1 — Correction vs. Bear Market Positioning:** A correction lasts 1–2 years; a true bear market lasts years (none since the 1970s). The US economy is too dynamic and banks are too well-capitalized for a long-term bear market.
  * *Long-term investors:* Dollar-cost average into the S&P 500 index and ignore the noise.
  * *Traders:* Rotate into a healthcare index, a staples index, and short-term Treasuries.
* **Mailbag 2 — Identifying "Liar Loans" in 2008:** Moody’s securitization database revealed that starting in 2006, ~50% of all securitized subprime loans were "low doc" or "no doc" loans.
