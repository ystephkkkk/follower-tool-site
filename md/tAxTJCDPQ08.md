**TL;DR**
Bayer CEO Bill Anderson is dismantling corporate bureaucracy by flattening 11 layers of management into six, replacing annual budgeting with 90-day agile cycles, and shifting the role of leadership from "command and control" to a "span of coaching."

## Radical Organizational Flattening
* **Structural De-layering:** Anderson reduced Bayer’s management layers from 12 to approximately six or seven to eliminate "decision gatekeepers" who are too far removed from customers and products.
* **Span of Coaching:** The company increased the average number of direct reports per manager from 6.5 to 14, with some managers overseeing as many as 90 people.
    * **Eliminating Micromanagement:** High spans of control make traditional one-on-ones impossible, forcing managers to stop directing daily activities and start "greasing the skids" to remove bottlenecks.
* **Bureaucracy as a System:** Anderson argues that bureaucracy is not a "virus" that infects a healthy company, but a natural byproduct of functional org charts and excessive layers.

## Dynamic Resource Allocation & 90-Day Cycles
* **Ending Annual Budgets:** Bayer moved away from "money traps"—thousands of tiny cost centers locked in yearly. They now operate on 90-day cycles for the entire 100,000-person organization.
* **The Two-Tier Model:** 
    * **Tier 1:** High-level annual agreements with investors/VCs regarding total spend and major milestones.
    * **Tier 2 (Dynamic Resource Flow):** Fluid resource pools where teams re-evaluate priorities every quarter. 10-15% of the organization may move to different teams every 90 days based on project needs.
* **Outcome-Based Rituals:** Every 90 days, teams perform a "retro" to evaluate impact and set new outcomes (customer-centric goals) rather than outputs (internal metrics).

## Rethinking Performance and People
* **Peer-Led Feedback:** Anderson implemented a system where peers, rather than managers, provide performance ratings (0 for needs improvement, 1 for great, 2 for off-the-charts).
    * **Human Psychology:** Most employees believe peers have a more objective view of their impact than a distant manager.
* **Decoupling Feedback from Pay:** To prevent "weaponized" ratings or collusion, peer feedback is not used as a direct multiplier for bonuses. Managers use the feedback as one data point among several to make compensation decisions.
* **The Missionary Concept:** Anderson rejects the idea that only early startup hires are "missionaries." He believes any employee can have missionary zeal if the organization removes the bureaucratic friction that forces them to "fight city hall."

## Scaling Advice for Growth-Stage Founders
* **Avoid "Professional Managers":** When scaling from 100 to 1,000 employees, Anderson warns against hiring managers from big corporations who are trained to implement bureaucratic "best practices."
* **Delaying Layers:** Founders should push off adding middle-management layers (like Directors) for as long as possible and maintain at least a 1:10 manager-to-report ratio.
* **The "Caretaker" Danger:** Whether an internal or external hire, a CEO must avoid "maintenance mode." An organization is either changing or dying; new leaders should be aggressive in rebuilding culture right out of the gate.
* **Compensation Strategy:** Decouple pay from the org chart. Top individual contributors (scientists or developers) should be able to earn more than Vice Presidents to avoid forcing talent into management roles they aren't suited for.
