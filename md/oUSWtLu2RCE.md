**TL;DR**
The internet economy is transitioning from seat-based SaaS and social networks to AI-mediated "agentic commerce," where the primary risks are a looming 2029 hardware bottleneck and a failure by Western regulators to recognize that algorithmic control, not data privacy, is the true center of geopolitical power.

## AI Advertising and the Shift to Agentic Commerce
* **OpenAI’s Advertising Misstep:** Ben Thompson argues that OpenAI’s current approach to ads (context-based banner ads) is the "easiest solution" but structurally flawed.
    * **The Meta Model:** AI apps should instead build "Meta-style" user profiles to show relevant ads regardless of the specific prompt, avoiding conflicts of interest where the AI’s answer is influenced by the ad.
    * **Google’s Advantage:** Google can use AI (Gemini) to refine user profiles to sell ads on other properties (YouTube/Search) without ever needing to put an ad inside the AI interface itself.
* **The Three Levels of Agentic Commerce:**
    * **Level 1 (UI Replacement):** Agents simply fill out web forms and handle checkouts on behalf of the user.
    * **Level 2 (Better Discovery):** Replacing keyword search with parameter-based search (e.g., "Find a jacket for a specific temperature/style").
    * **Level 3 (Anticipatory Needs):** Using a persistent user profile to predict needs before the user even searches—effectively turning advertising into a personal agent.

## The Looming 2029 Semiconductor Crisis
* **TSMC’s Risk-Aversion:** Thompson predicts a massive global chip shortage by 2029. 
    * **The Capacity Trap:** 99.9% of a "fab" cost is depreciation. TSMC is behaving rationally by not overbuilding, as excess capacity crashes prices for years. 
    * **Market Failure:** Despite the AI boom, TSMC actually decreased CapEx for two years. They are shifting the risk of foregone revenue onto hyperscalers (Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft).
* **The "Insurance" Problem:** Hyperscalers need to treat Intel and Samsung as "geopolitical insurance." Even if they are sub-optimal compared to TSMC today, failing to build a credible alternative now ensures a supply crunch that will stall AI expansion by the end of the decade.

## The "End of SaaS" and Media Sovereignty
* **SaaS "Canceled" by Public Markets:** Thompson argues SaaS isn't going to zero, but it is receiving a "valuation haircut."
    * **The Seat-Based Trap:** AI reduces headcount or increases efficiency, making the "per-seat" pricing model a liability.
    * **EPS over Revenue:** SaaS companies are being forced to transition from "growth-at-all-costs" (valued on revenue) to stable businesses (valued on Earnings Per Share).
* **The 1,000 True Fans Model:** Success in the newsletter/content era requires "artisan" focus and extreme cost management. 
    * **Audio vs. Text:** More than half of Stratechery subscribers use the audio feed, but Thompson notes audio is "bad for business growth" because, unlike text links, audio content is rarely shared.
    * **Community Value:** In an age of infinite AI content, "common experiences" (shared reading or live events) become more valuable because they cannot be individualized by an algorithm.

## Geopolitical Friction: Taiwan and TikTok
* **Taiwan as a Tech Hub:** Thompson characterizes Taipei as the most "convenient" place to live globally, driven by a Japanese-influenced "mixed-use" layout and a world-class Uber Eats market.
* **The TikTok Failure:** Thompson views the US government's handling of TikTok as a disaster.
    * **Algorithm vs. Data:** Regulators focused on "user data" (which is largely anodyne ad-targeting info) while ignoring the "algorithm."
    * **The Result:** The US violated property rights but failed to gain control over the recommendation engine, which remains under ByteDance’s control—allowing a geopolitical adversary to maintain a "thumb on the scale" of US information consumption.

## Executive Ratings of the "Big Five"
* **Meta (Highest Rating):** Currently the best-executed company in tech. Mark Zuckerberg’s "tight ship" and the resilience of the ad model are underrated.
* **Apple:** Remains the master of hardware (the "iPhone Air" is a standout), but is a "terrible steward" of its own platforms and has let Siri decay into a liability.
* **Microsoft:** Excellent at "platform stewardship" but unable to make "best-of-breed" products. They win because the average business wants "mediocre stuff that works together" rather than a suite of disconnected premium tools.
* **Google:** Characterized as "amorphous slime." They do almost everything sub-optimally, yet their core business is so cash-rich that their lack of optimization creates a hidden "resiliency" that eventually smothers competitors.
* **Amazon:** Historically dominated through a lower cost structure in a commodity market. The risk is that their "Trainium" chip strategy assumes a level of market stability that may not exist during the rapid AI innovation cycle.
