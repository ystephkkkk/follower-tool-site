## TL;DR
Zipline has reached a $21.5 billion valuation following a $1.5 billion funding round (bringing total funding to over $3 billion) as it scales autonomous drone delivery across the US. Anchored by massive new partnerships with Uber Eats (targeting 1 million deliveries per day) and Walmart (expanding to 300 Supercenters), CEO Keller Rinaudo Cliffton explains how Zipline is tearing up its operating plan to scale 3x to 5x faster over the next two years while navigating the hard realities of hardware manufacturing.

## Explosive Growth, Valuation, and Landmark Partnerships
* **Valuation & Funding:** Host Molly O'Shea noted that Zipline has tripled or quadrupled its valuation since last year to $21.5 billion, raising a $1.5 billion round and surpassing $3 billion in total funding.
* **Uber Eats Partnership:** Announced the day of the interview, Zipline is partnering with Uber Eats to scale to 1 million deliveries per day—roughly 25% of Uber Eats' total US deliveries.
  * The contract will take Zipline to almost every US metro, putting ~100 million homes within 10-minute instant delivery of 1.2 million merchants.
* **Walmart National Expansion:** Announced the prior week, Zipline is expanding its multi-year Walmart partnership from 22 Supercenters to 300 Supercenters in phase one, with further scaling based on performance.
  * Customers gain autonomous delivery access to 90,000 of the 100,000 SKUs in a Supercenter within minutes.
* **Rewriting the Operating Plan:** Zipline originally built its multi-year operating plan to reach 1 million deliveries per day; it now has 3+ strategic partners each trying to buy more than 1 million deliveries per day.
  * The company tore up its 3-year plan to figure out how to scale 3x to 5x faster over the next two years.

## Scaling Operations and "Approximating Teleportation"
* **Rapid Metro & Factory Expansion:** Zipline is growing 10% to 15% week-over-week, and its factory has quadrupled in size.
  * **Metro Rollout:** Zipline is at massive scale in Dallas (operating ~25 charging sites that continue to grow week-over-week), launched Houston a month ago, and just launched Austin.
* **Delivery Speed & Precision:** Average delivery time once food is prepared is 2 to 5 minutes.
  * **Approximating Teleportation:** Beyond speed, the system is quiet and innocuous, delivering to any GPS coordinate—including backyards, Little League practices, mountain peaks, and a 7th-floor college dorm pool party.
* **Fleet Command:** Formerly called the Remote Operating Command Center (ROCC), Fleet Command displays live streams of charging sites and a sky map of active Zips.
  * Visited by figures like Secretary Pete Buttigieg, it proves to skeptics that autonomous delivery is operating at scale—though Cliffton noted humanity is still only seeing the first 0.01% of this infrastructure layer.
  * O'Shea and Cliffton discussed making Fleet Command available as a public live cam on Zipline's website.

## Global Competition: US vs. Chinese Tech Stacks
* **A Decade Outside the US:** Founded in 2013, Zipline spent its first 10 years operating in obscurity outside the US (starting in Rwanda) because flying beyond visual line of sight autonomously was illegal in the US.
  * This forced Zipline to log hundreds of millions of commercial autonomous miles and build reliable hardware, maintenance, and fleet command before scaling domestically two years ago.
* **Trade, Not Aid:** Cliffton argued that developing economies (such as in Africa) are tired of low-quality, free NGO services and want high-paying jobs, foreign direct investment, robotics, and entrepreneurship to leapfrog into the future.
* **The Geopolitical Tech Race:** Developing nations—where the vast majority of the world's population lives—are choosing whether to build their AI and robotics infrastructure on a US tech stack or a Chinese tech stack, representing a massive, under-discussed global competition.

## The Hardware Reality Check and Vertical Integration
* **Full-Stack Advantage:** Unlike legacy systems that patch together software from 20 different vendors (e.g., air traffic control towers), Zipline is vertically integrated like SpaceX, making it easier to secure, innovate, and fix hardware bottlenecks.
* **The VC Pivot to Capex:** For 12 to 13 years, Capex was a "four-letter word" in venture capital as investors chased SaaS and social media. Now, VCs believe AI will "one-shot" SaaS companies, making hardware and infrastructure the only defensible moats.
  * O'Shea noted that the era of fast, cheap capital for high-margin software is resetting, forcing companies that want to be sovereign through-and-through to carefully weigh Capex and what to build in-house.
* **Hardware Takes Time:** Cliffton warned that investors underestimate the timelines for scaling factories, supplier industrialization engineering, end-of-line testing, permitting, and regulation.
  * He pointed to Tesla (15 years to meaningful scale), SpaceX (20 years), and Zipline (over 10 years), noting that even the best-funded AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic are discovering how hard it is to build physical data centers.
* **Culture:** Despite its valuation, Cliffton rejected the "Power Law winner" label, describing Zipline as "scrappy," "frugal," "paranoid," and operating like "special forces" against giant competitors investing billions.
