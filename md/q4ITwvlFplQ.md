## TL;DR
Runway co-founder and CEO Cristóbal Valenzuela explains how scaling next-frame video prediction teaches models real-world physics, enabling interactive world interface models, robotics, and early operating system prototypes like Continuum where any screen content becomes a live simulation.

## Evolution from Pixel Generation to State-of-the-Art Video
* **Prehistoric AI Roots:** Runway's first AI film in 2019, "The Orange," generated pixels at a 128x128 resolution before being forcibly upsampled.
* **Current Generation Quality:** Modern video models generate characters and locations that successfully pass the Turing test, with human evaluators unable to distinguish generated footage from real video.
* **Art and Science Intersection:** The company maintains deep roots in artistic and creative communities alongside a research lab of top scientists specializing in diffusion models and large video models.

## Physics and Simulation via Next-Frame Prediction
* **Cinema as Optical Illusion:** Video functions as an optical illusion created by stitching frames fast enough to produce motion pictures.
* **Emergent World Physics:** Scaling next-frame prediction causes models to inherently learn complex real-world mechanics without explicit programming:
  * **Core Physical Laws:** Models learn gravity, occlusion, and movement.
  * **Object Interactions:** Models simulate how objects interact, such as a camel standing on a ball or water moving inside a container.
* **Simulation Systems without Rules:** By scaling next-frame prediction in 3D environments, models function like physics engines and game systems—such as generating non-GTA interactive virtual worlds—without explicit descriptions of physical laws.

## Real-Time Interaction and World Interface Models
* **From Async to Real-Time:** While traditional generation takes seconds or minutes, the next 3 to 4 years of video models focus on real-time interactivity where users can act at any point during frame prediction.
* **Robotics Integration:** Runway has spun up an entire robotics lab in its New York office to use these video models as the brains for autonomous, embodied systems.
* **World Interface Models:** Images can be transformed into interactive applications (e.g., turning on car lights, opening a hood, or moving objects in a room) without any underlying 3D representation like Unity, Unreal, or Blender.

## Continuum: Video Models as Operating Systems
* **Early Research Prototype:** Continuum integrates video models directly into daily computing, streaming operating system content straight from a video model.
* **Portals and Memory:** Users can select any text on a website and instantly spawn an interactive real-time video "portal" based on the content, while a desktop memory tracks past visualizations.
* **Reactive Spatial Scaling:** Resizing an application window dynamically changes the behavior and visual experience—such as navigating cellular structures during a lesson on photosynthesis.
* **Visual Thinking for Coding Agents:** Connecting Continuum to a coding agent allows the agent to visually think through problems (like generating a 3D model of a bicycle) rather than relying solely on code.
* **Live Reactive Experiences:** Searching for topics like Mars prompts the system to generate a real-time, interactive Mars Outpost interface based on actual and projected data.
