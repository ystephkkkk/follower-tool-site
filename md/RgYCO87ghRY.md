## Overcoming European Cultural Barriers to Ambition
- **Cultural Skepticism:** In Europe, pitching a big idea often invites laughter or criticism, whereas the default reaction in the US is encouragement.
- **Failure Tolerance:** Unlike ecosystems where failure is valued as a learning experience that makes a candidate more hirable, European cultures often attach a social penalty to failure.
- **The Talent Confidence Gap:** 
  - **French Engineer Example:** Offered a role, declined out of fear that the team was too strong and they would fail.
  - **American Engineer Example:** Overconfident, accepted the offer, and successfully negotiated for compensation two levels above the baseline.

## Tactical Frameworks for Aiming Higher
- **The Magic Wand Exercise:** List current options and career levels, then use a thought exercise to determine the level just above, the level 10X above, and the level of a universe king with zero constraints.
- **Add a Zero:** Implement Sam Altman’s advice to add a zero to any success metric or target to force creative problem-solving around how to reach it.
- **Global Mindset Filtering:** Enforce English as the primary internal language (even between French co-founders) in the Paris office as a deliberate filter to hire talent committed to building a global company.
- **Depth as Ambition:** Focus aggressively to compound growth—Photoroom grew 10X by narrowing from video/photo to photo, and another 10X by focusing specifically on e-commerce photo.
- **V-Zero Prototyping:** Cut project scopes down to the bare minimum (v-zero) to test and learn in days rather than spending three months building the wrong thing.
