## Monetary Policy Reforms: Inflation, the Balance Sheet, and AI
*   **Reducing the $7 Trillion Balance Sheet:** Warsh advocated for a fundamental "regime change" at the Federal Reserve, criticizing its massive quantitative easing (QE) footprint.
    *   He characterized a large balance sheet as "fiscal policy in disguise" that disproportionately benefits the wealthy who own financial assets. 
    *   He proposed a gradual, well-choreographed reduction of the balance sheet to refocus monetary policy primarily on the interest rate tool, which he argued "gets in the cracks" and is fairer to the broader economy.
*   **Critique of Past Inflation Frameworks:** Warsh labeled the Fed’s post-COVID monetary policy in 2021 and 2022 as "the biggest economic policy error in 40 or 50 years."
    *   He proposed abandoning the practice of "forward guidance" (such as "dot plots"), arguing it causes the Fed to cling to incorrect forecasts for too long.
    *   To better measure true cost-of-living increases, Warsh proposed launching a project with the Bureau of Labor Statistics to survey "a billion prices" rather than relying on flawed, lagging CPI and PCE metrics.
*   **The Disinflationary Potential of AI:** Warsh described the artificial intelligence boom as "the most disruptive moment in modern economic history," arguing it could create a massive supply-side productivity surge that structurally lowers inflation.
    *   Democratic Senators Blunt Rochester and Smith voiced skepticism, warning that the Fed should not "make a bet" on unproven AI productivity gains to justify cutting rates prematurely while families continue to struggle with high costs.

## Debating Warsh’s Historical Record
*   **Criticism of the 2008 Financial Crisis Response:** Democratic critics attacked Warsh's tenure as a Fed Governor from 2006 to 2011, accusing him of protecting Wall Street while ignoring middle-class families.
    *   Senator Cortez Masto noted that in 2006 and 2007, housing advocates explicitly warned Fed governors about predatory subprime lending practices, yet Warsh publicly stated that subprime mortgages had received an unfair "bad name" and saw "no immediate systemic risk."
    *   Senators Warren and Reed criticized Warsh for pushing multi-billion-dollar bank bailouts during the crash, only to later pivot to a hawkish stance that advocated keeping interest rates high while millions of Americans remained unemployed.
*   **Endorsements of Crisis Leadership:** Senator Dave McCormick, who worked closely with Warsh during the 2008 crisis as Undersecretary of the Treasury, strongly defended his record.
    *   McCormick praised Warsh's "moral clarity" and intellect during the market meltdown, noting that former Fed Chairman Ben Bernanke relied heavily on him to navigate the crisis.
    *   Warsh defended his past actions, stating he had spent years warning about the systemic dangers of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and argued that his views have always evolved naturally as the economic facts on the ground changed.
