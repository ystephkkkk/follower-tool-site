## TL;DR
AI infrastructure has transitioned from a focus on single workloads to an era of heterogeneous compute, where sovereign AI, digital autonomy, and open ecosystems are critical for driving national security, scientific productivity, and economic competitiveness.

## Defining Sovereign AI & Digital Autonomy
*   **Comprehensive Lifecycle Control:** Trish Damkoeger (HPE) noted that sovereign AI encompasses end-to-end infrastructure, data, compliance, security, and trustworthy outputs.
*   **European Digital Autonomy:** Stéphane Arquena (GENCI) emphasized that sovereignty is not isolationism, but "digital autonomy"—mastering access, open-source models, culture representation, and talent.
*   **Global Collaboration:** Bruno Loquent (Bull) stated that sovereignty means being open to collaboration and integrating the best technologies globally rather than trying to build everything alone.
*   **Mission Alignment:** Arjun Shankar (Oak Ridge National Laboratory) defined sovereignty as principled data management aligned with national missions and global partnerships.

## Transitioning from Modeling & Simulation to AI Factories
*   **French National Strategy:** Stéphane Arquena noted that France's AI strategy (backed by €2.5 billion) scaled projects from 50 initially to over 2,000, with 60% now focused on AI for science via the Alice Recoque exascale system and European AI factories.
*   **DOE Scientific Acceleration:** Arjun Shankar highlighted that Oak Ridge has utilized accelerated computing since 2012, with AI now embedded naturally into scientific campaigns and the Genesis Mission to double scientific productivity.
*   **Real-World Convergence:** Bruno Loquent and Trish Damkoeger discussed how traditional HPC modeling and simulation (mod-sim) is converging with AI, citing examples like Lawrence Livermore's National Ignition Facility using AI models to optimize fusion energy pellets.

## Procurement, Benchmarks, and the Outcome-Focused Shift
*   **Moving Beyond LINPACK:** Panelists agreed that LINPACK is only useful for initial stress-testing for one week of acceptance, while real-world production relies on application-specific benchmarks.
*   **Investing in People:** Bruno Loquent highlighted a positive shift in EuroHPC procurements where budgets are explicitly allocated to Centers of Excellence and application specialists rather than just raw hardware.
*   **Supply Chain Realities:** Trish Damkoeger pointed out that procurement challenges—such as rising memory prices and scarcity—require continuous customer education to align expectations with reality.

## Open Ecosystems and Hardware Heterogeneity
*   **Future is Heterogeneous:** The panel agreed that future systems will combine CPUs, specialized accelerators, and quantum computing to handle diverse workloads like training, inference, agentic AI, and physical AI.
*   **The Software Challenge:** Bruno Loquent emphasized that the primary technological hurdle ahead is not hardware, but developing intelligent software stacks (moving beyond SLURM to Kubernetes and beyond) to orchestrate hybrid workloads seamlessly.
*   **The Open Source Imperative:** Arjun Shankar praised AMD's commitment to open ecosystems, drawing parallels to the historical success of Linux, GNU, and Apache in lifting the entire computing community.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
*   **Fault-Tolerant Quantum:** Arjun Shankar claimed that fault-tolerant quantum computing is "right around the corner" and that within the next 2 to 3 years, Oak Ridge will see initial quantum systems that are scientifically relevant.
*   **AI Co-Scientists:** Stéphane Arquena stated that agentic AI (specifically AI co-scientists driven by missions like Genesis) will completely change how research is conducted and automate large portions of the scientific process by 2030.
*   **Heterogeneous Rule:** Trish Damkoeger predicted that by 2030, heterogeneity will rule, with systems featuring a mix of CPUs, accelerated compute of various types, and potentially fault-tolerant quantum components.
