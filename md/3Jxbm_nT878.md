**TL;DR:** The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by a quarter-point to 3.75–4% to combat persistently high inflation, marking the first rate increase since 2023. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized a commitment to price stability and a rejection of short-term "data point dependence," while pointing to broader economic strength.

## Interest Rate Decision & Policy Action
* **Benchmark Rate Increase:** FOMC voted unanimously to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to **3-3/4 to 4 percent** to support the 2 percent inflation goal.
* **Removal of Accommodation:** Chair Kevin Warsh noted that broad financial conditions are not restrictive and credit flows remain robust, prompting the committee to "remove a dose of accommodation."
* **Neutral Rate Stance:** Dismissed the operational relevance of a short- or long-term neutral rate (Wicksellian rate), viewing it purely as an academic concept rather than a tool for immediate decision-making.
