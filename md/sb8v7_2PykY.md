**TL;DR:** While the US treats AI as a race toward frontier models and potential doomsday scenarios, China is treating it as a "full-stack" economic diffusion play—resulting in 83% public optimism, a booming "vibecoding" hub outside Hangzhou, and a massive, state-backed overcapacity of AI-generated "slop" and predatory AI education schemes.

## Divergent AI Races: Diffusion vs. Frontier
*   **Full-Stack vs. Frontier:** Afra Wang argues the US and China are running entirely different AI races. The US is focused on frontier models and AGI risks, while China is running a "full-stack" race encompassing electricity generation, supply chains, and broad economic diffusion.
*   **The "AI Plus" Mandate:** Joe Weisenthal and Tracy Alloway note that Beijing has explicitly directed the economy to embrace AI. Modeled after the earlier "Internet Plus" initiative, Xi Jinping’s "AI Plus" plan frames AI as a "high-quality productivity force" meant to upgrade traditional sectors like manufacturing, agriculture, and education.
*   **The DeepSeek Turning Point:** Before DeepSeek, China’s tech sector was experiencing a post-COVID "ego death" amid deflation and US ChatGPT dominance. DeepSeek R1—pre-trained on native Chinese text capable of effortlessly quoting Tang Dynasty poetry—was hailed as a sign of national destiny (*guoyun*) and sparked widespread domestic enthusiasm.
*   **Why Chinese Citizens Embrace AI (83% vs. 38%):** Polling shows 83% of Chinese respondents believe AI will benefit their lives and work, compared to just 38% in the US. Wang attributes this to two factors:
    *   **Choicelessness:** There is no organized public resistance to tech adoption; AI is viewed as an inevitable, Darwinian trend.
    *   **Tangible Progress:** Previous tech cycles visibly improved daily life. Wang cites her 81-year-old grandmother in rural Shandong, who once walked five hours to buy a clock in the 1980s and now uses a Xiaomi smartphone, voice-controlled smart bulbs, and China’s top chatbot, Doubao.

## Liangzhu: China’s "Vibecoding Capital"
*   **Escaping the 996 Grind:** Located 20–30 minutes outside Hangzhou (home to DeepSeek, Alibaba, and Unitree), the low-rise, scenic town of Liangzhu offers cheap rent and a relaxed lifestyle. It has become a magnet for laid-off big tech workers fleeing Beijing and Shanghai, as well as recent graduates opting out of traditional corporate jobs.
*   **One-Person Companies (OPCs):** Rather than building massive "super apps" like WeChat or Alipay, independent "vibecoders" in Liangzhu build whimsical, niche applications for ADHD, dyslexia, mental well-being, and productivity.
*   **Tech Meets Ancient Tradition:** Post-COVID economic stagnation and a burst real estate bubble have driven young Chinese toward astrology and *Bazi* (Four Pillars of Destiny) for certainty. 
    *   Out of 5 to 10 astrology/fortune-telling apps in Liangzhu, **FateTell**—founded by a former aspiring Daoist monk—is the only profitable one. Its corporate culture is so rooted in *Bazi* that employees undergo astrological compatibility checks before being hired.

## Economic Anxiety & The AI Education MLM
*   **A Low-Trust, Zero-Sum Society:** Wang describes post-COVID China as a low-trust society operating under a "Dark Forest" (zero-sum) mentality, resembling Japan’s stalled economy. Incomes have stagnated over the past decade, real estate and stock investments have burned households, 80%–85% of high-speed rail lines lose money, and official youth unemployment sits at 18.9% (a figure Wang doubts).
*   **Monetizing Hope:** Because few vibecoders actually make money from their apps, many pivot to selling AI courses. Wang investigated **The Art of Getting Rich**, one of China’s largest AI education firms, located on the 26th floor of a building near Alibaba’s Hangzhou headquarters.
    *   **MLM Structure:** Decorated in dollar-bill green, the company has **80,000 paying members** who pay **3,000–4,000 RMB** (roughly half an average Hangzhou monthly salary) for classes like "Use AI to Make Dollars" and "DeepSeek 101." Members are incentivized to recruit others and collect "Dragon Balls" to level up from "Voyager" to the exclusive "DeepSeek Circle."
    *   **Customer Base:** Its members fall into three buckets: "proper underdogs" (grads without elite degrees), small manufacturers (using AI for supply chains and Amazon listings), and solo vibecoders seeking community.

## The "Industrial Slop Complex" & Overcapacity
*   **Top-Down Overcapacity:** Alloway draws parallels between China's AI push and its historical boom-bust cycles in private tutoring (cracked down on in 2021–2022), pork farming, and EVs. When Beijing signals a strategic priority, local governments flood the zone with subsidies, leading to ruinous price wars.
*   **AIGC Microdramas:** To prove to Beijing that they are embracing AI, risk-averse local governments in non-tech cities like Qingdao, Wuhan, Zhengzhou, and Beijing’s Mentougou district (dubbed "the Staten Island of Beijing") are heavily subsidizing AI-generated content (AIGC) because it has the lowest barrier to entry.
*   **Ghost Cities of Content:** The AIGC microdrama market is governed by an extreme power law: only **0.16%** of content becomes a hit, and **90% of AIGC companies are losing money**, leaving behind digital "ghost cities" of unwatched AI slop.
*   **Why China Struggles to Export Culture:** Unlike Japan (J-pop) or South Korea (K-pop), China’s creative exports are flattened by strict censorship—which Wang compares to "walking in a dark room where there are ten pieces of furniture you cannot touch"—and a decades-long strategic choice to prioritize heavy industry over Hollywood-style soft power.
