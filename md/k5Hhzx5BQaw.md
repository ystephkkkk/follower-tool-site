**TL;DR**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent outlines a "maximum pressure" campaign against Iran via "Operation Economic Fury" while projecting resilient U.S. GDP growth of 3% to 3.5% and prioritizing strategic "de-risking" over "decoupling" in the U.S.-China trade relationship.

## Operation Economic Fury and Global Sanctions
*   **Maximum Pressure on Iran:** The administration has launched a concentrated effort to cut off Iranian supply lines and assets. This follows a campaign that contributed to the collapse of a major Iranian bank in December and high domestic inflation.
    *   **Strategic Partnerships:** The U.S. is collaborating with Gulf allies (UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia) to share bank account data of Iranian regime members.
    *   **Secondary Sanctions:** Treasury is warning international banks, specifically in China, that purchasing Iranian goods or using dollars for such transactions will trigger sanctions.
*   **The Petrodollar vs. RMB:** Bessent dismisses the immediate threat of China or Iran replacing the dollar with the Renminbi (RMB) or crypto.
    *   **RMB Stability Issues:** He identifies "three equilibria" preventing the RMB from becoming a reserve currency: academic undervalued status, massive internal pressure for capital flight, and the risk of government confiscation.

## U.S. Economic Outlook and Fiscal Policy
*   **Growth Projections:** Despite the ongoing war, Bessent expects the U.S. economy to grow by 3% to 3.5% this year. He notes that core inflation is trending down, though energy prices remain a volatile factor.
*   **Investment and CAPEX:** Business capital expenditure (CAPEX) and construction jobs are beginning to pick up following a "decision cycle" lag.
    *   **Tariff Certainty:** The administration plans to use Section 301 to restore previous tariff levels by July, providing legal certainty for corporations that had paused investments due to litigation.
*   **Capital Gains Indexing:** When asked about indexing capital gains for inflation to boost the stock market, Bessent stated, "Not now," noting that the market is performing well and the focus remains on "Main Street" households, 38% of whom do not own stocks.

## Federal Reserve Transition and Monetary Reform
*   **Leadership Uncertainty:** With Kevin Warsh’s confirmation hearing pending and Chair Jay Powell’s term nearing expiration, Bessent remained neutral on who should lead the Fed if a confirmation delay occurs, though he noted the President has a clear preference.
*   **Balance Sheet Reduction:** Bessent supports a "long process" to shrink the Fed's balance sheet and suggests moving away from the "plentiful reserves" regime.
    *   **The British Model:** He expressed admiration for the Bank of England’s approach—stepping in during market dislocations to stabilize and then quickly exiting.
    *   **Regulatory Changes:** He indicated that shifting the Fed’s balance sheet strategy could likely be achieved through agency rulemaking rather than new legislation from Congress.

## Strategic Competition with China and AI
*   **De-risking vs. Decoupling:** The administration seeks to "de-risk" critical supply chains—semiconductors, shipbuilding, rare earths, and medicine—rather than a full economic decoupling.
    *   **Trade Surplus Concerns:** Bessent highlighted the "excessive" trillion-dollar trade surplus in China as a global imbalance that must be addressed.
*   **The AI Race:** Bessent strongly disagrees that the U.S. has lost its lead to China in Artificial Intelligence.
    *   **Compute Dominance:** He projects the U.S. will soon hold 70% to 80% of global computing power, moving up from current levels in the 50s.
    *   **Workforce Challenges:** While acknowledging the need for technical talent, Bessent criticized "open borders" for creating a backlash against legal high-value immigration (H-1B reform). He argues that AI will not cause a net job loss, as innovation historically creates more employment.
