## TL;DR
Flock CEO Garret Langley addresses privacy backlash, police data abuse, and safety technology, emphasizing that local communities must democratically control data retention windows (recently reduced to a 7-day default) and adopt mandatory audit tools to catch rogue law enforcement officers.

## Company Footprint & Core Product Architecture
* **Founding & Mission:** Started 9 years ago by Garret Langley with an initial $8 neighborhood pitch to live in a safer community; now operating in roughly 6,000+ cities across the U.S.
* **Core Technology Function:** 
  * License plate reading (ALPR) cameras capture still images of vehicles and read license plates.
  * System can detect vehicle features such as roof racks, bumper stickers, dents, and damage.
  * **Explicit limitations:** Does not do facial recognition, does not capture video, does not look inside cars, and does not allow searching for individuals via license plate cameras.
* **Impact Metrics (Annual/Monthly):**
  * North of 1 million crimes solved using Flock last year.
  * North of 10,000 missing people found (e.g., grandparents with dementia, children).
  * July data points: ~1,125 missing people and over 20,000 stolen cars recovered.

## Data Retention, Privacy Limits, and Democratic Choice
* **Data Retention Policy:** 
  * Default retention has recently been shifted down from 30 days to 7 days.
  * Flock estimates **90% of crimes** get solved within 7 days of data retention.
  * Retention limits vary by jurisdiction: New Jersey state law mandates 5 years; Washington state and California have set maximum limits; New Hampshire enforces a 3-minute limit; New York state uses 21 days.
* **Democratic Control:** City councils, mayors, and democratically elected state lawmakers hold final decision-making power regarding data retention periods and data sharing policies.
* **Vandalism & Activist Backlash:** 
  * Vandalizing cameras is a felony; arrests have occurred with suspects facing 15 to 20 years in jail.
  * Roughly 60 to 70 cities (out of 6,000, or about 1%) have deactivated or removed cameras, often driven by misinformation regarding facial recognition or fears of a permanent surveillance state.
  * Close to 20 cities have already turned their cameras back on after experiencing a rise in unsolved violent crimes.

## Accountability, Police Abuse, and Audit Tools
* **The "Dirty Cop" Problem:** Acknowledges public concern regarding police abuse of data bases (referencing state databases like California reporting 7,000 reported abuses last year).
* **Audit Assistance Tool:** Introduced 4 months prior to the interview as a mandatory feature to detect abnormal search patterns (e.g., an officer repeatedly searching a specific target plate without adding it to a shared hot list).
* **Enforcement Outcomes:** The tool successfully exposed and helped fire 9 Georgia police officers who abused the system.
* **Future Safeguards:** Discussing potential multi-key authorization systems (requiring supervisor approval) for larger departments, weighed against the administrative burden for smaller 20-person police departments.

## Emerging Technologies: Drones & AI
* **Drone Business Growth:** Drones represent Flock's fastest-growing business unit; the LPR business accounts for just under 50% of forward-looking revenue.
* **How Drones Operate:** 
  * Housed in HVAC control boxes/docks on rooftops (police precincts or fire departments).
  * Deployed during 911 calls to arrive on scene ahead of officers (e.g., flying at 400 feet at 60 mph within 1 minute using 40x optical zoom).
  * Example given: Verified a suspect at a gas station was holding a lighter, not a gun, preventing a potentially fatal police confrontation.
* **Artificial Intelligence Stance:** 
  * Flock intentionally moves slowly with AI features to avoid the risks of hallucination or defining suspicion/predictive policing (e.g., Minority Report-style precrime).
  * Insists on maintaining a "human in the loop" for public safety decisions.
