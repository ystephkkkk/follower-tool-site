## TL;DR
Pershing Square founder and CEO Bill Ackman discussed his strategy for Howard Hughes Corp., the capitalization of insurance venture Vantage, Pershing Square USA's market discount, AI frontier models, and a new brain-care institute inspired by his daughter's recovery.

## Howard Hughes & Vantage Insurance Strategy
* **Taking a Page from Buffett:** Pershing Square is replicating Berkshire Hathaway's playbook by utilizing Vantage—a specialty insurer and reinsurer closed in June—as a cash-flow-generating vehicle with low leverage.
* **Leadership & Asset Allocation:** Mark Risan (former CEO of Arch Capital Group) serves as executive chair of Vantage, alongside David Gansburg and Lucy Vetto. 
* **Treasury & Equity Split:** Fixed income assets were immediately sold upon closing; approximately 65% of Vantage assets are in short-term treasuries and 35% in a managed common stock portfolio, with common stock exposure ultimately targeted at 40% to 45%.
* **Howard Hughes Real Estate Restructuring:** 
  * Howard Hughes is shifting from 100% equity financing to bringing in third-party partners (such as Brookfield or Blackstone) to reduce equity commitment in real estate by up to 80%.
  * The restructuring aims to free up **$2 billion to $3 billion** in equity by the end of next year to redeploy into Vantage.

## Pershing Square USA (PSUS) & Market Dynamics
* **Fund Structure & Cost:** PSUS is a liquid public closed-end fund charging no incentive fee, offering retail investors access to Pershing Square's strategy at the lowest cost globally.
* **Performance & Deployment:** Net Asset Value (NAV) is down about 3% from the IPO (with ~240 basis points driven by IPO costs); capital has been fully deployed into "super durable growth companies."
* **Discount and Valuation Realities:** PSUS shares trade at a 25% discount to NAV; Ackman attributes this to a marketing and awareness challenge rather than underlying portfolio weakness, pointing to discounted entry prices on holdings like Meta, S&P Global (bought at 21x earnings vs. typical 30x), and Intercontinental Exchange (bought at 17x vs. 24x).

## AI, Frontier Models, and IPOs
* **Anthropic and OpenAI:** Ackman praised Anthropic's rapid revenue ramp and product quality (noting internal use of Claude), questioning whether frontier model companies can maintain market presence against open-source and open-weight models.
* **Capital Intensity vs. Durability:** Pershing Square avoids fast-growing, capital-consuming AI companies where investors bet on future line crossings, preferring predictable businesses like Microsoft, Visa, and Mastercard.
* **Catastrophic Risk & Regulation:** Ackman is less concerned about regulated labs like Anthropic or OpenAI causing systemic harm—arguing that massive financial and legal disincentives provide self-regulation—and more concerned about rogue actors exploiting open-source models without guardrails.

## Medical Innovation & New Brain Institute
* **Daughter Lucy’s Recovery:** Ackman detailed the severe brain hemorrhage suffered by his daughter Lucy, who lost the ability to speak, walk, and see light, but is making progress through aggressive care, nutrition, and experimental mitochondrial therapy (which restored light perception to 22,000 in one eye).
* **New Brain Institute:** Inspired by Lucy's journey, Ackman and his wife are building a new institute focused on delivering direct clinical care, leveraging data generated by emerging brain-interface technologies (such as Neuralink, Precision Neuroscience, and Synchron) combined with AI data-processing capabilities.
