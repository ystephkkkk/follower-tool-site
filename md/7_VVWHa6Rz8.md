## The AI Hype Cycle Hits Structural Realities

* **Anthropic's Government Shutdown:** The U.S. government issued an export control directive suspending foreign national access (including Anthropic's own employees) to Fable 5 and Mythos 5, effectively shutting down its most advanced models.
  * **The Catalyst:** The government acted after being alerted to a "jailbreak" security vulnerability. Eisman notes that Amazon allegedly reported the vulnerability to the government, potentially motivated by its competitive position and investment in OpenAI. 
  * **Strategic Impact:** This aggressive regulatory action by the Trump administration severely hampers Anthropic's growth and highlights the risks of tech companies clashing with federal authorities.
* **The Emerging Bear Case for AI:** Despite sustained CAPEX growth—led by Nvidia’s accelerating Q1 2026 revenue growth of 85%—Eisman outlines six major structural concerns slowing the AI sector:
  * **Extreme Capital Intensity:** Software is shifting from a high-margin, low-capital business to a capital-heavy hardware model. Google is raising $85 billion in equity (its first equity raise since 2005) to fund its projected 2026 AI CAPEX of $180 billion to $190 billion, as cash flows can no longer cover these costs alone. Meta and Microsoft are rumored to be planning similar capital-raising moves.
  * **Absence of Moats:** Hyperscalers are projected to spend nearly $1 trillion on AI CAPEX this year, yet their models (Gemini, Anthropic, OpenAI) remain highly commoditized with little sustained differentiation.
  * **Deflationary Pricing:** OpenAI is reportedly considering lowering token prices, signaling margin erosion in a commodity market despite trillions in sector spend.
  * **Token Pricing Friction:** Tech firms are transitioning from cheap subscription models to usage-based token pricing to recoup costs. This is causing severe budget overruns; Uber reportedly exhausted its entire annual AI budget in just four months.
  * **Lack of ROI:** Clear financial returns on AI investment remain largely unproven relative to the massive infrastructure spend.
  * **Power and Energy Constraints:** Massive data center construction is facing local pushback and energy supply limits, slowing projected rollouts.
* **The Airline Analogy:** Eisman compares hyperscalers to capital-intensive, low-pricing-power airlines, while position-dominant suppliers (like semiconductor and power companies, or tech hardware suppliers like Arista and Cisco) represent the highly profitable "TransDigm" of the AI trade.
