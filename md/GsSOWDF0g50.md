## TL;DR
U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer stated that recent trade talks with Canada collapsed because Canadian officials "wanted more" concessions despite the U.S. offering the world's most preferential market access. Greer emphasized that the U.S. is moving forward with targeted 5% tariffs, dismissing market concerns and political friction with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

## Background & Historical Trade Friction
* **Long-Standing Grievances:** Greer stated the U.S. initiated talks after Canada maintained restrictions for over a year, specifically noting:
  * **Wine & Spirits:** Canada prohibited the sale of American wine and spirits in their stores.
  * **Auto Limits:** Canada placed limits on the number of U.S. autos entering the country.
  * **Dairy Restrictions:** Long-standing restrictions on the sale of U.S. dairy into Canada.
* **The Auto Pact Context:** Greer explained that Canada’s auto industry originated from a 1960s agreement where Canada required companies to build locally if they wanted to sell into the Canadian market.
* **Current Tariffs:** The U.S. proposed tailored tariffs covering roughly **5%** of what Canada sends to the U.S., which amounts to **0.06%** of overall U.S. economic consumption.

## Breakdown of the U.S.-Canada Trade Negotiations
* **Canadian Outreach:** Greer noted the U.S. did not initially ask to negotiate, but agreed in good faith after Canadian officials approached them about a month prior.
* **Near-Agreement & Collapse:** Parties reached sufficient agreement by Tuesday night to announce a deal framework, but negotiations collapsed in the final hours when Canadian officials demanded broader concessions.
* **U.S. Concessions Offered:** To accommodate Canadian sensitivities, the U.S. offered:
  * **Steel and Aluminum:** Cutting tariffs in half.
  * **Automobiles & Lumber:** Extensively reducing tariffs on autos and softwood lumber.
* **The Sticking Point on Trucks:** Greer confirmed that negotiations stalled over expansions in scope, such as Canadian demands to ease tariffs on the heaviest medium- and heavy-duty trucks rather than just passenger cars, SUVs, and pickups.

## Internal Administration & Policy Clarifications
* **Relationship with Commerce:** Addressing reports of friction between his office and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Greer dismissed them as "garbage in, garbage out" from *Politico*. 
  * He stated he and Lutnick talk and work together every day under presidential direction.
  * He acknowledged it may be frustrating for Canadians that tariff authorities are split across two agencies (with Commerce holding authority over **Section 232**), but noted "that's how Congress set it up."
* **Trade Deficits & Oil:** Addressing the U.S. trade deficit with Canada, Greer noted that while oil imports skew the numbers, the U.S. wants natural resources and minerals, provided there is a balanced economic and security partnership.
* **Language and Content Disputes:** Greer debunked rumors regarding English-language mandates, clarifying:
  * The U.S. supports French language and cultural identity, noting he speaks French and has family ties to Paris.
  * The actual objection is to policies requiring American tech and streaming companies (like Netflix) to take earnings and pay a percentage (e.g., **5%**) to local content competitors, which the U.S. views as a discriminatory tax.

## Market Reaction & Broader Economic Indicators
* **Market Resilience:** Greer argued that markets are shrugging off the trade dispute because they understand it affects a very small percentage of trade.
* **Economic Trajectory:** Greer highlighted positive domestic indicators, pointing to falling inflation prints in June, flat recent trends, and rising real wages for manufacturing workers.
