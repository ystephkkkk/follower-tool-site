## TL;DR
Peter Steinberger shares how his side project OpenClaw exploded from a simple WhatsApp relay built out of personal annoyance into an open-source juggernaut with 4.7 million weekly downloads, and explains why developer velocity depends entirely on building things that genuinely excite you.

## Origins and Product-Market Fit
- **The Annoyance Catalyst:** 
  - Created in November 2025 because there was no easy way to send prompts from a phone to a computer to check on coding agents while away.
  - Built a WhatsApp relay in about an hour by letting the model cook in a terminal session.
- **The Evolution to OpenClaw:** 
  - Expanded to Discord after receiving a community pull request, temporarily naming the project Claudius, then Claudebot, before settling on OpenClaw.
  - Initially struggled to get Twitter followers to care about the agent's magical, proactive feel until testing it in WhatsApp group chats and witnessing strong emotional reactions (amazement and fear).
- **Viral Explosion:** 
  - Launched a Discord server during the first week of January 2026 for people to watch him build in the open.
  - A launch daemon ensured the agent kept running after pressing Ctrl+C, resulting in 800 messages upon waking up and immediate viral pickup by tech reporters.

## Scale, Burnout, and the Open Source Toll
- **Staggering Metrics:** 
  - Reached over 18,000 different people opening an issue or pull request, totaling over 111,000 contributions, with almost 3,000 people contributing commits.
  - Downloads bottomed out at 835,000 weekly downloads in May before peaking at an all-time high of 4.7 million weekly downloads.
- **The Cost of Attention:** 
  - The sudden influx of reporters, leaked phone numbers, and security reports nearly caused Steinberger to delete the entire project and severely strained his mental health.
  - Shifted time away from building toward legal work (setting up a 501(c)(3) American nonprofit) and defensive security hardening.
- **Security and Maintenance Pitfalls:** 
  - Fought off a wave of security reports, noting that only about 0.3% of scanned skills were actually malicious (out of 67,000 scanned).
  - Accumulated around 9,500 configuration options, making the codebase increasingly difficult to maintain and breaking user setups when shipping updates.
  - Faced sudden dependency disruptions when Anthropic gave roughly 24 hours' notice before disabling subscriptions for Opus, which OpenClaw had been heavily optimized around.

## Philosophy of Building
- **Personal Brand Over Products:** 
  - Emphasizes that any product can be forked or cloned, making a personal brand vital before starting a new venture.
  - Believes programming is just a means to an end; the real passion lies in building.
- **The Power of Fun:** 
  - Discovered that "fun is velocity"—weeks spent enjoying the build process resulted in a visibly better product, whereas maintenance work slowed momentum to a halt.
  - Advises building software that solves personal annoyances, noting that user #1 should be yourself and users #2 through #20 should be friends.
- **Current Team and Infrastructure:** 
  - The OpenClaw foundation now has 10 people on payroll and is actively hiring additional roles, including a CEO.
  - Nvidia stepped in early to provide personnel who took over much of the project's security workload.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Open Source Agent Capabilities:** Peter Steinberger claims that perpetually running, proactive AI agents are entirely possible today, but are currently bottlenecked by token costs and the lack of efficient systems to handle large context resets without burning excessive money on idle computations.
