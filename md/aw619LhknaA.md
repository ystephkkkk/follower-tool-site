## TL;DR
At the Federal Reserve's productivity and jobs conference, Chairman Kevin Warsh and the CEOs of Affirm, Stripe, and Palo Alto Networks reported that AI is driving unprecedented productivity gains—including a 2x surge in new business creation on Stripe and an 80% model performance jump at Affirm—while accelerating a widening economic gap between the US and Europe. However, the leaders warned of an asymmetric cybersecurity crisis over the next two to three years and raised concerns about incumbent market concentration and long-term workforce reskilling.

## Enterprise Adoption & Productivity Gains
* **Engineering Velocity at Affirm:** Max Levchin (CEO, Affirm) reported that AI has made Affirm’s engineers roughly 5x more productive, cutting the cost per feature to 1/5th of prior levels (with expectations to reach 1/10th).
  * Affirm used LLMs to design its latest transformer-based underwriting model, achieving an 80% performance increase over its non-LLM baseline—translating to roughly $2 billion in additional annual loan volume.
  * Levchin noted Affirm has had zero AI-related layoffs, instead redeploying human talent toward "mass customization" and long-tail product ideas that previously sat on the cutting room floor.
* **Autonomous Agent Loops:** Patrick Collison (CEO, Stripe) noted that within the last 12 months, AI has evolved from accelerating narrow code production to running longer-horizon autonomous loops that identify tasks, implement features, fix bugs, and deploy code end-to-end.
* **The Economics of Compute:** Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh noted that token prices are falling by approximately 50% per quarter at an accelerating rate, suggesting markets are rapidly driving down the cost of intelligence.
* **The "Edge Case" Bottleneck:** Nikesh Arora (CEO, Palo Alto Networks) cautioned that while AI excels at the 80% of repetitive, data-heavy tasks, it still struggles with the 20% "edge cases" requiring human judgment.
  * Arora cited Waymo (which he first rode in as a Google Lexus in 2009) as a cautionary tale: it took 17 years (to 2026) and over $30 billion to replace a single human driving job.

## The Startup Surge vs. Incumbent Inertia
* **Unprecedented Business Formation:** Collison revealed that new businesses launching on Stripe every week are up ~2x year-over-year—a rate without historical precedent in Stripe’s 15-year history, even exceeding the pandemic boom. The 2026 startup cohort is massively outperforming all prior years.
* **The Productivity Paradox:** Collison referenced economist Paul David’s 1989/1990 paper on electricity, noting that transformative technologies often benefit nimble new entrants faster than legacy incumbents burdened by governance, SOC 2 compliance, and human-supervision requirements.
* **Surplus Capture:** Collison argued that for the foreseeable future, the vast majority of economic surplus from AI will be captured by the *users* of the technology rather than just the frontier AI labs.
* **Risk of a "Hollowed-Out Middle":** Levchin warned that unlike past tech revolutions, capital-rich incumbents are acutely aware of AI from day one. This could lead to an M&A boom and an economy dominated by 100 massive corporations that obliterate the Fortune 10,000, even as "solopreneurs" and long-tail creators thrive on platforms like Stripe.

## Cybersecurity & The "Technology Rebuild Moment"
* **Asymmetric Threat Landscape:** Arora warned that cybersecurity is an asymmetric battle where defenders must be right 100% of the time, while attackers only need to be right once.
  * AI tools—including open-source models and a technology called "Mythos" that emerged 6 months ago—have lowered the bar so that even "bad hackers" can now effectively probe for vulnerabilities.
  * Arora noted that the cybersecurity industry is currently completely backed up, with no spare experts available to call for emergency help.
* **Critical Infrastructure Vulnerabilities:** Both Arora and Collison emphasized that AI is exposing latent bugs in 50-year-old human-written code, even in heavily scrutinized software like the Linux TCP stack. Arora singled out the interconnected banking system as a high-risk area where the "weakest link" determines overall safety.
* **Fixing the Codebase:** Levchin took a more optimistic view, arguing that while AI is currently introducing some new bugs, it is also acting as a propellant to rapidly find and fix missing "if statements," ultimately resulting in more robust software.

## US Economic Divergence & Workforce Impact
* **Widening US-Europe Gap:** Collison stated that AI usage intensity and supply-side innovation (concentrated in the US and the Bay Area) are actively widening the economic growth divergence between the US and Europe. Levchin added that while the US has bipartisan momentum to build energy and compute infrastructure, the EU is still "debating the correct framework for debating about AI."
* **Gradual Reskilling vs. Shock:** Arora argued that workforce disruption will happen through gradual reskilling rather than an overnight shock—noting that every employee at Palo Alto Networks is already experimenting with AI.
* **Creativity as Currency:** Levchin emphasized that LLMs have yet to originate a single brilliant idea without a human, meaning human agency and creativity will become the primary currency as fulfillment and routine tasks are automated.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Drop-in Remote Workers:** "Many people in the AI sector expect drop-in remote workers within the next 12 months"—autonomous agents on Slack and Zoom capable of doing anything a human with a monitor and keyboard can do. — *Patrick Collison*
* **A 2–3 Year Tech Rebuild:** "The next two, three years is going to be a bit of a technology rebuild moment" as enterprises are forced to refactor legacy code and upgrade old infrastructure to defend against AI-enabled cyberattacks. — *Nikesh Arora*
* **Cyber Events Will Persist:** "We will see more cyber events... I don't think this is going to stop. I think it's going to persist." — *Nikesh Arora*
* **10x Engineering Cost Reduction:** Affirm expects its AI-driven engineering productivity gains to eventually reduce the cost per feature to 1/10th of its pre-AI baseline (up from 1/5th today). — *Max Levchin*
* **Extreme Energy Scenarios:** Two influential San Francisco AI figures have an active bet on whether global energy use will double in a single month within the next 10 years. — *Patrick Collison (relaying an industry bet)*
