**TL;DR**
Secretary Robert F. Kennedy, Jr. presented a 2027 budget centered on the "Make America Healthy Again" (MAHA) initiative, prioritizing the reversal of the chronic disease epidemic through nutrition reform and drug price transparency while facing sharp criticism over his skepticism of vaccine efficacy and the management of recent measles outbreaks.

## Reversing the Chronic Disease Epidemic
*   **National Health Crisis:** Kennedy asserted that American children are the "sickest generation in modern history," citing autism rates rising from 1 in 10,000 in 1970 to 1 in 31 today, and 77% of youth being unfit for military service.
*   **Nutrition Reform:**
    *   **Dietary Guidelines:** HHS issued new science-based guidelines prioritizing whole foods and de-emphasizing the "food industry-driven" pyramid.
    *   **Industry Commitments:** Over 40% of the food industry has committed to phasing out petroleum-based dyes, supported by the FDA's approval of natural alternatives.
    *   **Medical Education:** Secured agreements with over 50 medical schools to increase nutrition education from 2 hours to 40 hours.
*   **SNAP and Subsidies:** Bipartisan interest (expressed by Kennedy and Sen. Rand Paul) in reforming SNAP to restrict subsidies for "ultra-processed" foods and sugar-sweetened beverages.

## Healthcare Affordability and Operational Reform
*   **Drug Pricing:** Kennedy highlighted "Most Favored Nation" negotiations with 16 major drug companies to ensure Americans do not pay more than other wealthy nations for the same medications.
*   **Artificial Intelligence (AI):**
    *   **Approval Speed:** The FDA is using AI to compress final drug application reviews from 60 days to approximately two hours.
    *   **Cancer Treatment:** Kennedy noted two recent cancer drugs were approved in 45 and 54 days respectively using these technologies.
*   **Administrative Streamlining:** HHS is proposing a massive reorganization to eliminate duplicative offices, including 41 Chief Information Officers and 59 separate behavioral health programs, aimed at cutting 12% of the department's budget.
*   **Transparency & Choice:** Kennedy expressed support for the "Money and Value for Patients" (MVP) agenda, focusing on price transparency and the expansion of Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) to give patients more market power.

## Public Health and Vaccine Controversies
*   **Vaccine Efficacy Debate:** Senator Bernie Sanders and Chair Bill Cassidy challenged Kennedy on his past statements regarding the "germ theory" and vaccines. 
    *   **Historical Impact:** Kennedy argued that 20th-century mortality declines were driven by sanitation and engineering (citing the "Geyer 2000" study) rather than vaccines, a claim Sanders countered by citing 150 million lives saved by vaccines globally.
*   **Measles Outbreaks:** Kennedy defended the administration’s handling of measles, claiming the U.S. has limited cases to 2,200—better than many European and North American peers—while critics blamed falling vaccination rates on HHS "misinformation."
*   **Agency Independence:** Kennedy assured the committee that the new CDC Director would have the authority to act independently of political appointees regarding immunization schedules.

## Rural Health and Social Policy
*   **Rural Health Transformation Fund:** The budget allocates $50 billion over five years to strengthen rural hospitals, focusing on telehealth and transitioning failing business models.
*   **Reproductive Health:** Chair Cassidy pressured Kennedy on reinstating in-person requirements for chemical abortion drugs to prevent "coercion." Kennedy demurred due to ongoing litigation but noted the FDA is prosecuting the sale of misbranded/counterfeit drugs.
*   **Disability Services:** Senator Andy Kim questioned proposed cuts to the Limb Loss and Paralysis Resource Centers; Kennedy stated these were likely cut due to perceived duplication but offered to reconsider.
*   **Emerging Risks:** Senator Josh Hawley raised concerns regarding the health risks (EMF, noise, neurological issues) of 4,000+ data centers being built nationwide; Kennedy committed to meta-reviews by the Surgeon General.
