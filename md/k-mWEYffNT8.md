## The Sovereign Impossible Trinity
* **Core Thesis:** Tian Yang’s report, *The Sovereign Impossible Trinity*, argues that escalating US-China rivalry forces nations to prioritize sovereignty—the ability to "say no" to rivals by reducing dependence on food, energy, tech, and AI.
  * **The Three-Way Trade-Off:** Because defense/AI mobilization ("guns") is non-negotiable and domestic polarization requires continuous fiscal deficits ("butter") to maintain cohesion, **sound money** is the leg that historically gives way.
  * **China’s Path:** China has pursued self-reliance for over a decade (since the Syngenta/ChemChina deal in the 2010s, followed by ZTE and Huawei sanctions). Facing the same trilemma, China chooses guns and sound money while suppressing household consumption ("butter").
* **1960s Historical Echoes:** Yang drew a direct parallel to the Lyndon B. Johnson era, when the US tried to fund both the Vietnam War and the Great Society without raising taxes.
  * **Central Bank Friction:** Then-Fed Chair William McChesney Martin hiked rates against LBJ’s wishes and was physically confronted at LBJ’s Texas ranch.
  * **The Gold Pool & Bretton Woods:** After repealing the gold cover, France and other nations sold dollars and pulled gold, causing the London Gold Pool ($35 peg) to break in 1968, ultimately leading Nixon to end Bretton Woods in 1970–71. Yang views the 2023–2025 gold rally as a modern equivalent of that early warning.

## AI Financing: A Sisyphean Marathon of Sprints
* **Financing Fragility:** Yang described AI capital raising as a marathon made up of constant, short-sighted sprints to meet the next tranche of compute commitments.
  * **Circular Financing:** Nvidia’s role as an anchor investor in AI labs worked when macro conditions were benign, but higher interest rates make each marginal fundraise much harder.
  * **Market Bifurcation:** Non-AI sectors are experiencing a classic macro cycle, while AI has been a secular trade. Financing needs—now directly tied to interest rates—are finally intertwining the two.
* **Sovereign Backstops & Productivity:** Even if private markets balk at AI’s ROI, national security imperatives put a floor under spending as long as scaling laws (more compute = smarter models) hold.
  * **Changing Winners and Losers:** If governments step in to backstop the top two frontier labs, conditions will likely mandate domestic sourcing—benefiting domestic critical resource providers and equipment makers while leaving marginal AI labs behind.
  * **The Productivity Wild Card:** While bulls hope for a productivity boom, Yang cited Nick Bloom’s work on electrification (worth a max of 1.5%–2% GDP growth per year) and Daron Acemoglu’s research to argue that booms merely delay institutional reform. Furthermore, Alloway and Weisenthal noted that duplicating domestic supply chains across rival blocs is a productivity killer that leads to overinvestment.
