## TL;DR
Isomorphic Labs is shifting drug discovery from empirical trial-and-error to computer-aided precision engineering by using predictive AI models to achieve experimental accuracy. Founded in 2021 as an Alphabet-backed spin-out inspired by AlphaFold, the company builds disease-, target-, and modality-agnostic drug design engines to tackle diseases like cancer and immunology.

## AI & The Paradigm Shift in Drug Discovery
* **The Empirical Bottleneck:** **Max Jaderberg** notes that the pharma industry has historically relied on trial-and-error, guessing molecules without understanding their mechanisms (e.g., aspirin and paracetamol).
  * Drug development has grown increasingly difficult, expensive (~$1.5B per drug), and slow (up to 15 years), while new drug approvals remain flat despite an explosion of scientific knowledge and modalities.
  * The human cell is a high-dimensional chaotic system, and molecule design (small molecules, antibodies, peptides) is combinatorially large, limiting the complexity of solutions reachable via manual screening.
* **The Generalizability of AI:** **Max Jaderberg** explains that the success of AlphaFold served as the first proof of concept that deep learning models could generalize in meaningful biological spaces.
  * Unlike traditional pharma projects treated as bespoke frontier science projects with little transferable learning, Isomorphic's AI platform captures compounding learnings across medicines to achieve economies of scale in R&D.

## Isomorphic Labs: Approach, Models, & Data Strategy
* **Cross-Disciplinary Teams:** **Max Jaderberg** highlights that Isomorphic hires core AI/deep learning researchers (who may lack life sciences backgrounds) and pairs them with biology and chemistry domain experts in roughly a 1-in-5 ratio within problem pods.
* **In-Silico Simulation vs. Real World:** **Max Jaderberg** states the North Star is bringing drug discovery entirely onto computers to bypass the time and cost bottleneck of synthesizing real atoms.
  * **Experimental Accuracy:** Isomorphic tracks a benchmark target on all leaderboards aiming for "experimental accuracy," meaning models become so predictive that researchers do not need to run real-world assays for every iteration.
  * Real-world laboratories, CROs, and automated robotic systems are used to generate proprietary data and validate hypotheses rather than drive slow trial-and-error iteration loops.
* **Therapeutic Focus & Disease Agnosticism:** **Max Jaderberg** shares that Isomorphic's internal pipeline focuses on oncology, immunology, and inflammation—targeting "root node" problems like RAS mutations (e.g., Kraz inhibitors).
  * The underlying AI drug design engine is entirely agnostic of disease area, target class, and modality.

## Clinical Trials, Toxicity, & Future Therapeutics
* **Overcoming Clinical Failure:** **Max Jaderberg** points out that drugs primarily fail in clinical trials due to uncounted toxicities and off-target effects that only surface when introduced to real human patients.
  * Animal models (like rats) are only about 30% predictive of human clinical toxicity. Isomorphic aims to replace animal proxies with in vitro endpoints, predictive AI models, and emerging technologies like organoids and organs-on-a-chip.
* **Enabling Multi-Drug Cocktails:** **Max Jaderberg** argues that current medicine relies on single targets because poor molecule engineering creates harmful drug-drug interactions and liver toxicities when combined. Precise molecular engineering could unlock safe, complex multi-drug cocktails capable of suppressing multi-mutational diseases like cancer and HIV simultaneously.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Max Jaderberg** states he would be really disappointed if, in 5 years, the predictive understanding of disease biology and molecule design has not step-changed massively to shorten multi-year drug development timelines.
