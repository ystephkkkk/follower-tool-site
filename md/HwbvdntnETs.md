**TL;DR**
U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Slovak Prime Minister Robert Fico met to solidify a "pragmatic" bilateral partnership focused on nuclear energy expansion, a negotiated end to the Ukraine conflict, and a "V4 plus USA" regional cooperation framework.

## Energy Security and Nuclear Cooperation
* **Nuclear Expansion:** Prime Minister Fico emphasized Slovakia’s commitment to nuclear energy, noting that a bilateral agreement with the U.S. went into effect on February 13.
    * **Westinghouse Partnership:** Slovakia aims to sign specific agreements with Westinghouse in 2025 to build a new 1,200-megawatt nuclear block by 2040.
* **Fossil Fuel Transition:** Fico expressed deep concern over the EU's "REPowerEU" decision to stop the flow of Russian gas by November 2027, placing Slovakia at the "end of the pipe."
* **Oil Transit Crisis:** Fico alleged that the current disruption of oil through the Druzhba pipeline is a "political blackmail instrument" used by Ukraine against Hungary and Slovakia due to their stances on EU membership and the war.
    * **Regional Impact:** Because Slovakia’s Slovnaft refinery is owned by the Hungarian company MOL, threats to Hungarian oil security directly impact Slovak interests.

## The War in Ukraine and Peace Initiatives
* **Diplomatic Realism:** Both leaders expressed a desire for a rapid end to the conflict, though Fico was more critical of existing Western strategies.
    * **Rubio’s Perspective:** Described the war as "deadly, bloody, and costly," stating that the Trump administration seeks to be a "facilitator" of a negotiated end.
    * **Fico’s Perspective:** Argued that the EU strategy of sanctions and military aid has failed and is a "trap" that lacks a military solution.
* **Slovakia as a Mediator:** Fico offered Slovakia as a hub for opinion exchange and intelligence, citing the country's proximity to the conflict and its ability to "understand the language" of both Ukraine and the Russian Federation.
* **Sovereign Policy:** Fico defended his decisions to maintain dialogue with Moscow and Beijing, stating that "sovereign partners" should not lie to one another about the reality of the conflict.

## NATO and Defense Modernization
* **Airspace Sovereignty:** Slovakia is seeking to increase its procurement of U.S. F-16 fighter jets from 14 to 18 units.
    * **Current Dependency:** Fico noted it is a poor sign of sovereignty that Slovak airspace is currently protected by Poland, Hungary, and the Czech Republic after Slovakia "gifted" its equipment to Ukraine in previous years.
* **NATO Burden Sharing:** Secretary Rubio clarified that the U.S. is not leaving NATO but expects allies to increase their own capabilities.
    * **Collective Strength:** Rubio argued that "the stronger our allies are, the stronger we are collectively," and that individual national strength is a positive development for the alliance.
* **Dual-Use Infrastructure:** Slovakia is utilizing military budgets for dual-use projects, specifically citing a large military hospital currently under construction in Eastern Slovakia.

## Regional Diplomacy and the V4 Group
* **Visegrad Group (V4) Presidency:** Slovakia will assume the presidency of the V4 (Slovakia, Czech Republic, Poland, Hungary) on July 1, 2026.
* **"V4 Plus" Format:** Rubio and Fico discussed inviting the U.S. to participate in V4 meetings to create a "V4 plus USA" forum for energy and international cooperation.
* **U.S. Commitment to Central Europe:** Rubio signaled that under the Trump administration, Central Europe will be a "key component" of how the U.S. engages with the continent, moving beyond pleasantries to "concrete actions."

## Middle East and Global Policy (Q&A)
* **Syrian Stabilization:** Secretary Rubio confirmed he met with both the Syrian Foreign Minister and Kurdish leader General Mazloum in Munich. He defended working with the interim Syrian authorities as a pragmatic choice to prevent an ISIS resurgence and manage mass migration.
* **Iran Policy:** Rubio stated the U.S. prefers diplomacy and a "negotiated summit" with Iran, though the administration remains positioned in the region to defend U.S. forces if threatened.
