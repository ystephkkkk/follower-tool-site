## TL;DR
The FOMC raised the federal funds rate by 25 basis points to a target range of 3.75% to 4%, citing persistent inflation above its 2% goal, resilient economic growth, and strong labor markets. Chair Kevin Warsh emphasized a commitment to price stability and noted that the committee removed a dose of accommodation while remaining cautious about forward guidance.

## Monetary Policy Action & Rate Decision
- **Rate Hike:** Raised the federal funds rate target range by a quarter percentage point to **3.75% to 4%**.
- **Removal of Accommodation:** Chair Warsh noted that broad financial conditions were not restrictive, prompting the committee to remove a dose of accommodation.
- **Unanimous Vote:** The decision was approved by a unanimous vote of the FOMC, signaling collective resolve to combat ongoing inflation.
- **Rejection of Data Point Dependence:** Chair Warsh explicitly rejected short-term "data point dependence" (such as waiting breathlessly on individual CPI or retail sales reports), stating that the committee focuses on broader multi-month economic trends.
