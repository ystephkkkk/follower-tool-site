## TL;DR
Inference is now 12x more important than training in terms of FLOPs over a model's lifetime, driving a complex software and hardware stack of routing, caching, quantization, and specialized kernels that dictates the economic viability of AI.

## The Economics & Scale of Inference
* **Massive Market Impact:** Francois noted that inference has produced $220 billion in market cap over the past decade.
* **Training vs. Inference FLOP Ratio:** Dan Fu and Francois highlighted that DeepSeek V3's annual inference FLOPs are roughly 6x its training FLOPs per year, scaling to **12x** assuming a 2-year model lifespan.
* **Exceptional Margins:** DeepSeek achieves operating margins around **80%** due to aggressive inference innovations, producing throughput per GPU of **1.5 million tokens per second** on prefill (with cache hits) and **14,000 tokens per second** on decode.
* **Token Volume Milestones:** Dan Fu shared that Together AI reached **400 trillion tokens a month** earlier this year (about 1/8th of Google Gemini's total traffic), while open-source models on OpenRouter accounted for **more than half** of all tokens served.

## The Inference Lifecycle & Bottlenecks
* **Request Lifecycle:** Requests traverse Cloudflare/DNS, data centers, load-balancing routers, tokenizers (handling complex tool calls), scheduler queues, and finally the compute-bound **prefill** and memory-bound **decode** loops.
* **Prefill vs. Decode Disparity:** Prefill is compute-bound with high Model Flop Utilization (MFU) and arithmetic intensity; decode requires loading all model weights from GPU memory to compute units per token, resulting in low MFU (e.g., **5%**) and making decode the primary throughput bottleneck.
* **Agent Workload Challenges:** Jeffrey Morgan emphasized that agentic coding workloads amplify errors exponentially; a 10% error rate per step compounds to an **80% failure rate** over a 15-step task.

## Compiler & Hardware Innovations
* **Equality Saturation (Luminal):** Joe Fiaty explained how Luminal uses equality saturation (via the `egglog` engine) to search through PyTorch-complete primitive operations (boiling PyTorch's 2,000 ops down to **14-15 primitive ops**) without massive manual compiler engineering teams.
* **Zero Cost Models & Hardware Agnosticism:** Luminal avoids heuristic cost models by timing generated kernels directly on target hardware (NVIDIA, AMD, TPUs, Trainiums), achieving forward-pass profiling in **under 10 milliseconds**.
* **Massive Scale-Up Domains:** Dan Fu highlighted NVIDIA's GB/Blackwell racks connecting **72 GPUs** via high-bandwidth NVLink, enabling expert parallelism and massive batch throughput compared to legacy 8-GPU limits.

## Caching, Routing & Quantization
* **Radix & Unified Attention (SGLang):** Lianmin Zheng discussed Radix Attention, which automatically caches and reuses shared prefixes in a tree structure across multi-agent workflows, and the new **unified radix tree** and memory pools that handle variable-length multi-modal states with **compaction** to eliminate physical memory fragmentation.
* **KV Cache Policies:** DeepSeek retains user KV caches for **72 hours**, achieving a **96% input cache hit rate**.
* **Training-Free Quantization:** Dan Fu detailed Together AI's success using training-free quantization recipes (e.g., matching FP8 baseline performance on MiniMax's M3.1 model in **NVFP4**) backed by automated evaluation loops like a continuous "Flappy Bird" coding test.

## Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Model Arch Convergence** — Dan Fu predicts that experimental architectures like recurrent/SSM layers (e.g., Kimi K3's KDA) and looped models will continue to be rapidly absorbed by mainstream foundation models (GPT-6/7).
* **AI-Optimized Compilers** — Dan Fu notes a shift toward recursive self-improvement (RSI) loops using agents to automatically optimize inner-loop kernels based on real production traffic.
