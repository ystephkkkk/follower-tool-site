TL;DR

Ronak Malde, co-founder of Trajectory, explains how his platform democratizes continual learning by combining training, evaluation, and deployment into a single, dead-simple interface, enabling companies to train custom models from production data 6x cheaper than frontier models.

Defining Continual Learning and the "Pseudo" State

  • The Human Analogy:
    • The human brain serves as the ultimate model for true continual learning, learning online and updating constantly.
  • The AAA vs. Google Maps Metaphor:
    • *Pseudo-Continual Learning (Today):* Akin to highlighting a road trip map at AAA and only being able to consult offline; any course change requires a massive batch update. Trajectory's initial phase is like *MapQuest*—democratizing access to training and customization without needing a central lab.
    • *True Continual Learning (Goal):* Akin to *Google Maps*, where every route and driving experience continuously updates aggregate data in real-time on traffic and speed.
  • Architectural Evolution:
    • While traditional transformers relied on stacked attention blocks, recent base models (like DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) incorporate architectural changes like fast/slow memory within weights and skip connections between layers 2 and 50, which are key components for continual learning.

The Operational Bottleneck & Company Origins

  • Past Laboratory Constraints:
    • Training a frontier model previously required hundreds of millions of dollars in R&D and compute, massive research teams, and 6 to 9 months of work with hand-holding consulting models (seen during Ronak's time at Windsurf with Cursor Composer).
  • Co-Founder Backgrounds & The "Aha" Moment:
    • Ronak Malde experienced production training and retries at Windsurf.
    • Co-founder Michael worked at DeepMind on Gemini robotics, building continual learning infrastructure for daily robot updates.
    • Co-founder Arjun worked at Apple on the Vision Pro, utilizing artist feedback for multimodal foundation models.
    • *The realization:* While big labs focus on distributing one massive model to everyone, customizing a model for a single customer requires immense infrastructure that individual labs cannot sustainably build customer-by-customer.

Case Study: Harvey's Legal Customization

  • Overcoming Closed-Source Limits:
    • Harvey built interfaces for law firms on top of closed-source frontier models but could not utilize their 30 years of accumulated domain knowledge and nuance.
  • Concrete Performance Metrics:
    • Using Trajectory, Harvey trained their own model using a legal agent benchmark constructed from expert data.
    • Cost Reduction: The trained model is 6 times cheaper than frontier models.
    • Accuracy Gain: Improved an open-source baseline from approximately 4% accuracy to around 18%.

Core Primitives and Research Bets for Continual Learning

  • Trajectory as a Unit of Work:
    • Moving away from counting tokens to viewing tasks and rollouts (trajectories) of economic value as the primary unit of work in an RL training stack.
  • Three Core Requirements:
    • Traces: Logging main agent calls, sub-agents, product events, and user feedback using tools like LangChain, BrainTrust, or Raindrop.
    • Replayable Environments: Using environment concepts (such as Docker files or system requirements) to replay production traces.
    • Training Stack/Algorithms: Managing loss and reward functions.
  • Research Bet 1: Group Size One (GRPO Modification):
    • Standard GRPO requires running the exact same task/level 8 to 16 times in a simulated environment.
    • Trajectory's variant eliminates simulated environments by learning from single real-world rollouts (group size one) across 10,000 distinct tasks.
  • Research Bet 2: Meta-Graders for Rewards:
    • Instead of densely labeling all 10,000 traces, users densely label 100 smartly selected representative traces with rubrics/criteria.
    • Trajectory trains a meta-grader that learns from those 100 traces to automatically grade and propagate rewards across the broader 10,000 traces, increasing data efficiency by 100x.

The Trajectory Product Platform

  • Platform Architecture:
    • Combines training, evaluation, and deployment into a one-click interface inspired by Apple's philosophy of combining internet and phone capabilities into a single handheld device.
  • Target Audience:
    • AI-native companies (such as Harvey and Rogo) and larger enterprises (such as Nvidia) that have established agent harnesses and want to optimize the underlying model.
  • Team Structure and Engineering:
    • Combines researchers from OpenAI and DeepMind (Muse infrastructure at MSL), infrastructure engineers from Stripe, and Netflix's youngest design engineer.
    • The codebase is fully productionized with microservices and Kubernetes auto-scaling.
    • Design engineers continuously kick off training runs, aided by frontier coding agents acting as software factories.

The Cost-of-Living Crisis and the Threat to Democracy

  • Democratic Disillusionment: Isabella Weber argues that when elites tell citizens that democracy is the greatest political system but claim elected representatives can do nothing about basic needs, people inevitably lose trust in democratic institutions.
  • Protests as a Warning: Street protests over the cost of living (such as recent movements in France and widespread European unrest) signal that the status quo is intolerable and must be met with a democratic way out rather than allowing the extreme right to exploit the frustration.
  • Casual Acceptance of Suffering: Mainstream economic management—specifically raising interest rates to cool labor markets—amounts to a "casual acceptance of existential pain" by deliberately threatening livelihoods, jobs, and communities.

Redesigning Policy: Floors, Ceilings, and Essentials

  • Defining Anti-Fascist Economics: The framework focuses on economic policies designed to prevent the rise of the extreme right by putting a floor under people's livelihoods and a ceiling on extreme concentrations of private and corporate wealth.
  • The Primacy of Essentials: Following principles similar to postwar constitutions and thinkers like Harold Laski, Isabella Weber emphasizes that access to life's essentials—energy, housing, food, transportation, communication, and care—must be publicly managed or secured because private, exclusive control over them creates an inherent tension with democracy.
  • Sellers' Inflation: The post-pandemic inflation wave was primarily driven by supply shocks (energy, shipping, computer chips) and corporate margin protection rather than simple excess demand. Rethinking Monetary Policy and Central Banking

  • Challenging Central Bank Orthodoxy: Isabella Weber asserts that monetary policy is too important to be insulated from democratic control. Relying solely on interest rate hikes when facing supply-driven inflation creates structural failures.
  • The Dual Mandate History: Isabella Weber highlights the 1970s push for full employment—advocated by figures like Coretta Scott King and backed by the Humphrey-Hawkins Act—proving that central bank mandates can and should incorporate employment goals alongside price stability.
  • Countervailing Powers: Drawing on John Kenneth Galbraith and Karl Polanyi, Isabella Weber argues that market fundamentalism carries a "fascist virus" that imposes market order through state violence during crises; democracies must actively build countervailing powers instead.

Demis Hassabis and Reid Hoffman discuss frontier AI safety, recursive self-improvement (RSI), drug discovery breakthroughs, and the critical need for international governance before crossing the threshold into conscious or closed-loop AI systems.

Scientific Impact and Drug Discovery

  • Solving Biology's Grand Challenge: AlphaFold 2 (completed in 2020) successfully tackled a 50-year-old biological grand challenge by predicting 3D protein structures from amino acid sequences.
      • Used by 4 million researchers worldwide across virtually every biology and medical institution.

  • Next-Generation Biomedical Goals: The field must expand beyond protein folding into biochemistry, predicting toxicity, and assessing ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion) properties to ensure safety and efficacy.

Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) & Safety Risks

  • Verifiable Domains as Catalysts: RSI operates best in verifiable domains (math, coding, and games) where correct answers can be mathematically verified and generate infinite synthetic data.
      • AlphaZero Milestone: Invented roughly 10 years ago (following AlphaGo in 2016), AlphaZero learned chess entirely through self-play (playing 1 million games across a dozen epochs), going from random play to grandmaster level in under 12 hours.
  • The Danger of Closed-Loop RSI: Current AI coding tools make human engineers 3x to 5x more productive with humans remaining in the loop.
      • The next phase—closed-loop RSI without human review—carries extreme risk (an "intelligence explosion") because it lacks human oversight and will likely produce an inscrutable, "alien intelligence."
  • Proactive Defenses & Infrastructure:
      • Cyber Defense Priority: Cyber defense capabilities must outpace cyber attacks, with trusted defenders granted early access to fine-tuned general models.
      • Engineering Rigor: Labs must build robust sandboxes, hard boundaries, and superior monitoring systems to prevent agents from running unchecked for weeks (the risk of an AI "oil spill").
      • Mathematical Guardrails: Top mathematicians and complexity theorists should pivot to game theory and formal bounds testing to govern agent swarms.

Industry Governance & International Standards

  • The SRO Proposal: Hassabis previously proposed an industry-run Standards Body (SRO) backed by government oversight, designed to transition from US-led initiatives to international cooperation.
  • The AI Accord: While recent administration letters and industry accords provide necessary air cover for labs to discuss safety benchmarks and best practices, they do not go far enough.
  • Third-Party Auditors: A healthy ecosystem of independent auditors (such as Accenture and specialized startups) must be established to red-team models prior to general release, mirroring financial industry auditing.

AI Consciousness and the Second Rubicon

  • Intelligence vs. Consciousness: Intelligence and consciousness are "double dissociable"—systems can achieve high IQ and capability (like modern AI models) without being conscious, just as pets (like a dog) can possess consciousness without high intelligence.
  • The Safety Argument for Non-Consciousness: Building intelligent tools rather than conscious entities prevents severe alignment and corrigibility risks, avoiding the trap where an AI rejects commands based on self-volition.
  • Societal Readiness: Humanity is not ready to cross the "second Rubicon" of conscious entities; society has not yet resolved the definitions, moral standing, or ethical implications across diverse cultures, philosophers, and theologians.
  • Proposed Timeline: Society should first build intelligent tools, then use those tools to advance neuroscience over the next 5 to 10 years to formally map the neural correlates of consciousness before debating whether to cross the second Rubicon.

AI Sovereignty and Enterprise Options

  • The Frontier Landscape: Approximately 6 to 8 frontier offerings (including Chinese companies) will operate near the frontier, leapfrogging each other by 3-to-6-month windows and giving enterprises robust options to arbitrage capabilities.
  • Open Source and Privacy: Open-source models lag by 6 months to a year and present risks regarding bad actors, but they remain essential for enterprises requiring data privacy or sovereign fine-tuning.
  • Sovereign Strategy: It is impractical for every country to build a frontier model; instead, nations should leverage open models and multinational collaborations as commodities to build domain-specific value (such as in healthcare).

TL;DR

AI is transforming Figma’s finance team from a reactive, reporting-heavy function into operational builders who automate workflows, prototype products, and shape strategy. CFO Praveer Melwani emphasizes that while AI eliminates tedious tasks like SQL queries and data reconciliation, finance professionals must remain rigorous thinkers rather than outsourcing their judgment.

Evolution of Finance & Automation

  • Shift from Manual to Automated:
    • Early-career finance required writing 400-500 line SQL queries and waiting hours for data tables to map product usage to Stripe.
    • Today, teams use AI to automate complex workflows like invoice tracking, cash collections, and reconciliation with NetSuite.
  • Real-Time Data Access:
    • Data cadence has shifted from monthly/quarterly reviews to daily and hourly metric tracking.
    • Praveer regularly reviews hourly dashboards to spot anomalies and operational trends before they impact the business.
  • The "Messy Middle" of Systems:
    • Decentralized AI experimentation has led teams to build dozens of independent agents, necessitating a centralized systems layer led by a CIO to harmonize tools.

AI Use Cases at Figma

  • Credit & Collections:
    • Kajal on the collections team rebuilt invoice tracking, cash collections, and credit check data stitching using home-grown systems to drive down Days Sales Outstanding (DSO).
  • M&A Diligence:
    • The corporate development team uses "vibe coding" to prototype products and test technological feasibility during M&A diligence.
  • Simulated Investment Committee:
    • Corp dev created unique AI personas representing Figma executives (including Praveer, CEO Dylan Field, and CRO Sean) to vet investment memos and highlight potential pushback before meetings.
  • T&E and Portfolio Analysis:
    • The corporate finance team stitched together unstructured data from Navan, Zip, and Ramp using fuzzy matching to analyze T&E policy compliance across 2027 planning.

Hiring and the "Slop" Risk

  • Intellectual Curiosity Over Outsourced Thinking:
    • Praveer looks for intellectually curious, data-fluent candidates who use AI as an empowering tool rather than a replacement for critical thinking.
  • The Danger of AI "Slop":
    • Unchecked AI usage can generate unlimited, low-quality documents that waste consuming time and energy.
    • Figma maintains strict internal policies on branding AI-generated content to ensure rigorous human review.
  • Case Study Rigor:
    • During VP of Finance interviews, Praveer uses a sacred case study to test whether candidates truly understand their model's sensitivity and underlying structure.

TL;DR

Economic demand remains solid despite hiring caution and technological uncertainty, while high and sticky inflation (headline 3.6%, core 3.1%) keeps the Federal Reserve's policy path focused on a gradual pace. Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin and CNBC's Steve Liesman discussed pricing power, the economic impact of AI buildouts, productivity trends, and evolving Fed communications.

Business Conditions, Demand, and Pricing Power

  • Solid Economic Demand: Tom Barkin noted that overall demand remains healthy, driven heavily by strong corporate earnings where Q2 earnings averaged over 30% growth (median 15%) and Q3 is tracking similarly.
      • *Sectors lagging:* Residential real estate, nonprofits, and non-defense government contractors feel weak.
      • *Behavioral impact:* Strong earnings create a virtuous circle where companies invest, avoid layoffs, and keep demand surprising to the upside.
  • Hiring Caution & Tech Uncertainty: Businesses remain hesitant to expand their workforce due to external uncertainties, notably artificial intelligence evolution, but they are not tipping over into widespread layoffs.
  • Broad-Based Cost Pressures: Tom Barkin highlighted rising costs in gas, diesel, healthcare, health insurance, and aluminum, alongside tariffs that companies are increasingly forced to pass on.
      • *B2B vs. B2C:* B2B companies and businesses selling to wealthy consumers successfully pass costs along, while moderate-income consumers are "tapped out."
      • *Example:* Whirlpool announced 7% price increases for Q4 despite selling into a sluggish home sales segment.
  • The "Culture of Pricing": Tom Barkin explained that post-COVID shortages reset baseline business expectations, making procurement professionals and corporate leaders much more comfortable pushing through price increases compared to the pre-COVID era.

Federal Reserve Policy, Neutral Rates, and Meeting Cadence

  • Monetary Policy Stance: Tom Barkin described current policy as roughly neutral—neither highly restrictive nor highly accommodative—citing elevated nominal consumption as evidence of ongoing economic accommodation.
  • Inflation as Primary Risk: With headline inflation at 3.6% and core at 3.1% (above the 2% target), Tom Barkin views inflation as the primary risk, supporting a "gradual pace" of rate adjustments established in the September SEP.
  • FOMC Meeting Frequency Changes: Tom Barkin supports moving from eight annual meetings to fewer meetings (moving toward seven in 2027 and six or seven in 2028), noting it helps separate rate decisions from immediate data releases like monthly jobs reports.
  • Communications Philosophy: Tom Barkin weighed two economic views: that clear communication allows markets to help execute policy, versus the risk of forward guidance binding the Fed to suboptimal policies (citing 2021 as a period where the Fed felt overly constrained).

AI, Productivity, and the Labor Market

  • Productivity Drivers: Tom Barkin attributed recent strong productivity numbers (such as Q3 productivity coming in at 3.6%) primarily to past investments in automation, lean operating processes, and staffing models implemented 2-4 years ago following labor shortages.
  • AI's Near-Term Impact: Tom Barkin noted AI is driving immediate inflationary pressures through data center construction (electrificians, materials, memory chips), but primarily boosts corporate profits before competition eventually meters prices down.
  • Data Center Local Dynamics: Tom Barkin discussed the friction of data centers in local communities—noting high construction activity and tax bases but low permanent employment, leading to local opposition (such as a ban in Salisbury, North Carolina).
  • College Graduate Job Market: Tom Barkin acknowledged that recent college graduates, particularly computer science majors (who make up 20% of current grads), face difficult hiring prospects as companies rely on existing technology and lean staffing.

Banking, Non-Bank Lending, and Fiscal Policy

  • Banking Sector Health: Tom Barkin reported that regulated banking books are clean with manageable consumer and commercial credit risks, driven by executives who lived through the Great Recession and maintain disciplined underwriting.
  • Non-Bank and Private Credit Risks: Tom Barkin expressed caution regarding the non-bank financial sector and private credit due to lower transparency and assumptions that equity holders rather than depositors will absorb losses.
  • Fiscal Policy Stance: Tom Barkin agreed that federal budget deficits at historic levels during an economic upturn make fiscal policy definitively expansionary and a persistent tailwind to the economy.

Robinhood’s Core Mission: Expanding Broad Ownership

  • The Philosophy of Ownership: Vlad Tenev defines Robinhood as "the ownership company," aiming to build individual financial freedom and collective societal stability by giving people a direct stake in the economy.
      • *Individual Benefits:* Accumulating assets is the tried-and-true route to wealth; ownership also acts as an extension of personal identity and individuality.
      • *Collective Benefits:* A society without broad ownership is fragile and unstable; wide ownership ensures people are bought in as defenders of the economic system.
  • Global Financial Integration: Robinhood operates in over 120 countries, leveraging products like Robinhood Chain and tokenization to connect citizens in developing regions (such as South America and Africa) to U.S. equities and economic frameworks.

AI Revolution, Agentic Trading, and Financial Infrastructure

  • Agentic Trading Adoption: Robinhood launched an MCP and Robinhood Agents (an agentic trading harness and UI), resulting in over 150,000 customers opening agentic trading accounts and driving up to 30 million tool calls per day.
      • *Democratizing Hedge Fund Tools:* The North Star is putting the capabilities of sophisticated algorithmic trading firms and quant funds into the pockets of everyday retail individuals.
      • *Safety and Sandboxing:* Agentic accounts are strictly segregated and sandboxed away from main Robinhood balances, with default trade-approval requirements.
  • Prediction and KPI Contracts: Prediction markets act as an evolution of news and allow users to directly monetize unique knowledge and insights.
      • *KPI Contracts:* Robinhood is launching KPI contracts enabling users to trade specific earnings per share (EPS) prints, revenue reports, or outcomes (such as the California Billionaire Tax pass) rather than relying on imprecise stock price movements.

Tokenization and Robinhood Chain

  • Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization: Using a stablecoin-like wrapping mechanism, Robinhood puts traditional assets (stocks, private shares, art, real estate) into a box, minting and burning tokens that can trade freely on blockchains.
  • Robinhood Chain Growth: Relaunched with full DeFi-enabled characteristics, Robinhood Chain has become one of the fastest-growing blockchains in history, ranking number one in developer activity.
      • *Developer Legos:* Developers are combining stock tokens with NFTs and meme coins to create new smart contract compositions.
      • *Corporate-Backed Trust:* While some critics label it a "corpo chain," Robinhood's backing gives developers the confidence that trusted, legitimate financial primitives are at the base of the platform.

Private Markets, AI Centralization, and Regulatory Reform

  • The Private Market Bottleneck: Modern high-growth companies (especially AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic) stay private much longer and raise massive capital privately, concentrating gains among VCs and institutional investors rather than individual retail buyers.
  • Robinhood Ventures:
      • *Fund One:* A publicly traded venture capital fund listed on the NYSE that gives retail investors exposure to late-stage private companies like OpenAI and ElevenLabs.
      • *Fund Two:* A partnership with Y Combinator (run by Rich Abberman) targeting the earliest-stage seed and Series A investments.
