**TL;DR:** SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce outlines how the agency is using existing authority to advance crypto regulation through a new five-year tokenized stock innovation exemption following the Clarity Act's failure. She emphasizes the foundational importance of self-custody, financial privacy, and developer protections for keeping crypto innovation in the United States.

### The Post-Clarity Act Reality and SEC Authority
* **Unexpected Legislative Collapse:** **Hester Peirce** expressed surprise that the Clarity Act failed to pass after more than a year of intensive debate, noting that while a market structure bill would be helpful for regulators, the SEC can still accomplish significant work under its existing statutory authority.
* **Proactive Agency Positioning:** Both the SEC and CFTC had prepared for either legislative outcome, allowing the SEC to move forward immediately with proposals like the new innovation exemption using authorities it has exercised for nearly two years.
* **The Five-Year Innovation Exemption:** Designed as a bridge to a permanent regulatory framework, this time-limited order allows market participants to experiment with bringing tokenized NMS (National Market System) stock trading on-chain using automated market makers (AMMs).
    * **Issuer Controls:** The exemption includes a 30-day issuer consent condition, giving companies the explicit power to veto or approve the tokenization of their shares.
    * **Strict Parity Requirements:** Tokenized shares must carry the exact same rights as traditional shares, including identical dividends and voting rights.
    * **Permissionless Blockchain Focus:** The current order explicitly permits activity on permissionless blockchains, providing transparency while maintaining an open door for permissioned chain discussions.

### Bridging Tokens and Equities
* **Rehabilitating the "Security" Label:** **Hester Peirce** criticized past anti-crypto regulatory approaches that drove projects to fear the security designation, which inadvertently fueled the rise of utility-free meme coins as a gamble-driven workaround.
* **Unified Asset Structures:** The SEC is pushing for a regulatory environment where tokens and equity can be unified when it makes sense for a project, or separated cleanly with clear rights and benefits assigned to each.
* **Investment Contract Lifecycles:** Under guidance like Reg Crypto, teams are encouraged to be explicit about the promises attached to an investment contract token; once those promises are fulfilled or abandoned, the investment contract framework falls away from the token.

### Self-Custody, Privacy, and Developer Protections
* **Self-Custody as a Fundamental Right:** **Hester Peirce** asserted that holding one's own money and assets is a basic American right that does not require regulatory permission, contrasting it with intermediated channels that require standard regulatory oversight.
* **Financial Privacy Reset:** Describing current financial surveillance and the deputization of financial institutions as a system where Americans have surrendered too much privacy, she called for a constitutional balance that protects everyday citizen privacy while giving law enforcement proper channels to investigate illicit activity.
* **Shielding Software Developers:** Comparing blockchain software to general-purpose technology like iPhones, **Hester Peirce** argued that developers must be protected from liability simply because bad actors misuse their tools, warning against turning the SEC into a "developer approval agency."

### AI Integration and Future-Proofing Regulation
* **AI-Driven Fraud and Defense:** **Hester Peirce** noted that while AI magnifies the scale of securities fraud, it is simultaneously being leveraged by regulators and financial institutions for enhanced investigation and customer protection.
* **Future-Proofing Rules:** Acknowledging that future regime changes can reverse policy, she argued that the best way to make crypto regulations permanent is to build products that people genuinely want, need, and use, making them functionally unstoppable.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Hester Peirce** claimed that issuers will show high enthusiasm and uptake for tokenized stock trading under the new five-year innovation exemption framework.

**TL;DR**  
U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Chairman Paul Atkins and John Moses, Director of the Office of Investor Education and Assistance, discuss the critical importance of bottom-up financial education, highlighting the transformative rollout of tax-advantaged "Trump Accounts" for children and modern defense strategies against increasingly sophisticated AI and cryptocurrency scams.

---

### Fostering a Bottom-Up Investment Culture
* **Global Market Leadership:** The U.S. capital markets represent half of the world's total global capitalization. Chairman Atkins notes that international counterparts often ask how to replicate America's investing ethos, which he argues must be built from the bottom up rather than top down.
* **The "What We Own" Mindset:** 
    * Traditional personal finance focuses heavily on what people earn (income) and what they owe (debt). 
    * The SEC's educational mission focuses instead on what individuals *own*, positioning asset ownership as a core pillar of the American Dream as the nation approaches its "America 250" milestone.
* **Increased Personal Responsibility:** Market innovations have made investing more accessible than ever, coinciding with a shift where individuals are increasingly responsible for funding their own retirement, higher education, and long-term expenses.

### Grassroots Outreach and Financial Literacy Tools
* **Investor Assistance Services:** Through `help.sec.gov`, any American investor can submit inquiries to receive assistance in locating old 401(k) accounts, understanding financial products, or navigating problems with financial professionals (though the SEC does not provide specific investment advice).
* **Digital Tools on Investor.gov:**
    * **Compound Growth Calculator:** One of the most popular calculators on the web. Moses stresses that if financial assets are the "what" of investing, compound growth is the "why."
    * **DRIPs:** The SEC educates investors on the power of Dividend Reinvestment Programs to accelerate compound interest automatically.
* **Active Community Outreach:** 
    * The SEC conducts over 500 in-person and online outreach events annually, targeting military communities, older adults, high schools, colleges, and Historically Black Colleges and Universities (HBCUs).
    * **Military Engagement:** Atkins and Moses recently visited Camp Pendleton in California, speaking to 200 new Marine recruits about saving, investing, and starting early to maximize their greatest asset: time.

### "Trump Accounts" and the Future of Youth Investing
* **The Core Concept:** A new legislative initiative designed to make every eligible child’s first financial account an investment account, shifting the cultural paradigm around early wealth generation.
* **Key Features and Benefits:**
    * **Seed Funding:** Eligible newborns receive $1,000 in government seed money. Moses advises families to "take the free money" by opening accounts promptly.
    * **Structural Guardrails:** Accounts are legally mandated to be low-fee, tax-advantaged, and diversified across broad-based index funds. 
    * **Long-Term Horizon:** Funds are locked until the beneficiary turns 18. Upon reaching adulthood, the accounts can naturally transition into traditional or Roth IRAs to build a foundation for retirement.
* **Philanthropic Integration:** The program allows private philanthropists to contribute to youth accounts. For example, the Michael & Susan Dell Foundation is actively contributing, and other donors are "adopting" entire states to fund local children's accounts.
* **Educational Impact:** A dedicated resource hub is available at `investor.gov/trumpaccounts` to help families integrate these accounts into their broader financial planning.

### Protecting Investors from Modern and AI-Driven Fraud
* **Digital Assets and Crypto Safety:** Digital assets represent the most common category of inquiry for the SEC's investor assistance team.
    * **Regulated Alternatives:** For investors seeking exposure to digital assets, the SEC highlights regulated options like exchange-traded products (ETPs) and ETFs rather than complex, unregulated instruments.
    * **Classic Red Flags:** Investors are urged to watch out for high guaranteed returns, high-pressure urgency, and pitches that sound "too good to be true."
* **Emerging Scam Trends:**
    * **AI and Impersonation Scams:** Scammers are using AI tools on an industrial scale to clone voices and mimic authority figures. The SEC recently issued an alert warning investors against bad actors using the likeness of SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce to endorse fraudulent products.
    * **Relationship and Romance Scams:** Often referred to as "long cons," these schemes build trust over time through business or personal relationships before introducing a fraudulent investment pitch.
    * **Affinity Fraud:** Scammers exploit trust within tight-knit communities, such as religious organizations, churches, and social clubs, to bypass investors' natural skepticism.

## Hester Peirce: The Positive Future of Digital Asset Regulation (Huge Catalysts Coming)

**TL;DR** 
SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce highlights the agency's efforts to modernize market structures through the proposed removal of the 20-year-old Rule 611 and the upcoming "innovation exemption," emphasizing that future regulations must pragmatically support on-chain finance while fiercely protecting investor privacy, self-custody, and developer freedom.

---

### Reconsidering Legacy Market Structure (Rule 611)

* **Rule 611 Elimination:** The SEC has proposed a rule to eliminate Rule 611 (the "trade-through rule"), a 20-year-old equity market regulation.
    * **Historical Context:** Originally established in 2005, the rule was met with a 44-page dissent by then-Commissioner (now Chairman) Paul Atkins and Commissioner Cynthia Glassman.
* **Obsolete Protections:** Designed to protect the National Best Bid or Offer (NBBO) across fragmented markets, Peirce argues the rule is no longer necessary. 
    * **Market Mechanisms:** Arbitrageurs naturally bind markets together and align prices across venues without regulatory intervention.
    * **Technological Upgrades:** Modern, highly interconnected exchange technology has resolved the fragmentation issues that existed when the rule was written.
    * **Tokenization Enablement:** Removing Rule 611 is a key structural step that will allow future tokenized, on-chain trading systems to function without being forced to fit antiquated execution requirements.

### The Scope and Reality of the "Innovation Exemption"

* **Dispelling Myths:** Peirce clarifies that the highly anticipated "innovation exemption" has not yet been officially released, contrary to media rumors.
* **Exclusion of Synthetics:** The exemption will not apply to synthetic securities (the third tier in the SEC staff's tokenization taxonomy, where SPV wrappers offer economic exposure without direct asset custody).
* **On-Chain vs. Decentralized Finance:** The framework targets "on-chain finance" rather than fully decentralized finance (DeFi).
    * **Controlled Experimentation:** The exemption will serve as a permissioned, real-world sandbox for firms to test tokenized assets, allowing regulators to observe and determine where proper points of regulation lie.
* **Value Proposition of Tokenization:** Transferring securities to smart contracts on-chain unlocks significant efficiencies:
    * **Collateral and Lending:** Streamlines the process of lending out securities and posting collateral without relying on costly intermediaries.
    * **Programmable Compliance:** Enables automatic enforcement of restrictions, such as founder lock-up periods, directly within the asset's code.
    * **Investor Communication:** Allows companies to directly distribute communications or voting rights to wallets (e.g., via NFTs) without requiring personal investor identities.

### SEC Priorities and Regulatory Harmonization

* **Commissioner Transition:** Although her official term ended last June, Peirce can remain in her seat until the end of the year unless a replacement is confirmed. Her immediate post-SEC plan is to teach at a law school.
* **Immediate Policy Priorities:** For her remaining tenure, Peirce is focusing on:
    * Preparing the agency to coordinate rule-writing under the proposed legislative Clarity Act.
    * Building regulatory frameworks for using tokens in capital fundraising.
    * Tackling "nuts-and-bolts" issues including crypto custody (for broker-dealers and advisers), transfer agent blockchain integrations, and defining the role of user interfaces under broker-dealer rules.
* **CFTC Harmonization:** The SEC is actively collaborating with the CFTC to provide joint clarity on product classification and jurisdiction, particularly for complex retail products like perpetuals (perps).
    * **Proactive Engagement:** Peirce urges builders to bring concrete business models to the SEC’s crypto task force to identify required regulatory reliefs early.

### Reinvigorating Public Markets and Capital Formation

* **Encouraging Earlier Public Listings:** Peirce is advocating for reforms that encourage high-growth companies to go public sooner rather than remaining private through excessively late funding rounds (e.g., "Series M").
* **Reducing Burdensome Disclosures:** To combat the negative associations of going public, Peirce recommends trimming boilerplate disclosure requirements that do not serve investor decision-making.
* **Mitigating Litigation Risks:** The SEC is exploring ways to allow public companies and shareholders to voluntarily utilize arbitration rather than facing class-action shareholder litigation, without the regulator putting a "thumb on the scale."

### Protecting Core Principles in the Digital Age

* **Non-Negotiable Regulatory Pillars:** Peirce outlines three fundamental principles that future regulatory regimes must respect:
    * **Self-Custody:** Protecting the basic right of individuals to hold their own digital assets.
    * **Developer Freedom:** Ensuring software developers can write and publish open-source code without prior government permission.
    * **Financial Privacy:** Recognizing that the desire to protect financial privacy is natural and should be defended.
* **Credible Neutrality and Layering:** To balance privacy and law enforcement, Peirce suggests keeping the base layer of blockchain technology credibly neutral.
    * **Application-Level Compliance:** Specialized features—such as freezing or reversing transactions—can be built as opt-in application layers on top of a neutral base layer, rather than compromising the underlying blockchain architecture.

SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce highlights a pivotal shift toward digital asset integration, emphasizing the SEC’s proposed elimination of the 20-year-old Rule 611 and the development of an "innovation exemption" to facilitate controlled, on-chain financial experimentation. As she prepares to transition out of her role, Peirce urges regulators to maintain an open mind toward innovation while fiercely protecting user privacy, self-custody, and developer freedom.

### Reconsidering Rule 611 and Modernizing Market Structure
* **The Rule 611 Proposal:** The SEC has proposed a rule to eliminate Rule 611 (the "trade-through rule"), a 20-year-old equity market regulation.
    * **Historical Context:** In 2005, current Chairman Atkins and Commissioner Cynthia Glassman issued a 44-page written dissent against the implementation of Rule 611.
    * **The Trade-Through Mechanism:** Designed to prevent trading through a better price, the rule mandates that transactions occur at or improve upon the national best bid or offer (NBBO).
* **Obsolete Technology:** Peirce argues that while the rule may have been conceived when venues like the New York Stock Exchange lacked modern technology, today's highly interconnected, high-tech markets no longer require it.
    * **The Role of Arbitrage:** Market participants and arbitrageurs naturally bind fragmented marketplaces together without the need for rigid regulatory mandates.
    * **Implications for Tokenization:** Removing Rule 611 will reduce market fragmentation and complex order types, creating a friendlier environment for future asset tokenization.

### Dispelling Myths Around the "Innovation Exemption"
* **Current Status:** Peirce clarifies that the highly anticipated "innovation exemption" has not yet been officially released, despite various media reports and speculation.
* **Exclusion of Synthetics:** The framework will not apply to synthetic securities (where an SPV holds an asset and grants investors economic exposure without direct ownership). Synthetics must still go through traditional U.S. registration processes.
* **On-Chain Finance vs. DeFi:** The exemption is designed for permissioned, "on-chain finance" rather than fully decentralized finance (DeFi).
    * **Controlled Experimentation:** The goal is to allow market participants to conduct real-world, limited-scale experiments to prove the viability of tokenized securities.
    * **Regulatory Insights:** This trial period gives regulators a window to observe how tokenized trading operates and identify correct points of regulatory intervention.
* **Smart Contract Utility:** Bringing securities on-chain enables programmable features, such as embedding transfer restrictions for company founders, simplifying collateral management, facilitating investor communication via NFTs, and combating naked short selling.

### Driving Capital Formation and Encouraging Earlier IPOs
* **The Problem of Delayed IPOs:** Companies are staying private much longer—sometimes raising up to "Series M" private funding rounds—which deprives retail investors of early-stage wealth generation.
* **Public Market Benefits:** Public listings provide retail investors with tested valuations and transparent disclosures, while giving companies acquisition capital and employee liquidity.
* **Mitigating Disincentives to Go Public:**
    * **Disclosure Requirements:** The SEC is evaluating redundant disclosure requirements that add cost burden without providing useful information to investors.
    * **Shareholder Litigation:** The SEC is staying neutral on companies and shareholders opting for arbitration over traditional litigation to resolve disputes.
    * **Analyst Coverage:** The commission is exploring ways to make it easier for companies to obtain research analyst coverage to support healthy trading volumes.

### SEC-CFTC Harmonization and Regulatory Priorities
* **Joint Agency Collaboration:** The SEC and CFTC are actively working together to establish clear jurisdictions for novel products, such as perpetual contracts (perps), to ensure market participants know where and how to list them.
* **Operational "Nuts and Bolts":** The SEC’s current focus includes defining the role of broker-dealers, establishing guidelines for transfer agents interacting with blockchains, regulating custody on both the broker-dealer and investment adviser sides, and addressing clearing agency requirements.
    * **The DTCC Initiative:** The DTCC was recently granted a no-action letter by SEC staff to experiment with blockchain-based settlement systems.
* **Peirce's Departure:** Peirce’s official term ended last June, and though she can stay until the end of the year, she will be departing early to teach law school. She expresses strong confidence that Chairman Atkins will maintain a fast, pro-innovation regulatory pace.

### Protecting Core Principles: Privacy, Self-Custody, and Developer Freedom
* **Non-Negotiable Pillar Principles:** Peirce emphasizes that future statutory and regulatory frameworks must respect three foundational tenets:
    * **Self-Custody:** Individuals must retain the right to self-custody their own digital assets.
    * **Privacy:** The financial transaction privacy of innocent citizens must be safeguarded.
    * **Developer Freedom:** Software developers must retain the right to write and publish open-source code without needing prior government permission.
* **A Layered Approach to Privacy and Security:** Peirce suggests balancing illicit-finance monitoring with consumer privacy by maintaining a "credibly neutral" base blockchain layer. 
    * **Application-Level Compliance:** Regulatory features—such as asset freezes, identity verification, or transaction reversals—should be built on the application layer rather than forcing privacy compromises at the protocol level.

**TL;DR**
SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce acknowledges that the U.S. "lost time" on crypto regulation due to an enforcement-first approach under previous leadership, but she expresses optimism that a new DC consensus—driven by both legislative efforts like the Clarity Act and SEC-led guidance—is finally providing the industry with the necessary regulatory framework.

### The Shift from Enforcement to Collaborative Guidance
*   **Retrospective on "Lost Time":** Commissioner Peirce admits that while the intentions under former Chair Gary Gensler may have been to protect investors, the execution was flawed.
    *   **Regulatory Instinct:** Regulators often try to reach outside their jurisdiction to stop "bad activity," but Peirce argues the SEC must be disciplined enough to say, "It’s a problem, but it’s not my problem."
    *   **Listening Tours:** She notes that a "listening tour" and industry engagement should have preceded enforcement actions to allow for more productive progress.
*   **Current Strategy:** The SEC is now leveraging its existing authority to provide staff-level guidance and interpretive statements, such as the recent joint statement with the CFTC on token taxonomy.
*   **The "Safe Harbor" Framework:** Peirce’s long-standing idea for a "token safe harbor" is becoming complementary to congressional efforts, providing a path for fundraising and decentralization without immediate enforcement.

### Legislative Outlook and Global Competition
*   **The Clarity Act:** There is a strong consensus in DC to pass a regulatory framework, though Peirce notes that the bill's wide-ranging nature leads to inevitable hurdles regarding stablecoins and capital flight.
*   **The "May Deadline":** While some senators suggest a May window for crypto legislation, Peirce emphasizes that the SEC and CFTC can continue to work in parallel to provide durable rules even if legislation faces delays.
*   **U.S. Market Position:** Despite jurisdictions like Dubai (VARA) moving faster, Peirce remains confident that the U.S. is the primary destination for innovation.
    *   **Rulemaking Phase:** She cautions that even after legislation passes, a lengthy rulemaking phase will follow, making current SEC guidance essential for immediate stability.

### Emerging Trends: Tokenization and Digital Asset Treasuries
*   **Real-World Asset (RWA) Tokenization:** Peirce highlights a significant shift toward tokenizing securities and real-world assets. Unlike meme coins, these efforts fall squarely within existing SEC jurisdiction.
*   **Digital Asset Treasury (DAT) Companies:** Regarding the trend of companies (similar to MicroStrategy) stacking crypto on balance sheets, Peirce clarifies the SEC's stance:
    *   **Disclosure-First Approach:** The SEC’s "point of entry" is disclosure. Companies must clearly state who is managing the treasury, what the risks are, and what the specific arrangements look like.
    *   **Market Freedom:** She asserts it is not the regulator's job to decide what types of companies people can start, provided they are honest with investors about risks.
*   **Meme Coins and "Self-Regulation":** Peirce notes that meme coins often fall outside SEC jurisdiction if they are purely for "fun" or entertainment, but warns that the SEC will not "stand behind" investors if they are lied to in these unregulated spaces.

### DeFi Security and Principles-Based Oversight
*   **Avoiding Prescriptive Rules:** Peirce argues against the SEC setting specific technical standards (e.g., multi-sig requirements) for DeFi protocols.
    *   **The Durability Problem:** Writing technical standards into law is risky because tech evolves faster than rules can be updated.
    *   **Principles-Based Regulation:** She prefers requiring firms to take "proper precautions" while leaving the specific technical execution to the industry.
*   **The "DeFi Mullet" Concept:** Peirce anticipates a future where traditional financial firms provide a regulated front-end while utilizing DeFi on the back-end, placing the burden of diligence on those traditional intermediaries.

### Enforcement Metrics and the Atkins Transition
*   **Quality over Quantity:** Addressing criticisms (including from Sen. Elizabeth Warren) regarding a drop in enforcement actions, Peirce argues that "counting numbers" is a poor metric for efficacy.
    *   **Unjustified Actions:** Peirce remains vocal that many past enforcement actions were unjustified and a poor use of agency resources.
    *   **Holding Accountable:** She warns that the current regime is not an "anything-goes" period; the SEC will still hold actors accountable for actual securities violations.
*   **Departure Timeline:** Peirce confirmed she will leave the SEC by the end of 2024, noting that the next commissioner will face a "plethora of interesting questions" regarding stablecoin integration and money market fund tokenization.

> "I think we really lost time because of [the previous approach]... it would have been nice if we had been able to do the listening tours before we jumped right in and started issuing proposals." — **Hester Peirce**

> "It’s sometimes hard for a regulator to say, 'Yeah, it’s a problem, but it’s not my problem.' Congress does not want you to reach outside your jurisdiction to try to grab things and pull them in." — **Hester Peirce**

> "If people are thinking that this is an 'anything-goes' time, I think they’re going to be quite disappointed." — **Hester Peirce**

**TL;DR**
SEC Chairman Paul Atkins is shifting the agency away from "regulation by enforcement" toward a growth-oriented "ACT" strategy (Advance, Clarify, Transform) designed to lure innovative firms back to the U.S. and remove regulatory barriers to revitalize the public IPO market.

### The "ACT" Strategy and Regulatory Pivot
Chairman Atkins highlighted a fundamental shift in the SEC’s philosophy, moving away from what he described as the "opaqueness" of the previous administration.

*   **Pivoting from Enforcement:** The agency is moving away from "regulation through enforcement," particularly in the crypto sector, to provide clearer guidelines for market participants.
*   **The ACT Framework:** 
    *   **Advance:** Modernizing the SEC's approach to embrace new technologies rather than "fending them off," with the goal of bringing offshore business back to the U.S.
    *   **Clarify:** Increasing transparency through initiatives like the interpretative release with the CFTC, which defines the difference between tokenized securities and digital commodities.
    *   **Transform:** Updating the SEC rulebook to make it "fit for purpose" for the modern economy.

### Revitalizing the IPO Market
Atkins expressed a commitment to "making IPOs great again," noting that the number of public companies has halved over the last 30 years.

*   **The Private-to-Public Gap:** Companies are staying private much longer; what used to be an "Initial Public Offering" is now occurring at Series C or D funding rounds.
*   **Barriers to Entry:** Atkins identified three primary impediments keeping companies private:
    *   **Compliance Costs:** The extreme complexity and total cost of the current disclosure system.
    *   **Vexatious Litigation:** Uncertainty regarding whether the SEC will permit mandatory arbitration or fee-shifting to prevent meritless lawsuits.
    *   **Politicized Governance:** The "weaponization" of corporate governance by activist shareholders pursuing political agendas.
*   **The Liquidity Need:** He noted that massive private companies (e.g., SpaceX, OpenAI) eventually need public markets to provide true daily valuations and liquidity for employees.

### Market Integrity and Political Trading
The Chairman addressed concerns regarding market manipulation and the influence of political figures on market movements.

*   **Washington Influence:** Atkins noted that "disturbing" instances of market-moving comments—and subsequent suspicious trading activity—are being examined.
*   **Agency Collaboration:** The SEC is working closely with the Department of Justice (under US Attorney Jay Clayton) and the CFTC to monitor traceability and potential insider trading.
*   **Prediction Markets:** 
    *   Atkins deferred sports betting questions to the courts and the CFTC.
    *   **SEC Jurisdiction:** The SEC focuses on "event contracts" that impinge on securities, such as binary options regarding whether a company will meet its quarterly earnings.

### Private Credit and Retail Access
As private credit grows, the SEC is evaluating whether "normal" retail investors should have broader access to these traditionally opaque markets.

*   **Semi-Liquid Instruments:** The agency’s examination division is looking into disclosures for "interval funds" that use gates to limit withdrawals (often up to 5%). 
*   **Retirement Guardrails:** The SEC is collaborating with the Treasury and Department of Labor on proposals to allow private market access for 401k plans.
*   **Cautious Approach:** Atkins emphasized that if retail access expands, "really strong guardrails" are mandatory because private markets remain significantly more opaque than public ones.

### Notable Quotes

> "We’ve pivoted from the old practice of regulation through enforcement and the opaqueness of the agency as it was... that was job one." — **Paul Atkins**

> "We have half the number of public companies as we had 30 years ago. And so where did that go? That went into the private markets." — **Paul Atkins**

> "I promised a new day at the SEC when I came aboard and we have." — **Paul Atkins**

**TL;DR**
The SEC is pivoting back to its "core mandate" of disclosure-based regulation and capital formation, prioritizing the establishment of a clear, "fit for purpose" regulatory framework for digital assets to ensure the United States remains the global leader in financial innovation.

### Restoring the SEC’s Core Mission
The leadership team emphasizes a return to the agency’s "tripartite mission": protecting investors, maintaining fair and efficient markets, and facilitating capital formation.

*   **Critique of Recent Policy:** Commissioner Mark Uyeda characterized the last four years as a "complete deviation" from the SEC's historical role.
    *   **Regulatory Overreach:** The agency moved toward being a "business conduct regulator," micromanaging areas such as share repurchases, cybersecurity, supply chain management, and DEI at the board level.
    *   **Focus on Non-Financial Disclosures:** Uyeda specifically cited greenhouse gas emissions and resource extraction as being "outside the lane" of traditional securities regulation.
*   **A "Disclosure-First" Approach:** The leadership intends to move away from prescriptive mandates and return to a model that relies on disclosure, allowing investors—rather than the government—to judge the merits of business decisions.

### Revitalizing Public Markets and Capital Formation
A major priority for the Commission is addressing the decline in the number of public companies, which has dropped by nearly 50% over the last 30 years.

*   **Making IPOs "Great Again":** The SEC aims to shift the perception of the IPO from a mere "liquidity event" for early investors to a viable capital-raising tool for growth-stage companies.
*   **Democratizing Opportunity:** Leadership emphasized that public markets allow the general public to participate in wealth creation that is currently often restricted to private markets.
*   **Portfolio Diversification:** Commissioner Uyeda argued that the SEC should not block high-risk investments for "protection," noting that even high-risk assets can be beneficial when part of a diversified portfolio.
*   **The Entrepreneurial Spirit:** Chairman Atkins and Commissioner Uyeda framed financial regulation as a tool to sustain the American tradition of free enterprise and minimal government interference in trade.

### Digital Assets and "Project Crypto"
Commissioner Hester Peirce is leading "Project Crypto," an initiative designed to move away from "regulation by enforcement" toward a durable, understandable framework.

*   **Technological Utility:** Peirce highlighted that cryptography solves the "double spending" problem, allowing for the secure transfer of value over the internet without traditional intermediaries.
*   **Benefits of On-Chain Finance:**
    *   **Disintermediation:** Reducing reliance on third parties who may mishandle or lose assets.
    *   **Programmability:** Using smart contracts to automate regulatory requirements, collateral payouts, and restrictions efficiently.
*   **Global Competitiveness:** The Commission is aligned with the goal of making the U.S. the "crypto capital of the world" by providing the legal clarity needed to keep innovators from moving offshore.
*   **Urgency and Durability:** While there is a push to move quickly to provide clarity, Peirce stressed the need for "future-proof" and "principles-based" rules that can survive technological shifts.

### Inter-Agency Cooperation and Investor Education
The Commission is fostering a more collaborative relationship with other regulators and focusing on "meeting investors where they are."

*   **SEC-CFTC Alignment:** With former SEC staffer Michael Selig now chairing the CFTC, the agencies intend to coordinate closely to avoid redundant resource spending and clearly allocate jurisdiction over crypto assets.
*   **Legislative Role:** The Commissioners acknowledged that Congress must ultimately play a role in allocating authority between the two agencies to provide a permanent framework.
*   **Investor Protection through Education:**
    *   **Building Skepticism:** Education efforts are focusing on empowering investors to ask questions and recognize "too good to be true" offers.
    *   **Early Intervention:** Commissioner Peirce advocated for starting financial education at the elementary school level to normalize financial literacy before individuals begin managing their own capital.

## SEC Chair Paul Atkins and SEC Commissioner Hester Peirce in Conversation

---

**TL;DR**
The SEC is pivoting from "regulation by enforcement" to a collaborative, guidance-based framework, spearheaded by "Project Crypto"—a joint initiative with the CFTC—and a proposed "Innovation Exemption" to facilitate regulated experimentation in tokenized securities and DeFi.

### A New Era of SEC-CFTC Coordination
*   **Project Crypto:** SEC Chair Paul Atkins and CFTC Chair Mike Selig have launched a joint initiative to harmonize rules and end the "sparring" between the two agencies.
    *   The goal is to provide a common, coordinated approach to crypto oversight that prevents jurisdictional confusion.
    *   If Congress fails to pass new legislation, the agencies intend to use "substituted compliance" to allow firms to operate across securities and commodities boundaries without redundant registration.
*   **Ending "Regulation by Enforcement":** Commissioner Peirce highlighted the shift toward transparency, noting that the agency is now focused on providing staff guidance, FAQs, and no-action letters rather than litigation-first tactics.
*   **Regulatory Rescissions:** The agency has already moved to eliminate unhelpful guidance, most notably the rescission of **SAB 121**, which had previously hindered institutional custody of digital assets.

### The Proposed "Innovation Exemption"
*   **Experimental Safe Harbor:** Chair Atkins proposed a temporary, "cabined" exemption allowing both TradFi incumbents and crypto-native firms to experiment with blockchain technology without immediate full registration.
*   **Specific Applications:**
    *   **Automated Market Makers (AMMs):** Facilitating the trading of tokenized securities via decentralized liquidity pools.
    *   **Public Blockchains:** Supporting the use of permissionless blockchains for trading, provided certain conditions are met.
    *   **White-listing:** To ensure safety, buyers and sellers of tokenized securities under this exemption would likely undergo a white-listing process.
*   **Volume and Time Limits:** The exemption is intended to be a stepping stone, limiting trading volume and duration while the SEC gathers data to build permanent, "fit-for-purpose" rules.

### The 2024-2025 Regulatory Roadmap
Chair Atkins detailed several specific actions the Commission expects to address in the coming weeks and months:
*   **Investment Contract Framework:** Clarifying not only how an investment contract is formed but, crucially, how such a contract is **terminated**.
*   **Capital Raising:** Establishing "common-sense pathways" for firms to raise capital through the sale of crypto assets.
*   **Custody and Stablecoins:** Rulemaking regarding the custody of non-security crypto assets, specifically **payment stablecoins**, by broker-dealers.
*   **Transfer Agent Modernization:** Updating rules to accommodate the role of blockchain in corporate record-keeping and settlement.
*   **Exemptions for UI/UX:** Issuing no-action letters to clarify that wallets and other user interfaces are not subject to registration as exchanges or brokers.

### Integrating Technology into Compliance
*   **Embedded Compliance:** Atkins noted the potential to code regulatory requirements (such as lock-up periods or resale restrictions) directly into **smart contracts**.
*   **Privacy-Preserving Tech:** The SEC is exploring how **Zero-Knowledge Proofs (ZKPs)** can satisfy Bank Secrecy Act requirements without requiring users to relinquish their financial privacy to intermediaries.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** The agency views tokenization as a tool to shorten settlement cycles and automate complex corporate actions like dividend distributions and proxy voting.

### Market Volatility and "Schadenfreude"
*   **Regulatory Neutrality:** Chair Atkins emphasized that it is not the regulator's job to manage daily price swings; their sole focus is ensuring investors have the disclosures necessary to make informed decisions.
*   **Value Creation:** Commissioner Peirce warned the industry that "regulation is not the well from which value springs." Success depends on building useful products that people want, which in turn creates the political capital necessary to protect the industry in Washington.

> "Our rulebook should not be a barrier to innovation, but it should be a way to further our objectives of protecting investors, facilitating capital formation, and fostering fair, orderly, and efficient markets." — **Paul Atkins**

> "Regulation, if done wrong, can deny the American people benefits they otherwise would enjoy... I’d like to get to a place in which project developers would not fear to create tokens that carry some claim on revenue streams." — **Hester Peirce**

> "Put your nose to the grindstone and work to build things that really matter to people... That’s how you transform *Schadenfreude* into *Freudenfreude*—the joy of sharing in other people's success." — **Paul Atkins**
