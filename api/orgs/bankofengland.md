## AI Could Push Inflation Either Way, Says BOE’s Bailey

- Channel: Bloomberg Tech
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m1oGaHK38Cg
- Published: 2026-08-28
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260828.html#video-m1oGaHK38Cg
- Speakers: Andrew Bailey (Governor, Bank of England); Lisa Abramowicz (Host, Bloomberg Tech); Kevin Warsh (Former Member, Federal Reserve Board of Governors)

---

### TL;DR
Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey stated that AI could be either inflationary or disinflationary depending on whether demand shocks arrive before supply-side productivity gains. Speaking with Bloomberg's Lisa Abramowicz at Jackson Hole, Bailey also discussed UK inflation, defended a pluralistic committee approach, and emphasized that the BOE will make independent rate decisions regardless of Federal Reserve actions.

### AI and Economic Shocks
* **Dual Economic Impacts:** AI and robotics will generate both demand and supply shocks that affect price stability.
* **Sequencing Dictates Inflation Direction:** 
  * **Upward Pressure:** If demand shocks and related asset market effects outpace supply-side productivity gains, the net impact will be inflationary.
  * **Downward Pressure:** If productivity and supply-side efficiencies materialize first, the technology will prove disinflationary.
* **UK Productivity Need:** Bailey noted that the UK specifically requires faster growth and improved productivity, making AI and robotics a critical source for long-term economic gains.

### Monetary Policy Frameworks and Forward Guidance
* **Flaws of Forward Guidance:** Bailey agreed with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh that forward guidance can become "antiquated," overly complicated, and tend toward inflexible, unconditional statements that complicate policy—noting the 2021 rate-hiking delay as an example of challenges during massive shocks like COVID-19.
* **Conditional Decision-Making:** Monetary policy must look to the future due to the transmission lag of interest rates, but decisions remain inherently conditional amid ongoing uncertainty.
* **Decoupled Central Banks:** The Bank of England will base its rate decisions exclusively on UK domestic economic conditions and inflation outlooks, regardless of whether the Federal Reserve hikes rates.

### Committee Governance and Transparency
* **Pluralism and Deliberation:** Bailey defended the Bank of England's nine-member Monetary Policy Committee as a deliberate body designed for diverse viewpoints and rigorous debate.
* **Enhanced Transparency Innovations:** The BOE adopted recommendations from Ben Bernanke, introducing transparency measures such as paragraphs detailing individual members' views alongside a clear majority consensus statement.
* **Rejecting Binary Labels:** Bailey declined to label himself a hawk or a dove, pointing out that second-round inflation effects in the UK remain relatively subdued due to a softening labor market.

### Structural and Fiscal Challenges
* **Balance Sheet Management:** Central bank balance sheet reduction is a complex global challenge; Bailey noted the importance of retaining future policy scope while reducing interest rate risk.
* **Long-Term Macro Pressures:** Developed economies face interconnected pressures outside direct monetary control, including aging populations and the reversal of post-Cold War defense expenditure dividends, which are impacting fiscal policy and debt levels.

### Notable Quotes
> "Monetary policy by its nature is in the business of making policy forwards. We can't influence what inflation is gonna be tomorrow. We can influence what inflation is gonna be at points further into the future." — **Andrew Bailey**

> "The whole reason we have nine members on the committee is to have a plurality, to have different views, to have deliberation, which is absolutely key to this process." — **Andrew Bailey**

> "If you get the demand shocks ahead of the supply shocks, then you will get pressure on inflation upwards... If you get the supply shocks first, you'll get the opposite." — **Andrew Bailey**

**TL;DR** 
At the Reykjavik economic conference, central bankers from the UK and US emphasized that persistent inflation—exacerbated by Middle East energy shocks and structural shifts like AI and aging demographics—requires highly flexible, state-contingent monetary frameworks and prolonged "higher-for-longer" interest rate environments.

---

### Geopolitical Energy Shocks and the Bank of England's Response

* **UK Inflation Miss:** Bank of England (BoE) Governor Andrew Bailey reported that UK inflation hit 2.8% in April, missing the 2% target by 0.8 percentage points due to fuel and household energy price spikes caused by the Middle East conflict. 
* **The "Lambda" ($\lambda$) Policy Trade-off:** Bailey detailed how the BoE manages the trade-off between inflation and output volatility using constrained discretion:
  * **Direct Effects (High $\lambda$):** Monetary policy should "look through" temporary, direct energy price spikes to avoid unnecessary output volatility.
  * **Indirect Effects (Moderate $\lambda$):** A more active response is required to prevent protracted indirect costs from keeping inflation elevated.
  * **Second-Round Effects (Low $\lambda$):** Tolerance is minimal if wage-price spirals or elevated inflation expectations begin to take root.
* **Scenarios Replacing Central Projections:** Due to extreme unpredictability in the Middle East, the BoE has suspended its standard "central projection" in favor of three distinct economic scenarios (A, B, and C) to guide policy decisions.
* **Rate Decision and Financial Impact:** The BoE made an active choice to hold its policy rate at 3.75%, taking rate cuts off the table for the immediate future. 
  * **Mortgage Rate Hikes:** Key quoted rates for 2-year and 5-year fixed mortgages in the UK have risen by approximately 1% since the conflict began.
  * **Labor Market Shift:** Unlike the highly tight and hard-to-read labor market of 2022, current data shows a gradually and predictably softening UK labor market.

---

### Regional Fed Insights: Oil, AI, and Demographics

* **The Power of Regionalism:** Jeffrey Smith, President of the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, highlighted that the Fed’s 12-district system prevents monetary policy from being dominated solely by Wall Street and Washington.
* **US Energy Efficiency:** Although Middle East tensions have impaired roughly 10% of global oil production, the US economy is far more resilient to oil shocks than it was in the 1970s:
  * **Efficiency Gains:** The US economy requires only 3,000 BTUs of energy to produce $1 of GDP today, compared to 12,000 BTUs in the 1970s.
  * **Terms of Trade:** Thanks to the shale revolution, the US is now a net oil exporter, meaning wealth transfers from higher prices remain domestic rather than flowing abroad.
* **The Looming Demographic Shift:** The US is facing a major demographic inflection point, with the population aged 75 and older set to double from 6% to 12% between 2020 and 2040.
  * **Healthcare Jobs Surge:** The healthcare and social assistance sector added 650,000 jobs over a 12-month period, while non-healthcare employment actually contracted.
  * **Downward Demographics Risk:** Rising demand for elder-care aids could pull workers from the child-care sector, driving up child-care costs and creating a negative demographic loop.
* **AI and the Labor Force:** Smith noted that AI is not actively replacing workers or depressing hiring. Instead, businesses are leveraging AI, automation, and robotics to offset the rapid attrition of retiring baby boomers (roughly 300,000 workers aging out of the workforce monthly).

---

### Formulating a Flexible Monetary Framework at the Federal Reserve

* **The Dual Mandate Balance:** Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman outlined a flexible framework that places equal weight on price stability and maximum employment, arguing that policymakers must focus on whichever objective deviates furthest from its target.
* **Key Policy Indicators:** Bowman monitors private domestic final purchases (excluding volatile components of GDP) to gauge true aggregate demand, alongside average payroll gains and core PCE inflation (excluding temporary tariff effects).
* **Decisions on Rate Hikes and Cuts:** Bowman explained the logic behind her historical and potential future votes:
  * **Rate Hikes (2022–2023):** Strongly supported aggressive tightening when demand-side stimulus and post-pandemic supply constraints drove US inflation to a 40-year high.
  * **Rate Cuts (Late 2025):** Dissented in July 2025 to advocate for a proactive 25 basis point cut to protect a fragile labor market, subsequently supporting 75 basis points in cumulative cuts to ease the policy stance from restrictive toward neutral.
* **Current US Economic Health:** Real GDP continues to grow at a moderate-to-solid pace, supported by heavy AI-related business investments. However, the labor market remains vulnerable with unemployment at 4.3% (as of April), and energy-driven price increases require continued vigilance.

## WATCH LIVE: BOE Governor Andrew Bailey to speak after rate decision

**TL;DR**  
The Bank of England held its benchmark interest rate steady at 3.75% in an 8-1 vote, taking previous expectations of rate cuts off the table due to Middle East conflict-driven energy price volatility. To navigate this uncertainty, the bank introduced three distinct economic scenarios ranging from a rapid return to its 2% inflation target to a severe 6% inflation spike that would require aggressive interest rate hikes.

---

### The Rate Decision and the "Active Hold"

* **The 8-1 Rate Vote:** The Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) voted to maintain the bank rate at 3.75%. 
    * **The Dissenting Voice:** Chief Economist Huw Pill was the sole hawk, voting instead for a 25 basis point hike to 4.0%.
    * **Market Relief:** Victoria Clarke (Chief UK Economist at Santander) noted that the 8-1 split came as a sigh of relief for markets that feared a more aggressive hawkish shift.
* **An "Active Hold" Stance:** Governor Andrew Bailey emphasized that holding the rate is an active, deliberate policy decision rather than a passive "wait-and-see" approach. 
* **Rate Cuts Off the Table:** The MPC officially abandoned its previous projections for interest rate cuts this year. Bailey confirmed that the "headroom" previously reserved for cuts will instead be used to absorb the inflationary impact of the geopolitical shock.

### Three Economic Scenarios for the UK

Because of the extreme volatility of global oil and gas prices, the Bank of England departed from its traditional single forecast to present three illustrative scenarios:

* **Scenario A (Benign Case):** Assumes energy prices follow market futures curves downward and consumer demand drops sharply due to weak sentiment.
    * **Inflation Outcome:** No second-round wage-price effects occur, and inflation falls below the 2% target by the end of next year without further rate hikes.
* **Scenario B (Moderate/Base Case):** Assumes more persistent energy price pressures due to slow repairs of damaged gas infrastructure in the Gulf.
    * **Inflation Outcome:** Modest second-round effects are felt, but inflation gradually returns to the 2% target within two to three years. Reversing out the previously assumed 55 basis point market rate cuts is expected to accommodate this scenario.
* **Scenario C (Severe Shock Case):** Assumes prolonged disruptions pushing oil to $130 a barrel and gas to 210 pence per therm, alongside strong wage-price feedback loops.
    * **Inflation Outcome:** Inflation peaks above 6% in early 2027 and remains above the 2% target after three years.
    * **Policy Response:** This scenario would require a materially tighter monetary policy, potentially pushing interest rates to 5.25% or higher, and would likely trigger a mild recession.

### Labor Market and Wage Dynamics

* **Labor Market Slack:** The MPC noted that the UK labor market has significantly more slack now than during the 2022 energy crisis. This weakness is expected to lower employee bargaining power and prevent aggressive wage-price spirals.
    * **Unemployment Outlook:** The unemployment rate recently dipped toward 5% but is projected to rise back to 6% under a severe shock scenario.
* **Private vs. Public Sector Pay:** 
    * Private sector wage growth is moderating toward levels comfortable for the bank.
    * Public sector pay remains elevated and represents a key domestic metric to watch.
* **Indirect Energy Impacts on Food:** High energy costs are expected to feed indirectly into food production and distribution. While short-term forecasts project food inflation at 4.6% through September, reports from Bank of England agents suggest it could climb to a range of 6% to 7% by the end of the year.

### Market Conditions and Quantitative Tightening

* **Tighter Financial Conditions:** Financial markets have already responded to the geopolitical crisis, pushing 10-year gilt yields comfortably above 5%.
* **Commodity Volatility:** Bailey highlighted the extreme volatility facing policymakers, pointing out that the Brent crude oil price fluctuated by over $10 a barrel in a single morning.
* **Quantitative Tightening (QT) Here to Stay:** Victoria Clarke observed that the Bank of England is highly unlikely to pause its QT program unless the UK experiences an extremely severe episode of gilt market stress, and even then, any pause would only be temporary.
