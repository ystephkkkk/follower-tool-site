## Prosus CEO: Why AI Is Still Under-hyped and What to Do About It | Podcast | In Good Company

- Channel: Norges Bank Investment Management
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yU68BbLp1Y0

- Speakers: Fabricio Bloisi (CEO, Prosus); Nicolai Tangen (CEO, Norges Bank Investment Management)

**TL;DR**  
Prosus CEO Fabricio Bloisi is driving a radical transformation of the global tech giant into an "agentic lifestyle ecosystem," utilizing a fast-paced "Jet Ski" innovation model and AI-first operations. Bloisi believes AI remains fundamentally under-hyped and aims to scale Prosus from its current footprint into a half-trillion-dollar global powerhouse over the next decade.

---

### ## The Prosus Ecosystem and Regional Strategies

Prosus operates as an active, hands-on operational technology builder—not a passive investor—reaching over 1.5 billion customers across three core regions:

*   **Latin America (The Mature Ecosystem):** Led by iFood, which has grown from a $5 million acquisition doing 20,000 orders per month to a 10,000-person powerhouse handling 200 million orders per month. 
    *   **Cross-Sector Synergy:** Much like China's tech giants (Tencent, Alibaba), Prosus integrates beverage delivery, pet shops, payments, meal vouchers, grocery, and travel (such as Despegar, which doubled its growth rate post-acquisition) into a single ecosystem.
*   **India (The High-Growth Frontier):** Bloisi views India's current landscape as highly reminiscent of China's market 25 years ago. 
    *   **Key Assets:** Prosus operates the leading payment gateway PayU, the major food delivery platform Swiggy, and has recently backed ride-hailing company Rapido and travel business Ixigo. 
    *   **Financial Integration:** The company is deploying cross-platform micro-credit and frictionless payment services across its 20 Indian portfolio companies to build a unified commerce model.
*   **Europe (The Tech-First Pivot):** Prosus recently acquired Just Eat with the explicit goal of transforming it from a traditional food-delivery business into an AI-first technology company, supported by a newly established AI lab in Amsterdam.

---

### ## The "Ambidextrous" Management Model and "Jet Skis"

Bloisi attributes his success to balancing institutional scale with extreme, decentralized entrepreneurial speed:

*   **The "Jet Ski" Innovation Model:** To prevent the bureaucratic stagnation of typical corporate budgeting, Prosus launches "Jet Skis"—autonomous, 5-to-10-person teams empowered to operate like independent startups.
    *   **Iterative Failure:** These small teams are given the mandate to fail fast and test dozens of iterations without waiting for massive budgets or annual planning cycles. Once a "Jet Ski" finds a successful path, Prosus deploys its massive corporate scale to institutionalize it.
*   **Ambidextrous Organizations:** Bloisi stresses that a successful tech giant must simultaneously respect financial discipline and operational scale (the "boring" management) while fiercely protecting and funding disruptive innovators.
*   **Radical Talent Philosophy:** Bloisi actively seeks out entrepreneurs who thrive under extreme uncertainty and big goals. He practices radical empowerment but pairs it with a clear-cut willingness to part ways with underperforming staff.

---

### ## The Under-Hyped AI Revolution and "Agentic" Operations

Bloisi strongly rejects the notion that AI is over-hyped, arguing that the technological leaps achieved between late 2023 and mid-2024 are profoundly misunderstood by non-technical executives.

*   **Autonomous Organizations:** AI is moving past simple individual productivity enhancements (such as drafting emails) toward fully autonomous workflows where custom-built agents run corporate operations for days at a time.
*   **AI-Driven Efficiencies at iFood:** iFood’s core operations—including landing page personalization, customer push notifications, anti-fraud measures, and CRM—are entirely automated by AI, resulting in higher customer satisfaction (NPS) and reduced marketing spend.
*   **"Tukan" Platform and Executive Coding:** Prosus built "Tukan," an internal platform allowing non-technical employees to build custom AI agents. Bloisi mandated that all portfolio CEOs take software development courses to learn how to automate their own workflows, moving the company from monthly to weekly operating cycles.
*   **The Agentic Lifestyle Ecosystem:** Prosus’s primary R&D focus is building "life assistants"—autonomous agents that do not just text with users but securely execute real-world financial transactions, such as booking flights, ordering food, and hailing rides on behalf of the consumer.

---

### ## Geopolitical and Regulatory Friction in Europe

Despite his optimism, Bloisi expressed deep frustration with Europe’s regulatory framework, which he argues actively stifles the continent's tech sector:

*   **Forced Divestments:** European regulators forced Prosus to sell its stake in Delivery Hero, despite Bloisi’s expressed willingness to invest $10 billion into building a European tech champion.
*   **Disadvantage to Domestic Players:** Bloisi notes that while the US, Chinese, and Indian governments actively foster and protect their domestic tech champions, European M&A regulations hinder local consolidation. This creates a vacuum that allows American and Asian tech giants to dominate European markets.

## Young Turks Reloaded In Davos | India, A.I. & The New Global Order

- Channel: CNBC-TV18
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JhT21QBQZIU
- Published: 2026-02-09
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260209.html#video-JhT21QBQZIU
- Speakers: Fabricio Bloisi (CEO, Prosus)

---

**TL;DR**
Davos 2026 highlights a shift toward a distributed global order where India is emerging as a primary engine for AI innovation and Europe is rapidly remilitarizing through deep-tech partnerships. Global investors are moving beyond speculative AI toward "agentic" applications and physical robotics, signaled by massive capital inflows into companies building foundational "brains" for autonomous systems.

### India’s Growth and Prosus’s Strategic Pivot
* **Investment Outlook:** Fabricio Bloisi (CEO, Prosus) emphasized that India is a top-three global market alongside Europe and Latin America, citing a 7.5% GDP growth rate and its position as the world's fourth-largest economy.
    * **Portfolio Value Goal:** Prosus aims for its Indian portfolio companies to reach a collective valuation of $50 billion within the next four years, up from its current $10 billion investment base.
    * **Profitability Milestones:** PayU is now profitable in India; Swiggy’s core food delivery business is profitable, though its "Quick Commerce" arm continues to require investment for market expansion.
* **AI Infrastructure:** Prosus is collaborating with the Indian government on the "AI for All" initiative.
    * **R&D Expansion:** Following the success of its AI house in Amsterdam, Prosus plans to launch a dedicated AI Lab and R&D center in India in 2026 to train local language models.
* **IPO Momentum:** Following a successful 2025 featuring IPOs for Meesho, Urban Company, and ixigo, Prosus anticipates several more Indian portfolio companies will go public in 2026.

### The Rise of Physical AI and General-Purpose Robotics
* **The "Brain" Gap:** Deepak Pathak (Co-founder, Skild AI) identified the lack of a versatile "brain" as the primary reason robots are not yet ubiquitous. Skild AI is developing "omni-bodied intelligence" to allow one AI model to control any hardware—from humanoids to quadrupeds.
* **Capital Intensity:** Skild AI recently closed a $1.4 billion Series C funding round at a valuation exceeding $14 billion, backed by Nvidia, Softbank, and Jeff Bezos.
* **Target Markets:** 
    * **Near-term:** Enterprise applications including manufacturing, inspection, data centers, and delivery.
    * **Long-term:** Consumer and home robotics to address labor shortages and aging populations.
* **India’s Hardware Opportunity:** India is well-positioned to become a robotics hardware hub by leveraging its existing automotive and electronics supply chain expertise.

### Defense-Tech and Global Security Innovation
* **Satellite Disruption:** Rafal Modrzewski (CEO & Co-founder, ICEYE) highlighted a shift in military intelligence using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) satellites. ICEYE has launched 64 satellites to provide real-time global monitoring.
* **Strategic Speed:** A recent 1.4 billion euro contract with the German government (Rheinmetall) will deploy a 40-satellite constellation within just two years—a fraction of the traditional decade-long procurement cycle.
    * **Tactical Advantage:** This "swarm" technology allows for troop monitoring every 20 minutes, compared to the previous 24-hour revisit rates.
* **Funding Climate:** The European defense-tech sector has seen a "transformational shift" since the onset of the war in Ukraine. Previously "unimaginable" funding is now available as Europe seeks to remilitarize and bypass traditional supply chain bottlenecks.

### Investment Discipline in Deep Tech
* **Valuation Skepticism:** While acknowledging that some AI companies are overvalued, Fabricio Bloisi argued that AI is not a bubble, comparing the current era to the internet in 2000—a foundational shift that will redefine all business processes over the next decade.
* **Founder Mentality:**
    * **Capital Management:** Founders warned against "spending sprees" after large raises. Rafal Modrzewski noted that while deep tech requires massive capital, discipline is required to avoid "toxic traits" associated with over-funding.
    * **First Principles:** Building successful deep-tech companies requires solving fundamental science problems rather than chasing financial exits.

> "AI over 10 years is going to change how everything works. It's like discussing in 2000, 'is the internet overvalued?' Probably some companies are, but in five to 10 years, everyone is going to run all their process through AI." — **Fabricio Bloisi**

> "Our dream is just any robot, any task, one brain. We would like to be the brain... like Android governs smartphones, Skild would power the brain behind [all robots]." — **Deepak Pathak**

> "We focus on disrupting the field of intelligence... we are really looking forward to deploy such large intelligence systems that nation-states would have enough information to know about threats before they happen." — **Rafal Modrzewski**
