## CNBC Squawk Box with Brad Gerstner - November 19th, 2026

- Speakers: Brad Gerstner (CEO, Altimeter Capital); Andrew Ross Sorkin (Host, CNBC Squawk Box); Jensen Huang (CEO, Nvidia)

---

**TL;DR:** Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner joined CNBC's *Squawk Box* to discuss Anthropic's landmark IPO—in which Altimeter placed a $5 billion order—arguing that the company's 10x growth, $50 billion in GAAP revenue, and recursive compute efficiency prove frontier AI labs can be extraordinarily profitable. Gerstner also addressed the abrupt ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, urging the Nvidia board not to rush to judgment amid shifting US-China chip export policies, and expressed support for the industrial logic of a potential SpaceX-Tesla combination.

---

### Anthropic's Historic IPO & Capital Efficiency
* **Record Institutional Demand:** Altimeter Capital placed a $5 billion order for Anthropic's IPO—more than five times the largest order the firm has ever placed in an IPO.
  * Gerstner noted that demand held up despite a difficult market backdrop, including a recent election that raised concerns about data centers and the ouster of Jensen Huang as CEO of Nvidia.
  * He praised the IPO for democratizing access for retail investors and celebrating American risk capital markets.
* **Pricing & Valuation Trajectory:** Responding to Andrew Ross Sorkin's question about whether lowering talk from $2 trillion to $1.3 trillion helped drive demand, Gerstner clarified that Anthropic never stated a $2 trillion figure.
  * In April and May, when the semiconductor index was up nearly 100% in two and a half months, hype pushed market chatter higher; since then, a lot of gas has come out of the market.
  * Anthropic's last private round in May priced at $900 billion, meaning today's IPO price represents a 50% increase in a relatively flat market.
* **Unprecedented Capital Efficiency:** Anthropic is raising over $100 billion in the IPO, bringing its total cash reserve to $220–$230 billion.
  * The company has only burned $9 billion in its entire history while growing over 10x this year.

### Financials, Margins & Compute Moats
* **Revenue & Headcount Leverage:** Anthropic generated roughly $50 billion in GAAP revenue this year and $100 billion in run-rate revenue.
  * Gerstner contrasted this with Google, which reached $100 billion in revenue with 105,000 employees, whereas Anthropic achieved it with 5,000 employees.
  * At $500,000 per employee, that 100,000-employee difference represents $50 billion in annual savings that can be reinvested into compute.
* **Margin Resilience:** Addressing Sorkin's question on whether cheaper models from OpenAI, Microsoft, Meta, xAI, and Chinese open-source labs will erode frontier margins, Gerstner stated the market needs both frontier and cheaper models.
  * Despite token costs falling this year amid fierce competition, Anthropic's margins have increased.
* **Recursive Compute Efficiency:** Gerstner highlighted Anthropic's "compute multipliers" revealed in its S-1 filing, noting that Claude is now in a recursive loop creating algorithmic improvements that yield more tokens per watt.
  * Over the last 9 months, only 26% of Anthropic's compute—about 500 megawatts—went toward inference to serve customers ($50 billion GAAP / $100 billion run-rate revenue).
  * Because inference requires relatively little power, Anthropic and OpenAI will be able to serve all their customers next year even if data center buildouts slow down; additional compute is primarily needed for frontier training.

### Nvidia, Jensen Huang & US-China Chip Policy
* **Board Scrutiny Over Huang's Ouster:** Following the abrupt push for Jensen Huang to resign last Friday, Gerstner wrote a letter to Nvidia's board urging them to take their time and get all the facts.
  * Gerstner agreed with Elon Musk and Palmer Luckey that removing Huang was "crazy" unless he violated national security laws, emphasizing that no founder is above the law.
  * He warned that if there was no violation of law, shareholders and stakeholders will have a strong appetite to go after the board for rushing to judgment.
* **MegaSpeed & DOJ Investigation:** Becky Quick raised questions about whether MegaSpeed acted as a front to siphon chips to China and why the Singapore revenue line disappeared from Nvidia's earnings release last year.
  * Gerstner pointed to a Commerce Department release showing the DOJ has been investigating the matter for 14 months, naming 17 defendants—none from Nvidia or Huang—and noted Nvidia placed MegaSpeed on its do-not-sell list over a year ago.
* **Divided Administration Policy:** Responding to Joe Kernen and Sorkin on Washington's stance, Gerstner described a legitimate policy debate between "China hawks" (citing Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Marco Rubio, Jamieson Greer, and Jay Clayton) and "China constructivists" (citing Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, AI czar David Sacks, Tim Cook, Michael Dell, and Elon Musk).
  * While it remains legal to sell H200s to China (though Nvidia voluntarily stopped on its earnings call), rules were tightened earlier this week to require additional licenses for data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, and Singapore.
  * Gerstner noted that President Trump appears to be letting the two camps have a "cage match" as a trade negotiation tactic ahead of his trip to China, and implied Trump sees no reason not to bring Huang back.

### SpaceX, Tesla & Industry Partnerships
* **Anthropic & SpaceX/xAI:** Gerstner dismissed rumors that Elon Musk would cancel Anthropic's compute deal or hike spot prices, noting that both Musk and Anthropic's Dario Amodei and Tom Brown view it as an important partnership.
  * He pointed out that Grokbot is already using Anthropic to power much of its activity and expects the two companies to collaborate more in the future.
* **Potential SpaceX-Tesla Combination:** Gerstner backed the industrial logic of combining SpaceX and Tesla to unite massive data repositories, Terrafab, chipmaking, and the AI stack.
  * On Sorkin's question about Musk's Tesla compensation package, Gerstner stressed that any deal would be an arms-length transaction requiring Musk to recuse himself and would require a "major premium" for Tesla shareholders.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Anthropic 2027–2028 Revenue & Valuation:** Consensus estimates from Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, and Altimeter project Anthropic will reach $140–$150 billion in GAAP revenue next year (2027) and $270–$300 billion in GAAP revenue in 2028; applying a 10x multiple at the end of 2027 to 2028 numbers implies roughly $3 trillion in total enterprise value. — *Brad Gerstner*
* **Customer Inference Capacity:** Anthropic and OpenAI will be "absolutely fine" to serve all of their customers next year even if the amount of new data centers and compute comes down. — *Brad Gerstner*
* **Anthropic–SpaceX/xAI Partnership:** Anthropic will continue buying a lot of compute from SpaceX, and the two companies will do more partnerships together in the future rather than less. — *Brad Gerstner*
