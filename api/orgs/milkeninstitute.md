## Conversation with Pershing Square Capital Management Founder & CEO Bill Ackman | GIS Mexico City '26

- Channel: Milken Institute
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f2Geqbsiagc
- Published: 2026-10-29
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261029.html#video-f2Geqbsiagc
- Speakers: Bill Ackman (Founder and CEO, Pershing Square Capital Management); Richard Ditizio (CEO, Milken Institute)

---

#### TL;DR
Bill Ackman identifies the artificial intelligence revolution as the definitive economic driver of 2026–2028, remaining highly bullish on compute demand and capital sustainability while noting that high debt levels will keep interest rates persistently high. Richard Ditizio moderates the conversation at the Milken Institute Global Investors' Symposium in Mexico City.

### The Macro Landscape: AI Infrastructure, Debt, and Rates
- **AI as the Primary Growth Engine:** Bill Ackman highlights that the global economy is changing rapidly via AI, approaching super intelligence as the cost of intelligence falls and democratizes.
- **Sustainability of Infrastructure Spending:** Acknowledges that the largest tech companies will spend $650 billion to $750 billion on AI infrastructure using borrowed money, but argues this is entirely sustainable because the return on investment (ROI) on compute far outstrips the cost of capital.
- **Hyperscalers vs. Neo-Cloud Risk:** Ackman expresses confidence in major hyperscalers due to their creditworthiness and financial resources, while warning that pure-play neo-cloud companies resemble highly leveraged real estate developers and carry significantly higher risk.
- **Sovereign Capital Competition:** Notes that companies like SpaceX (with Elon Musk projecting massive gigawatt data center buildouts) may need to borrow up to $1 trillion, competing directly with sovereign levels for debt and keeping rates persistently high.
- **Geopolitical Opportunities:** Points to potential resolutions in Middle Eastern and Russian conflicts as possible catalysts for a global peace dividend and lower energy prices that could reduce inflationary pressures.
- **Global Political Risks:** Highlights the socialist state model as unsustainable over the long term, citing France spending 59% of GDP on social programs alongside ongoing civil unrest as a major threat to global prosperity.

### Portfolio Strategy, Public Equities, and Mexico
- **Stock Selection and Valuation Anomalies:** Notes that Pershing Square owns companies like Uber and Amazon, pointing out that Uber trades at 16 times earnings with projected earnings growth of 35% over the next year and 25% per annum over the next five years.
- **Autonomous Vehicle Competition:** Rejects the market fear that Tesla’s cyber cab will monopolize autonomous transport, predicting instead roughly a dozen autonomous vehicle companies competing to offer consumers the cheapest ride.
- **Internet Bubble Comparison:** Distinguishes the current AI wave from the 1999 internet bubble by noting that foundational AI companies like Anthropic, Cognition, and Databricks are scaling at unprecedented rates with massive, profitable business models unlike "pets.com".
- **Mexican Market Potential:** Acknowledges that the Mexican stock market is cheap and contains interesting gems—mentioning a special situation pitched to him recently—but explains that Pershing Square rarely invests locally because family-controlled companies limit public float and liquidity for multi-billion-dollar investments.

### Philanthropy and the Hackman Oxman Institute
- **Catalyst for Medical Innovation:** Following his daughter Lucy's survival of a severe brain hemorrhage with massive swelling, Ackman and his family committed $400 million (now nearing $800 million) to establish the Hackman Oxman Institute.
- **Incentive Flaws in Healthcare:** Points out that healthcare systems heavily incentivize high-margin surgeries ($40,000 for neurosurgery in minimal square footage) while severely underfunding rehabilitation, which requires large space and poor reimbursement rates.
- **Institute Scope and Vision:** Led by Mount Sinai neurosurgeon Chris Kellner, the institute acquired a 3.4-acre site in New York City with a brand-new biotech building to eventually scale 2 to 2.5 million square feet.
- **Cross-Disciplinary Approach:** Combines neurosurgery, brain rehabilitation, longevity, and technology development—recruiting top-tier neuroscientists, partnering with Formula 1 response trainers, and deploying elite athletes and Navy SEALs alongside patients.
- **Brain-Computer Interfaces:** Reveals that the institute has already deployed nearly $100 million into brain-computer interface and device development, signaling an underinvested space poised for rapid growth.

## Brookfield's Bruce Flatt discusses the global markets at the Milken Institute Global Conference

- Channel: Brookfield
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D-yafROdlQ4
- Published: 2026-05-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260507.html#video-D-yafROdlQ4
- Speakers: Bruce Flatt (CEO, Brookfield Corporation); Michael Milken (Chairman, Milken Institute); Larry Fink (CEO, BlackRock)

---

**TL;DR**
The global economy is entering a $10 trillion "rewiring" phase driven by AI and digital infrastructure needs, shifting the investment landscape from traditional assets to long-term "continuity" assets managed by private capital firms rather than debt-constrained governments.

### The $10 Trillion Infrastructure "Rewiring"
*   **Scale of Investment:** Bruce Flatt (CEO, Brookfield) estimates a $10 trillion global expenditure over the next decade to build out power grids, AI factories, data centers, and fiber networks. 
*   **Infrastructure Pivot:** The global economy is transitioning from legacy networks (highways and railways) to a digital backbone. 50% of the assets Brookfield owns today did not exist as investable asset classes 15 years ago.
*   **Supply Shortages vs. AI Bubble:** Larry Fink (CEO, BlackRock) argues that we are in a supply shortage rather than a bubble. The U.S. is currently short on power, compute capacity, chips, and memory. 
*   **New Security Paradigms:** The rise of drone warfare is necessitating a reevaluation of physical security for infrastructure, potentially moving critical $50B–$75B data center investments underground to protect against domestic and international threats.

### Stability and the Private Capital Advantage
*   **Asset-Liability Matching:** Michael Milken highlighted that recent bank failures (Silicon Valley Bank, First Republic) resulted from mismatched liabilities. In contrast, both Brookfield and BlackRock report that less than 1% of their managed assets are redeemable on a quarterly basis.
*   **The Role of Private Markets:** With governments facing mounting deficits, the private sector is now the primary engine for massive infrastructure builds. Long-term private capital acts as a stable intermediary that the two-year capital structure of the banking system cannot support.
*   **Solution-Based Investing:** Large firms are moving away from selling "products" and toward "solutions." This includes bespoke vehicles, such as one mentioned by Flatt that locks in capital for 27 years to match long-tail institutional liabilities.

### Global Market Democratization and Retirement
*   **Shift to Self-Directed Retirement:** BlackRock is working with countries like Saudi Arabia and India to move away from government-sponsored pensions toward personal, defined-contribution plans.
*   **The Japanese Catalyst:** Fink noted that the Japanese stock market's recent surge was directly tied to the doubling of tax exemptions for "NISA" (self-directed retirement accounts), proving that domestic capital participation drives market valuation.
*   **Wages vs. Capital:** Fink warns that in an AI-driven world, wages will not keep pace with the growth of capital. Broad economic success now requires getting individual citizens to invest in capital markets rather than keeping money in low-interest bank accounts.

### The Strategy of Long-Term Compounding
*   **The "Miracle" of 12%:** Bruce Flatt emphasized that consistent 12% compounding over long periods is more valuable than chasing volatile 35% returns. The goal is to own "great businesses" for decades rather than churning assets.
*   **Consolidation of Winners:** The panel predicted a "K-shaped" economy where one or two winners dominate each industry due to the massive CAPEX required to integrate AI. 
*   **Institutional Scale:** The era of $100 billion funds is ending; the market is moving toward a landscape of 30+ funds managing over $1 trillion each. These "mega-funds" require "continuity assets" they can hold indefinitely to avoid the friction of constant asset churning.

### Notable Quotes
> "We are rewiring the global economy to lay the networks that we laid before, which were highways, utilities, railways. And now we're laying cloud, artificial intelligence factories, and data centers." — **Bruce Flatt, CEO of Brookfield Corporation**

> "There is not an AI bubble. There is the opposite. We have supply shortages. Demand is growing much faster than anyone had ever anticipated." — **Larry Fink, CEO of BlackRock**

> "Having your money in a bank account is one of the worst financial decisions of a lifetime... we are not going to be able to broaden economic success only by wages because wages in this AI world are not going to grow as fast as the potential of the AI growth." — **Larry Fink, CEO of BlackRock**

> "The greatest miracle of investing, finance, in fact, almost everything, is compounding... if you can earn north of 12%, nothing else matters. Don't try to earn 35%." — **Bruce Flatt, CEO of Brookfield Corporation**
