## Stripe's John Collison on How Agentic Commerce Will Reshape the Internet | Odd Lots

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QhGqLqCzFac
- Published: 2026-05-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260516.html#video-QhGqLqCzFac
- Speakers: John Collison (Co-Founder, Stripe); Joe Weisenthal (Host, Bloomberg Odd Lots); Tracy Alloway (Host, Bloomberg Odd Lots)

---

**TL;DR** 
Agentic commerce represents the transition from manual online transactions to AI agents researching and purchasing on behalf of humans, promising a massive reduction in "friction" and a significant surge in new business formation and economic dynamism.

### The Definition and Mechanics of Agentic Commerce
John Collison defines "agentic commerce" simply as AI buying things for you, split into two primary categories:
*   **Consumer-Facing Commerce:** AI handles the "research-to-purchase" pipeline. This includes everything from finding a niche bike case to booking a recipe’s ingredients.
*   **B2B and Developer Commerce:** Agents handle technical transactions autonomously. 
    *   **The Cloudflare Example:** A developer can now use an agent to register, buy, and host a domain name via Stripe’s agreement with Cloudflare, bypassing manual DNS configurations and form-filling.
*   **The Friction Reduction Path:** The history of payments is the elimination of friction (Credit Cards → PayPal → Apple Pay → Agents). 
    *   **Agentic Checkout:** Stripe is working with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Meta to build the "wiring" that allows agents to securely use saved credentials without "flinging" credit card data across the web.

### Reshaping Discovery and Consumer Behavior
The shift toward agents changes how products are found and marketed, moving away from legacy "keyword search" which Collison describes as "ridiculous" for the modern era.
*   **The End of Keyword Search:** AI allows for constraint-based searches (e.g., "Find a furniture piece exactly this wide") that traditional search boxes cannot handle.
*   **Surfacing Niche Brands:** Agents are proving to be "tasteful" and effective at finding high-quality, smaller merchants that are often buried by traditional SEO aggregators.
*   **Human-in-the-Loop:** For high-stakes or high-enjoyment purchases (e.g., fashion or vacation planning), humans will remain the final decider. AI serves as the researcher and executive assistant rather than a total replacement.
*   **Optimizing for Agents:** Retailers must move toward "machine-readable" catalogs. This requires providing real-time data on stock (SKUs) and pricing so agents don't rely on outdated training data.

### The "End of Free Roaming" and Micro-transactions
A major shift in internet economics is expected as AI agents change the value of data and the viability of advertising.
*   **The Pay-by-the-Sip Model:** Micro-transactions, which failed for humans due to "mental load," are viable for agents. 
    *   **Example:** An agent could pay 40 cents for a specific Bloomberg data request or a few cents for a single API call without the human needing to sign up for a full subscription.
*   **Stablecoins in Commerce:** Collison highlights stablecoins as a potential solution for low-cost B2B transactions, allowing agents to pay for resources (like server time) instantly without traditional banking relationships.
*   **Impact on Advertising:** While agentic research might hurt "crummy aggregator pages" (e.g., top-10 listicles with heavy ads), Collison believes brand advertising will persist because human preference still drives the final decision in most commerce.

### Economic Dynamism and Internal AI Adoption
Collison notes that the "intelligence explosion" is already manifesting in Stripe’s internal and external data.
*   **Surge in Entrepreneurship:** New business creation on Stripe in Q1 2025 was up **71% year-over-year**. The barrier to starting a company is lower because "intelligence" (via AI) is now a recruitable resource.
*   **Internal Productivity at Stripe:**
    *   **Software Engineering:** High adoption. Many bugs at Stripe are now fixed via a "Fix this bug" button where an agent writes and commits the code autonomously.
    *   **Sales/GTM:** Highly measurable productivity gains, though specific causality is difficult to isolate.
    *   **Legal/Finance:** Harder to automate due to the "innumeracy" of current LLMs; they can build beautiful spreadsheets where the numbers are fundamentally wrong.
*   **The "TSA Pre for Bots":** Stripe is developing protocols to identify "good bots" to ensure they aren't blocked by traditional anti-bot security measures while attempting to make purchases.

## How a Former Fed Vice-Chair Is thinking About the Next Fed Chair | Odd Lots

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TXmKePGHwJQ
- Published: 2026-02-09
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260209.html#video-TXmKePGHwJQ
- Speakers: Kevin Warsh (Former Governor, Federal Reserve); Joe Weisenthal (Host, Bloomberg Odd Lots); Tracy Alloway (Host, Bloomberg Odd Lots)

---

**TL;DR**
Former Fed Vice Chair Richard Clarida views Kevin Warsh as a sensible pick for Fed Chair who will prioritize supply-side economics and a collaborative relationship with the Treasury. While Warsh may seek to roll back "modern" communication tools like forward guidance, his influence will be tempered by the Fed’s committee structure and the market’s reaction to potential inflation risks.

### The Strategy Behind the Warsh Nomination
* **Treasury-Fed Synergy:** Richard Clarida describes Warsh as a "sensible choice," noting his likely strong working relationship with Treasury Secretary nominee Scott Bessent. 
    * **Coordination Areas:** Clarida emphasizes that while the Fed is independent, it must collaborate with the Treasury on bank regulation (working with the OCC and FDIC) and acting as the government's fiscal agent to ensure Treasury market liquidity.
    * **The "Breakfast" Tradition:** Communication typically happens through informal bilateral meetings (often breakfast) between the Chair and the Treasury Secretary, rather than through formal staff channels.
* **Economic Philosophy:** Clarida expects Warsh to pivot the Fed toward a "supply-side" framework, focusing on how deregulation, AI, and productivity gains can allow for growth without triggering inflation.

### The Internal Mechanics of Fed Power
* **Power of Persuasion:** Despite the "Volcker" or "Greenspan" labels, Clarida notes that any material decision requires an affirmative vote from a 12-member committee. The Chair’s primary power is the ability to persuade these members and set the meeting agenda.
* **Pre-Meeting "Scripting":** Under Jerome Powell, the Chair would hold individual calls with all 18 participants (voters and non-voters) before every meeting. 
    * **The "Arc" of the Year:** Clarida likens the Chair’s role to a football coach scripting the first 50 plays; they must plan for the "data flow" and potential reactions over an entire year, not just meeting-to-meeting.
* **Staff Influence:** The Board staff, which prepares the economic briefings and forecasts, reports directly to the Chair, giving the Chair significant control over the "alternatives" discussed during policy debates.

### Re-evaluating Communication and Forward Guidance
* **Post-ZIRP Tools:** Warsh has been a vocal critic of "forward guidance" and the "dot plot." Clarida points out that these tools were born out of "desperation" during the 2008 crisis to convince markets that rates would stay at zero.
* **Historical Precedent:** Clarida reminds listeners that Paul Volcker and Alan Greenspan (for most of his tenure) conducted successful policy without any formal forward guidance.
* **Market Volatility Risks:** Removing forward guidance would likely return the bond market to "normal" pre-2008 levels of volatility.
    * **The MOVE Index:** Clarida suggests that the suppressed volatility of the last decade was an anomaly of QE and forward guidance, and a shift away from these tools would increase interest rate fluctuations.

### Balance Sheet Reform and the "New Accord"
* **The Maturity Swap:** Clarida clarifies a common misconception: Modern QE is not "printing money" in the traditional sense. Since the Fed now pays interest on reserves, QE essentially changes the government's debt composition from fixed-rate (long-term bonds) to floating-rate (bank reserves).
* **The "New Accord":** Warsh has called for a new agreement between the Fed and Treasury regarding the balance sheet. 
    * **Composition Changes:** This could involve the Fed exiting mortgage-backed securities (MBS) in favor of T-bills, though Clarida notes there is currently "no appetite" within the existing Fed to sell MBS aggressively.
* **Legacy of Criticism:** Warsh has been a consistent critic of balance sheet expansion since QE2, arguing that the Fed should not be in the business of backstopping fiscal policy or manipulating the long end of the yield curve.

### Managing Political Pressure and Independence
* **The "Disappointment" Factor:** Clarida notes that many Presidents are eventually disappointed by their Fed nominees because the institution and the data eventually force the Chair to prioritize price stability over political desires.
* **Inflation Guardrails:** If Warsh were to cut rates too aggressively to satisfy political pressure, Clarida argues that "break-even inflation" and market measures would spike, forcing the Fed to respond to protect its 40-year legacy of price stability.

> "The power of the Fed Chair is the power of persuasion because at the end of the day, he or she only has one vote... rarely do Fed Chairs get outvoted, but that's because they know where the committee is leaning." — **Richard Clarida**

> "Forward guidance and quantitative easing are not exempt from the laws of economics. They have benefits but also a cost... it’s entirely appropriate that Kevin Warsh or anyone who becomes a Fed Chair now think about the cost and benefits." — **Richard Clarida**

> "No Fed Chair wants to go down in the history books as the Fed Chair that squandered 40 years of price stability." — **Richard Clarida**
