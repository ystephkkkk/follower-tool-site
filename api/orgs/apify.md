## Why AI Agents Still Won't Pay for Anything — Jan Curn, Apify

### TL;DR
Despite the proliferation of fintech agent payment standards, real transaction volume remains near zero because agents still rely on traditional API keys, and the agent payment funnel faces friction at every stage from setup and trust to variable billing. However, emerging crypto-backed rails and pay-per-use batch settlement mechanisms are beginning to clear the path for an autonomous agent economy.

### The Agent Payments Boom and Protocol Proliferation
* **Rapid Ecosystem Growth:** Driven by predictions that agents will require autonomous budgets to complete longer-term tasks, fintech and payment giants have launched a crowded field of agent payment standards over the past year and a half.
* **Marketplace Context:** Jan Curn (Founder, Apify) notes that Apify runs a marketplace of 80,000 AI tools ("actors") built by a global creator community, paying out almost **$2M** to creators last month, making agentic payments a critical expansion channel for their platform.
* **Proliferation of Standards:** The landscape includes numerous protocols launched since mid-2024, such as:
  * **Lightning Labs:** L42 (mid-2024)
  * **Coinbase:** X402 (May 2025)
  * **Visa:** KYA pay (June 2024), Trusted Agents Protocol (TAP, October 2024)
  * **Mastercard:** Agent Pay (April 2024), Agent Pay for Machines
  * **Google & Partners:** Agent Payments Protocol (AP2), Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP with Shopify, January 2026)
  * **OpenAI & Stripe:** Agent E-commerce Protocol (ACP, September 2024)
  * **Stripe & Temple:** Machine Payments Protocol (MPP, early 2025)
  * **Other Regional & Platform Standards:** Alipay (ACTP, MMP/AM), Union Pay (APOP), and OKX Agent Payment Protocol.

### Why Crypto Fits Agent Payments
* **Structural Advantages over Traditional Rails:** Traditional credit cards, PayPal micropayments, and bank debits are too expensive for micro-transactions and introduce severe chargeback risks that bad actors could easily abuse in an autonomous agent world.
* **Absence of Centralized Control:** Crypto ecosystems operate without a single corporate controller, making them well-positioned to dominate the agentic payments wars.
* **Real-World Usage Contrast:** 
  * **API Key Dominance:** Services like Apify, Exa, Perplexity, and Farcaster make far more than **$1 million per month** combined from agents, but they do so through fixed accounts, API keys, and credit cards rather than autonomous discovery and payment protocols.
  * **Anemic Protocol Volume:** Monitoring tools like X402 scan and MPP scan show minimal on-chain activity over a recent 30-day window—roughly **$1 million** in payment volume for X402 and only **$1.5 thousand** for MPP.

### Friction Points Across the Agent Payment Funnel
* **Need (API Keys vs. Autonomous Pay):** Building and connecting agents via fixed accounts and API keys using MCP (Model Context Protocol) is currently far more convenient than setting up autonomous agent spending.
  * *Unlocks:* Better foundational models, the rise of always-on personal agents (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic), and the eventual end of generous AI token subsidies will force agents to calculate whether buying an existing service is more economical than building it from scratch.
* **Setup (Crypto Friction):** Crypto wallets and terminology remain opaque to most users, creating friction compared to ubiquitous credit cards.
  * *Unlocks:* Improved UX and native integration of crypto/payment capabilities directly inside major agent platforms (like Claude or ChatGPT) where users can allocate a fixed dollar budget (e.g., **$50**) out of their main account credits.
* **Discovery & Trust:** Agents need to verify that a service on a random URL will deliver the promised output at the stated price.
  * *Unlocks:* Verified directory layers and curated marketplaces—such as Anthropic's connector directory and Apify's marketplace—acting as trusted middlemen.
* **Buying (Pay-Per-Use and Settlement Limitations):** Fixed-price models (like exact payment schemes) fail for variable workloads like compute-heavy or LLM-driven jobs.
  * *Evolution of X402/MPP:* Early variable payment attempts ("up to" schemes) allowed double-spending risks prior to blockchain settlement, requiring workarounds like exact charges with manual refunds. The introduction of **batch settlement** (X402) and **sessions** (MPP) finally solves this via cryptographic blockchain escrow for microtransactions.
