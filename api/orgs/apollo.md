## AI Dominates Economy and Markets with Torsten Slok | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 68

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xrduTt2651c
- Published: 2026-07-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260713.html#video-xrduTt2651c
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Torsten Slok (Chief Economist, Apollo)

---

**TL;DR**
The U.S. economy is currently buoyed by three non-interest-rate-sensitive "tailwinds"—AI spending, re-industrialization, and tax refunds—which are masking a severe K-shaped divergence and preventing the Federal Reserve from cutting rates. However, the extreme concentration of AI-related assets in both equity and debt markets has created a systemic "single factor" risk for investors.

### The Three Engines of U.S. Growth
*   **GDP Breakdown:** Torsten Slok estimates that current U.S. GDP growth (roughly 2%+) is driven by three specific, non-traditional sources:
    *   **AI Spending Boom (1.0%):** Massive investment in data centers and energy infrastructure, plus the "wealth effect" of high stock prices for the top tier of consumers.
    *   **Industrial Renaissance (0.3%):** "Home-shoring" driven by the CHIPS Act and defense spending. 
    *   **Tax Refund Windfall (0.9%):** Retroactive tax changes (the "One Big Beautiful Bill") increased average refunds from $3,000 to $4,000 this year, though this is a one-time boost that disappears by 2027.
*   **The Fed's Dilemma:** Because these growth drivers are insensitive to interest rates, traditional Fed tightening hasn't slowed the economy as expected.
    *   **Rate Cut Probability:** Slok asserts there is "literally zero chance" of a rate cut this year; the market is currently pricing in potential hikes for September and December.
    *   **Losing Sectors:** Traditional interest-rate-sensitive sectors, specifically housing and autos, are "dying" under current rates while the rest of the economy ignores them.

### The K-Shaped Reality and "Ghost" Unemployment
*   **Wealth Divergence:** High-income households (top 20%) hold $1.5 trillion more in savings than they did in 2019, while the bottom 20% have seen their savings return to 2019 nominal levels (essentially zero in real terms).
*   **The Inflation Burden:** The lower leg of the "K" spends a higher percentage of income on food, energy, and rent—sectors where inflation remains stickiest.
*   **Employment Dynamics:** While traditional narratives suggest AI causes mass unemployment, Slok argues it is actually fueling a "dorm room entrepreneur" boom. 
    *   **Business Creation:** U.S. business applications are at historic highs as AI tools allow individuals to start companies with minimal overhead.
    *   **The Immigration Factor:** Net immigration has dropped from 3 million per year to nearly zero. This has lowered the "breakeven" for non-farm payrolls from 200k/month to roughly 30k/month, making current job numbers look stronger than they are.

### The $500 Billion Software Debt Wall
*   **Private Credit Concentration:** Of the $2 trillion private credit market, roughly $500 billion is indexed to the software sector.
*   **The Leverage Problem:** Software companies currently exhibit high leverage and deteriorating "coverage ratios" (the ability to pay interest from earnings) due to higher-for-longer rates.
*   **The Maturity Wall:** Most of this debt was originated in 2021-2022 with 7-year maturities. A massive refinancing crisis looms for 2028-2029 if interest rates remain high.
*   **Capital Intensity & Moats:** Eisman notes that AI has shifted from a high-margin software play to a capital-intensive hardware play. He compares the hyperscalers to airlines: high capex and potentially no "moats," as users can easily switch between different LLMs (Gemini, Claude, GPT).

### Portfolio Risk: The "Single Factor" Trap
*   **The Illusion of Diversification:** Slok argues that the traditional 60/40 portfolio is no longer diversified because AI has permeated every asset class.
    *   **Equities (60%):** The top 10 stocks make up 42% of the S&P 500, nearly all driven by AI.
    *   **Fixed Income (40%):** Investment-grade indexes are shifting away from banks and toward "hyperscalers" (Big Tech) issuing hundreds of billions in debt to fund data centers.
    *   **Venture Capital:** Approximately 87% of all VC funding is now AI-focused.
*   **The Strategy Shift:** Slok suggests the "new 60/40" should be 60% AI and 40% non-correlated assets (value stocks, consumer staples) to protect against a potential "terminal value" reset in the tech sector.

## Apollo's Private Credit Exposure: Chris Edson Weighs In | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 57

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q6PUFCBbiBU

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260427.html#video-Q6PUFCBbiBU
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Chris Edson (Partner and Global Head of Originations, Apollo)

---

**TL;DR**
Chris Edson (Apollo) argues that the "private credit panic" is overblown by clarifying that the $40 trillion industry extends far beyond risky software buyouts into diversified, asset-backed lending, where Apollo maintains low software exposure and high capital alignment.

### The Scope of Private Credit
* **Market Misconception:** The press often conflates the entire private credit industry with the $1–$2 trillion "direct lending" market used for private equity buyouts. 
* **The Broader Landscape:** Edson defines private credit as a $40 trillion market encompassing mortgages, commercial real estate, trade finance, equipment loans, aircraft leasing, and fleet financing.
* **Apollo’s Evolution:** Apollo has shifted away from traditional private equity; less than 10% of the firm is now PE-focused. The core business is now "retirement services" (insurance-led lending).

### Software Exposure and the AI Threat
* **Industry Concentration:** Approximately one-third of all private equity buyouts over the last five years have been software companies, creating hundreds of billions in capital exposure.
* **The "Claude" Factor:** Edson demonstrates AI’s disruptive power by noting he built a functional math game for his daughter in 60 seconds using Claude. This speed threatens software companies with no proprietary data or "moat."
* **Apollo’s Defensive Posture:** 
    * **Minimal Exposure:** Apollo’s software exposure is "a couple percent" of AUM, significantly lower than industry peers.
    * **Value Orientation:** Apollo avoids software because the firm lends against cash flow and collateral rather than high-multiple enterprise value.
    * **Athene Exposure:** Edson notes that Athene (Apollo’s insurance arm) has effectively zero exposure to software credit.

### Apollo’s Origination Ecosystem
Apollo operates 16 distinct origination platforms with over 4,000 employees to "manufacture" yield rather than buying liquid, low-alpha public bonds. Key platforms include:
* **Atlas:** A $50 billion warehouse lending business acquired from Credit Suisse. It provides temporary financing for lenders (e.g., mortgage originators) before they hit the securitization markets.
* **Wheels:** A vehicle fleet leasing business managing over one million vehicles, primarily work trucks and utility vehicles.
* **PK AirFinance:** An aircraft lending business acquired from GE Capital that lends against commercial planes.
* **The "Alpha" Strategy:** By providing custom solutions (like draw-down lending or inventory buffers) that public markets cannot offer, Apollo earns an extra 25–150 basis points of spread.

### Risk Management and Transparency
* **Liquidity vs. Credit Risk:** Edson identifies "asset-liability mismatch" (liquidity risk) as the primary systemic threat, citing the collapse of First Republic as a duration mismatch issue rather than a credit quality issue.
* **Alignment of Interests:** 
    * **Balance Sheet Commitment:** Unlike "asset-light" managers who only invest 2% in their own funds, Apollo has $35 billion of its own capital in Athene.
    * **First Loss Position:** Apollo owns the "first loss" in Athene, meaning they are directly penalized by poor underwriting.
* **Transparency Claims:** Edson refutes the "opaque" label of private credit, noting that Athene’s regulatory filings list every single loan by name, maturity, and size.
* **Credit Ratings:** Apollo avoids using Egan-Jones for ratings, sticking to the "Big Three" (S&P, Moody’s, Fitch) to ensure standard investment-grade transparency.
