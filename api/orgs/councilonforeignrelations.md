## Inflation, The Deficit & Why the Dollar Stays King with Benn Steil | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 81

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZR5-znz_WcM
- Published: 2026-10-26
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261026.html#video-ZR5-znz_WcM
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Benn Steil (Director of International Economics, Council on Foreign Relations)

---

### TL;DR
Despite persistent inflation, a massive $40 trillion federal deficit, and an aging investor base, the U.S. dollar retains its status as the global financial anchor because there are no viable currency alternatives.

### The Fed, Inflation, and Rates
- **Inflation Persistence:**
  - On October 14, inflation came in at **3.7%**, exceeding market expectations of **3.6%** and creating pressure on the Federal Reserve.
  - While temporary oil shocks are typically "looked through," the Fed is deeply concerned that delaying action could unmoor longer-term inflation expectations.
- **Federal Reserve Dynamics:**
  - Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh faces an unenviable political and policy bind, caught between a potential majority committee vote for rate hikes and political pressure from Donald Trump.
  - Benn Steil supports the Fed's elimination of formal forward guidance, noting that empirical evidence shows the Fed is systematically worse at forecasting than blue-chip private sector forecasters.
  - Steil defends the Fed's large balance sheet of **$6.5 to $7 trillion**, stating it does not hinder the Fed's ability to control inflation using interest on reserves.

### Fiscal Policy, Scott Bessent, and the Deficit
- **Bessent's Operation Twist:**
  - Treasury official Scott Bessent launched an operation to sell short-term Treasuries and buy long-term Treasuries to the tune of **$6 billion** to lower long-term rates.
  - The market quickly dismissed the move, calculating that **$6 billion** represents just **0.016%** of the **$40 trillion** national debt.
- **The U.S. Deficit & Crowding Out:**
  - The U.S. federal deficit currently runs at about **6% of GDP annually**, totaling **$40 trillion**.
  - The trajectory is concerning due to a crowding-out effect: Treasury is competing globally for capital against booming AI infrastructure investments, pushing interest rates upward.
  - Congress and the executive branch currently lack the serious political will to cut spending or address entitlement reform.

### The Global Dollar Standard & The France Sovereign Debt Crisis
- **The Exorbitant Privilege:**
  - Historically, 76% of the market for U.S. debt consisted of "rate-insensitive investors" (mostly central banks holding Treasuries as a reflex).
  - That figure has dropped to **43%**, meaning the U.S. must increasingly attract rate-sensitive investors, creating an accelerationist effect on interest rate rises as deficits grow.
- **The Lack of Alternatives:**
  - The U.S. dollar remains dominant simply as the "winner of an ugly dog contest"; no major fiat alternative exists.
  - Central banks (particularly China and Russia) have increasingly diversified into gold and repatriated reserves to protect themselves against aggressive U.S. dollar-based financial sanctions (such as those applied to Iran).
- **France's Vulnerability:**
  - France faces an even uglier fiscal position than the U.S. with large deficits and zero political will for reform (e.g., Marine Le Pen's proposal to lower the retirement age from **62 to 60**).
  - Unlike U.S. Treasuries, no bank or sovereign wealth fund globally holds French sovereign bonds on reflex; French debt is treated strictly like corporate risk.

### Historical Counterfactuals: Henry Wallace, Bretton Woods, and the Cold War
- **The Henry Wallace Counterfactual:**
  - In Henry Steil's book *The World That Wasn't*, he explores the counterfactual where Henry Wallace (FDR's third-term Vice President) succeeded a failing Roosevelt instead of Harry Truman.
  - Wallace, despite not being an ideological spy, was a Soviet fellow traveler who praised Soviet economic planning and would have opposed the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, the creation of West Germany, and NATO.
- **Bretton Woods & Harry Dexter White:**
  - The 1944 Bretton Woods conference established the IMF, the World Bank, and the dollar-based international financial system.
  - The U.S. architect of the system, Treasury official Harry Dexter White, was later identified in FBI files (via 18 intercepted Soviet intelligence cables) as a Soviet asset who actively protected other agents and undermined U.S. policy (such as giving currency plates to the Soviets, which cost the U.S. Treasury about **$8 billion** in today's dollars).
- **The Marshall Plan:**
  - Following WWII, the IMF and World Bank were largely mothballed through the 1950s, making the Marshall Plan the primary vehicle for rehabilitating war-torn Europe and countering Soviet expansionism.

## Renminbi and the World

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlJjwlaUUfk
- Published: 2026-06-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260623.html#video-rlJjwlaUUfk
- Speakers: Zoe Liu (Fellow, Council on Foreign Relations); Zhu Ning (Professor, Shanghai Advanced Institute of Finance)

---

**TL;DR**
The internationalization of the Renminbi (RMB) has shifted from a prestige project to a critical strategic resilience effort, achieving substantial progress in cross-border trade settlements but remaining constrained as a global store of value by China's capital controls.

***

### The Strategic Evolution of RMB Internationalization
*   **Shift from Prestige to Resilience:** **Zoe Liu** highlights that RMB internationalization has transitioned from a status symbol to a vital strategic project designed to build economic resilience and insulate China from geopolitical risks.
*   **A Twenty-Year Cumulative Effort:** **Zhu Ning** stresses that recent milestones are the result of a two-decade-long process, supported by a decade of building offshore clearing centers and standardizing financial products, rather than a short-term policy push.
*   **The Global South’s Focus on Diversification:** For middle powers like South Africa, transacting in RMB is driven by a desire to avoid systemic vulnerabilities rather than picking sides in a bipolar currency regime.
    *   **Bilateral Swaps:** The South African Reserve Bank and the People's Bank of China (PBOC) have maintained a bilateral currency swap agreement since 2015 to buffer short-term balance-of-payment pressures.
    *   **Direct Clearing Infrastructure:** In November 2023, South Africa’s Standard Bank became the first African bank authorized to clear and settle transactions directly in RMB through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS).

### Measuring RMB Performance: The Three Functions of Money
*   **Means of Exchange (High Progress):** The RMB has made significant inroads as a payment currency, particularly in bilateral trade with major partners.
    *   **Sino-Russian Trade:** Approximately 99% of bilateral trade between China and Russia is now settled in local currencies, primarily the RMB.
    *   **Cross-Border Settlement:** Between 60% and 80% of China’s own cross-border trade is now settled in RMB.
    *   **The "Invisible" SWIFT Shift:** **Diana Choyleva** notes that while SWIFT data suggests RMB usage has slightly dipped in global payments, this is misleading. An increasing volume of RMB trade has moved entirely off traditional Western "dollar rails" to CIPS and digital currency platforms like mBridge, escaping SWIFT visibility.
*   **Unit of Account (Emerging Potential):** China is actively promoting RMB-denominated commodity contracts—including copper, Shanghai oil futures, Guangzhou lithium, and Ganzhou rare earths—to establish the currency as a benchmark in non-oil energy markets.
*   **Store of Value (Limited Progress):** Despite trade gains, the RMB accounts for less than 2% of official global foreign exchange reserves (compared to the U.S. dollar's 58%), primarily due to China's strict capital controls.

### Currency Stability, Valuation, and Exchange Rate Regimes
*   **Managed Flexibility vs. Market Forces:** The PBOC is gradually engineering a "two-way fluctuation" exchange rate mechanism. **Zhu Ning** explains that "stability" in this context refers to a predictable, market-reflective mechanism rather than a fixed exchange rate.
*   **Predictability and Geopolitical Backing:** **Zoe Liu** argues that the PBOC has successfully maintained currency predictability since the end of COVID-19. Despite domestic economic weaknesses, the RMB has avoided competitive devaluation because of its role as a strategic resilience tool.
*   **The Manufacturing-Currency Disconnect:** A stark gap exists between China's industrial footprint and its currency status. China commands nearly 30% of global manufacturing power but holds under 2% of global currency reserves. 

### Addressing the Shortage of Safe Assets and Investment Channels
*   **Expanding Bond Markets:** While China lacks a risk-free benchmark equivalent to U.S. Treasuries, it has rapidly expanded its municipal and corporate bond markets over the last 20 years to offer viable fixed-income alternatives.
*   **The Green Transition Advantage:** **Zoe Liu** suggests that as the global energy mix diversifies away from oil, China’s primary financial benchmark advantage will lie in green bonds and transition finance, heavily backed by its massive domestic industrial capacity.
*   **Bypassing Controls via "Connect" Schemes:** To attract foreign capital without fully opening its capital account, China is leveraging regulated investment pathways like the Bond Connect and Stock Connect programs, linking mainland exchanges with Hong Kong and London.

### The Evolving Roles of Hong Kong and Shanghai as Financial Hubs
*   **Complementary Financial Roles:** Rather than competing destructively, Shanghai and Hong Kong have established distinct operational zones:
    *   **Shanghai:** Focuses on onshore wealth management, supporting the domestic economy, and managing onshore exchange rates.
    *   **Hong Kong:** Functions as the world’s largest international wealth management center, facilitating two-way global investment flows.
*   **Internationalizing Capital Controls:** **Diana Choyleva** argues that Beijing's crackdowns on illicit cross-border retail brokerages actually strengthen Hong Kong's position. The crackdowns signal that while illegal capital flight is blocked, legitimate capital will increasingly flow through Hong Kong via regulated connect schemes, essentially allowing China to internationalize its capital controls on its own terms.

## CFR Fellows' Book Launch Series: The Infinity Machine

- Channel: Council on Foreign Relations
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqURNDQiJr0
- Published: 2026-04-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260413.html#video-jqURNDQiJr0
- Speakers: Sebastian Mallaby (Author and Fellow, Council on Foreign Relations); Demis Hassabis (Co-founder and CEO, Google DeepMind)

---

**TL;DR** 
Sebastian Mallaby’s *The Infinity Machine* chronicles the rise of Demis Hassabis and DeepMind, illustrating how the pursuit of superintelligence evolved from a niche scientific obsession into a high-stakes commercial and geopolitical arms race.

### The Genesis of a Genius: Demis Hassabis
* **Child Prodigy Roots:** Demis Hassabis was the second-best junior chess player in the world by age 12 and a self-taught programmer who created the hit game *Theme Park* (selling 5 million copies) by age 16.
* **Academic Range:** Choosing "computer science over money," Hassabis declined a $1 million check from his employer to attend Cambridge. His career path reflects a "range" of expertise, spanning theoretical physics, neuroscience, and competitive poker.
* **Founding DeepMind:** Hassabis co-founded DeepMind in 2010. He secured initial funding by pitching Peter Thiel on the "balance between the bishop and the knight" in chess, eventually raising $2 million for 50% of the company—a deal Mallaby describes as "derisively small" in hindsight.

### Key Breakthroughs: AlphaGo and AlphaFold
* **AlphaGo (2016):** Unlike chess, the game of Go has an almost infinite decision space. DeepMind’s victory over the world’s top human player in Korea served as the moment AI transitioned from "nerd obscurity" to global notoriety.
* **AlphaFold and the Nobel Prize:** DeepMind tackled the 50-year-old "protein folding" problem, predicting the shapes of nearly all proteins in nature. 
    * **Scientific Management:** When the team leader suggested quitting while they were ahead, Hassabis used a "fluidity" test—judging the density of scientific avenues remaining—to insist they continue.
    * **Impact:** The project won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (awarded to Hassabis and John Jumper), proving AI could solve fundamental challenges in biology and medicine.

### The Competitive Landscape: Google vs. OpenAI
* **The Google Acquisition:** Google acquired DeepMind in 2014 for approximately $658 million. Mallaby argues this was a victory for the UK, as it secured nearly $1 billion a year in R&D funding from California for a London-based lab.
* **OpenAI Origins:** Founded by Elon Musk and Sam Altman in 2015, OpenAI was originally positioned as a "virtuous," non-profit rival to "evil" Google. 
* **The "Tanks on the Lawn":** When ChatGPT launched, Hassabis described it as "war." In response, Google merged DeepMind with Google Brain (Mountain View) to accelerate development. Despite cultural differences and eight time zones, Mallaby notes they successfully overtook OpenAI within two and a half years.

### Existential Risks and Global Governance
* **The "Manhattan Project" Parallel:** AI leaders frequently compare their work to the development of nuclear weapons. Hassabis’s first office in London was located on the same street corner where Leo Szilard first conceived of the nuclear chain reaction.
* **Primary Risks:** 
    * **Bad Actors:** The potential for unidentified entities to launch massive cyberattacks on infrastructure.
    * **The "Terminator" Scenario:** The machine itself turning against humans, which Mallaby notes is taken seriously by developers once systems reach recursive self-improvement.
* **Regulatory Challenges:** Hassabis and others advocate for safety, but they are trapped in a "race dynamic." Mallaby highlights the "open-weight" model risk, where AI code can be downloaded and safety guardrails stripped away, suggesting a "Grand Bargain" with China is needed to end open-weight releases.

### Economic and Geopolitical Outlook
* **The Labor Market:** Mallaby warns of a "coming jobs apocalypse." He notes that while 1990s polling showed people felt the internet would empower them, 66% of people today view AI as a threat to their livelihood.
* **Military Application:** The next frontier of warfare involves AI-controlled "drone swarms" that can overwhelm traditional defenses.
* **China’s Standing:** Despite a lack of compute power, Chinese labs like DeepSeek and companies like ByteDance are producing world-class models, proving that China remains a formidable competitor in the AI race.
