## A Conversation with David Sacks moderated by Morgan Hitizg

- Channel: Council on Foreign Relations
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=H6PVPsLtScM
- Published: 2026-04-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260422.html#video-H6PVPsLtScM
- Speakers: David Sacks (Entrepreneur and Investor); Morgan Hitzig (Moderator, Council on Foreign Relations)

---

**TL;DR**
The Trump administration has adopted a "moonshot" approach to AI, treating it as a global race that the U.S. must win through a three-pillar strategy of pro-innovation private sector support, massive infrastructure/energy expansion, and aggressive technology exports.

### National AI Strategy: The "Moonshot" Framework
David Sacks outlines the administration’s focus on maintaining American dominance through three core policy pillars:

*   **Pro-Innovation:** Ensuring the private sector leads development rather than government-driven initiatives.
*   **Pro-Infrastructure:** prioritizing the construction of data centers and the energy production required to power them.
*   **Pro-Export:** Promoting the global adoption of American models and chips to ensure market share. 
    *   **Strategic Goal:** Sacks emphasizes that "market share is the test." Success is defined by the world running on American models/chips rather than Chinese alternatives like DeepSeek or Huawei.
*   **The "One Rulebook" Initiative:** The administration is pushing for a single national AI policy to preempt a "patchwork" of 1,200+ conflicting state-level bills that burden entrepreneurs with compliance costs.

### Energy and the Infrastructure Bottleneck
Energy availability and data center permitting are currently the primary bottlenecks for AI advancement.

*   **Ratepayer Protection Pledge:** To combat "NIMBY" backlash and rising residential electricity costs, the administration reached an agreement with "hyperscalers" (major tech firms).
    *   **"Bring Your Own Power":** Companies pledge that new data centers will not raise residential rates; in exchange, the government eases permitting for firms to stand up their own power generation.
*   **Grid Modernization:** Large AI companies are expected to invest in grid upgrades, creating an "economic incentive" for infrastructure that ultimately benefits all consumers through increased scale.

### Debunking the AI Labor "Disruption" Narrative
Sacks argues that fears of mass unemployment are currently unsupported by data and that AI acts as a productivity "unlock" rather than a job killer.

*   **Lack of Disruption:** Sacks cites a Yale Budget Lab study showing "no discernible disruption" to the labor market in the three years since ChatGPT’s launch. 
    *   **Layoff Data:** Less than 5% of layoffs last year were attributable to AI according to the Challenger Gray report.
*   **Blue-Collar Boom:** AI infrastructure has created a massive demand for electricians, welders, and construction workers, leading to a 25–30% increase in wages for these trades.
*   **The "Radiologist" Analogy:** Despite advancements in computer vision, the number of radiologists has increased. Sacks notes that as the cost of a "task" (like a scan) drops, total demand for the service—and the humans needed to manage the results—increases.
*   **Software Development:** Despite coding assistants, demand for software developers is up 10% year-over-year. Sacks believes AI will allow companies to finally address long-standing "product roadmap" backlogs by making engineers 10x-100x more productive.

### Strategic Capital and Institutional Shifts
The administration is reshaping how the government interacts with science, technology, and defense.

*   **PCAST Evolution:** Sacks describes the new President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology (PCAST) as a group of "doers" and "company builders" rather than just academics, aiming to bring a business-oriented, long-dated perspective to D.C.
*   **Defense & "DR. AIM":** Sacks highlights a shift toward cost-effective defense tech. He notes the absurdity of using $4 million Patriot missiles to down $4,000 drones.
    *   **The DR. AIM Framework:** Sacks identifies the future of conflict as a combination of **D**rones, **R**ockets, **A**I, and **M**issiles.
*   **Crypto Regulation:** Sacks remains optimistic about the **CLARITY bill** passing the Senate, which would provide permanent regulatory certainty for the crypto industry and end the era of "regulation by enforcement."

> "If five years from now you look around the world and the whole world is running on DeepSeek models and Huawei chips, we lost. If everyone’s running American models on American chips, that’s what we want to see. Market share is ultimately the test." — David Sacks

> "AI doesn't—this is what I believe—it turbocharges the individual. It allows them to accomplish more of what they want to accomplish. The idea that we’re just going to throw AI over the wall and it’s going to magically figure out how to get rid of all the humans is just not the way this stuff works." — David Sacks

> "You can’t replace something with nothing. If you want to replace the patchwork of what the states are doing, you have to offer a national framework." — David Sacks


## CFR Fellows' Book Launch Series: The Infinity Machine

- Channel: Council on Foreign Relations
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jqURNDQiJr0

- Speakers: Sebastian Mallaby (Author and Fellow, Council on Foreign Relations); Demis Hassabis (Co-founder and CEO, Google DeepMind)

---

**TL;DR** 
Sebastian Mallaby’s *The Infinity Machine* chronicles the rise of Demis Hassabis and DeepMind, illustrating how the pursuit of superintelligence evolved from a niche scientific obsession into a high-stakes commercial and geopolitical arms race.

### The Genesis of a Genius: Demis Hassabis
* **Child Prodigy Roots:** Demis Hassabis was the second-best junior chess player in the world by age 12 and a self-taught programmer who created the hit game *Theme Park* (selling 5 million copies) by age 16.
* **Academic Range:** Choosing "computer science over money," Hassabis declined a $1 million check from his employer to attend Cambridge. His career path reflects a "range" of expertise, spanning theoretical physics, neuroscience, and competitive poker.
* **Founding DeepMind:** Hassabis co-founded DeepMind in 2010. He secured initial funding by pitching Peter Thiel on the "balance between the bishop and the knight" in chess, eventually raising $2 million for 50% of the company—a deal Mallaby describes as "derisively small" in hindsight.

### Key Breakthroughs: AlphaGo and AlphaFold
* **AlphaGo (2016):** Unlike chess, the game of Go has an almost infinite decision space. DeepMind’s victory over the world’s top human player in Korea served as the moment AI transitioned from "nerd obscurity" to global notoriety.
* **AlphaFold and the Nobel Prize:** DeepMind tackled the 50-year-old "protein folding" problem, predicting the shapes of nearly all proteins in nature. 
    * **Scientific Management:** When the team leader suggested quitting while they were ahead, Hassabis used a "fluidity" test—judging the density of scientific avenues remaining—to insist they continue.
    * **Impact:** The project won the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry (awarded to Hassabis and John Jumper), proving AI could solve fundamental challenges in biology and medicine.

### The Competitive Landscape: Google vs. OpenAI
* **The Google Acquisition:** Google acquired DeepMind in 2014 for approximately $658 million. Mallaby argues this was a victory for the UK, as it secured nearly $1 billion a year in R&D funding from California for a London-based lab.
* **OpenAI Origins:** Founded by Elon Musk and Sam Altman in 2015, OpenAI was originally positioned as a "virtuous," non-profit rival to "evil" Google. 
* **The "Tanks on the Lawn":** When ChatGPT launched, Hassabis described it as "war." In response, Google merged DeepMind with Google Brain (Mountain View) to accelerate development. Despite cultural differences and eight time zones, Mallaby notes they successfully overtook OpenAI within two and a half years.

### Existential Risks and Global Governance
* **The "Manhattan Project" Parallel:** AI leaders frequently compare their work to the development of nuclear weapons. Hassabis’s first office in London was located on the same street corner where Leo Szilard first conceived of the nuclear chain reaction.
* **Primary Risks:** 
    * **Bad Actors:** The potential for unidentified entities to launch massive cyberattacks on infrastructure.
    * **The "Terminator" Scenario:** The machine itself turning against humans, which Mallaby notes is taken seriously by developers once systems reach recursive self-improvement.
* **Regulatory Challenges:** Hassabis and others advocate for safety, but they are trapped in a "race dynamic." Mallaby highlights the "open-weight" model risk, where AI code can be downloaded and safety guardrails stripped away, suggesting a "Grand Bargain" with China is needed to end open-weight releases.

### Economic and Geopolitical Outlook
* **The Labor Market:** Mallaby warns of a "coming jobs apocalypse." He notes that while 1990s polling showed people felt the internet would empower them, 66% of people today view AI as a threat to their livelihood.
* **Military Application:** The next frontier of warfare involves AI-controlled "drone swarms" that can overwhelm traditional defenses.
* **China’s Standing:** Despite a lack of compute power, Chinese labs like DeepSeek and companies like ByteDance are producing world-class models, proving that China remains a formidable competitor in the AI race.

> "How do you judge somebody who, on the one hand, says AI is dangerous... but then continues to build it more and more and more powerful? ... Maybe staying at the table is a legitimate choice." — **Sebastian Mallaby**

> "We are the new Manhattan Project." — **Demis Hassabis**
