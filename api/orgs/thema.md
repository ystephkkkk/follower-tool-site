## Ex-NVIDIA Engineer: Why AI Is Running Out of Copper

### TL;DR
As AI models exceed single-GPU capacity, traditional copper interconnects are hitting physical limits, driving the industry toward optical interconnects and silicon photonics. Thema's Herwig Van Hove and Yannick De Koninck are tackling this infrastructure bottleneck by building large-scale silicon photonics manufacturing in Europe.

### The Infrastructure Bottleneck: Why Copper is Reaching Its Limit
* **GPU-to-GPU Communication Limits:** 
  * As AI models grow too large for a single GPU, multiple GPUs must be interconnected.
  * Copper interconnects are running out of steam because modern AI workloads require higher data transfer rates and bandwidth.
* **Shift to Optical Interconnects:** 
  * The transition from copper to optical interconnects and silicon photonics is necessary to support the immense data throughput required by modern AI.
* **The AI Factory Architecture Shift:** 
  * **Network Topology:** Traditional hyper-scale data centers relied on a deep hierarchical tree of switches for Google searches. Modern AI infrastructure requires a much flatter network with direct connections between switches and GPUs to handle high-volume, latency-sensitive GPU-to-GPU traffic (including agentic AI tool calls).
* **The Energy Crisis:** 
  * **Existential Threat:** Data center energy consumption is the number one issue keeping CTOs awake at night because local power grids cannot deliver sufficient power.
  * **Grid Constraints:** Google spent three years negotiating with Amsterdam before moving plans to Belgium due to a lack of power grid capacity. Upgrading grids or building new power plants cannot keep pace with AI demand.
  * **Photonics as the Killer App:** Energy consumption is the ultimate killer app for silicon photonics, a technology 20 to 30 years in the making.

### Market Dynamics & The Supply Chain Deficit
* **Explosive Market Growth:** 
  * BlackRock's Tony Kim estimates a $10 trillion industry rebuild, with data centers being heavily impacted due to outdated, water- and energy-inefficient infrastructure.
  * NVIDIA made massive investments in 2024 and 2025, investing 4x the total market size in the first 6 months of the year into companies like Lumentum and Coherent to push the market toward photonics.
* **Supply Chain Bottlenecks:** 
  * **Component Scarcity:** While foundries (TSMC, GlobalFoundries, Tower) handle front-end silicon photonics wafers, final chips require lasers (produced by Lumentum, Coherent) and raw substrates, which face severe supply limitations.
  * **Manufacturing Challenges:** Tool builders are not producing nearly enough tools, and existing tools suffer from low throughput, efficiency, and yield.
  * **Geopolitical Divide:** The East-West divide creates additional friction in securing raw materials and substrates.

### Thema's European Manufacturing Strategy
* **Founding and Infrastructure:** 
  * Thema acquired a large-scale European foundry that went out of business, gaining ready-to-go cleanrooms and complex infrastructure that would normally take 3 to 5 years to permit and build.
  * The location is strategically positioned 20 kilometers away from IMEC (Interuniversity Microelectronics Center), a world-leading research center and birthplace of silicon photonics.
* **Talent Acquisition:** 
  * Despite initial skepticism about hiring, Thema successfully attracted top-tier talent from companies like Amazon Web Services (AWS), TSMC, Lam Research, and NVIDIA.
  * The board includes three senior figures from IMEC, including the visionary professor who founded the photonics department at the University of Gent (which produced 35% of the 4,000 peer-reviewed global photonics white papers between 2000 and 2015).
* **Overcoming Misconceptions:** 
  * American tier-one customers initially questioned Thema's ability to secure funding, talent, machines, and substrates while meeting aggressive timelines due to the stereotype that Europe is slow and committee-driven.
  * Thema is intentionally adopting a "fail fast" and agile corporate culture to counter traditional European bureaucracy.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Production Timeline:** Thema plans to start production in **2027** and work toward a full ramp-up by **2028** — *Herwig Van Hove & Yannick De Koninck*.
