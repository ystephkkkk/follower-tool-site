### TL;DR
Opencode, an open-source coding agent that works with any model, scaled to 13 million monthly active users and 7 trillion daily tokens processed, driven by a 16-year overnight success story of persistence.

### Scale and Explosive Growth
- **User and Token Volume:**
  - Reached **4.6 million weekly active users** and **13 million monthly active users** by the end of June, representing a **20X increase** since the beginning of the year.
  - Processes approximately **7 trillion tokens per day** (surpassing OpenRouter's 6 trillion daily tokens).
- **Revenue Run Rate:**
  - Inference business annualized run rate sits between **$38M to nearly $40M** (extrapolated from recent weekly data), built in about 8 months since launching in late September/early October.
  - Subscription plan (**"OpenCode Go"** at $10/month), launched in late February/early March, grew to **160,000 monthly subscribers** generating about **$18 million** in annualized revenue.
- **Ecosystem Adoption:**
  - **Codex Integration:** Codex officially supports OpenCode; roughly **5% of all Codex subscribers** choose OpenCode as their main harness to use underlying APIs.
  - **Enterprise Footprint:** Dozens of top Fortune 500 companies have thousands of employees using OpenCode internally, prompting inbounds for enterprise security questionnaires.

### The Anthropic Controversy & Global Distribution
- **The Anthropic Catalyst:**
  - In the first week of January, when monthly users were at **650,000**, Anthropic attempted to block Claude Code subscriptions from being used within OpenCode by rejecting system prompts containing the word "OpenCode."
  - This enforcement inadvertently put OpenCode and Claude Code on the same pedestal, equating the two products and driving massive brand awareness and new user acquisition.
- **Global User Demographics:**
  - **China:** Accounts for **17%** of traffic on the OpenCode Go plan (making Opencode a rare YC company with meaningful usage in China, driven by domestic preference for local models like GLM).
  - **Developing Markets:** Highly popular in price-sensitive regions where a $200/month subscription is prohibitive, including **Brazil (5%)**, **Indonesia (4%)**, and **Vietnam**.
  - **United States:** Experiencing strong growth despite initial expectations that US users would stick exclusively to premium frontier subscriptions.

### Model Dynamics & Open Source Economics
- **Model Shift:** 
  - Open-source models crossed a viability threshold for real work in February, notably when models like Kimmy (e.g., Kimmy 2.5) briefly flipped usage metrics to surpass Anthropic models over a 4-week span among OpenCode users.
- **Token Usage Breakdown:**
  - **DeepSeek Flash** is heavily utilized as a cost-effective fallback when users approach daily or weekly limits.
  - **GLM (e.g., GLM 5.2)** drives heavy usage due to perceived superiority in front-end design.
- **GPU Inference Stability:** 
  - Because of global usage across the East and West work cycles, Opencode maintains a remarkably stable 24-hour GPU utilization cycle, creating a structural unit-economics advantage over region-specific competitors.

### Product Architecture & Strategic Intent
- **Core Architecture:** 
  - Opencode operates as a two-part product: a terminal UI application and an agent server loop (which companies like Ramp previously embedded behind custom Slack bots).
- **Deliberate Positioning:**
  - **Naming Strategy:** The name "OpenCode" was a deliberate choice to capture the open alternative position when dominant players control the market, following a similar playbook to their past project "OpenNext."
  - **Dataset Creation:** Built `models.dev`, an open-source dataset tracking over 70 models and providers, to establish default status in multi-model routing.
  - **Terminal-First UX:** Designed by NeoVim/Vim users to feel like a modern, high-performance terminal experience, differentiating itself from IDE-heavy tools like Cursor.

### 16-Year Origin Story
- **Founding Timeline:**
  - CEO J.V. started the company with college roommate Frank in **2006/2007** as sophomores at the University of Waterloo, incorporating the legal entity in **2010** (a **16-year-old legal entity**).
  - Applied to Y Combinator **nine times** between 2016 and 2021, enduring four interviews before finally getting accepted into the **2021 batch** with a serverless framework project (which eventually tied into OpenCode).
- **Team Continuity:**
  - J.V. and Frank operated as a two-person co-founder team for over a decade before adding a third co-founder four years ago.
- **Building in Public:**
