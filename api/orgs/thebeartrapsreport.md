## AI Markups Distorting 'True Earnings Power of S&P,' Says Larry McDonald

### TL;DR
Larry McDonald warns that AI-related asset markups and off-balance-sheet financing are severely distorting the true earnings power of the S&P 500. While the broader index trades near all-time highs driven by mega-cap tech, underlying internals reflect a severe multi-sector correction akin to 1987.

### AI Markups & S&P 500 Earnings Distortion
* **Valuation Explosion:** 
  * Over the last three years, the combined value of SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI surged from **$360 billion** to **$5.1 trillion**.
  * Hyperscalers (including Meta, Oracle, and Microsoft) are incorporating these asset markups into S&P 500 earnings, creating a potential one-time distortion of the index's true earnings power.
* **Concentration Risk:** 
  * Out of a typical retirement portfolio investment in the S&P, roughly **$120,000** is concentrated in just two stocks (Apple and Nvidia).
  * Following upcoming IPOs and OpenAI entering the index, AI-related names will comprise **55%** of the NASDAQ.
* **The Depreciation Challenge:** 
  * Major tech firms are engaging in a massive "depreciation game," utilizing chips and hardware whose long-term collateral value is highly uncertain beyond a couple of years as technology and overseas competition (including China and the memory chip sector) accelerate.
* **Cash Incineration:** 
  * Meta escalated its off-balance-sheet financing from **$35 billion** to **$600 billion** to protect its free cash flow from incineration while funding heavy AI capital expenditures through double-A-rated, full-recourse bank lending.
  * Companies that were once world-class oligopolies are burning cash to build products (like Muse) with low barriers to entry, paving the way for copycat offerings across Amazon, Apple, and others.

### Credit Markets & Neocloud Stress
* **Convertible Bond Market Signals:** 
  * A proliferation of serial convertible bond issuers—such as CoreWeave and other neoclouds—indicates that the high-yield market has shut them out ("no mas").
  * Because these firms access public markets rather than traditional bank lending, tightening conditions in neocloud bonds act as a leading indicator for a broader credit problem moving upstream.
* **Asset-Liability Mismatch:** 
  * Big family offices are beginning to buy tail-risk funds (such as credit default swaps on companies exposed to credit like MetLife) and are willing to burn **10% to 20%** annually in carry in anticipation of a major credit event.
* **Fed Posture & Consumer Realities:** 
  * Behind the scenes, the Federal Reserve is playing a tougher game and showing significantly more concern about credit and underlying weaknesses than it did in 2007.
  * In a historical anomaly, the Fed initiated its hiking cycle while McDonald's and Home Depot faced **30% to 35%** drawdowns, illustrating that consumer financial conditions are heavily squeezed while corporate data center expansion remains wide open.

### Market Internals & Structural Disconnects
* **The Two-Tiered Market:** 
  * The broader index is pinned near all-time highs, but underneath the surface, 40 world-class consumer and retail brands (including Nike, Lululemon, and Harley-Davidson) are down **30% to 70%**, mirroring internal carnage similar to the 1987 crash.
* **Hedge Fund Pod Mechanics:** 
  * Multi-strategy hedge fund pods—which have tripled in volume over the past decade—operate under strict risk controls, simultaneously going long the index and short the underlying stocks. 
  * When one or two pods get forced out during volatility spikes (such as the event in June), dozens more get liquidated simultaneously, creating major dislocations in market volatility.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Larry McDonald:** Predicts that buying a basket of heavily beaten-down index names between now and June will significantly outperform the broader market, driven by passive indexing dysfunctions.
* **Larry McDonald:** Bets that the Federal Reserve will implement rate cuts before rate hikes (specifically favoring 75 basis points of cuts over 75 basis points of hikes).
