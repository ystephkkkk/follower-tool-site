## Real Estate Investors on Commercial Market Opportunities

- Channel: Bloomberg Live
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WFrfYymp04A
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261027.html#video-WFrfYymp04A
- Speakers: Laura Hines-Pierce (Co-CEO, Hines); Michael Hyun (Chief Investment Officer, Crow Holdings); Torrey Littlejohn (Senior Managing Director and North Texas Broker Lead, JLL)

---

### TL;DR
Despite macroeconomic headwinds like higher construction costs, interest rates, and localized office vacancies, Texas commercial real estate leadership remains overwhelmingly optimistic due to strong underlying fundamentals, accelerating corporate relocation velocity, and massive secular demand for digital and residential infrastructure.

### Texas Commercial Market Dynamics & Corporate Migration
* **Shifting Corporate Footprints:** 
  * **Torrey Littlejohn** noted that while Texas recorded 25 corporate headquarters moves this year—the lowest since 2017—the state is experiencing unprecedented velocity in companies establishing new footprints with 50, 100, or 200 employees to test the market before growing exponentially.
  * Companies initially evaluate 3 to 4 submarkets before narrowing down to 1 or 2 ecosystems that seamlessly accommodate both front and back offices.
* **The Urban Core vs. Suburban Barbell:**
  * Downtown Dallas is experiencing positive momentum through residential population growth, new grocery stores, restaurants, and spillover activity from Uptown and the Arts District, despite older, obsolete buildings requiring capital investment.
  * Northern suburbs like Plano and Frisco have developed distinct centers of gravity, creating a balanced "barbell" ecosystem where residents have access to diverse school districts and localized amenities without needing to visit downtown daily.

### Data Centers & Digital Infrastructure
* **The Public Relations vs. Reality Gap:**
  * **Laura Hines-Pierce** described the conversation around data centers and digital infrastructure as overly polarized ("black and white"), noting a critical need for balanced community investment covering housing, social, and physical infrastructure.
  * **Michael Hyun** agreed that data centers have briefly become a "four letter word" in an election year, requiring a pivot toward enhanced community engagement and better-designed, architecturally integrated facilities that avoid looking like landed "Star Wars" ships.
* **Capital Competition & Constraints:**
  * Data center developments are soaking up massive amounts of debt and equity capital, competing with traditional real estate asset classes (like office, retail, and multifamily) as well as long-dated government bonds (with data center bond issuance reaching 68% of long-dated government treasuries over the past year).
  * Data center builds compete directly with traditional real estate for labor and supply, specifically driving up costs for construction contractors.

### Multifamily, Housing, and Residential Master-Planned Communities
* **Multifamily Supply Cycles:**
  * **Michael Hyun** characterized multifamily today as "the office of today"—following the previous retail cycle—driven by oversupply resulting from low interest rates 5 years ago (when 5 and 10-year rates were 350 basis points lower).
  * Multifamily is currently experiencing decade-low starts and supply, with market pain expected to persist for the next 10 to 24 months before returning to equilibrium.
* **Single-Family & Master-Planned Demand:**
  * **Laura Hines-Pierce** highlighted that Hines' highest conviction sector is living/residential, driven by a global under-supply of over 6 million units and a 2025 influx of 400,000 people moving to Texas.
  * Crow Holdings and Hines are actively expanding lot-development platforms nationally (such as Hines' delivery of 50,000 lots) to bridge the gap for homebuilders serving millennials seeking single-family master-planned communities.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Michael Hyun** predicts that multifamily market pain and oversupply distress will last for at least the next 10 to 12 to 24 months before the sector reorganizes.
* **Michael Hyun** predicts that state-level regulatory frameworks for data centers will change to mandate deeper community engagement, noting that while growth may slow down temporarily, Texas will remain a winner in data center CapEx spend due to its business-friendly environment.
* **Michael Hyun** predicts that total state-level bans or halts on data center development will not happen because doing so would be "economic suicide" for the Texas economy.

### Notable Quotes
> "Multifamily today is off is the office of today just like it was here before that. It was retail. And uh, in five years it'll be something else because all of these sectors cycle."
> — **Michael Hyun**

> "Data centers don't need to be a Star Wars ship that has landed in the middle of a community. ... There's an opportunity to deliver a better financial product that leases faster, stays leased longer and gets higher rental rates, but is also an investment in the community."
> — **Laura Hines-Pierce**

> "If there is a significant slowdown in that data center development, that impacts so many areas of the Texas economy... your will then require less natural gas. That will impact the the energy markets, of which Houston and Texas broadly is exposed to."
> — **Michael Hyun**


## Property Play: JLL CEO on how global conflicts and capital shifts are reshaping CRE

- Channel: CNBC Television
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UJnItgjkDvU
- Published: 2026-03-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260324.html#video-UJnItgjkDvU
- Speakers: Christian Ulbrich (CEO, JLL); Diana Olick (Real Estate Correspondent, CNBC)

---

**TL;DR**
Despite geopolitical volatility and market fears over AI disruption, JLL is seeing a recovery in commercial real estate driven by a "flight to quality" in core assets and significant productivity gains from decade-long investments in proprietary data and AI tools.

### Geopolitical Volatility and Capital Shifts
* **Geopolitical Impact:** Global conflicts, particularly in the Middle East, have interrupted a strong regional growth trajectory and triggered a "normal" short-term reaction in interest rates.
    * JLL remains optimistic for a strong 2026 outlook, noting the global economy entered this period of uncertainty from a position of strength.
* **Shift to Real Assets:** Investors are increasingly viewing real estate as a hedge against uncertainty. 
    * **The "Core" Strategy:** Private capital is gravitating toward the most conservative routes—investing in Grade A buildings in prime locations across all asset classes (office, logistics, and multifamily).
    * **Investor Tiers:** Smaller investors are targeting public REITs, while institutional and ultra-high-net-worth individuals are moving toward direct real estate investments.

### Sector Outlook: Office, Multifamily, and Data Centers
* **The "Grade A" Office Premium:** There is a severe shortage of high-end office space globally. In markets like New York, the newest sustainable buildings command a **94% rent premium** over older inventory.
    * **Class B Conversions:** Upgrading Class B buildings to Class A status is viable only where rent growth justifies the high cost of construction.
* **Multifamily Weakness:** The sector is experiencing "wobbling" due to a surge in new supply and shifting household demographics. 
    * Success in multifamily is now highly dependent on specific sub-market locations and clearly defined target demographics (e.g., affluent vs. middle class).
* **Data Center Bottlenecks:** While demand for AI-driven data capacity is massive, growth is currently limited by a **lack of power grid availability** rather than a lack of capital or interest.

### AI: From Perceived Threat to Growth Driver
* **Addressing Market Misconceptions:** Ulbrich characterizes the recent sell-off of real estate service stocks due to AI fears as "unfair" and a misunderstanding of the business model.
    * **Market Share Gains:** JLL views itself as an "AI winner," using proprietary tools (like "Blackbird" and "Falcon") to gain market share and increase margins by boosting employee productivity.
* **Workforce Transformation:** Rather than mass layoffs, AI is allowing JLL to scale revenue without significantly increasing headcount. 
    * The company has already trained **30,000 employees** on AI tools to transition them into more insightful, data-driven roles.
* **Data as the "Untouched Treasure":** JLL’s competitive advantage stems from 10 years of collecting operational building data, which AI then processes to provide client advice that "monoliner" competitors cannot match.

### Sustainability and Urban Evolution
* **Sustainability as a Business Case:** Ulbrich argues that sustainability is a fundamental financial imperative, not a political one. 
    * Rising energy prices make low-consumption buildings a necessity for corporate bottom lines, regardless of the political climate.
* **The Future of CBDs:** Central Business Districts (CBDs) are evolving into "liveable, walkable" hubs that mix office, residential, and entertainment. 
    * **The AI Effect:** In cities like San Francisco, the AI boom is actively bringing tenants back into the city center, proving to be a stronger recovery catalyst than post-COVID return-to-office mandates.

### Strategic Advice for Investors
* **Operational Reality:** Real estate should no longer be treated like a passive bond investment. 
    * **Management Intensity:** Success requires active operation and data-driven expertise; investors who "buy and go on holiday" risk significant losses in the current environment.
* **Nearshoring Opportunities:** Supply chain disruptions and geopolitical turmoil are driving a massive shift toward "nearshoring."
    * This creates a long-term "uptick" opportunity for modern industrial and logistics facilities located closer to home markets.

> "We cannot see any significant risk of disruption to our business model. In fact... we are a clear winner of the AI age because we have invested very heavily, early on, ahead of our competitors." — **Christian Ulbrich**

> "Sustainability is not a political question. It's very much a fundamental business question... it makes a lot of sense to place your people into buildings which are sustainable, which have low energy consumption." — **Christian Ulbrich**

> "Investing into real estate, you are investing into something you have to operate... It is a business. And for every business you are investing in, you either should be the expert yourself or you should get yourself an expert, otherwise you will lose money." — **Christian Ulbrich**
