## Jensen Back, AI Trade on - Industrial Deep Dive with Steve Tusa | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 85

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cTZ_eF8GkjY
- Published: 2026-11-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261123.html#video-cTZ_eF8GkjY
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Steve Tusa (Industrials analyst, Wells Fargo)

---

### TL;DR
Wells Fargo industrials analyst Steve Tusa explains to host Steve Eisman that half of his 16 covered industrial companies are now deeply embedded in the AI data center build-out, driving unprecedented growth—but leaving both the sector and the broader U.S. economy highly vulnerable to a potential recession if AI capex pulls back. Meanwhile, physical construction timelines (roughly three years per gigawatt) and power bottlenecks—rather than Texas political pushback—represent the primary near-term constraints on a massive 450-gigawatt U.S. pipeline.

### The AI-Driven Industrial Boom & Macro Sensitivity
* **Unprecedented Sector Growth:** 8 out of 16 companies in Tusa’s coverage are directly in the thick of AI data center construction, driving group growth at **2x normal GDP rates** (compared to ~3% historically). Companies are beating and raising almost every quarter, trading at a slight premium to the S&P 500.
* **Massive Capex Tailwinds:** Consensus data center capex is projected at **$850 billion to $1 trillion** this year and **$1.4 trillion** next year.
* **"Nothing Else Matters" Thesis:** Tusa initiated on the group in late September under the title *Nothing Else Matters*. Outside of AI, traditional economic drivers like autos, housing, and general industrial volume (e.g., 3M’s core volume ex-data centers was just **1%** this quarter) are sluggish.
* **Recession & K-Shaped Consumer Risk:** Both Eisman and Tusa agree that a material pullback in AI capex (e.g., Google cutting capex in half) would almost certainly trigger a recession—though not a 2008/2009-style financial crisis, as banks are well-capitalized. Tusa notes that high-end consumers in the current K-shaped economy are holding up spending largely because they are benefiting from the AI trade.
* **The Unwind / Safety Trade:** During recent market unwinds, investors rotated into non-data-center safety plays like **Otis** (elevators) and **Lennox** (residential HVAC, zero data center exposure), which benefits if an AI slowdown drives interest rates down.

### Key Players & The Liquid Cooling Transition
* **Vertiv:** **85%** of revenue comes from data centers (both power and cooling). Its market cap has surged to **$70 billion**—matching former parent-style industrial giant Honeywell—up from a leveraged SPAC five years ago.
* **Eaton:** Over **20%** data center exposure, primarily in electrical power, though it recently acquired a liquid cooling/CDU company.
* **nVent:** Smaller-cap player with **20–25%** data center exposure, focused on liquid cooling (coolant distribution units, or CDUs) and some electrical gear.
* **Forgent:** Smaller-cap company that went public last year with **~55%** data center exposure, specializing in electrical equipment (transformers, switchgear) to step down and condition power.
* **Rockwell Automation:** ~**3%** of sales in data centers, adding **2 percentage points** to its ~**8%** total growth (~6% ex-data centers), alongside semiconductor exposure.
* **The Liquid Cooling Shift:** Data center racks have jumped from **10 kW** to **200 kW**, heading toward **1 MW**, packing up to **70 GPUs** (e.g., Nvidia Blackwell B200/B300) per rack. Air/fan walls can no longer cool them, forcing a mandatory shift to cold plates and liquid cooling. This has transformed liquid cooling from a **$1 billion** niche (aerospace/HPC) into a **$7 billion to $10 billion** mainstream industry.
* **Legacy HVAC Chillers:** **Johnson Controls (JCI)**, **Trane**, and **Carrier** remain essential for providing the massive chillers that reject heat from liquid cooling loops into the atmosphere.

### Onshoring & Data Center Construction Bottlenecks
* **Limited Broad Onshoring:** Despite tariff talk and 100% factory write-off incentives, broad manufacturing onshoring is incremental. Life sciences/pharma has a **$35 billion to $40 billion** pipeline—too small to move the needle.
* **Semiconductor & Electrical Exceptions:** Driven by AI demand rather than government subsidies, semiconductors have a **$250 billion** project pipeline over the next two years. Additionally, **16 million square feet** of new electrical/power/HVAC manufacturing capacity has been announced to supply **~$20 billion** in extra data center gear.
* **Scale of the Build-Out:** Prior to AI (2020–2023), the entire U.S. data center installed base was **20 gigawatts** (1% of U.S. power usage), built over 20 years at 1–2 GW/year. Tusa’s database of permits and land buys shows a current pipeline of **450 gigawatts**—more than **20x** the 2023 base.
* **Texas & Construction Realities:** Texas’s official ERCOT queue sits at **375 gigawatts**, yet as of mid-year, only **5 GW** were lit up and **3 GW** approved to be energized (~8–10 GW total). For example, the **Stargate** project in Abilene (800 MW to 1 GW) broke ground in June 2024 and is only **75%** complete.
* **Supply vs. Demand Debate:** Eisman highlights that an electrical contractor in Michigan noted a shortage of transmission gear and even utility poles. While Eisman views physical construction time (~3 years per gigawatt) as the binding constraint that could pause AI growth, Tusa argues Texas politics won't halt in-flight projects; the real long-term risk is whether end-user AI demand justifies the returns.

### Top Stock Picks & Sell-Side Dynamics
* **Johnson Controls (JCI):** Tusa’s first pick, featuring **~20%** data center exposure and **~30%** recurring services revenue. A new management team (~1.5 years in) is driving idiosyncratic margin upside, making it a safer "mid-cycle" play.
* **Vertiv:** Tusa’s second pick. Following a pullback to **$200**, he sees downside risk to **$175** against upside reward **north of $300**.
* **Client Meeting Dynamics:** Tusa notes that meetings with dedicated industrial analysts at large asset managers go five questions deep into macro funding (e.g., CoreWeave bonds), micro tech transitions, margins, and financial models. Meetings with generalist portfolio managers at smaller firms stay high-level, boiling down to three metrics: earnings growth, beat/miss vs. the Street, and valuation.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Recession on Capex Pullback:** If AI capex materially reverses, the U.S. economy will enter a recession (though not a 2008/2009-style banking crisis) — *Steve Tusa & Steve Eisman*.
* **Liquid Cooling Market Size:** The liquid cooling industry will scale from a $1 billion niche to a **$7 billion to $10 billion** mainstream industry — *Steve Tusa*.
* **Semiconductor Onshoring:** **$250 billion** in U.S. semiconductor project pipeline will materialize over the **next two years** — *Steve Tusa*.
* **Vertiv Price Target/Risk-Reward:** Following its pullback to $200, Vertiv carries downside risk to **$175** and upside potential **north of $300** — *Steve Tusa*.

---

### TL;DR
North Texas has experienced a doubling of wealth over the past decade, driven by a massive influx of people, corporate headquarters, and a resilient entrepreneurial culture, even as wealth managers navigate the human complexities of the great wealth transfer and AI integration.

### The North Texas Wealth Boom & Influx
- **Population & Economic Inflow:** Approximately 900,000 people have moved to Texas in the last five years, contributing to a diverse economy spanning energy, tech, healthcare, commercial real estate, and finance (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo; William Thompson, UBS; Clinton Warren, Citi).
- **Corporate Relocations:** Major companies have established global headquarters in the region, such as Charles Schwab near Westlake and Fidelity Investments, alongside a massive wave of real estate development in western suburbs (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Cross-Industry Collaboration:** New arrivals are networking and doing deals across traditional and emerging sectors, bolstered by networks and venture capital initiatives built by figures like Ross Perot and Aaron Pierce (Clinton Warren, Citi).

### Alternative Investments, Real Estate, and AI Exposure
- **Private Markets & Democratization:** Ultra-high-net-worth investors and family offices remain heavily committed to private markets, looking for liquidity and deal opportunities without any slowdown in commitment (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Private Credit Compression:** An influx of capital chasing private credit deals has caused expected yields to compress, with interest rates dropping from 15% down to 9% or 10% in some cases (William Thompson, UBS).
- **Data Center & Land Transactions:** Massive valuations and eye-watering offers—driven by AI infrastructure and data center development (including projects tied to Elon Musk)—are prompting multi-generational ranch-owning families to sell land they never expected to part with (William Thompson, UBS).
- **AI Portfolio Diligence:** While major IPOs like Anthropic draw interest, advisors stress that investors must audit their portfolios to understand their actual AI exposure and avoid repeating historical valuation bubbles like the .com era (Clinton Warren, Citi; William Thompson, UBS).

### Generational Wealth Transfer & Family Dynamics
- **Misaligned Inheritances:** According to wealth studies, 67% of business-owning parents do not anticipate leaving their businesses to their children, creating a disconnect with kids who assume they will inherit them (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **Entrepreneurial Next-Gen:** Millennials and Gen Z heirs are showing high entrepreneurial spirits, often seeking capital to start new businesses or repurpose legacy assets—such as converting ranch lands into new ventures (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **The Role of Human Advisors:** As multi-generational family goals splinter and asset legacy meanings shift, the core value of wealth managers increasingly relies on navigating complex family psychology and human "sticky muck" rather than commoditized portfolio management (Clinton Warren, Citi; Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).

### Technology & Election Impact on Wealth Management
- **AI in Investment Committees:** Some large family offices are experimenting with unconventional governance, such as replacing a human investment committee member with an AI agent to vote on cash flows, capital calls, and exposures (Clinton Warren, Citi).
- **The Human Element in Advisory:** Despite clients experimenting with AI tools like ChatGPT or Claude for financial questions, zero advisors reported clients challenging them without human advisory backing; firms are leaning into high-touch human elements (Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).
- **Election and Policy Sentiments:** While clients frequently inquire about portfolio hedging during election cycles, wholesale liquidation is rare due to tax consequences; investors are primarily focused on practical risk management, interest rate trajectories, and potential regulatory shifts concerning AI and energy (Clinton Warren, Citi; William Thompson, UBS; Heather Hunt-Ruddy, Wells Fargo).

## Wells Fargo's Van Beurden on Leveraging AI

- Speakers: Saul Van Beurden (Head of AI & Co-CEO of Consumer Banking and Lending, Wells Fargo); Lisa Mateo (Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR**
Wells Fargo is leveraging generative and agentic AI to transition from a period of regulatory remediation to a "growth" era, achieving significant productivity gains—including a 25% increase in new account openings—while prioritizing workforce "employability" through internal upskilling.

### Strategic AI Implementation & Operational Impact
* **Core Strategy:** Wells Fargo is using AI to fundamentally transform bank operations by focusing on three pillars: increasing banker productivity, enhancing customer experience, and eliminating manual tasks.
* **Measurable Results:** Implementation of AI-generated summaries and propensity models for branch bankers has led to a **25% increase** in both checking account and credit card openings, alongside higher deposit balances.
* **Customer Service Evolution:**
    * **Fargo Virtual Assistant:** Moving from traditional natural language processing to a full LLM-generated assistant after surpassing 1 billion customer interactions.
    * **Agentic AI:** Deploying autonomous agents in call centers and operations to handle complex workflows like complaint management and quality assurance (QA).
* **Efficiency Gains:** By automating the data-gathering process for the bank’s **55 million customers**, bankers can focus on high-value client conversations rather than "mundane" administrative prep.

### The Human Element: Upskilling & "Employability"
* **The "Two-Way Street" Concept:** Van Beurden emphasizes "employability" over mere job security, arguing the bank must provide tools for employees to move from "work to work" within the company or remain competitive in the broader labor market.
* **Internal Education:** The bank has established specialized internal "Colleges" (Consumer, Operations, and Credit) to automate career pathing.
    * **Participation:** Currently, **60% of staff** in the Consumer College have signed up for AI-driven upskilling paths.
* **Changing Roles:** In operations, the human role is shifting toward "overwatch," where employees oversee and validate the outputs of AI agents rather than performing the manual processing themselves.

### Risk Management & Post-Asset Cap Growth
* **The "Risk of Non-Action":** While acknowledging technical risks (cyber, data leaks, resiliency), Van Beurden asserts that the risk of failing to adopt AI is greater than the risk of mindful implementation.
* **The Asset Cap Transition:** Following the lifting of the federal asset cap in May, the bank has pivoted toward aggressive growth across several sectors:
    * **Marketing:** Quadrupled marketing spend in the consumer business.
    * **Branch Expansion:** Opening new physical locations in New York, Chicago, Tennessee, and Texas.
    * **Corporate Investment Banking (CIB):** Scaling the advisory business through significant Managing Director (MD) hiring.
* **Balanced Scorecard:** Success is measured through a combination of ROI (balancing the high cost of tokens/compute against revenue), risk mitigation (tracking material AI incidents), and workforce sentiment.

### Future Tech Frontiers: Quantum & Cognitive Effort
* **Quantum Computing:** Van Beurden identifies quantum as the next "popping moment" (similar to ChatGPT’s 2022 debut), noting its potential to revolutionize quantitative trading and market modeling through massive compute power.
* **The "Student Syndrome" Warning:** Despite the power of AI, Van Beurden warns against "cognitive laziness," urging professionals to continue using their own brains for critical thinking and empathy to maintain control over technology.
