**TL;DR**
Stripe CEO Patrick Collison discussed Stripe’s $7.5 billion acquisition of OpenRouter as a parallel to Stripe’s own neutral infrastructure—unifying AI tokens rather than dollars—while outlining the rise of an "AI economy" driven by agent-to-agent micropayments, the enduring value of human-made art, and the urgent need for meta-science reform.

### The OpenRouter Acquisition & AI Infrastructure
* **Neutral Infrastructure Parallel:** Stripe acquired OpenRouter for $7.5 billion (roughly 6x its prior valuation) after realizing both companies share the same core mission: providing neutral, unified infrastructure for everyone from solo developers to large public companies—Stripe for dollars and payment regulations, and OpenRouter for AI tokens.
* **Hyper-Growth Metrics:** 
  * Stripe grew its revenue 41% year-over-year in the first half of the year (disclosed in an investor letter).
  * OpenRouter’s volume of tokens served grew 37% month-over-month in October.
  * Collison noted that traditional valuation multiples break down at these growth rates, making it more useful to evaluate the market equilibrium 3 to 5 years out.
* **Model Diversity & Rapid Turnover:** 
  * 73% of OpenRouter’s customer usage is for models launched within the last 60 days.
  * Developers are highly sensitive to small differences across language, image, speech recognition, and decision models, driving demand for a diverse ecosystem.
* **Open-Source & Sovereignty:** Despite geopolitical concerns around Chinese open-weight models, Collison emphasized that open-source AI is essential for customers—particularly in Europe and outside the U.S.—who require sovereignty, self-hosted inference, observability, and full-stack governance.

### Agentic Commerce & The "AI Economy"
* **Machine Payments Protocol (MPP):** Stripe launched MPP a couple of months ago alongside **Tempo**, a new blockchain protocol.
  * MPP already processes tens of thousands of machine-to-machine transactions per day, though the absolute dollar volume is currently *de minimis*.
  * Many of these transactions are settled using stablecoins.
* **Pay-at-the-Moment-of-Utility:** Rather than AI agents "going on a bender," these human-driven transactions allow software to consume usage-based APIs (e.g., weather or price data) and pay instantly at the moment of utility—eliminating the need for pre-registration, advance contracts, or billing in arrears.
* **Shift to Consumption Models:** Agentic transactions are the next logical progression in a broader internet economy shift away from traditional seat-based pricing toward usage- and consumption-based models.

### Arts, Architecture, and AI Creativity
* **New Aesthetics Initiative:** Collison and economist Tyler Cowen launched *New Aesthetics*, awarding $10,000 to $250,000 grants to artists, architects, and designers.
  * Addressing moderator Reed Albergotti’s question on why Silicon Valley lags behind older industrial hubs like Detroit in funding grand cultural institutions, Collison agreed Silicon Valley should play a much bigger role in supporting the arts.
* **Breaking the "Modernist Rut":** Collison argued that architecture has been stuck in a modernist rut for a century—noting that the Bauhaus Dessau building in Germany looks like a modern Silicon Valley office—and needs a new 21st-century aesthetic.
* **Human Art in an AI Era:** Despite being deeply AI-forward, Stripe’s publishing arms (**Stripe Press** and **Works in Progress** magazine) exclusively use human-created art. Collison argued that intentionality, purpose, and relational engagement matter to consumers, and AI-generated art would make the products worse.

### Meta-Science & Research Funding
* **Arc Institute & Policy Engagement:** Collison co-founded the Arc Institute with his wife to help academic researchers avoid "grant writing hell." He recently spoke in Washington, D.C., with Michael Kratsios (head of the Office of Science and Technology Policy) about reforming basic research grants.
* **Reviving Meta-Science:** Collison noted that "meta-science"—studying how best to fund scientists (e.g., funding projects vs. funding people) and structure institutions—has been moribund for decades. He welcomed recent policy reports calling for renewed focus on these questions, especially amid the upheaval of AI.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Agent-to-Agent Transaction Volume:** Within a few years, most transactions (by volume, though not necessarily by dollar value) will be between AI agents — *Patrick Collison (referenced by Reed Albergotti and affirmed by Collison)*.
* **Emergence of Three Economies:** Going forward, the global economy will be best understood as three distinct spheres: the non-internet economy, the internet economy, and the **AI economy** — *Patrick Collison*.
* **Endurance of Open-Source AI:** The open-source AI model ecosystem "is something that's here to stay" due to international demand for inference sovereignty — *Patrick Collison*.
* **Persistence of Human Art:** Consumer and artist preference for human-created art—caring about what humans chose to depict—will persist despite the rise of AI — *Patrick Collison*.

### TL;DR
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh and tech CEOs Max Levchin (Affirm), Patrick Collison (Stripe), and Nikesh Arora (Palo Alto Networks) argue that superintelligence (SI) is poised to break decades of secular stagnation by automating idea generation, with the U.S. holding a decisive lead in both model creation and enterprise diffusion. While frontier companies are already seeing 5x engineering productivity gains and a 2x surge in new business formation, the next two to three years will require a massive cybersecurity and infrastructure rebuild as AI exposes latent software vulnerabilities and reshapes labor markets.

### The Fed's Economic Framework & Contingency Planning
* **Ideas as the Ultimate Resource:** Drawing on endogenous growth theory (Paul Romer, Chad Jones), Fed Chair Kevin Warsh noted that while U.S. research efforts have increased more than 20x since the 1930s, research productivity has fallen by an even larger amount, fueling fears of secular stagnation.
  * Unlike past general-purpose technologies (the internal combustion engine, electricity, transistors), SI could automate and supercharge the creation of new ideas itself, driving sustained above-trend growth.
  * Warsh emphasized that machines cannot replace human judgment, morality, or responsibility: "Humans are the designers and the builders, and humans must be the deciders."
* **Three Illustrative Fed Scenarios:** Emphasizing that the Fed is focused on contingency planning rather than forecasting—having put this task force at the front of the line 5.5 months ago as one of five convened under his chairmanship—Warsh outlined three stylized economic outcomes:
  * **Broadly Shared Gains:** SI boosts total factor productivity across labor and capital, keeping real GDP persistently above its long-term average with full employment.
  * **Labor Market Disruption:** GDP growth rises above average, but widespread automation and labor frictions push unemployment above full-employment levels.
  * **AI Disappointment:** After heavy capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns turn down, equity valuations fall, and the economy slows with rising unemployment and no lasting productivity gains.
* **Deflationary Compute Costs:** Warsh noted that token prices are falling roughly 50% per quarter at an accelerating rate, suggesting that market forces may push the vast majority of the economic surplus toward technology users rather than just model builders.

### Enterprise Adoption & Productivity at the Frontier
* **Engineering & Underwriting Gains (Max Levchin, Affirm):** Affirm has achieved roughly 5x engineering productivity (cutting cost per feature to 1/5th, with expectations to reach 1/10th) with zero AI-related layoffs.
  * Affirm used LLMs to design a new transformer-based underwriting model that outperformed its non-LLM baseline by 80%, translating to roughly $2 billion more in annual loan volume.
  * Levchin argued that AI enables "mass customization," allowing engineers to rescue long-tail ideas from the cutting room floor, though human prioritization remains critical to avoid shipping bugs ahead of high-stakes periods like Black Friday.
* **Startups vs. Incumbents (Patrick Collison, Stripe):** Echoing Paul David’s 1989/1990 "productivity paradox" paper on electricity, Collison noted that new firms are capturing SI gains faster than incumbents encumbered by governance and compliance.
  * The 2026 cohort of new businesses launching on Stripe every week is up approximately 2x year-over-year—an unprecedented jump in Stripe's 15-year history, surpassing even pandemic-era surges.
  * Internally at Stripe, G&A hiring has been substantially reduced as a share of revenue, while R&D/engineering and sales hiring are accelerating because cheaper feature production and higher seller ROI make it mathematically advantageous to buy more.
* **Democratizing Intelligence (Nikesh Arora, Palo Alto Networks):** Arora highlighted that while enterprise ROI dominates current headlines due to the $50 billion cost of training frontier models, AI's broader societal impact is putting "genius in our pocket" at near-zero marginal cost for the 6 billion people globally who lack access to specialized expertise.

### Cybersecurity, Infrastructure & Edge-Case Risks
* **The "Technology Rebuild" Moment:** Both Arora and Collison warned that AI has shifted the asymmetric battle in cybersecurity. Open-source models like Mythos (released six months ago) allow even unsophisticated hackers to rapidly uncover latent bugs in decades-old code.
  * Collison noted that even heavily scrutinized software like the Linux TCP stack has been found to be "riven with bugs" and exploitable issues.
  * Arora expects more frequent cyber events—especially targeting interconnected systems like banking—but noted that AI can also be used to refactor and rewrite legacy code over the next two to three years.
* **The 20% Edge-Case Problem:** Arora cautioned against overestimating near-term autonomous job replacement, citing Waymo: since he rode in Google's first driverless Lexus in 2009, it has taken 17 years (to 2026) and over $30 billion to replace a single human job, largely because handling the final 20% of edge cases is extraordinarily difficult.
* **Market Concentration & "Soft Despotism":**
  * Levchin warned that unlike past tech revolutions, capital-rich incumbents are adopting AI from day one, risking an oligopolistic economy dominated by the Fortune 100 while hollowing out the middle tier (Fortune 10,000), even as solo-preneurs thrive in the long tail.
  * Collison cited Alexis de Tocqueville’s warning of "soft despotism," questioning what human ambition and the nature of work will look like if AI eliminates labor and creates a "suffocating tranquility."

### Global Competition: U.S. vs. The Rest of the World
* **Widening U.S. Lead:** All panelists agreed that the U.S. is in a preeminent position, not just in building models (centered in the Bay Area), but in diffusing them.
  * **Collison:** Stripe data shows AI usage intensity is higher in the U.S. than elsewhere, widening the economic divergence with Europe.
  * **Levchin:** The European Union is still "a couple of meta levels away" from accepting AI's reality, focusing on regulation while the U.S. and China race ahead on energy and compute investments.
  * **Arora:** Few countries can afford $50 billion model runs, meaning intelligence will be built in a few global hubs, while the U.S. captures the fastest enterprise margin and competitiveness gains.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Drop-in remote workers:** "Many people in the AI sector expect drop-in remote workers within the next 12 months"—autonomous agents on Slack and Zoom capable of doing anything a human with a monitor and keyboard can do (Patrick Collison).
* **Global energy bet:** Two influential San Francisco AI figures have a bet on whether global energy use will double in a single month within the next 10 years (cited by Patrick Collison).
* **Technology rebuild:** The next two to three years will be a "technology rebuild moment" as companies use AI to find and fix decades of legacy software vulnerabilities (Nikesh Arora).
* **Engineering cost floor:** Affirm expects its cost per engineering feature to fall from 1/5th of its pre-AI baseline to 1/10th (Max Levchin).
* **Gradual reskilling:** Job displacement will not arrive as a sudden shock, but as a gradual reskilling where entire job categories quietly disappear over a 10-year horizon (Nikesh Arora).

### Notable Quotes

Boulton & Watt incubator co-founder Sam Gerstenzang and Stripe co-founder John Collison discuss building vertical SaaS companies by working backwards from a business's P&L, using AI coding to build customized software for every user, and leveraging scale and risk-taking to compete with private equity rollups.

### The Boulton & Watt Incubator Model
* **The "Slow" Strategy:** **Sam Gerstenzang** explains that Boulton & Watt starts only one company every few years, dedicating 100% of focus to it until it is past product-market fit before bringing in a replacement CEO.
* **Target Industries:** They hunt for businesses that are overlooked, regulated, complex, taboo, or local with geographic monopolies (e.g., med spas, funeral homes, freight brokerages).
* **P&L Inversion:** Instead of starting with a standalone product, they work backwards from the P&L—identifying where businesses make and spend money, and aiming to become the overarching infrastructure (Moxy, taxes, and real estate).
* **Company 1 (Moxy):** **Sam Gerstenzang** states Moxy helps doctors and nurses open med spas (Botox and laser shops), solving state legal licensing (similar to Stripe Atlas), providing marketing services, software, and bulk pharma purchasing.
* **Company 2 (Funeral Homes):** Operates as the end-to-end operating system for independent funeral homes.
* **Company 3 (Freight Brokerage):** **Sam Gerstenzang** notes they empower individual freight brokers (often working with 1-10 trucks) to leave big firms, take on risk independently, and compete with PE-backed rollups.

### The Impact of AI on Software Development
* **Custom Software Per User:** **Sam Gerstenzang** notes that post-AI coding, instead of using a single standardized product, they now rapidly build custom software for individual customers (e.g., building a custom automated portal for a freight broker in two hours).
* **The 1:1 PM-to-Engineer Ratio:** **Sam Gerstenzang** highlights that post-AI, Boulton & Watt maintains a 1:1 product manager-to-engineer ratio (compared to traditional 1:6 or 1:8 ratios), keeping engineering headcount flat while radically increasing surface area and deployment speed.
* **Architecture & Refactoring:** **Sam Gerstenzang** notes they had to refactor older codebases (dating back 4 years) so that AI tools can understand them as well as humans. All customer-facing code still undergoes manual software engineer review to prevent maintainable "slop."
* **AI Limitations:** **Sam Gerstenzang** notes that AI still struggles with synthesis (e.g., generating financial forecasts or creating new business ideas) and high-stakes automation where users have zero tolerance for mistakes (e.g., front-desk med spa phone calls).

### Taking Risk as a Core Business Competency
* **Risk Brokerage:** **Sam Gerstenzang** argues that pure software businesses must evolve because software gets commoditized. Boulton & Watt's core competitive advantage is taking on real-world risk for customers (e.g., HIPPA compliance, credit risk, shipper/broker liabilities).
* **Systems of Record vs. Workflow:** **John Collison** and **Sam Gerstenzang** discuss how systems of record (like Salesforce, Workday, or Toast) must reach deeper into solving end-to-end business problems and risk management to survive against cheaper point solutions and AI-driven workflow alternatives.
* **Independent vs. PE Competition:** **Sam Gerstenzang** explains that their model counters private equity (PE) rollups by giving independent operators superior buying power (e.g., Moxy becoming one of the largest buyers of Botox in the country) and complete operational tooling.

### Talent, Go-To-Market, and Future Horizons
* **Hiring Criteria:** **Sam Gerstenzang** looks for three traits in talent: high agency, high curiosity, and high judgment.
* **Real-World GTM Integration:** Go-to-market strategies are deeply integrated into the physical world, such as hosting med spa nurses at shoe-shopping events or opening a training center in LA with TikTok influencers.
* **Future Sectors:** **Sam Gerstenzang** reveals they are actively looking at trends in the restoration space (pipe bursts, climate change insurance claims), fire safety, and animal hospice over Zoom.

### 2. AI-Powered Payments Optimization & Pricing Flexibility
* **Platform Growth Studio & Stripe Console:**
  * **AI-Generated Insights:** Console acts as an agentic payments expert built directly into the Stripe dashboard, proactively raising issues (e.g., volume rising 7.9% while take rate fell 6.5 basis points).
  * **Automated Root-Cause Analysis:** Console investigates payment mix, methods, geographies, and network costs; in a demo case, it traced a margin decline to a single connected account (*Northeast Yoga*) due to an increase in average transaction size.
  * **Network Benchmarking:** Compares platform performance against Stripe network medians (e.g., comparing Northeast Yoga's 271 bps take rate to a platform median of 400 bps and a Stripe vertical median of 290 bps).

  * **Developer Access:** Data teams can access this intelligence via Stripe Sigma, Stripe data pipeline, and Stripe's MCP for external agents like GPT.
* **Granular Pricing Controls:**
  * **Funded Discounts:** Platforms can choose individual merchants, give custom discounts, and fund the difference from their revenue share.
  * **Pass-Through vs. Absorbed Fees:** Platforms owning their own pricing can configure fee-by-fee costs (e.g., *TSD Mobility* charges core processing fees like per authorization and network fees; *CAB9* charges for value-added services like 3D secure lookup) using the platform pricing tool.

  * Platforms embedding multiple financial products see **49% faster revenue growth** than those offering payments alone.

  * **Federal Reserve Connection:** Direct connection enables same-day ACH payments hours closer to end-of-day cutoff, launching in two weeks.
  * **Direct Treasury Deposits:** Customers can receive payments directly into their Treasury balance without transferring from a separate payments balance.
* **Stripe Issuing:**
