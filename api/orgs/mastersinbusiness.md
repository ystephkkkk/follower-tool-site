### TL;DR
William "Bill" McNab, former CEO of Vanguard and current board member at IBM, United Health, and various fintech startups, discusses his 30+ year career scaling index funds, mastering crisis leadership during the 2008 financial crash, transitioning to modern board governance, and backing disruptive tech startups in wealth management and estate planning.

### The Vanguard Era: From $15 Billion to Trillions
* **Joining Vanguard in 1986:** **Career pivot:** McNab left a traditional Wall Street role at JP Morgan Chase after an interview with Jack Bogle, who managed $15 billion at the time and lectured him on asset management flaws for 1.5 hours. **Early competitive landscape:** Vanguard fought intense money market fund wars (yielding 17-18%) against Dreyfus and Fidelity, and matched Fidelity’s Peter Lynch/Magellan fund with John Neff’s Windsor Fund.
* **The Mathematical & Psychological Shift to Indexing:** **The math:** On an after-tax basis, index funds beat 90% of active equities over any rolling period greater than 10 years because active and passive combined must equal the market before costs. **The stickiness:** Vanguard shareholders stayed with the firm three times longer than the industry average duration.
* **The 2008 Crisis Leadership:** **Zero layoffs:** Rather than cutting staff like competitors whose revenue dropped 35-50% (equity markets down 57%), McNab and the board executed zero redundancies, doubling down on client service and transferring excess employees to fix operational bottlenecks. **Strategic moves:** The firm went all in on the independent advisor channel, asset-based fees, ETFs (after missing out on iShares due to regulatory intervention halting their bid against BlackRock), and target date funds (moving away from flawed risk-tolerance quizzes toward a simple retirement-date default).

### Corporate Governance & Public Boards
* **IBM Transformation:** **Strategic agility:** Serving as an IBM board member, McNab highlights CEO Arvind Krishna's successful pivot of the legacy typewriter/mainframe company toward hybrid cloud and AI, while balancing shareholder perspectives with tech/quantum domain experts.
* **United Health Turnaround:** **Managing externalities:** Navigating unprecedented external shocks, including the murder of the CEO and leadership changes, required a venture/startup level of agility from the board, culminating in former CEO Steve Hemsley returning to run the company.
* **Quarterly Reporting vs. Long-Term Guidance:** **Guidance critique:** McNab views eliminating quarterly reporting as a false move that achieves nothing, but advocates holding companies accountable for multi-year (5 to 10-year) strategic outlooks rather than managing strictly to short-term Wall Street estimates.

### Venture Capital & Fintech Startups
* **Advising Venrock & Altruist:** **Scaling through tech:** McNab serves as a senior advisor to Venrock and an investor/adviser in Jason Wank’s Altruist, noting its rise as the third-largest RIA custodian by advisor count by undercutting legacy sweep-fee models (which generated 57% of Schwab's RIA revenue at peak spreads).
* **Estate Planning and Legal Tech Disruptors:** **Vanilla:** A software product transforming estate planning by codifying expert Steve Leimberg's tax-planning brain into automated client balance sheets. **Finny & Sillow:** Finny focuses on client-prospect fit algorithms and success-based SaaS pricing models; Sillow merges legal talent with engineering for non-correlated litigation asset investments.
* **Fixed Income Trading:** **Moment:** Founded by ex-Citadel traders, Moment brings fractional bond trading to fixed income similar to equities.

### The Future of Advice & Retail Speculation
* **Human-in-the-Loop Enablement:** **Advisor leverage:** Technology (AI note-taking, automated workflows) will enable a single advisor to manage 300 clients instead of 100, freeing them up to provide critical behavioral guardrails during market drawdowns.
* **The Dangers of Hyper-Financialization:** **Day trading surge:** McNab warns against the dangerous return of rampant retail day trading, single-day options, prediction markets, and gambling apps, emphasizing that "only the house wins."

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Advisor Capacity Growth:** Technology will allow financial advisors to triple their client capacity, scaling from an industry norm of 100 clients to 300 clients per advisor while improving service quality — Bill McNab.

## The Science Behind The Markets | Masters in Business with David Booth

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7GeN1R5b3oE

- Speakers: Barry Ritholtz (Host, Masters in Business); David Booth (Founder and Chairman, Dimensional Fund Advisors); Eugene Fama (Nobel Laureate Economist)

---

### TL;DR
David Booth reflects on his career, the foundation of Dimensional Fund Advisors (DFA), and his new book *Stay Calm*, emphasizing that embracing uncertainty and trusting the science of efficient, factor-based markets are the keys to long-term investment success.

### Academic Roots and the Birth of Quantitative Finance
- **The University of Chicago Influence:** In the late 1960s and early 1970s, finance emerged as a science backed by CRSP market data starting from 1926, laying the groundwork for Eugene Fama’s Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH).
- **Early Career Pivot:** Booth left his PhD studies at Chicago—earning an MBA on his way out—to join Mac MacQuown at Wells Fargo in San Francisco, where early institutional indexing and quantitative methods were being pioneered.
- **The Samsonite Account:** Early quantitative experiments at Wells Fargo tested high-beta portfolios, while parallel trust department efforts led by MacQuown created early index funds that eventually evolved into multi-trillion-dollar industry giants like BlackRock.

### Founding Dimensional Fund Advisors (DFA)
- **Small-Cap and Multi-Factor Genesis:** Launched out of a Brooklyn Heights apartment in 1981, DFA initially focused on a small-cap strategy based on Fama's research and Ralph Banz’s dissertation on size quintiles.
- **Factor Evolution:** Moving beyond single-factor beta, the firm incorporated Fama-French multi-factor models (size, value, and others) to build systematic, factor-based portfolios rather than simple market-cap weighting.
- **Trading as an Edge:** Rather than trying to beat the market through stock-picking, DFA leverages execution mechanics and volatility to provide liquidity and achieve better pricing than rigid index funds that suffer from costly additions and deletions.

### Philosophy of *Stay Calm*
- **Embracing Uncertainty:** Uncertainty is not something to fear; it is the fundamental driver of possibility and opportunity in both life and investing.
- **First Principles and Human Ingenuity:** When bad news hits (e.g., the 2020 pandemic or the 1998 financial crisis), markets drop because the bad news is already priced in; recovery is driven by human ingenuity, rewarding investors who stay calm and stay invested.
- **Plan, Don't Predict:** Forecasting macroeconomic events or trying to time the market is a waste of time; investors should build a long-term plan, control what they can (risk allocation, savings), and tune out financial media noise.

### Distribution, ETFs, and the Advisor Revolution
- **Fee-Only Advisors:** Beginning in 1989 with Dan Wheeler, DFA partnered with fee-only financial advisors who acted as behavioral guardrails, preventing clients from abandoning their portfolios.
- **Late Entry to ETFs:** DFA stayed out of ETFs for decades because institutional and advisory clients were satisfied with traditional mutual funds and dual-class structures that neutralized tax disadvantages.
- **Recent ETF Innovation:** DFA launched its first ETF in 2020 and grew to become the largest active ETF issuer in the country, recently securing SEC approval to merge mutual fund and ETF pools of assets.

### Philanthropy and Legacy
- **University of Chicago Booth School of Business:** In November 2008, Booth gave a historic $300 million gift to the University of Chicago business school, leading to its renaming as the Chicago Booth School of Business.
- **University of Kansas Athletics:** As a Lawrence native, Booth donated $300 million to KU athletics to support competitive programs and name, image, and likeness (NIL) student funding.
- **The Naismith Rules:** In 2010, Booth purchased James Naismith's original 1891 typewritten rules of basketball for $4.5 million at auction (outbidding David Rubenstein) and returned them to Lawrence, Kansas.
- **MoMA Conservation Center:** Serving on the board of the Museum of Modern Art for roughly 10 years, Booth endowed the conservation lab to advance the scientific preservation of modern art collections.
