## Secretary Hegseth, Chairman Caine & PDO OUSW-CFO Hurst Testify Before the HAC FY 27 Budget Request

- Channel: Department of War
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kaprb49P08I
- Published: 2026-05-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260512.html#video-Kaprb49P08I
- Speakers: Pete Hegseth (Secretary, Department of War); Dan Caine (Chairman, Department of War); Jules Hurst III (PDO War Department Comptroller)

---

**TL;DR** 
The Department of War's $1.5 trillion FY27 budget request faces intense congressional scrutiny over a controversial "divest-to-invest" aviation procurement strategy, the rising $29 billion cost of the war with Iran, and a new capital expenditure paradigm aimed at rapidly expanding the U.S. defense industrial base to counter China.

---

### Aviation Procurement and the Shift Away from "Divest-to-Invest"
* **Re-evaluating Aviation Sector Cuts:** The department's FY27 budget request proposes cutting over $5 billion from the aviation sector, effectively halting current procurement for core Army platforms (including Black Hawk, Chinook, and Apache helicopters). 
    * Representative DeLauro and Judge Carter raised concerns that halting these production lines creates a critical near-term capability gap and risks permanently degrading the specialized supplier base and skilled workforce.
    * Secretary Hegseth confirmed the department is reviewing these cuts and will provide the committee with minimum sustaining production rates, supplier-base risks, and mitigation plans for the Black Hawk and Chinook platforms.
* **Moving Beyond Austerity Mindsets:** Secretary Hegseth announced the department is actively shedding the "divest-to-invest" austerity strategy—where current platforms are retired early to fund future, unfielded capabilities. 
    * The department is actively funding platforms currently utilized on the battlefield (e.g., MQ-9s, A-10s) and has submitted a budget amendment to OMB to secure the future of the E-7 platform.
* **Managing Transition Risks:** Chairman Dan Caine emphasized that the Joint Staff is closely tracking transition risks to avoid a mobility, lift, and sustainment gap as legacy platforms are phased out and Future Vertical Lift schedules evolve.

### Escalating Iran War Costs and Stockpile Debates
* **Updated War Projections:** PDO War Department Comptroller Jules Hurst III testified that the total cost of the war with Iran has risen to approximately $29 billion (up from a $25 billion estimate on April 29, 2026).
    * **Expended Materiel:** Replacement and repair of expended military hardware (excluding military construction) is currently estimated at $24 billion, which includes the loss of 39 aircraft.
    * **Unestimated Costs:** Operational fuel replenishment is currently absorbed into O&M costs, but future Military Construction (MilCon) costs for damaged Middle East infrastructure remain unquantified due to geopolitical and posture uncertainties.
* **Munitions & Stockpile Readiness:** Despite congressional concerns over depleted stockpiles, U.S. leadership defended current readiness levels.
    * Chairman Caine affirmed that the combatant commands maintain sufficient munitions to execute current tasks.
    * Secretary Hegseth rejected public assertions that U.S. munitions are depleted, stating the $1.5 trillion budget request is explicitly designed to supercharge future munitions manufacturing.
* **Ceasefire Status and "Project Freedom":** A bilateral ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran is currently in effect. "Project Freedom"—a CENTCOM-led initiative to coordinate and guide commercial ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz—was temporarily paused at the request of Pakistan but remains an active operational option.

### Pacific Deterrence and Next-Generation Capabilities
* **Countering China's Production Speed:** The FY27 budget dedicates $11.7 billion to the Pacific Deterrence Initiative (PDI) to address China as the pacing military and industrial challenge.
    * Chairman Caine emphasized that the U.S. must scale up manufacturing speed across second-, third-, and fourth-tier suppliers to match China’s shipbuilding and production capabilities.
* **Naval Power and Power Projection:** Following a record-setting 11-month deployment of the USS *Gerald R. Ford*, officials highlighted the critical role of aircraft carriers as mobile airfields in the Indo-Pacific. Next-generation capabilities like the F-47 and the F-A-XX (slated for a down-select this summer) are deemed vital to operating outside the range of Chinese hypersonic weapons.
* **Nuclear Deterrence and Microreactors:** The department is investing $2 billion in FY26 for the Sea-Launched Cruise Missile-Nuclear (SLCM-N) to provide non-strategic options lower on the escalation ladder. Additionally, the department is accelerating nuclear propulsion and the adoption of advanced microreactors (such as the Army's JANUS program) to guarantee resilient expeditionary power in contested environments.

### Fiscal Management, Procurement Reform, and Reprogramming
* **Controversy Over Mandatory Funding:** Several committee members, including Ms. McCollum and Mr. Morelle, strongly criticized the department's reliance on mandatory funding and reconciliation bills (which account for 38% of the $413 billion FY27 procurement request), arguing it bypasses standard discretionary oversight.
* **The Private Capitalization Paradigm:** Secretary Hegseth defended a major procurement shift: private defense primes are now paying for their own plant construction and equipment capitalization (saving taxpayers an estimated $50 billion in upfront infrastructure costs) in exchange for guaranteed 5-to-7-year munitions procurement contracts.
* **Critical Reprogramming Deficits:** Ms. McCollum raised alarms over massive unbudgeted operational deficits requiring immediate funding reallocations:
    * **National Guard:** $600 million in unbudgeted mission requirements.
    * **Army Personnel:** Nearly $1 billion in personnel shortfalls.
    * **Navy Personnel:** $907 million in personnel shortfalls.

### Regional Security, Border Operations, and Personnel Care
* **Countering Cartel Drone Threats:** Cartels are increasingly adopting advanced military tactics, such as "fiber-optic drone systems" learned from the Russia-Ukraine conflict. Joint Interagency Task Force (JIATF) 401, led by Brigadier General Matt Ross, is utilizing U.S. Southwest border test sites for rapid counter-UAS field testing.
* **National Guard Reimbursement Gaps:** Mr. Womack highlighted that between FY20 and FY24, states paid over $71 million to the National Guard for community disaster response under Title 32. These funds were never reimbursed to the National Guard Bureau because non-federal reimbursements currently default directly to the U.S. Treasury.
* **JBSA Barracks Reform:** In response to reports of unacceptable, unlivable housing conditions for 68W combat medic students at Joint Base San Antonio, Secretary Hegseth promised immediate physical inspections and broader MilCon bureaucratic reforms.
* **Toxic Exposure Records for Veterans:** Representative Susie Lee urged the department to immediately release service records for toxic-exposed veterans from the Nevada Test and Training Range (NTTR) to facilitate VA medical coverage. Secretary Hegseth committed to personally reviewing the department's position on these records.

## Secretary Hegseth and Chairman Caine Testify Before the SAC FY 27 Budget Request

- Channel: Department of War
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i-2XYXR1ktU
- Published: 2026-05-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260512.html#video-i-2XYXR1ktU
- Speakers: Pete Hegseth (Secretary, Department of War); Dan Caine (Chairman, Department of War)

---

**TL;DR** 
Secretary of War Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine testified on the Department of War's $1.5 trillion FY27 budget request, defending the administration's military campaign in Iran and its transition to a "business model" for defense acquisitions. However, they faced intense bipartisan questioning over a $350 billion reliance on one-time reconciliation funding, rising domestic energy costs from the Strait of Hormuz blockade, and months-long delays in releasing congressional security assistance to Ukraine and Baltic allies.

### The FY27 Budget Structure: Base vs. Reconciliation
* **The $1.5 Trillion Split:** The Department of War is requesting $1.1 trillion in base appropriations and an additional $350 billion through a one-time budget reconciliation request.
* **Bipartisan Budgetary Concerns:** Senate Appropriations Committee members strongly questioned why key, long-term military priorities are built into the volatile reconciliation request instead of the stable base budget.
    * **Program Vulnerabilities:** High-priority items dependent on the $350 billion reconciliation package include 53 of the requested 85 F-35 fighter jets, $53 billion for drone dominance, and $113 billion for munitions production and industrial base investments.
    * **Lack of Industry Stability:** Senators emphasized that base funding is critical for creating budget stability and sending consistent demand signals to the defense industrial base, whereas reconciliation is highly vulnerable to political gridlock.
* **Troop Quality of Life:** Secretary Hegseth highlighted that the FY27 request includes a historic 7% troop pay increase and fully funds the elimination of all poor or failing barracks.

### Military Operations in Iran and the Strait of Hormuz Blockade
* **Escalating Campaign Costs:** The official operational cost estimate for Operation Epic Fury and Operation Freedom Now has risen to $29 billion. 
    * **Underreported Damage:** Critics pointed out this estimate excludes the extensive cost of repairing damaged US facilities. Independent reports indicate Iran has struck at least 228 structures or pieces of equipment across 16 US military sites.
* **Strategic Success vs. Domestic Fallout:** Gen. Caine outlined the military's objectives as sinking the conventional Iranian Navy, degrading ballistic missile/drone capabilities, and destroying Iran's defense industrial base to prevent them from obtaining a nuclear weapon.
    * **The Strait of Hormuz Standoff:** Despite the destruction of Iran's conventional navy, Iran's asymmetric use of fast boats and cheap Shahed drones has brought maritime shipping to a standstill, leaving 1,500 commercial tankers stranded.
    * **US Energy Impact:** US consumers are facing gas prices ranging from $4 to $7 per gallon due to the shipping disruptions. Hegseth defended the US naval blockade on Iranian ports, asserting that economic pressure on Tehran is overwhelming and that the US maintains the military means to reopen the strait.
* **Battle Damage Assessment Discrepancies:** Senators challenged the administration's claim that 80% of Iran's missile capacity has been destroyed, citing intelligence estimates indicating that up to 70% of Iran's missile and drone capabilities remain intact. Both Hegseth and Caine declined to validate or comment on these figures in an open setting.

### Allied Burden Sharing and Delayed Ukraine Aid
* **Frustration Over Delayed Ukraine Funding:** Senators of both parties demanded to know why the department has failed to spend $400 million in European capacity-building funds appropriated by Congress in January to aid Ukraine.
    * **Spend Plan Delays:** Hegseth testified that the funds have been released from his office and that a final spending plan—formulated by European Command (EUCOM)—is expected to be delivered to Congress "this week."
* **Baltic Security Initiative (BSI) Halt:** The department confirmed that $200 million designated for the Baltic states and Poland is currently frozen. The department is attempting to resolve an administrative drafting error in the appropriations bill through a reprogramming action to transfer the funds to the Defense Security Cooperation Agency (DSCA).
* **The Role of Global Alliances:** Hegseth emphasized a focus on "burden sharing," stating that the US wants capable allies willing to deploy without political restrictions, rather than "carrying the burden for other countries." Senators Coons and Reed pushed back, warning that launching the war in Iran without consulting NATO partners has created a dangerous rift at a time when adversaries like Russia, China, North Korea, and Iran are increasingly aligned.

### Defense Industrial Base and Shipbuilding Transitions
* **Pivot to a "Business Model":** Secretary Hegseth testified that the Department of War has shifted its procurement strategy from a bureaucratic model to an outcome-driven business model.
    * **Private Investment Surge:** The department has stimulated over $50 billion in private investments across 39 states and 180 cities, resulting in 280 new or expanded manufacturing facilities, 18 million square feet of new manufacturing space, and 70,000 new jobs without utilizing taxpayer capital.
* **Domestic Shipbuilding Cutbacks:** Senator Collins questioned why the department requested funding for only one DDG-51 Arleigh Burke-class destroyer (down from two in FY26) while allocating $1.8 billion in reconciliation funds for foreign-built surface combatants. Hegseth cited domestic shipyard capacity limits as the reason for the reduction.
* **Wisconsin Frigate Cancellation:** Senator Baldwin pressed leadership over the abrupt cancellation of the Constellation-class frigate program in Wisconsin, which has disrupted the local workforce. She criticized the department's decision to place the construction of six replacement medium landing ships into the uncertain reconciliation budget.

## Multi-Domain AI: The Future of Command and Control | CDAO at AIPCon 9

- Channel: Palantir
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yrtDgoqWmgM
- Published: 2026-03-13

- Speakers: Cameron Stanley (CDAO, Department of War)

---

**TL;DR**
Project Maven has shifted from a technology-first AI approach to a "decision-centric" workflow model, leveraging the Maven Smart System to integrate disparate data feeds and collapse combat "kill chain" timelines from hours to minutes.

### The Shift to Decision Advantage
* **The Third Offset:** While historical offsets focused on nuclear capabilities (1st) and stealth/precision munitions (2nd), the 21st-century advantage is defined by "Decision Advantage"—making better decisions faster than an adversary.
* **Initial Hypothesis Failure:** Early efforts focused on AI for UAV surveillance (PED) to prevent human fatigue. However, the Department realized that simply giving AI to warfighters didn't solve the core problem: the underlying workflows remained manual, reliant on whiteboards and static PowerPoint slides.
* **Decision-Centric Approach:** The strategy shifted to prioritize the workflow over the AI. This is governed by nine key questions, including:
    * What part of the process is being accelerated?
    * How will the user interact with the data?
    * What is the specific reduction in human input?

### Synchronizing the Dual Flywheels
* **Coupled Development:** Successful digital transformation requires the simultaneous rotation of two "flywheels":
    * **Technology Development:** Standard spiral development of software and models.
    * **Process Improvement:** Redesigning how the military actually operates.
* **Operational Feedback:** True advantage is gained only when technology is delivered in sync with the operational process, allowing user feedback to drive rapid, iterative improvements rather than waiting for multi-year requirements cycles.
* **Human-Centric Integration:** The goal of data professionals is not to replace senior leaders but to augment them by providing data in the right format and capacity to enhance their existing professional competence.

### Maven Smart System: Integrated Command and Control
* **Data Fusion:** Using Palantir’s software-as-a-service (SaaS) product, the Department has moved away from forcing decision-makers to monitor 8-9 different systems.
* **The Abstraction Layer:** The system uses a common data ontology and formatting to interconnect disparate legacy systems that were never designed to communicate.
* **Digitized Workflows:** The "Maven Smart System" allows for a seamless transition from detection to action:
    * **Targeting:** Users can right-click a detection to move it immediately into a targeting workflow.
    * **COA Generation:** The system automatically suggests "Courses of Action," identifying the best assets to prosecute a specific target.
    * **Time Reduction:** This integration has reduced the "kill chain" process from several hours to a matter of minutes.

### Future Outlook and Deployment
* **Continuous Updates:** Unlike traditional military hardware, the system gets better daily because it is integrated with active customers who provide real-time feedback to developers.
* **End-User Mission:** The ultimate goal is to provide a "no fair fights" advantage for 18-to-20-year-old warfighters, ensuring they have the technological edge to win and return home safely.

> "The real advantage that we're going to have in the 21st century is decision advantage. How do you get better decisions faster than your adversary? That's what wins wars." — **Cameron Stanley**

> "We rejected the hypothesis that getting the AI in the hands of the warfighter was the right answer. What we really needed to do was take three steps back and say, the real issue isn't AI, the real issue is workflow." — **Cameron Stanley**

> "I live my life [for] no fair fights. If I can avoid it, let's not have fair fights. Our guys win and we come home." — **Cameron Stanley**

**TL;DR**
Congressman James Comer and industry leaders emphasized the urgent need to modernize defense procurement by shifting away from slow, bureaucratic legacy systems toward rapid, software-defined hardware and competitive innovation to counter the technological advancements of adversaries like China.

### The Volatile Global Security Environment
*   **Pacing Challenges:** The U.S. currently faces an "unprecedented era of volatility," characterized by the rapid rise of China as a pacing threat and heightening Russian aggression in Ukraine.
*   **Regional Instability:** Ongoing conflicts in Israel and the potential for a Taiwan conflict represent significant risks to global stability and the fragility of international supply chains.
*   **Adversarial Speed:** China is investing heavily in AI, hypersonics, space, and autonomous systems. Unlike the U.S., they are moving with "speed and purpose," unconstrained by the same levels of institutional bureaucracy.

### Modernizing Defense Procurement
*   **Criticism of Legacy Systems:** The traditional defense procurement process is criticized for being slow, bureaucratic, and dominated by a few large companies that have utilized the same stagnant methods for decades.
*   **The Innovation Mandate:** To maintain a competitive edge, the Department of Defense (DOD) must embrace risk-taking and prioritize speed over bureaucratic tradition.
*   **Shifting the Reward Structure:** Congressional leaders aim to reform the system to reward performance and results rather than institutional longevity or political connections.

### The "Software-Defined" Defense Model
*   **Software-Defined Hardware:** Industry leaders argue that integrating advanced software with hardware is the only viable path to achieving the "mass and speed" required for modern warfare.
*   **Proactive Problem Solving:** New defense-tech firms are encouraged to develop innovative solutions based on battlefield needs rather than waiting for specific government requirements.
*   **Modular and Scalable Systems:** The future of defense relies on building systems that are modular, scalable, and cost-effective—ideally at a fraction of the cost of traditional defense contracts.

### Congressional Oversight and Legislative Goals
*   **Transparency and Efficiency:** As Chairman of the Oversight Committee, Rep. Comer emphasized the goal of ensuring that billions in defense spending are used effectively to provide the best possible equipment for troops.
*   **Lowering Barriers to Entry:** There is a bipartisan commitment to breaking down the barriers that prevent innovative startups and small businesses from entering the defense market.
*   **Partnership for Leadership:** Forging strong partnerships across the aisle and with private industry is viewed as essential to maintaining U.S. technological and military leadership.
