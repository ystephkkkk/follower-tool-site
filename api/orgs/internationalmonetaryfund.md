## IMF Chief Says Wake Up, Next Global Shock is Coming

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts

- Speakers: Kristalina Georgieva (Managing Director, International Monetary Fund)

---

**TL;DR** 
IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva urges global leaders to prepare for a future of continuous, unpredictable macroeconomic shocks while proactively regulating artificial intelligence to prevent the same economic inequality and backlash caused by past waves of globalization.

---

### ## Navigating Global Shocks and Geopolitical Friction

* **Preparing for Continuous Crises:** Georgieva warns that the global economy is in a permanent state of high uncertainty. Leaders must build strong domestic economic foundations to withstand inevitable future shocks, rather than hoping for a return to stability.
* **The Russia Assessment Controversy:** In September 2024, the IMF faced severe backlash from European member nations (including Sweden, Denmark, and Norway) over a planned annual review of the Russian economy. 
  * **The Decision to Pivot:** Georgieva ultimately canceled the physical mission to Russia, citing active bombing hazards and Russia's reluctance to share critical import, export, and trade data, which would have compromised the objectivity of the assessment.
  * **Duty of Care to Ukraine:** Despite geopolitics, Georgieva emphasizes that the IMF secured near-unanimous consensus (excluding one country) to provide an economic support program for Ukraine, underscoring the Fund's duty to protect vulnerable member nations.
* **Globalization Backlash Lessons:** Georgieva acknowledges that global institutions, including the IMF, failed to anticipate the severe backlash against globalization. While the global economy grew as a whole, individual communities were hollowed out by job losses—a mistake she vows not to repeat with artificial intelligence.

### ## Managing AI Risks and Regulatory Alignment

* **Preventing AI-Driven Labor Displacement:** As AI advances rapidly, the IMF is analyzing its immediate impacts across three major dimensions:
  * Productivity gains
  * Labor market disruptions
  * Financial stability risks
* **Coexistence of Three Regulatory Models:** Currently, three distinct AI regulatory frameworks are emerging globally: the European model, the American model, and the Chinese model. 
  * **Global Harmonization:** Georgieva calls on the United Nations system to bring these three models together to ensure they can coexist and guide AI development responsibly.
* **Government Levers:** Georgieva rejects the idea that politicians are powerless against private tech giants. She asserts that governments possess direct regulatory authority over data governance and the distribution of AI-driven profits, and they must exercise this authority to protect their citizens.

### ## Redefining IMF Leadership and Organizational Culture

* **Consensus-Driven Diplomacy:** Managing the IMF’s 191 member countries requires providing completely objective, data-driven economic analysis. Georgieva stresses that credibility relies on treating large, wealthy nations and small, poor nations with equal seriousness.
* **The "Family Doctor" Approach:** The IMF acts as an economic physician, monitoring countries' vital signs and prescribing difficult, sometimes painful reforms. Georgieva notes that nations that followed these tough prescriptions—such as Portugal, Ireland, Greece, and Iceland during past financial crises—have emerged as the best-performing economies in Europe.
* **Fostering a Team Culture:** Georgieva has actively worked to break down the rigid, "small army" bureaucracy of the IMF.
  * **Inclusivity:** She dismantled strict hierarchical rules, such as conference room seating based on seniority, to give younger staff a voice.
  * **Gender Diversity:** Since starting her tenure, the representation of women in senior IMF leadership positions has risen from 25% to nearly 40%.

### ## Georgieva's Philosophy on Leadership and Accountability

* **Ultimate Accountability:** Georgieva defines a strong leader by the "buck stops with me" mentality. She believes true leaders make correct, long-term choices for their people rather than focusing on popularity or the next election cycle.
* **Leading by Example:** A core tenet of her leadership is never asking others to do what she is unwilling to do herself.
  * *Example:* Following the 2011 Fukushima disaster, Georgieva dispatched a European emergency response team to Japan. Because of the radiation danger, she immediately booked herself on the next flight to join them, demonstrating solidarity in the field.
* **Owning the Problem:** Reflecting on her career, Georgieva regrets spending too many early years being shy or deferring to "important people" in meetings. She advises leaders and professionals to aggressively own problems and speak up as soon as they identify a solution.

---

**TL;DR**
The global economy faces a "new normal" of persistent uncertainty driven by Middle East conflicts and supply disruptions, prompting the IMF to call for fiscal discipline and the adoption of the landmark "Daria Principles" to reform fund governance for the first time in 15 years.

### Geopolitical Risks and Energy Stability
* **The "Slow-Moving Vessel" Crisis:** Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned that supply disruptions in the Middle East are cumulative; every day tankers are delayed adds at least 40 days to delivery cycles, impacting not just oil and gas but also fertilizers and naphtha.
* **Escalation Scenarios:** While the IMF’s baseline scenario assumes a short-lived conflict with modest growth hits, prolonged instability risks a "more adverse scenario" characterized by lower growth and higher inflation.
* **Physical vs. Paper Markets:** Minister Aljadaan noted a "serious disconnect" between the paper market (speculative trading) and the physical market. 
    * De-escalation signals (like the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz) may cool paper prices, but physical stability requires insurance companies to resume coverage and tanker owners to commit to the region.
* **Infrastructure Recovery:** Impacted countries have varying recovery timelines; some have restored production quickly, while others face long-term damage to energy assets.

### Landmark IMF Governance Reforms
* **The Daria Principles:** Members unanimously adopted the first major quota and governance reforms in over 15 years.
* **Fair Representation:** The principles aim to increase transparency and fairness in quota reviews, ensuring regional balance at the Executive Board.
* **Leadership Selection:** The process for selecting the Managing Director will now officially uphold open, inclusive, merit-based, and transparent procedures.
* **Protecting Vulnerable Nations:** A core tenet of the reform is the protection of voting shares and quotas for low-income countries.

### Monetary and Fiscal Policy Mandates
* **Fiscal Prudence:** The IMF cautioned against "untargeted measures" such as broad-based tax cuts or export controls, which could prolong inflation. 
    * **Policy Advice:** Governments are urged to implement "targeted, temporary, and timely" measures to support the most vulnerable without undermining debt sustainability.
* **Central Bank Commitment:** Central banks remain "laser-focused" on price stability. 
    * **Balance of Risks:** Policymakers must carefully calibrate the timing of interest rate adjustments to avoid tightening too soon or waiting too long.
* **Structural Reforms:** Leaders emphasized that strong growth via productivity-improving reforms is the best "shock absorber" for a world prone to frequent disruptions.

### Financial Support and Global Outreach
* **Program Augmentation:** Approximately 12 countries have indicated a need for additional support. The IMF estimates potential program augmentations and new financing could range between **$20 billion and $50 billion**.
* **PRGT Funding:** Georgieva issued an urgent call to fully fund the Poverty Reduction and Growth Trust (PRGT) to support low-income and fragile states during this period of high interest rates and debt vulnerability.
* **Venezuela Re-engagement:** After a seven-year pause, a majority of the membership has voted to resume relations with Venezuela. The IMF will coordinate with the World Bank and the Inter-American Development Bank to help the country achieve macroeconomic stability.

---

**TL;DR**
The global economy is facing a significant asymmetric supply shock driven by the Middle East conflict, requiring policy makers to navigate high energy prices and infrastructure damage through calibrated monetary tightening and disciplined, targeted fiscal support.

### The Scale and Nature of the Supply Shock
The current economic environment is defined by a massive, global, and asymmetric supply shock that has disrupted energy and commodity markets.

*   **Energy Disruptions:** The world has seen a 13% cut in daily oil flow and a 20% reduction in LNG flow. 
    *   **Price Volatility:** Brent crude prices surged from $72 per barrel to a peak of $120 following the outbreak of hostilities.
    *   **Infrastructure Damage:** The Ras Laffan complex in Qatar, which produces 93% of the Gulf’s LNG, suffered a direct hit and may take three to five years to restore to full capacity.
*   **Trade and Shipping:** Ship passages through the Red Sea (Babel Mandep) remain at roughly 50% of 2023 levels, with no clear timeline for a return to normal traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.
*   **Humanitarian and Industrial Costs:** 
    *   **Food Insecurity:** An additional 45 million people are facing hunger due to transport restrictions, bringing the global total to over 360 million.
    *   **Industrial Dependencies:** Supply chain disruptions in sulfur, helium (for MRI and chip making), and neon are impacting global tech and medical sectors.

### Economic Transmission Channels and Growth Impacts
The IMF expects a downward revision to global growth projections due to "scarring effects" from infrastructure damage and losses in confidence.

*   **Inflation Expectations:** While near-term inflation forecasts for the US and Euro area have shifted upward, long-term expectations remain anchored, which is a critical "cushion" for the current shock.
*   **Financial Tightening:** Markets have moved from a highly supportive stance to an orderly tightening, characterized by widening emerging market bond spreads and US dollar appreciation.
*   **Growth Scenarios:** The IMF’s upcoming World Economic Outlook will present scenarios ranging from "swift normalization" to a "middle scenario" where high energy prices persist, leading to second-round effects.
*   **Asymmetric Impact:** 
    *   **Winners:** Oil and gas exporters who remain undisturbed.
    *   **Vulnerable:** Oil-importing nations with low sovereign credit ratings, specifically in Sub-Saharan Africa and small Pacific Island nations.

### Strategic Policy Recommendations
Georgieva issued a "word of caution" that demand adjustment is unavoidable and policy makers must ensure they "do not make matters worse."

*   **Rejection of Protectionism:** Countries are urged to reject "go-it-alone" actions such as export and price controls that further distort global markets.
*   **Monetary Policy:** Central banks should adopt a "wait and watch" approach but must be prepared to step in firmly with rate hikes if inflation expectations threaten to unanchor. 
*   **Fiscal Discipline:** 
    *   **Targeted Support:** Fiscal authorities should provide temporary assistance only to the most vulnerable, rather than broad-based tax cuts or energy subsidies.
    *   **Fiscal Space:** Public debt is significantly higher than 20 years ago. Interest payments are consuming a rising share of revenues across all income levels.
*   **Financial Regulation:** Supervisors must remain nimble to address risks from non-bank financial intermediaries and potential reversals in "tech optimism."

### Structural Transformation and AI
The IMF MD highlighted that the strength of economic fundamentals is a nation’s best defense against recurring shocks.

*   **AI Productivity Potential:** IMF analysis suggests AI could enhance productivity by 0.1% to 0.8%, potentially lifting global growth above pre-pandemic levels.
*   **Labor Market Displacement:** Despite the growth potential, AI is expected to affect 60% of jobs in advanced economies, creating a "tsunami" of labor market disruption that requires conscious steering.
*   **Economic Interdependence:** In a fragmented world with multiple "centers of gravity," countries must move from the weaponization of geography toward "mutually beneficial engagements" where value propositions are expanded for all partners.

### IMF Firepower and Support
The IMF is positioning itself as a "firefighter" for member nations facing balance of payment crises.

*   **Funding Demand:** Near-term demand for IMF support is expected to rise by $20 billion to $50 billion.
*   **Lending Capacity:** The Fund currently has 191 member countries and claims to be "well resourced" to meet the current shock.
*   **Interest Earnings:** Highlighting its financial model, Georgieva noted that the IMF paid the US $3.1 billion in interest over the last two years on the money the US provided for lending.

> "Policy makers can help to reduce this pain in multiple ways and certainly one way in which they can help is please don't make matters worse... Don't pour gasoline on the fire. You need this gasoline to drive your cars." — **Kristalina Georgieva**

> "The reality is we don't truly know what the future holds... What we do know is that growth will be slower even if the new peace is durable." — **Kristalina Georgieva**

> "We probably should create the club of optimists. We need to recognize that a changing world is also a world in which unthinkable opportunities are presenting themselves, not only challenges." — **Kristalina Georgieva**
