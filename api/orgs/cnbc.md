## Fed Governor Waller: Need to get a little more aggressive in our rate hikes to tame inflation

- Channel: CNBC Television
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SuzARBoNncQ

- Speakers: Christopher Waller (Governor, Federal Reserve); Steve Liesman (Reporter, CNBC)

---

**TL;DR:** Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller warned that inflation is broadening beyond one-off shocks and becoming entrenched near 3%, arguing the Fed must be more aggressive—potentially delivering a 50-basis-point rate hike in December—and use clearer forward guidance to break firms' pricing psychology.

### Inflation Outlook & Broadening Price Pressures
* **Persistent, Broadening Inflation:** Waller expressed strong concern that inflation has remained above the Fed's 2% target for over five years and is now spreading across the economy rather than staying confined to a few categories.
  * **Core Services:** Roughly 70% of core services items—representing about 50% to 55% of the overall CPI basket—are rising at rates above 3%, unaffected by tariffs or oil prices.
  * **Recent CPI Readings:** Monthly CPI inflation hit 0.3% for the third consecutive month, which annualizes to roughly 3.5% rather than moving closer to 2%.
* **Pricing Psychology & Calendar Effects:** Firms are increasingly passing through 3% price increases because consumers remain resilient, creating a risk that 3% becomes the accepted norm.
  * Waller noted that Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) seasonal adjustments have been unsatisfactory for several years, raising concern that inflation will not fall off at the start of the year as it historically has.
* **Supply Shocks:** While Waller defended initially looking through tariffs as a one-off shock (akin to a 1% sales tax), he noted that tariff pass-throughs have been drawn out amid uncertainty from a Supreme Court decision, and two separate oil shocks (last spring and this fall, including gas price spikes from the Iran war) have kept energy prices elevated.

### Rate Hike Strategy & Forward Guidance
* **Case for a 50-Basis-Point Hike:** Following his speech last week, Waller reiterated that the Fed needs to consider a 50-basis-point hike in December if it cannot credibly signal a follow-up hike in January.
  * **25 vs. 50 bps:** Doing 25 bps in December and 25 bps in January only achieves the same market pricing effect if the Fed explicitly signals the January move; without that signal, Waller favors going 50 bps in December to lock in the tightening.
* **Rate Path:** To get inflation under control, Waller stated the Fed must first take back the three "insurance cuts" made in 2025, and will likely need to go beyond three hikes because the summer 2025 policy rate was not bringing inflation down.
* **Communication & Forward Guidance:** Steve Liesman pressed Waller on whether the Fed is hobbling itself by moving away from forward guidance and shrinking the FOMC statement to around or under 130 words.
  * Waller emphasized that 30 years of central banking literature supports using communication as a policy tool—whether through the statement or the Chair's press conference—to get markets to help the Fed do its job.
  * **December SEP Limitation:** Unlike the September Summary of Economic Projections (SEP), which clearly signaled two or three hikes over the next few months, the December SEP will only show rate expectations for the end of 2027, offering no signal for January, March, or the first half of the year.

### AI Boom, Anthropic IPO & Financial Stability
* **No AI Bubble:** Ahead of Anthropic's IPO pricing and market debut—one of the largest IPOs in history—Liesman asked whether the AI build-out signals a market bubble.
  * Waller described himself as an "AI optimist," arguing AI is a fundamental, transformative technology rather than a "2000s-era sock puppet selling dog food."
  * He cited AI's ability to solve the Navier-Stokes fluid dynamics problem, noting that PhD mathematicians are facing existential questions about their roles.
* **Systemic Risk & Bank Exposure:** Addressing concerns from Friday's Financial Stability Report regarding circular financing and debt in the AI build-out, Waller said equity or debt losses by individual firms are normal market functioning.
  * However, he warned that the Fed is closely monitoring bank exposure to leveraged lending to ensure banks do not become overextended to AI firms and face solvency risks if some companies fail.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **December Rate Action:** The Fed should execute a 50-basis-point rate hike in December if it does not clearly signal to markets that it will follow a 25-basis-point December hike with another 25-basis-point hike in January — *Christopher Waller*.
* **Total Tightening Needed:** The Fed will have to reverse the three insurance cuts from 2025 and likely raise rates even further ("three hikes plus maybe some more") to bring inflation back down — *Christopher Waller*.
* **Anthropic IPO:** Anthropic will price its IPO today and begin trading tomorrow in one of the largest IPOs in history — *Steve Liesman*.

### Notable Quotes
> "Inflation's been above our target for over five years. So, if you really want to get it down, you've got to start taking more aggressive actions." — *Christopher Waller*

> "If you're not going to signal this very clearly to the markets you're going in January, my view is then you have to go 50 in December, to put it in place and get it done." — *Christopher Waller*

### AWS AI Growth, CapEx, and Demand Signals
* **Unprecedented AI Revenue Velocity:** In response to CNBC's Kate Rooney on Wall Street's reaction to Amazon's $220 billion CapEx plan, Jassy noted that AWS's generative AI business has reached over $25 billion in annual revenue within its first three years, compared to a $57 million annual revenue run rate in AWS's first three years (~440x faster).
* **Long-Term Capacity Reservations & Backlog:**
  * Big chunks of AWS capacity are already largely reserved for 2027, much of it for 2028, and a significant portion for 2029.
  * Amazon's backlog stands at ~$580 billion, with no single company representing even 25% of it.
* **Enterprise Adoption & Barbell Demand:**
  * Over 80% of the Fortune 100 are using Bedrock, Amazon's inference service.
  * Jassy described current demand as "barbelled": AI labs consuming massive compute (e.g., ChatGPT, coding) on one end, and enterprises using AI for cost avoidance and productivity (customer service, business process automation, fraud) on the other, with core enterprise production applications in the middle still ramping up.
* **Capital Cycle & Free Cash Flow:**
  * Data centers require capital outlays ~2 years ahead of monetization, whereas servers and networking gear (the largest capital expense) are purchased just a few months ahead, giving Amazon real visibility into demand before pulling the trigger.
  * While heavy CapEx creates short-term free cash flow headwinds, Jassy expects strong long-term return on invested capital.

### Data Center Buildout, Community Impact, and US-China Competition
* **Historic Infrastructure Scale:** Jassy compared the current AI infrastructure buildout to the 1950s U.S. highway and bridge construction, noting that the country and the world had underinvested in upgrading the power grid.
* **Geopolitical Imperative:** Addressing state-level moratoriums and public backlash, Jassy warned that stopping U.S. data center construction and allowing China to establish global AI norms would be a "historic mistake" that the U.S. would regret for a long time.
* **Community Partnerships & Tax Contributions:**
  * In St. Joseph County, Indiana, Amazon will contribute over $3 billion in taxes over the next few years, compared to ~$1 million from previous landowners.
  * Through its Build Together program, Amazon has contributed another $1 billion to local neighborhood priorities.
* **Hyperscaler Capacity:** Amazon added significant capacity in 2026 and has substantially larger capacity in progress for 2027 and 2028.

### Custom Silicon, Nvidia, and AI Model Partnerships
* **Custom Silicon Scale:** Amazon's chips business—comprising Trainium (AI accelerator) and Graviton (CPU)—has surpassed a $25 billion annual revenue run rate (as of Q2). The majority of Bedrock inference runs on Trainium chips.
* **Nvidia & "Coopetition":** Jassy downplayed any impact on Amazon from recent Nvidia news, calling Nvidia and Jensen Huang a long-term, important partner alongside Amazon's own custom silicon. On export controls, Jassy stated Amazon will abide by all U.S. laws.
* **Multi-Model Strategy (Anthropic & OpenAI):**
  * Anthropic, which is set to price and list on the Nasdaq, trains models and runs inference on Trainium. Jassy dismissed "circular financing" criticisms, noting that interconnected customer-vendor relationships (like Amazon using Adobe, which runs on AWS) are standard.
  * Driven by customer demand for choice, Amazon also partnered with OpenAI to offer its models on AWS.

### Agentic Shopping and Consumer/Enterprise Health
* **Third-Party Shopping Agents:** Jassy explained that Amazon has not permanently closed amazon.com to third-party agents (like Instinct or Dots), but requires a good customer experience and a fair exchange of value.
* **Alexa for Shopping:** Over 400 million people have used Alexa for shopping in the last 12 months, driving significant downstream revenue. Jassy believes most agentic shopping will happen natively within the retailer's own app, though Amazon is in talks with horizontal agent companies.
* **Resilient Consumer & Enterprise Spending:**
  * Consumer spending has been consistent over the last 18 months, with shoppers remaining price-conscious and deal-seeking ahead of events like Amazon's "Turkey 12."
  * On the enterprise side, Jassy has seen "no attenuation" in demand, with companies resuming cloud migrations over the past 18 months.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AWS $1 Trillion Run Rate:** AWS can become an over $1 trillion annual revenue run rate business within the next 5 to 10 years (Andy Jassy).
* **Frontier Model Landscape:** In 3 to 5 years, there will be 6 to 8 frontier models that are roughly comparable in quality—each excelling at different tasks like coding, math, reasoning, visual arts, or lower cost/latency—and one of them will be Amazon's own model (Andy Jassy).
* **Cloud Migration Flip:** Today, 80% to 85% of global IT spend is still on-premises; that equation will flip to majority cloud over the next 10 to 20 years (Andy Jassy).

## Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin on the Fed's next move — 11/05/2026

---

### TL;DR
Economic demand remains solid despite hiring caution and technological uncertainty, while high and sticky inflation (headline 3.6%, core 3.1%) keeps the Federal Reserve's policy path focused on a gradual pace. Richmond Fed President Tom Barkin and CNBC's Steve Liesman discussed pricing power, the economic impact of AI buildouts, productivity trends, and evolving Fed communications.

### Business Conditions, Demand, and Pricing Power
* **Solid Economic Demand:** **Tom Barkin** noted that overall demand remains healthy, driven heavily by strong corporate earnings where Q2 earnings averaged over 30% growth (median 15%) and Q3 is tracking similarly.
    * *Sectors lagging:* Residential real estate, nonprofits, and non-defense government contractors feel weak.
    * *Behavioral impact:* Strong earnings create a virtuous circle where companies invest, avoid layoffs, and keep demand surprising to the upside.
* **Hiring Caution & Tech Uncertainty:** Businesses remain hesitant to expand their workforce due to external uncertainties, notably artificial intelligence evolution, but they are not tipping over into widespread layoffs.
* **Broad-Based Cost Pressures:** **Tom Barkin** highlighted rising costs in gas, diesel, healthcare, health insurance, and aluminum, alongside tariffs that companies are increasingly forced to pass on.
    * *B2B vs. B2C:* B2B companies and businesses selling to wealthy consumers successfully pass costs along, while moderate-income consumers are "tapped out."
    * *Example:* Whirlpool announced 7% price increases for Q4 despite selling into a sluggish home sales segment.
* **The "Culture of Pricing":** **Tom Barkin** explained that post-COVID shortages reset baseline business expectations, making procurement professionals and corporate leaders much more comfortable pushing through price increases compared to the pre-COVID era.

### Federal Reserve Policy, Neutral Rates, and Meeting Cadence
* **Monetary Policy Stance:** **Tom Barkin** described current policy as roughly neutral—neither highly restrictive nor highly accommodative—citing elevated nominal consumption as evidence of ongoing economic accommodation.
* **Inflation as Primary Risk:** With headline inflation at 3.6% and core at 3.1% (above the 2% target), **Tom Barkin** views inflation as the primary risk, supporting a "gradual pace" of rate adjustments established in the September SEP.
* **FOMC Meeting Frequency Changes:** **Tom Barkin** supports moving from eight annual meetings to fewer meetings (moving toward seven in 2027 and six or seven in 2028), noting it helps separate rate decisions from immediate data releases like monthly jobs reports.
* **Communications Philosophy:** **Tom Barkin** weighed two economic views: that clear communication allows markets to help execute policy, versus the risk of forward guidance binding the Fed to suboptimal policies (citing 2021 as a period where the Fed felt overly constrained).

### AI, Productivity, and the Labor Market
* **Productivity Drivers:** **Tom Barkin** attributed recent strong productivity numbers (such as Q3 productivity coming in at 3.6%) primarily to past investments in automation, lean operating processes, and staffing models implemented 2-4 years ago following labor shortages.
* **AI's Near-Term Impact:** **Tom Barkin** noted AI is driving immediate inflationary pressures through data center construction (electrificians, materials, memory chips), but primarily boosts corporate profits before competition eventually meters prices down.
* **Data Center Local Dynamics:** **Tom Barkin** discussed the friction of data centers in local communities—noting high construction activity and tax bases but low permanent employment, leading to local opposition (such as a ban in Salisbury, North Carolina).
