### TL;DR
Terry Duffy reflects on his 46-year career at the CME Group, warning against the degradation of financial regulation, the risks of unregulated perpetual futures and prediction markets, and the inevitability of 24/7 trading pending banking infrastructure updates.

### Regulatory Evolution and the State of Oversight
* **Historical Context:** Terry Duffy began his career when the CFTC was a brand-new, principles-based agricultural oversight agency; over five decades, it expanded to cover financial futures, equity indexes, and crypto.
* **Current Regulatory Concerns:** Duffy characterizes the current regulatory environment as a "ready, fire-aim strategy," noting that under the second Trump administration, 2,500 self-certifications have been submitted to the CFTC without a single denial.
* **Vulnerability of Agency Rules:** Duffy warns that rules and regulations enacted by agencies like the CFTC and SEC are not sustainable because they can be easily wiped away by the stroke of a pen when a new administration takes office.

### Prediction Markets and Market Credibility
* **Criticism of Trivial Contracts:** Duffy criticizes prediction markets that violate core principle three of the Commodity Exchange Act (susceptibility to manipulation), citing absurd approved contracts such as Nathan's hot dog eating contests, George Santos's attendance at the State of the Union, and Donald Trump's teleprompter.
* **Opposition to Insider Information:** Arguing against the idea that insider information on prediction platforms has informational value, Duffy states that level-playing-field information is fundamental to fair markets, comparing privileged prediction feeds to paid access on Truth Social.
* **Legitimate Alternatives:** Duffy contrasts unregulated prediction bets with FutureSports—an index allowing teams to monetize brand value (such as a sponsorship deal with the Chicago White Sox and recent deals with the NBA and MLB)—arguing this provides an economic ecosystem rather than mere consumer gambling.

### Perpetual Futures, Leverage, and Offshore Platforms
* **Legal and Tax Status of Perps:** Duffy explains why CME litigation targets perpetual futures: perps function as swaps (two parties paying each other via a funding rate) rather than futures, yet attempt to claim favorable 1256 tax treatment (60% long-term, 40% short-term) without complying with swap dealer and clearing regulations.
* **Hyperliquid and Offshore Risks:** Discussing offshore blockchain-based perps platforms like Hyperliquid, Duffy highlights systemic risks including zero KYC ("know your customer"), auto-liquidation, and extreme leverage reaching up to 250%, which can trigger cascading market-moving events.
* **Real Motive for Perps:** Duffy argues that perpetuals are being pushed into the U.S. strictly for leverage and retail speculation rather than concerns over contract roll dates.

### Asset Classes and 24/7 Market Inevitability
* **GPU Futures Potential:** Duffy defends GPU unit futures (developed with partner Don Wilson), noting that while skeptics question the asset class, the GPU market will be multiples the size of crypto by 2030, exceeding a projected trillion-dollar valuation.
* **Banking Infrastructure Bottleneck:** Duffy asserts that 24/7 trading is ultimately inevitable, but warns that the broader $76 trillion U.S. equity market cannot trade continuously until the underlying banking system supports 24/7 pre-funding and real-time payments.

### Career Reflections and Crisis Moments
* **Discipline and Exit Strategies:** Reflecting on going broke with a $150,000 borrowed loss in 1981, Duffy advises young traders to maintain a moral compass, accept when they are wrong, and always establish a clear exit strategy before entering a trade.
* **Scariest Career Moment:** Duffy identifies the April 20, 2020 pandemic crash—where oil prices plunged to negative $37.50—as the scariest moment of his career due to the sudden evaporation of demand and the feeling of someone yelling "fire" in a crowded theater.
