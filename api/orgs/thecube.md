## Tony Kim, BlackRock | theCUBE + NYSE Wired: AI Factories - Data Centers of the Future

- Speakers: Tony Kim (Lead Portfolio Manager, BlackRock); Dave Vellante (Host, theCUBE)

#### TL;DR
The AI buildout remains in its early stages as model capabilities scale faster than capital expenditure, upending traditional technology assumptions by making semiconductors the world's most profitable sector while collapsing the traditional software stack.

### Market Landscape & The AI Buildout Timeline
- **Early Stage Dynamics:** We are in year four since ChatGPT and two years into heavy capital spending, with infrastructure buildout expected to continue through the end of the decade.
- **Exponential Scaling:** Model capabilities are currently scaling faster than capital expenditure (CapEx), signaling that the buildout remains in its early phases despite a decade-long gestation of the transformer/GPT cycle.
- **Compute and Infrastructure Focus:** The majority of tech market capitalization—estimated at 70% to 80% of the $60 trillion global technology market cap (including the Magnificent 7 infrastructure companies)—has concentrated in the compute and infrastructure layer.

### Inversion of Technology Assumptions & "Token Flow"
- **Margin and Value Inversion:** Decades-old assumptions regarding margins, market capitalization, durability, and moats have inverted; CapEx-heavy investments are now in favor, while marginal economics at volume and Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR) are out of favor.
- **Semiconductor Profitability:** Semiconductors—once dismissed as a cyclical, low-margin commodity—have flipped to become the most profitable sector in the world.
- **The Physics of AI and Token Flow:** 
  - **Token Generation Cogs:** Software can no longer be viewed purely as asset-light and super high-margin because generating tokens has real Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
  - **Follow the Tokens:** Value follows "token flow"—creating tokens, serving tokens, and wrapping applications around them—with margin battles occurring at every layer of the stack.

### Macroeconomic Shift: IT Spending and Labor
- **IT Spending Expansion:** Global IT spending currently accounts for roughly 4% of global company revenue (within a $120 trillion global GDP split roughly 60/40 between labor and capital).
- **Capital vs. Labor Flip:** IT spending is forecasted to double or triple over the next decade, shifting investment away from labor toward CapEx on tokens and token generation, potentially flipping the labor-to-capital GDP ratio.

### The "SaaS-pocalypse" and Stack Collapse
- **Frontier Labs as Apex Predators:** Frontier labs act like an apex predator in a trophic cascade, setting the stage and driving spending down through hyperscalers, neoclouds, inference clouds, and compute layers.
- **Collapsing Application Layers:** 
  - Historically distinct layers—data/context, applications, and services—are colliding as deterministic and stochastic worlds merge.
  - Frontier and open-source models threaten to absorb software functionality by building their own context, consuming data, writing code, building harnesses, and deploying agents to take on work.
- **The Role of Open Source:** Proponents champion open weight models so enterprises can avoid being beholden to proprietary systems and control their own destiny amid software stack disruption.
- **The Productization Challenge:** Frontier labs are transitioning from pure research engineering to product development and commercial execution, driving a necessity for partnerships, inventiveness, and M&A to bridge the gap with enterprise customers.

## Sarbjeet Johal,  Stackpane | MWC26 Barcelona

- Channel: SiliconANGLE theCUBE
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JwHm3Zhwo78
- Published: 2026-03-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260304.html#video-JwHm3Zhwo78
- Speakers: Sarbjeet Johal (Founder & CEO, Stackpane); John Furrier (Host, theCUBE)

---

**TL;DR**
Artificial Intelligence has shifted the telecommunications industry from a focus on simple connectivity to a high-stakes "compute and data factory" model, forcing traditionally slow-moving telcos to adopt Silicon Valley-style innovation to monetize upcoming 6G and edge-computing architectures.

### The Convergence of AI, 6G, and Quantum
*   **The "Trifecta" of Future Tech:** The industry is moving toward a transformative intersection of AI, 6G, and Quantum computing, expected to fully land in approximately three years.
*   **Skyrocketing Compute Demand:** AI is described as a "data factory" that generates massive data volumes, requiring a fundamental shift in how telcos architect their networks to handle increased processing needs.
*   **AI RAN (Radio Access Network):** AI is now pervasive across the infrastructure, moving from static rules to intelligent orchestration.
    *   Emerging models allow radios to "talk" to each other to manage traffic, routing, and Quality of Service (QoS) dynamically rather than through legacy static configurations.

### Infrastructure and the "Edge AI Factory"
*   **Shift to Specialized Hardware:** The success of players like Nvidia is increasingly tied to networking (InfiniBand) and interconnects rather than just GPUs. 
    *   The market is moving toward "specialty compute" that feeds inference engines and quasi-training at the far edge.
*   **Distributed AI Factories:** The future network architecture consists of mini AI factories at the edge connected to centralized "mega-factories."
*   **The O-RAN Catalyst:** AT&T’s $14 billion commitment to Ericsson for Open RAN (O-RAN) signifies a massive investment in retrofitting legacy infrastructure to become software-defined.
    *   Sarbjeet Johal notes that the "RIC" (RAN Intelligence Controller) remains too proprietary; for true innovation, it must be opened to the broader VC and startup community.

### Telco Business Models and Monetization
*   **Silicon Valley Velocity vs. Telco "Glaciers":** Historically, telcos have moved slowly due to heavy regulation and risk-management cultures. To survive the AI era, they must adopt the "break the rules" mindset of the tech sector.
*   **Identity-Based Commerce:** Telcos are exploring ways to monetize through APIs that map a user’s SIM identity to their commerce identity, enabling "one-button" purchases without credit card entries.
*   **Fiber-First Strategy:** Referencing AT&T’s approach, Johal highlights that fiber is the foundational strength from which all other services—edge computing, commerce, and AI—must hang.

### Geopolitics and Sovereign AI
*   **Sovereignty as a Value Proposition:** Beyond simple data residency, "Sovereign AI" allows nations to tailor AI services to specific cultural and economic needs (e.g., India’s rural population vs. China’s urbanized economy).
*   **The Proximity Factor:** High-level AI innovation remains concentrated in Silicon Valley due to the density of know-how. Johal argues that global telcos must establish a significant physical presence in these tech hubs to remain competitive.
*   **The Scale Problem:** Because most telcos operate locally or regionally, they often lack the scale required to compete with global hyperscalers. Innovation thrives on scale, which allows for the division of labor and massive R&D.
