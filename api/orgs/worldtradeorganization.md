## Trade Wars, Fighting Corruption and Rethinking Globalization | Leaders with Francine Lacqua

- Channel: Bloomberg Originals

- Speakers: Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Director-General, World Trade Organization); Francine Lacqua (Anchor, Bloomberg)

---

### TL;DR
Global trade is facing unprecedented disruption and fragmentation reminiscent of the 1930s, driven by geopolitical tensions, shifting subsidies, and protectionism. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, Director-General of the World Trade Organization, advocates for "re-globalization" to decentralize supply chains and reform multilateral rules while maintaining resilience.

### The State of Global Trade and WTO Pressures
- **Unprecedented Stress:** Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala notes that global trade is experiencing fragmentation and stress unseen in 80 years, with dissatisfaction leading some members toward unilateral and bilateral arrangements.
- **The 1930s Parallel:** Turning inward mirrors the protectionist era of a century ago; however, modern supply chains, financial linkages, and digital trade make any collapse far more damaging today.
- **Resilience of the Core:** Despite strains, about **75%** of world trade continues to function under WTO rules, demonstrating a solid core.
- **The 25% Friction Points:** Contentious areas include outdated 1990s subsidy rules, state-directed economic models, and disputes over developing country status (such as China's ongoing classification alongside positions held by India and Brazil).

### Subsidies, Industrial Policy, and China
- **Rising Subsidies:** Government support via tax breaks, grants, and cheap loans distorts trade by lowering company costs artificially rather than relying strictly on efficiency.
- **Semiconductor Investments:** Citing estimates from the Semiconductor Industry Association, China is on track to invest **$142 billion** into chip making.
- **Evolution of Rules:** Okonjo-Iweala emphasizes the need to update subsidy and trade frameworks established before major developing economies like China joined the WTO.

### Personal Cost and the Fight Against Corruption
- **Nigeria Finance Minister Tenure:** Okonjo-Iweala served two non-consecutive terms as Nigeria's finance minister, waging a central life's work against corruption that diverted public funds from education, health, and infrastructure.
- **Gas Subsidy Fraud:** An audit initiated by Okonjo-Iweala uncovered **$2.4 billion** worth of fraud in fuel import subsidy claims, leading to her refusal to pay fraudulent marketers.
- **Abduction Ordeal:** The crackdown triggered death threats and the harrowing 5-day kidnapping of her 96-year-old mother (then younger), during which kidnappers demanded Okonjo-Iweala's resignation and departure from the country.
- **Resilience Under Fire:** Despite immense fear and guilt, she continued her governmental duties following her father's firm insistence that the family would not submit to blackmail.

### African Trade and Structural Barriers
- **Low Intra-Continental Trade:** Africa accounts for only **3%** of global exports, with roughly **15%** of the continent's formal trade occurring between African nations.
- **Structural Hurdles:** High trade barriers, shipments of raw materials abroad followed by reimportation of finished goods, dysfunctional transport, numerous currencies, and red tape hinder economic growth.
- **Mobility Obstacles:** Okonjo-Iweala highlighted needing **35 different visas** on her passport as an investor and noted personal incidents where the African Union's experimental African passport was initially rejected at borders.
- **AfCFTA Initiative:** Launched in **2021**, the African Continental Free Trade Area groups **54 countries** into a single market to stimulate manufacturing and intra-African commerce.

### Re-Imagining Globalization and Digital Trade
- **Re-Globalization Concept:** Okonjo-Iweala champions decentralizing and de-concentrating production away from single geographies to multiple locations with comparative advantages to build global resilience.
- **Digital Trade Growth:** Total world trade stands at **$33 trillion**, with digitally delivered services (movies, music, education, health) growing the fastest at about **6% per year**, reaching roughly **$4 trillion**.
- **Digital Moratorium Stalemate:** A longstanding moratorium on electronic transmissions faced resistance at the latest WTO ministerial conference—pitting the US and EU against Brazil, India, and developing nations—raising fears of national rules and internet taxation.

> *"We have come up with a concept at the WTO that we call re-globalization. We believe that we need to reimagine globalization and reimagine value and supply chains in a way that builds greater resilience for the world."* — **Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**


## 2026 Annual Wenger Lecture with Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala

- Channel: AUWCL International and Comparative Legal Studies
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSsbctbsYLM
- Published: 2026-04-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260422.html#video-iSsbctbsYLM
- Speakers: Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Director General, World Trade Organization)

---

**TL;DR**
The global trading system is at a critical "turning point" where the World Trade Organization (WTO) must transition from a state of over-dependence and unilateralism toward "reglobalization"—a process of diversifying supply chains to include marginalized regions while reforming foundational multilateral rules to ensure long-term peace and stability.

### Current Disruptions and "Over-Dependencies"
*   **Systemic Stress:** The global trading system is experiencing its most significant disruptions in 80 years, fueled by geopolitical tensions, ongoing wars, and a rise in unilateral actions that bypass collective rules.
*   **The Risk of Over-Dependence:** Dr. Okonjo-Iweala emphasized that the system was designed for *interdependence*, but has devolved into risky *over-dependencies* in several sectors:
    *   **Market Demand:** Excessive reliance on the United States.
    *   **Critical Inputs:** Over-dependence on China for rare earths and Taiwan for cutting-edge semiconductors.
    *   **Health Security:** During the pandemic, 80% of vaccine exports were concentrated in just 10 countries.
*   **Physical Choke Points:** Recent crises have highlighted the fragility of geographical trade routes.
    *   **The Red Sea & Hormuz:** Conflicts and militia activity have upended energy, fertilizer, and chemical shipments, leading to global price spikes.
    *   **Climate & Accident Risks:** Drought in the Panama Canal and the 2021 Suez Canal blockage demonstrate that physical vulnerabilities can cost the global economy nearly $10 billion per day.

### The Strategy of "Reglobalization"
*   **Defining the Concept:** Coined by economist Bob Koopman, "reglobalization" refers to the restructuring of trade to be more inclusive and diverse rather than retreating into protectionism.
*   **Diversification as Robustness:** Moving beyond simple "resilience" to "robustness" requires drawing in regions currently on the margins of trade, such as the African continent, which has accounted for less than 3% of global trade for decades.
*   **Supply Chain Adaptability:** Despite recent shocks, global value chains have shown surprising flexibility.
    *   **Creative Logistics:** Shipping companies are rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope.
    *   **Market Depth:** When the war in Ukraine disrupted Egypt’s grain supply, the multilateral system allowed for a rapid pivot to sourcing wheat from the U.S. and EU.

### Reforming the Multilateral System (MC14)
*   **A "Member-Driven" Challenge:** In the WTO, the Secretariat does not make decisions; the members do. Dr. Okonjo-Iweala noted that when members criticize the WTO, they are effectively criticizing their own collective inability to update the rulebook.
*   **The Cameroon Ministerial (MC14):** Described as a "turning point" ministerial, the conference focused on foundational reforms.
    *   **Core Principles:** Discussions addressed the Most Favored Nation (MFN) principle, non-discrimination, and the balance of rights and obligations.
    *   **Plurilateral Agreements:** There is a growing movement toward "plurilaterals" (agreements between subsets of members) for issues like e-commerce and investment facilitation to bypass the paralysis of full consensus.
*   **Ongoing Negotiating Goals:** Immediate priorities include a permanent solution for the moratorium on e-commerce customs duties, new disciplines for fisheries subsidies, and agricultural reform.

### The Intersection of Trade, Law, and Peace
*   **Trade as Human Instinct:** Professor Patty Day characterized trade as a "fundamental human instinct" (Eat, sleep, breathe, trade) and argued that human history is essentially the history of trade.
*   **The Rule of Law:** President Jonathan Alger and Dean Heather Hughes highlighted that international stability depends on the rule of law. They noted that American University’s international law program (ranked #4 in the U.S.) is dedicated to fostering the democracy and stability that trade requires.
*   **Trade for Peace:** The WTO maintains a "Trade for Peace" program, operating on the belief that a rule-based multilateral system is the only sustainable way to prevent global conflict.

> "The global trading system was designed for interdependence, not over-dependence. And these over-dependencies have fueled resentment, anxiety, and trade and political tension." — **Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**

> "Trade may not directly lead to peace at all times, but there is an indisputable connection between the two... the rule-based system is the only way to maintain peace in the world." — **Professor Patty Day**

> "In a member-driven organization, when members level criticisms, they are in effect criticizing themselves. Because there's no WTO out there to hit at other than members." — **Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**

> "Studying the history of trade is studying human history... it's a requirement of a well-rounded education." — **Professor Patty Day**


## Can the WTO Survive a New World Order?–Conversation w/ Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala | The Futures Summit

- Channel: Center for Strategic & International Studies
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RXr8G-TCs0s
- Published: 2026-04-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260416.html#video-RXr8G-TCs0s
- Speakers: Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Director-General, World Trade Organization)

---

**TL;DR**: While the global trading system faces significant geopolitical disruptions and supply chain "overdependencies," it remains remarkably resilient; however, the WTO must reform its consensus-based decision-making to better integrate digital trade, green initiatives, and South-South cooperation to move from mere adaptability to long-term robustness.

### Navigating Global Disruption and Overdependence
* **Current Economic Outlook:** Despite global instability, trade has shown surprising resilience.
    * **Growth Statistics:** Global goods trade grew by 4.6% last year (surpassing the projected 2.4%), driven largely by AI investment and frontloading ahead of tariffs.
    * **2024 Projections:** Growth is projected at 1.9%, though ongoing conflicts (such as in the Middle East) could cause a 0.5% drop to 1.4%.
* **The "Choke Point" Problem:** Dr. Okonjo-Iweala highlighted dangerous overdependencies on specific markets and geographic transit points.
    * **Market Concentrations:** Over-reliance on the U.S. for markets, China for critical supplies, and Taiwan for semiconductors.
    * **Geographic Fragility:** Vital transit routes including the Malacca Strait, Panama Canal, and the Suez Canal are increasingly vulnerable.
* **Resilience vs. Robustness:** While global trade has adapted to shocks (e.g., finding new wheat sources in Latin America when Black Sea routes were blocked), Dr. Okonjo-Iweala argues that "resilience is not robustness." The system needs proactive de-concentration to ensure stability.

### WTO Reform and the "Consensus" Dilemma
* **The Turning Point Ministerial (MC14):** The recent ministerial in Yaoundé was intended as a pivot toward institutional modernization. 
    * **Reform Pathway:** Ministers successfully agreed on a program of reforms with specific modalities, timelines, and six-monthly progress reports.
    * **The E-Commerce Moratorium:** A critical 28-year-old agreement to not charge customs duties on digital transmissions reached a stalemate. While 164 of 166 members reached a "landing zone" for agreement, the WTO's requirement for unanimity blocked the final gavel.
* **Redefining Consensus:** The Director General emphasized that "consensus" at the WTO is currently practiced as "unanimity," which allows a single country to block the majority.
    * **Proposed Shift:** Reform efforts are looking at how to maintain consensus as a core value while practicing it in a way that allows the organization to remain agile and responsive to a fast-changing world.

### Trade as the Primary Engine of Development
* **Integration vs. Separation:** Dr. Okonjo-Iweala strongly disagreed with the premise that trade and development are separate fields. 
    * **Means to an End:** Trade is an essential instrument for economic growth, which is the necessary precursor to development.
    * **Beyond Technical Assistance:** Developing nations should focus less on "Special and Differential Treatment" (SDT) and more on how specific trade agreements can fundamentally drive their internal growth.
* **The Rise of South-South Trade:** Southern countries are increasingly trading with one another, rising from 10% of global trade in 1995 to 25% today.
    * **CELAC-Africa Cooperation:** A landmark meeting between Latin American/Caribbean (CELAC) and African trade ministers occurred at MC14 to diversify trade and reduce dependence on traditional hubs.
    * **AfCFTA:** The African Continental Free Trade Area is viewed as a vital instrument, though Dr. Okonjo-Iweala urged for faster implementation.

### The Future: Digital, Green, and AI-Driven Trade
* **The Digital Economy:** Digitally delivered services are the fastest-growing segment of global trade, increasing at 6% annually and currently valued at $5.3 trillion.
    * **Plurilateral Agreements:** 66 members recently launched an interim agreement on basic rules for e-commerce to provide business certainty.
* **Climate and Carbon Pricing:** There is a critical need for a global "methodological approach" to carbon pricing to prevent trade fragmentation.
    * **CBAM Concerns:** Many members view Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanisms as restrictive trade measures.
    * **Institutional Silos:** Dr. Okonjo-Iweala noted a disconnect between environment ministers and trade ministers, urging for better communication to ensure climate goals don't accidentally stifle trade.
* **AI’s Impact:** Investment in AI goods was a massive driver of trade performance last year, accounting for 42% of the total growth in goods trade.

> "The purpose of the WTO written into its founding document is to enhance living standards, to help create jobs, and to support sustainable development. Can you believe that? So it’s about people. It’s not just about globalization." — **Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**

> "At the WTO, consensus is practiced as unanimity. And that’s one of the things we are looking at in the reform. It’s great—the smallest country has the same power as the largest. The only place where you have that. But it also holds you back in your ability to make decisions." — **Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**

> "I’ve been trying to persuade the developing countries at the WTO that development... is about asking the deep and essential questions: 'This agreement that we are entering into, how will it drive economic growth in my country?'" — **Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala**


## Transparency, Trade and Fighting Corruption Across the Globe - Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala

- Channel: Association of Certified Fraud Examiners (ACFE)
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BltAK3nQNg0
- Published: 2026-03-06
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260306.html#video-BltAK3nQNg0
- Speakers: Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala (Director-General, World Trade Organization)

---

**TL;DR**
Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala, Director-General of the WTO, asserts that institutional transparency and the strategic use of technology are the most effective deterrents to global corruption, emphasizing that the fight against fraud requires both strong systemic frameworks and unwavering personal integrity.

### The Intersection of Global Trade and Anti-Corruption
* **Transparency as a Deterrent:** Dr. Okonjo-Iweala highlights that the World Trade Organization’s mission of lifting living standards relies on a body of rules that mandate transparency, stability, and predictability.
    * **The "Daylight" Effect:** Because fraud and corruption thrive in secrecy, the WTO’s requirement for members to report trade policies acts as a structural barrier to illicit activities.
    * **MFN Terms:** The "most favored nation" arrangements ensure similar trading terms for all members, reducing the discretionary "backroom deals" that foster corruption.
* **Institutionalizing Openness:** While current systems are not perfect, ongoing WTO reforms aim to strengthen notification rules to ensure trade policies remain visible to all stakeholders.

### Technology and the Human Element in Fraud Prevention
* **Digitizing High-Risk Processes:** Implementing technology in sectors prone to corruption, such as ports and customs, removes the human interaction that often leads to bribery and fraud.
    * **Trade Facilitation Agreement:** Global implementation of this agreement has lowered fraud by automating inspections and documentation.
* **Balancing Automation and Judgment:** While technology should be used to streamline processes that don't require human intervention, Dr. Okonjo-Iweala maintains that human judgment remains indispensable for complex oversight.
* **Reducing Discretionary Power:** By moving decision-making out of the hands of individuals and into digitized systems, organizations can significantly reduce the "demand" side of corrupt money.

### Strengthening Global and Regional Governance
* **Institutional Weakness vs. Implementation:** Success in fighting corruption requires both the existence of strong institutions (judiciaries, anti-corruption agencies) and the political will to implement their mandates.
    * **The Need for Consequences:** Without visible penalties for wrongdoing, the incentive to engage in fraud remains high.
* **Universal Vigilance:** Developed nations must not become complacent. Dr. Okonjo-Iweala warns that even "rich" nations must remain vigilant, as their institutions can be undermined if not constantly protected.
* **Africa’s Rising Role:** With Africa projected to have the world’s largest reservoir of young workers by 2050, the continent’s future depends on strengthening internal governance and leveraging the talent of youth in AI and Fintech to bypass legacy corruption issues.

### Negotiation Strategies and Leadership Lessons
* **Fact-Based Negotiation:** Effective consensus-building requires a foundation of irrefutable facts, though leaders must be prepared for political actors who may ignore data or attack the messenger.
    * **Identifying Incentives:** Successful negotiators must identify what the opposing party stands to gain or lose to bring them to the table.
* **Lessons for Women in Leadership:** Based on her research and career, Dr. Okonjo-Iweala identifies key traits for success:
    * **Subject Mastery:** Women are often held to different expectations; mastering the subject matter is the best way to command a room.
    * **The 70% Rule:** Personal commitment is essential for high-stress roles. If a leader does not feel motivated to go to work 70% of the time, the role is likely a poor fit.
    * **Moral Compass:** Integrity is not just a government requirement but an individual responsibility that must be modeled from the top down.

> "Fraud and corruption does not like transparency and openness... When you're transparent and you're open, then it's much more difficult for fraud to take place." — Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala

> "If there are no consequences, then the incentive to continue to do the wrong thing abounds. So that's what I think in a lot of our developing countries we need... we need consequences when things happen." — Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala

> "Avoiding fraud and corruption also starts from the top. If from the top the example is not there and citizens see that leaders can get away with doing the wrong thing, then why wouldn't they do the wrong thing themselves?" — Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala

> "If I don't get out of bed 70% of the time feeling like I want to go to work that day, then I know that job isn't working for me." — Dr. Ngozi Okonjo-Iweala
