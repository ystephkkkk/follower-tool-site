## Bessent says Trump administration has "been in touch" with Nvidia leadership but still needs answers

- Channel: Face the Nation
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xAc7zaM6lZg
- Published: 2026-11-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261122.html#video-xAc7zaM6lZg
- Speakers: Scott Bessent (Treasury Secretary); Margaret Brennan (Moderator, Face the Nation); Jensen Huang (Subject of scandal)

---

**TL;DR:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told *Face the Nation* moderator Margaret Brennan that the Trump administration has been in touch with Nvidia's temporary leadership and legal team but is still awaiting answers on potential illicit chip sales to China, following a 10-day deadline issued a week prior. Bessent also defended the resilience of U.S. banks amid recent tech outages and outlined the administration's push to onshore semiconductor manufacturing and tighten AI chip export controls.

### Banking Resilience & Market Volatility
* **Bank Tech Outages:** Brennan noted that recent tech outages at major banks caused thousands of customers to panic and withdraw cash. Bessent reassured Americans that banks are the "most resilient companies in the U.S. economy" and have been at the forefront of cybersecurity for decades.
  * He compared recent glitches—such as the one at JP Morgan—to a vaccine, explaining that security patches may cause temporary disruptions ("your arm may hurt") while increasing long-term immunity.
* **Drivers of the Market Sell-Off:** Addressing recent stock market anxiety and AI-related sell-offs, Bessent attributed the downturn to a combination of factors:
  * **Political Shifts:** He stated the sell-off began as Democrats moved ahead in the polls and accelerated after election day, arguing that the Democratic position on slowing down the AI build-out spooked investors.
  * **Monetary Policy:** He noted that "very hawkish remarks from the Federal Reserve" and a substantial tightening in financial conditions over the past few months have led many to worry the Fed is making a policy mistake.

### U.S. AI Policy, Data Centers & On-Shoring
* **Offshore Data Center Ban:** In a clip from a 90-minute Friday press conference, President Donald Trump stated: "We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries cuz China will take all of them... And we're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it."
  * Bessent said he had not yet spoken directly with Trump about the policy, but explained the president wants to prevent offshore technology transfers.
  * Clarifying the scope, Bessent confirmed the policy targets American companies rather than reversing U.S. encouragement of data centers in Gulf allies like the United Arab Emirates.
* **Semiconductor On-Shoring:** Bessent highlighted a strategic vulnerability in the U.S. chip industry, noting that **97% of high-end chips** are currently made in Taiwan.
  * He pointed to large fabs being built by **TSMC in Arizona** as part of the administration's effort to bring manufacturing back to the U.S.
* **Rebuilding Public Trust:** Responding to a *Wall Street Journal* editorial board argument that restricting offshore data centers could give Chinese competitors an edge, Bessent argued the AI industry has done a "terrible job of explaining themselves to the American people" and must rebuild trust with local communities to enable a larger domestic build-out.

### Nvidia Investigation & Leadership Fallout
* **Awaiting Answers:** Brennan noted that multiple congressional committees, federal prosecutors, and regulators are probing Nvidia's role in sending advanced AI chips to China, and reminded Bessent that he gave Nvidia a **10-day deadline** seven days prior to explain a potential "culture of non-compliance."
  * Bessent confirmed the administration has been in touch with Nvidia's temporary management, lead directors, and lawyers, but has not yet received the answers it needs.
  * He emphasized that while Nvidia's CFO is now in charge and the company remains "fantastically profitable" with near-unlimited global demand, it "behooves Nvidia to move quickly" to clarify whether there was a "promiscuous attitude" toward compliance.
* **Tracing "Handshake Deals":** Addressing reports that Nvidia's deals were often based on handshakes without a paper trail, Bessent stated that for deals of that size, "there's no such thing as an untraceable handshake deal," pointing to bills of lading and money movement.
* **Jensen Huang's Status:** When Brennan asked about President Trump's comment that former CEO Jensen Huang should get his job back—contrasting with Bessent's earlier call for an outsider—Bessent noted that "Jensen is on the outside now." He added that the administration needs to see "where the buck stops" and whether non-compliance was driven by lower-level actors or went to the top.
* **Alice Huang & Mega Speed:** Brennan raised reports linking Alice Huang—who ran Singapore-based **Mega Speed**—to Chinese security. Bessent called the focus on one person's relationship "kind of a canard" compared to the broader question of Nvidia's compliance culture, assuming Huang did not know she was an alleged spy.

### China Chip Exports & National Security Controls
* **Mega Speed Scrutiny:** Brennan cited Republican Intelligence Committee Chair Sen. Tom Cotton's frustration that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick allegedly ignored repeated letters about Mega Speed. Bessent said investigators will determine if Mega Speed was a pass-through front company, noting that Nvidia removed Mega Speed from its vendor/sales list in **August 2025**.
* **Remote Access Rule:** While Treasury and other departments have pushed to blacklist Mega Speed, Bessent highlighted a "remote access rule" enacted the previous week. Brennan noted the rule tightens licensing for countries including **Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil** to prevent restricted entities from accessing chips via leased capacity.
* **Commerce Department Approvals:** Brennan cited documents released by the Republican chair of the House Foreign Affairs Committee showing the Commerce Department approved the sale of **more than 800,000 Nvidia and AMD AI chips** to China, including to two companies linked to the Chinese military.
  * Bessent defended the approvals by stating President Trump has drawn a "very hard line at the **H200s**" (the chip performance threshold) and noted that one of the approved companies, **Alibaba**, is a general social media and online platform that merely does business with the Chinese military rather than being the military itself.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Semiconductor On-Shoring:** "We hope to have substantial on-shoring of the chip business by the end of this administration." — *Scott Bessent*
* **Offshore Data Center Penalties:** "We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries cuz China will take all of them... And we're going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it." — *President Donald Trump*
* **Nvidia Investigation Timeline:** "I have confidence that the Nvidia committees are moving very quickly on this and will come up with some very fulsome and robust answers." — *Scott Bessent*

## Full Interview: International Monetary Fund Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva

- Channel: Face the Nation
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2HWcL9KYUqA
- Published: 2026-04-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260412.html#video-2HWcL9KYUqA
- Speakers: Kristalina Georgieva (Managing Director, IMF); Margaret Brennan (Moderator, Face the Nation)

---

**TL;DR**
The global economy faces a significant "asymmetric shock" driven by Middle East conflict and AI disruption, yet remains surprisingly resilient due to private sector agility and technological innovation. While the IMF warns of a "tsunami" hitting labor markets and prolonged energy supply drags, it maintains that well-managed institutions and targeted fiscal policies can prevent a global recession.

### Global Energy and Economic Shock
* **Asymmetric Impact:** IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva notes that while the Middle East conflict affects the entire world through energy prices, the "hit" is disproportionately felt by oil importers and vulnerable nations in Asia and Sub-Saharan Africa.
    * **Supply Chain Disruptions:** Roughly 13% of global oil and 20% of gas flows have been stalled; specific shortages include helium (crucial for semiconductors/MRIs) and fertilizers, which threaten future food security.
    * **Prolonged Recovery:** Even with a ceasefire, the economic impact is "baked in" through 2026 due to infrastructure damage (72 energy facilities hit) and the 3-5 year timeline required to return gas fields to full capacity.
* **Resilience Factors:** Despite consecutive shocks (COVID-19, Ukraine, Middle East), the world has avoided a global recession. Georgieva attributes this to:
    * **Private Sector Agility:** Governments have moved away from managing companies, allowing faster, more efficient private sector responses.
    * **Stronger Fundamentals:** Emerging markets have implemented independent central banks and fiscal councils.
    * **Technological Transformation:** Innovation has acted as a buffer against contraction.

### Artificial Intelligence and the Labor Market
* **The "Tsunami" Effect:** Georgieva describes AI as a looming "tsunami" for labor markets, particularly in advanced economies.
    * **Shrinking Middle Class Jobs:** While low-skilled jobs and high-paying specialist roles may see demand, the "middle"—routine starter jobs for college graduates—is shrinking rapidly.
    * **"AI or Die":** Georgieva has issued this mandate to IMF staff, emphasizing that institutions not embracing AI will become obsolete.
* **Cybersecurity Risks:** There is deep concern regarding the lack of global "guardrails" to protect the international monetary system from AI-driven cyberattacks.
    * **Institutional Gaps:** Current international systems are not yet equipped to manage massive, AI-powered cyber risks to financial stability.

### Geopolitics, Trade, and the U.S. Dollar
* **Trade "Like Water":** Despite tariffs and trade wars, Georgieva argues that trade is "like water"—when it hits an obstacle, it finds a new path. This has led to a massive increase in regional trade agreements outside the U.S. sphere.
* **U.S. Economic Position:** The U.S. is less impacted by energy shocks as a net exporter, but inflation remains a "tax" on low-income citizens. The IMF projects the return to target inflation may be delayed.
* **Dollar Dominance:** Despite a "multi-polar" world and some diversification of reserves, the dollar remains the dominant global asset, with 75% of global financial assets held in U.S. markets.
* **Ukraine Support:** The IMF remains a primary supporter of Ukraine, noting the country’s remarkable "functional" status and high tax collection (34% of GDP) despite five years of war.

### Policy Recommendations for Member Nations
* **Avoid Trade Restrictions:** The IMF warns countries against imposing trade barriers in response to shocks, as this further inflates prices.
* **Targeted Fiscal Support:** Governments should avoid broad subsidies; support must be "targeted and temporary" to preserve limited fiscal space and manage the high cost of borrowing.
* **Education Reform:** To prevent a widening "accordion of inequality," education systems must shift toward "permanent change and constant learning" to prepare young people for an AI-driven economy.

> "The total employment actually went slightly up, but because of low-skilled jobs. So are young people dreaming to be baristas? I don’t think so. Unless we concentrate on understanding labor market impact... we would find ourselves with winners big time and many many losers." — **Kristalina Georgieva**

> "Trade is like water. You put an obstacle, it goes around it... Small open economies have no choice; they have to find ways to trade with each other because otherwise it would be very costly for their people." — **Kristalina Georgieva**

> "I very often feel like a captain on a ship in rough waters. And rough waters we will have... In this world, you need even more to have an anchor, an institution that protects you." — **Kristalina Georgieva**
