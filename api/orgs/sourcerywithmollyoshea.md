## Dylan Patel, SemiAnalysis

**TL;DR**
Dylan Patel is expanding SemiAnalysis into a research and investment empire—spanning 115 employees, the acquisition of Citrini Research, and a new $500 million hardware-focused venture fund—while tracking major shifts in AI, including multi-gigawatt data center permitting delays and a market transition where product execution (like Meta’s Muse and Perplexity) is outperforming raw foundation model labs.

### Data Center Bottlenecks, Regulation & Public Perception
* **Permitting Delays Over Supply Chain:** SemiAnalysis has pushed back multiple gigawatts of data center capacity from 2027 into 2028, and pushed several gigawatts out of 2028 entirely—not due to labor or equipment shortages, but because permits are unavailable.
* **Texas Regulatory Landscape:** Governor Hinojosa in Texas has been constructive, signaling she will not stop data centers as long as they do not raise electricity prices and are reasonable stewards of water.
  * However, she holds significant leverage via water, wastewater, stormwater, and air permits (which can even stop off-grid data centers).
  * A potential sticking point is union labor mandates, as Texas has very little union labor and non-union electricians and industrial plumbers on data center sites already make $200,000+ per year.
* **Public Ignorance vs. Sentiment:** A poll conducted by SemiAnalysis and David Shor across counties with 100MW+ data centers revealed that **49%** of residents did not even know a data center was in their county. In counties with a 100MW+ data center planned in the next three years, only **40%** knew it was coming.
  * Most local opposition stems from nebulous anti-AI sentiment online rather than actual local impact, though poorly executed projects can be loud or fail to return tax value.
  * Conversely, well-structured projects generate massive local revenue—such as a county in Louisiana that paid all its teachers $20,000 bonuses from data center property taxes. Patel notes companies can easily buy goodwill by subsidizing power for everyone within a 30-mile radius.
* **The Water Usage Myth:** Patel debunked exaggerated water narratives (citing a *New York Times* bestseller that overestimated data center water usage by 1,000x), noting that all of Elon Musk’s data centers use less embedded water than **three In-N-Out** restaurants when accounting for the agricultural supply chain of beef versus chip fabs and power generation.

### The AI Race: Models vs. Products
* **Lab Competition:** Responding to host Molly O'Shea’s framing of the "Four Seasons of AI" (Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX), Patel noted that markets smooth out valuations rather than swinging wildly on single releases. Anthropic dominated Q2, OpenAI dominated Q3, and Anthropic is back ahead with Opus 3.5 and Fable 5.2 while OpenAI delays model releases.
* **Product Execution Beats Raw Models:** Patel argued that OpenAI and Anthropic are bad at building products. Meta’s **Muse** (beta-tested by Patel) and **Instinct** (which primarily uses open models) are vastly superior products compared to OpenAI’s **Dots** or Codex, which are crippled by overzealous safety filters ("Guardian"). He also criticized OpenAI’s chat app UI as confusing for a billion-user app.
* **SemiAnalysis’s $15M AI Spend:** SemiAnalysis spends **$15 million per year** on AI. Over the last few months, its spend on persistent agents jumped from 0% to **45%**—with the vast majority flowing through **Perplexity** (which acts as a better harness over Claude and OpenAI on the backend) rather than Anthropic’s Claude tags or OpenAI’s Dots.
* **Enterprise Stickiness & Startup Viability:** While developer tools like CloudCode and Codex are easy to switch away from, enterprise contracts are becoming sticky (e.g., Anthropic offering discounts if **70%+** of an enterprise's AI spend is with them). Among application startups, Patel believes most will fail, though **Instinct**, **Sierra**, and **Harvey** are showing real revenue growth and staying power (even as Harvey’s margins seesaw).

### Building the SemiAnalysis Empire & $500M VC Fund
* **Global Footprint & Business Model:** SemiAnalysis now employs **115 people** across **15 countries** (with offices in New York, Chicago, SF, Oregon, Singapore, and Korea). Its core revenue comes from selling proprietary datasets (tracking every data center, chip, and supply chain metric globally) and one-on-one consulting for hyperscalers, semiconductor firms, and AI labs.
* **Acquiring Citrini Research:** Patel acquired Citrini Research (led by James and director of research Kyle) to help operationalize their business. Citrini remains a completely separate company with full editorial independence—even when their macro/AI calls turn bearish, contrasting with Patel’s long-term bullishness.
* **$500M Solo GP Venture Fund:** Patel raised a **$500 million** early-stage (Seed/Series A) venture fund as a solo GP, focusing on hardware, infrastructure, silicon, optics, manufacturing, networking, and robotics—areas where SemiAnalysis’s deep technical bench (former ASML, TSMC, Intel, Nvidia, and hyperscaler engineers) gives him an edge.
* **Strict Independence & Firewalls:** The fund is legally firewalled from SemiAnalysis and Citrini. Patel refuses to take paid partnerships or ads, and maintains strict objectivity—publicly criticizing his biggest customers (e.g., calling out Nvidia’s anti-competitive practices with neoclouds) and even his own portfolio companies (e.g., publicly noting Fluidstack’s data center delays, or critiquing Iris Energy/IREN’s unreliable cloud tech while acknowledging the value of their powered land).

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Space Data Centers:** Engineering data centers in space is extremely difficult and will not solve capacity constraints in the next 3–4 years. While Elon Musk is aiming for 2028, Patel "takes the over" on that timeline, predicting **1 gigawatt in space** sometime this decade (around **2030**) — *Dylan Patel*.
* **Hardware Innovation Acceleration:** Within **1 year**, the number of companies building hardware—and doing it well—will skyrocket, driven by AI models improving on complex technical benchmarks like SemiAnalysis’s new **GDPX** (which tests cyber, chip design, hardware design, material science, bio, and quant RSI) — *Dylan Patel*.
* **Startup Attrition:** Most AI application startups will fail, though select companies with strong product-market fit and revenue velocity (such as Instinct, Sierra, and Harvey) will achieve long-term staying power — *Dylan Patel*.

## The $100 Heart Scan Brad Gerstner Says Could Save Your Life

### TL;DR
Heart disease kills 800,000 Americans annually, yet the $100 Coronary Artery Calcium (CAC) scan can detect plaque precursors early and prevent up to 90% of heart attacks. Brad Gerstner, Jason Chang, and Rob Goldberg are championing a movement to scale CAC scans to 40 million people a year—transforming it into the "mammogram for the heart."

### The Case for Mass CAC Screening & Prevention
* **The High Cost of Inaction:** Heart disease is the world's number one killer, claiming 800,000 American lives annually.
* **The Mammogram Comparison:** 
  * Breast cancer accounts for roughly 40,000 deaths per year, supported by 40 million annual mammograms (setting the playbook for preventative medicine).
  * Heart disease kills 800,000 Americans annually, yet only 1 million CAC scans are performed each year.
* **Scan Economics & Logistics:** Brad Gerstner and Jason Chang brought a mobile CT scanner to the All-In Summit to demonstrate that CAC scans cost $100, take under 5 minutes on the bed (15 minutes total), are non-invasive, and have high ROI for individual and national healthcare.
* **Daily Trailer Throughput:** In a 9-hour window at the All-In Summit, the mobile trailer completed 33 scans, revealing a wide distribution from zero to severe scores.

### Personal Stakes: The Death of Dave Goldberg
* **A Shocking Loss:** Rob Goldberg’s brother, Dave Goldberg (husband of Sheryl Sandberg and an inspiration for the All-In / Bestie group), died suddenly at age 47 from undiagnosed heart disease while on a friend's 50th birthday trip in Mexico.
* **The EKG False Sense of Security:** Dave had passed a running EKG just five months before dropping dead from ventricular arrhythmia. 
  * EKGs have a ~4% false positive/negative rate.
  * Dave had done so much cardio that he trained his heart to temporarily overcome the underlying heart disease until it ultimately stopped.
* **Autopsy Findings:** An autopsy—made possible by Sheryl Sandberg, Chamath Palihapitiya, and family—confirmed severe, undiagnosed heart disease. His death inspired Sheryl Sandberg’s book *Option B*.

### Flaws in Traditional Healthcare & The Cholesterol Myth
* **The Limits of Standard Labs:** Half of all people who suffer heart attacks have cholesterol levels falling within the normal "green zone" (80 to 120).
* **Why General Practitioners Miss It:** Relying solely on general blood work and EKGs leaves patients falsely confident. Traditional markers fail to show actual arterial plaque buildup.
* **Regulatory Capture & Stent Lobbying:** Brad Gerstner notes that 50 years ago, following Eisenhower’s heart attack, Silicon Valley and Washington favored the stent and cholesterol-tracking lobby over promoting early calcium CT scanning.
* **Aggressive Modern Targets:** Top preventative cardiologists at Stanford, Harvard, and Columbia recommend taking a CAC scan by age 30–35. If a non-zero score appears, patients should aggressively lower LDLs below 40 using statins and ezetimibes.

### Scaling the Mission: Corporate Programs & Policy Goals
* **The Buy-One, Give-One Model:** Brad Gerstner is partnering with major Silicon Valley companies (such as Salesforce/Mark Benioff) and the NFL (during the Super Bowl in San Francisco) on a "buy-one, give-one" program to fund free scans in underserved communities like East Palo Alto.
* **Washington DC Outreach:** 
  * Brad Gerstner has helped 40 to 50 members of the House and Senate get CAC scans, uncovering target-rich populations.
  * The team is actively working with Washington to transition CAC scans into standard care.
* **Federal Coverage Target:** Brad Gerstner anticipates that by the end of the year, diagnostic CAC scans will be approved and covered by Medicaid and Medicare, driving doctors to adopt it as standard clinical practice.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Standard of Care Timeline:** Brad Gerstner predicts that by the end of the year, CAC scans for diagnostic purposes will be approved and covered by Medicaid and Medicare, establishing it as the national standard of care.
* **Heart Attack Reduction:** Leading preventative cardiologists at Stanford told Brad Gerstner that if everyone adopts routine CAC screening, 80% to 90% of all heart attacks can be eliminated.
