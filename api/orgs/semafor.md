### TL;DR
At Semafor’s *Silicon Valley & The World*, billionaire activist investor Bill Ackman outlined how he would theoretically run a campaign to reinstate ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang—though he noted his direct involvement is "unlikely." Alongside the President’s chief technology advisor (referred to as "Director"), Ackman also discussed the rise of founder-controlled public companies, U.S. AI export controls, and how commercial instincts and AI are reshaping scientific philanthropy.

### The Activist Playbook for Reinstating Jensen Huang
* **Ackman’s Defense:** Bill Ackman rushed to Huang’s defense out of a belief in "innocent until proven guilty" and a favorable past relationship with Huang, noting that the best time to help someone is when they are down.
* **Likelihood of Involvement:** Ackman said it is "unlikely" he will get directly involved, though he called it a "pretty interesting activist setup" with "Steve Jobsian" elements that would likely garner significant shareholder support.
* **Tactical Execution:** 
  * Establish the facts first to resolve current legal uncertainties; if Huang’s missteps were a "personal foible as opposed to a corporate defense conspiracy," there is a strong case for reinstatement.
  * Partner with Huang, who owns 3% of Nvidia, to call a special shareholder meeting.
  * Present the facts and propose board changes—Ackman expects "the writing will be on the wall very quickly," forcing the board to reinstate Huang before a vote even occurs.
* **Board Ethics on Lying:** While "truth is pretty darn important" between a board and a CEO, Ackman distinguished between a lie regarding a personal relationship versus a lie about next quarter's revenue or core business operations.

### Founder Control & Public Market Short-Termism
* **The Rise of the "Golden Share":** Moderator Liz Hoffman noted that Anthropic is going public this week with a structure giving its seven cofounders a "golden share" that controls nearly everything at the company.
* **The Case for Founder Control:** Ackman argued that founder-led companies historically create far more long-term value than dispersed-ownership companies run by hired CEOs, though he cautioned against blindly passing that golden share to the "next generation."
* **Wall Street Short-Termism:** 
  * Index funds—which Ackman described as automated, passive entities operating for basis points rather than "real owners"—now own 25% of corporate America.
  * Combined with quantitative trading, options, and 3x levered ETFs, public markets force short-term thinking, making founder control or large, long-term anchor investors increasingly logical.

### U.S. Tech Stack, Export Controls & Security
* **Exporting the U.S. Stack:** The Director emphasized that winning in superintelligence (SI) requires U.S. companies to have the chips they need, while also sharing a trusted Western tech stack globally via the **American SI Export Program** (created by executive order in July of last year).
* **Chip Diversion & Enforcement:** Addressing tensions over Nvidia's chip sales, the Director pointed to new Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) rules announced today regarding remote chip access and licensing requirements for certain countries.
* **Workforce & IP Security:** With young AI companies growing rapidly, the Director urged CEOs to take security seriously and protect frontier model weights—calling them some of the most valuable assets in the world.

### AI in Science & Reforming Philanthropy
* **Ackman’s AI-Native Brain Institute:** Ackman and his wife are endowing a Brain Institute in New York (initially $400M, now closer to $800M). Operating in "super-founder mode," Ackman’s first hires include a Chief AI Officer, and he previously connected Huang’s healthcare team with Mount Sinai’s brain data.
* **Free Market Over Mandates:** Both Ackman and the Director rejected the idea of forcing AI labs to devote 25% of their compute to curing cancer or brain disease.
* **SI for Science Momentum:** 
  * The Director highlighted that philanthropic organizations like the Chan Zuckerberg Biohub and Patrick Collison’s Arc Institute are investing hundreds of millions to billions into SI-powered science.
  * A month ago, the administration hosted an "SI for Science" event announcing **$2.4 billion** in donations from the SI community to provide free compute to American scientists.
  * Roughly a quarter of OpenAI (nearly a quarter of a trillion dollars) and similar commitments from Anthropic's founders are earmarked for philanthropic endeavors.
* **Applying Commercial Instincts to Nonprofits:** 
  * Having given away **$1.5 billion** over 20 years through the Pershing Square Foundation, Ackman criticized traditional philanthropy for enormous waste, lack of M&A, and lack of equity compensation.
  * His Brain Institute will operate with commercial instincts: creating and monetizing IP through company formation, sharing equity with employees, and utilizing a captive venture fund (which has already invested **$90 million** in brain-computer interface and device companies) to funnel returns back into the institute.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **On Nvidia Governance:** If an activist partners with Jensen Huang (3% owner) to call a special meeting, "the writing will be on the wall very quickly... at which point you don't even have to go to the meeting, you just put him back in." — *Bill Ackman*
* **On Scientific Discovery:** "SI is going to transform entirely the way that scientists do their work from material science, to chemistry, to math, to physics, to biochemistry, to the way the pharmaceuticals are developed." — *Director (President's Chief Technology Advisor)*
* **On Philanthropic Capital:** "There's going to be more money moving into the SI for Science field than we could ever imagine." — *Director (President's Chief Technology Advisor)*

### US Presence & Enterprise Strategy: "Sovereign AI"
* **Silicon Valley Footprint:** Mistral maintains its US headquarters in Palo Alto with a sizable team of researchers and go-to-market staff.
  * The company is increasingly partnering with cyber vendors to deploy its latest open-source release, which Mensch noted has strong cyber capabilities.
* **Defining "Sovereign AI":** Rather than competing on sheer scale with OpenAI/Anthropic or strictly on price with Chinese labs, Mistral targets mission-critical enterprise processes (such as R&D and manufacturing) with three guarantees: portability, business continuity, and affordability.
  * **Industrial Customers:** Mistral powers agentic engineering loops and physical simulations for major manufacturers, including **Airbus** (aircraft), **ASML** (lithography machines), and **BMW** (automobiles).
* **Horizontal vs. Vertical Model:** Mensch contrasted Mistral's horizontal, open-weight platform—which lets customers build custom systems—against competitors that go vertical to capture more of the value chain.

### Data Privacy, Portability & Competitive Positioning
* **Zero-Trust Architecture:** Addressing Shelly Banjo's question on whether OpenAI and Anthropic use customer data to build competing products, Mensch noted that enterprise clients in manufacturing and financial services do not want to rely on vendor promises.
  * Mistral proves it cannot see customer data either by deploying models **on-premises** (so data never flows back to Mistral) or through **confidential cloud guarantees**.
  * This ensures customers' IP and processes continue running even if Mistral were to disappear, and protects them if a vendor suffers a cyberattack.
* **Two-Tier Model Portfolio:** While Mistral's core platform is portable by design, it operates two distinct model categories:
  * **Open-source models:** Available for free.
  * **Proprietary models:** Specialized models deployed on-premises for customers rather than exposed for free.
* **Pricing:** Mensch emphasized that Mistral's models—as well as the third-party open-source models it serves—are "quite cheap."

### Geopolitics & Hedging Against US/China Dependencies
* **Global vs. Regional Drivers:** While Mistral's US growth is driven purely by technological differentiation, its growth in Europe and Asia is also fueled by geopolitical fragmentation.
* **Hedging Supply Chain Risk:** European and Asian enterprises are actively hedging against excessive reliance on US providers, fearing they could lose access to US models or compute.
  * Customers increasingly demand compute located in Europe and operated by European players.

### French Politics, Youth Employment & European IPO Plans
* **Youth & AI Anxiety:** Responding to Banjo's question on political unrest in France, Mensch attributed part of the youth anguish to companies freezing entry-level hiring in favor of senior staff due to AI, alongside unsustainable European pension systems.
  * Mistral actively hires young talent, arguing they adopt AI far better and create "much more value than people that are 10 years older than them."
  * Mensch criticized "Doomsday" AI narratives for fueling public anxiety.
* **Navigating French Politics:** Pushing back on the idea that Mistral is a "personal project" of President Emmanuel Macron, Mensch said sovereign AI is a bipartisan necessity to avoid trade imbalances and foreign dependency.
  * Ahead of France's **2027** presidential election, Mensch has been conducting **1-hour** education sessions with political candidates to prepare them for "five years of very technical discussions" on AI policy and data center capacity.
* **European IPO:** Europe represents Mistral's headquarters, the majority of its investor base, and "a little more than half" of its customer base, making it the natural listing venue.

* **Anthropic IPO & Market Access:** Responding to Semafor Executive Editor Gina Chon on Anthropic's upcoming IPO, Friar wished them well, calling it positive for the ecosystem because public market investors currently lack direct access to frontier model companies (having to invest in chips, infrastructure, or hyperscalers instead).
* **OpenAI IPO Criteria:** Friar views an IPO as a milestone toward OpenAI's mission of AGI for the benefit of humanity, which will also unlock debt-based financing and lower the overall cost of capital. She outlined four readiness criteria:
  * Business readiness (which she believes OpenAI has achieved).
  * Momentum (demonstrated by Q3 performance).
  * Market readiness.
  * Safety and alignment, noting that remaining private allows OpenAI to pace itself on safety work while staying well-capitalized.

### Consumer Scale, Advertising, and Search Growth
* **User & Subscriber Base:** ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion weekly users, adding 200 million users in Q3 alone, with over 100 million paying subscribers.

  * Weekly usage includes 300 million people asking health or wellness questions and 200 million asking personal finance questions, with interaction modes increasingly shifting toward voice.
* **Advertising Business:** With 90% of its user base on free tiers, OpenAI launched an advertising model that hit a $1 billion run rate in August—reaching that milestone in under 200 days, making it OpenAI's fastest-growing business.
  * Ads are now live in 87 countries, delivering high contextual and personal relevance.
* **Search Momentum:** ChatGPT now handles roughly 20% of the volume of all Google queries, up from approximately 11% in March.

* **Enterprise Growth:** OpenAI's enterprise business grew 100% quarter-over-quarter in Q3 and is already a highly profitable model, supported by frontier APIs and a growing system integrator ecosystem (e.g., Capgemini).

  * Friar emphasized that coding has shifted to autonomous, agentic workflows, predicting that manual coding will soon feel like hand-knitting compared to using a machine.
  * OpenAI is rolling out **dots**—proactive virtual coworkers that sweep Slack and Gmail to prepare talking points and track tasks. After using dots for 6–8 weeks, Friar compared their necessity to Excel or email.
* **Vertical Expansion:** OpenAI is expanding into verticals including life sciences, manufacturing, the Office of the CLO, and the Office of the CFO.

* **Custom Chip (Halapeno):** OpenAI is building its own chip, **Halapeno**, to enable "soup-to-nuts" self-builds.

  * **Paid Users:** Currently at 56% gross margins on the $1 billion ARR.

* **Lean Headcount:** The company operates with roughly 500 employees, which Srinivas described as a "sweet spot" that provides researchers ample compute per person without requiring trillion-dollar CAPEX commitments.

### Agent Orchestration & The Frontier Research Program
* **The "Mode-Neutral" Moat:** In response to moderator Ben Smith's question about competing against hyperscalers in the agent space, Srinivas argued that Perplexity's moat is accuracy and model-neutral orchestration. Unlike hyperscalers—who are incentivized to lock users into their own clouds or equity-backed models to recoup massive CAPEX—Perplexity is strictly user-aligned.
* **10,000 GPU Commitment:** Perplexity announced a new frontier research program committing at least 10,000 GPUs to post-train models specifically for multi-agent orchestration and continual learning.
* **Open-Weight vs. Frontier Models:**

  * **Benchmarking GLM vs. Opus:** Currently, post-trained GLM models (GLM 53 / GLM 31) take about 15 minutes to complete a task that Opus 55 finishes in 3 minutes, largely because GLM wastes time on erroneous tool calls.

* **Air-Gapped Enterprise Security:** Perplexity is partnering with companies like NVIDIA to deploy fully local, air-gapped, on-premises agents for regulated institutions—including sovereign wealth funds, banks, credit card companies, exchanges, insurance firms, and pharma/bio companies—that cannot risk sending proprietary data or IP to frontier labs for post-training.
* **Escalation & Hybrid Frameworks:**
  * **Local-to-Cloud Escalation:** Local models can request user permission to orchestrate up to cloud frontier models when a task requires higher intelligence.
  * **Hybrid Mode:** Released on Apple Silicon, allowing users to default to frontier cloud models and orchestrate down to local hardware for sensitive data.
* **Jevons Paradox & AI Compute:** Addressing Smith's question on whether the industry is overbuilding compute, Srinivas argued that local AI will not pop the AI bubble. Instead, Jevons Paradox will take effect: lowering the cost of AI will drive massive adoption among small businesses and expand overall usage of frontier AI as a gateway.

### Media Relations & Ecosystem Integration
* **Pre-Training vs. Summarization:** Pressed by Ben Smith on media lawsuits and Perplexity's 2025 revenue-share program, Srinivas emphasized that Perplexity does not pre-train on or distill books and publisher content. * **Premium Connectors:** Perplexity is expanding its role as an orchestrator across all data sources—including the web, CRMs, Slack, and premium connectors like the *New England Journal of Medicine*.

### Leadership Lessons
* **Jensen Huang's Grit:** Reflecting on NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's advice, Srinivas praised Huang's relentlessness and "day-one" paranoia—operating even the world's most valuable company as if it could go out of business in 30 days—calling him one of the greatest founders in American history.

* **Widespread Public Anxiety:** Unlike previous tech waves, AI faces a deep, bipartisan undercurrent of public distrust that is already driving aggressive local and state-level legislative action in both red and blue states (including Texas, Tennessee, Minnesota, Indiana, and New York).

    1. **Consumer Protection:** Managing the direct human impacts of AI, such as local data center resource strain, AI chatbots, and automated workflows in healthcare, insurance, hiring, and education.
    2. **Catastrophic Risks:** High-profile, existential policy concerns surrounding frontier models.
    3. **Job Displacement:** Short-term economic pain and labor displacement, which Tusk notes will cause severe political friction despite the long-term creation of new industries.
    4. **Positive Utility:** Harnessing AI for high-upside public goods like climate change mitigation, drug discovery, and governmental efficiency.
* **The "Luke Skywalker" vs. "Darth Vader" Dynamic:** Tusk argues that AI lacks a clear "hero" to combat entrenched incumbents. He notes that Anthropic is successfully positioning itself as the responsible "Luke Skywalker," while OpenAI has willingly embraced a villainous "Darth Vader" persona.

* **The Problem of a Utility-Like User Base:** Uber succeeded in beating local regulations because its early users found the app "magical" and actively lobbied politicians to keep it. In contrast, Tusk notes that services like ChatGPT are treated like utility companies—widely used, but unloved and incapable of inspiring grassroots political defense.
* **High-Stakes Legal Risks:** While early Uber could absorb low-stakes penalties like impounded cars to force its way into markets, the regulatory and compliance risks for frontier AI models are massive and potentially criminal, making a "brute force" strategy highly dangerous.
* **Regulatory Moats as a Defense:** Tusk advises best-in-class startups in highly regulated sectors (like healthcare or insurance) to lean into public anxiety by actively lobbying for exceptionally high regulatory standards. 
    * By helping write stringent laws that they can meet but competitors cannot, startups can build highly defensive regulatory moats.

### The Impending Jurisdictional Battle Over Prediction Markets

* **The FanDuel Playbook:** During the 2015 fantasy sports crisis, Tusk kept FanDuel alive by mobilizing passionate, highly targeted user bases in key legislative districts, threatening local politicians with direct electoral consequences if they banned the service.
* **An Impending Supreme Court Showdown:** Prediction markets (e.g., Polymarket and Kalshi) face a looming 50/50 jurisdictional battle in the Supreme Court over whether they should be regulated by the federal Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) or individual state gaming boards.
    * **State Jurisdiction Scenario:** If the Court hands authority to the states, platforms like Polymarket will face sluggish state gaming boards and will need to launch massive, high-budget user advocacy campaigns to secure licenses.
    * **Federal Jurisdiction Scenario:** If the CFTC wins sole jurisdiction, traditional sports betting giants may pivot to prediction markets to escape high state taxes, creating massive, unexpected tax revenue deficits for state governments.
* **Underage Betting Safeguards:** Watching his own teenage children bypass age gates on sports betting apps led Tusk to work with New York Governor Kathy Hochul on a proposal to mandate biometric face-screening for logging into state-regulated betting platforms.

* **Winding Down Tusk Venture Partners:** Tusk is winding down his $300 million venture fund to return to his original model of advising early-stage startups in exchange for equity rather than managing LP capital.
    * Tusk cited a dislike for LP relations, board seats, and compliance, noting that mid-sized funds are structurally inefficient because they must return their entire pool of LP capital before general partners see a dollar of carry.

* **Investing in Nuclear and Alternative Compute:** Tusk believes data centers will inevitably be forced to pay for their own power infrastructure. He is backing startups focused on behind-the-meter nuclear reactors and high-efficiency compute methods (such as biological or dendritic computing) to bypass the public grid entirely.
