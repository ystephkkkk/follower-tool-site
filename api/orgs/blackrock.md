## The Invisible Backbone of AI: Why Light Powers Intelligence | Lumentum | RAISE Summit 2026

- Channel: RAISE Summit
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2kApI4tJtr8

- Speakers: Michael Hurlston (President & CEO, Lumentum); Tony Kim (Head of Fundamental Equities, BlackRock)

---

### TL;DR
Michael Hurlston, CEO of Lumentum, and Tony Kim of BlackRock discuss how the exponential rise of AI model sizes is forcing data centers to transition from copper wiring to photonics (optics) across a four-stage framework: scale across, scale out, scale up, and scale in.

### Data Center Evolution: From Copper to Optics
- **The Copper Wall:** As speeds and bandwidths increase inside data centers, the reach and capability of copper wiring decreases due to electrical resistance and heat.
- **The Physics Advantage of Light:** Optics eliminates resistance, allowing data to transmit over longer distances with far less loss compared to electrical signaling.
- **The Four-Stage Data Center Framework:**
  - **Scale Across (Kilometers):** The historic internet backbone connecting data centers; now operating at full bandwidth over kilometers to support massive inferencing models.
  - **Scale Out (Meters):** Connecting compute racks to switch banks using pluggable optical transceivers (roughly 10× the optical lanes of scale across).
  - **Scale Up (Inside the Rack / Centimeters to Meters):** Replacing internal rack copper links with optics to handle 1.6 terabits per second speeds; represents a 100× market expansion relative to scale across.
  - **Scale In (Millimeters / On-Chip & PCB):** Future transition to replace copper traces on printed circuit boards and disaggregate high-bandwidth memory from compute using optical interconnects (representing a 1,000× market scale).

### Optical Component Supply Chain & Manufacturing Challenges
- **Scale of Production Shift:** The optical industry historically served telecom customers (AT&T, Verizon) requiring hundreds of lasers; AI data centers now demand hundreds of millions of lasers.
- **Indium Phosphide Fab Constraints:** 
  - Lasers require a specialized light-emitting semiconductor material called **indium phosphide**.
  - Lumentum has brought online **5 different indium phosphide wafer fabs** to scale production from thousands of wafers to millions.
- **Industry Shortages:** Hurlston notes that severe supply shortages of indium phosphide will likely become even more acute than current memory chip shortages, prompting strategic investments from companies like NVIDIA into optical manufacturers.
- **Laser Density Requirements:** A typical Grace Blackwell server rack utilizing scale-up optics requires external pluggable light sources containing 8 distinct lasers each, totaling approximately **8,000 lasers per rack**.

### Laser Power Classes and Extreme Applications
- **Scale Out Power:** Modestly powered lasers operating at roughly **100 milliwatts**.
- **Scale Across and Scale Up Power:** Higher-powered lasers operating at roughly **400 milliwatts** to span kilometers or navigate rack-level distances.
- **Space and Undersea Deployments:** The same 400-milliwatt lasers used for scale-up and fiber optics (such as subsea cables in the Mariana Trench) can be adapted with amplification for free-space optics in satellite constellations and data centers in space.
- **Scale In Power:** Shorter-range, lower-power laser arrays designed to handle high data rates over millimeter and centimeter distances on printed circuit boards.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Scale-Up Deployments:** First deployments of scale-up optics will occur in the **second half of 2027**, with optical racks appearing in mid-2027 and backplane connectivity following in **2028** — Michael Hurlston.
- **Scale-In Timeline:** Optical interconnects for on-chip and high-bandwidth memory disaggregation will begin to see adoption by the **end of the decade (2029)** — Michael Hurlston.

### Notable Quotes

> — **Michael Hurlston**

### TL;DR
Sid Sheth and Tony Kim discuss how d-Matrix's purpose-built inference hardware—transitioning from SRAM-based accelerators to 3D-stacked DRAM-on-compute architectures—addresses the unique demands of the agentic AI era by optimizing for interactivity and energy efficiency.

### Company Overview & Market Positioning
* **Company Background:** Founded in 2019 by Sid Sheth, d-Matrix is a chip company focused entirely on AI inference, operating in a capital-intensive sector for seven years.
* **Current Status:** d-Matrix has announced that its products are officially moving into production and are ready for deployment.
* **The Inference Thesis:** Analogous to human development (spending 20+ years learning vs. 40+ years applying knowledge), inference represents a vastly larger and more economically critical market opportunity than training.
* **Optimization Metrics:** Unlike training (which prioritizes raw performance, data scale, and cluster throughput), inference prioritizes efficiency metrics:
  * Speed: Milliseconds per token
  * Cost: Dollars per token
  * Energy: Throughput per watt and interactivity limits

### Architectural Approaches: SRAM vs. HBM
* **The X-Axis vs. Y-Axis Trade-off:** 
  * *Y-Axis (HBM/GPUs):* Optimized for throughput and batching multiple users, but struggles with extreme speed/interactivity.
  * *X-Axis (SRAM/d-Matrix):* Optimized for speed, low energy consumption, and high interactivity through in-memory compute (minimizing data movement).
* **The SRAM Capacity Constraint:** Pure SRAM architectures (like those from Grock, Cerebras, and d-Matrix) face capacity challenges with very large models or long context lengths.
* **Heterogeneous Disaggregated Computing:** 
  * Workloads are split by running the compute-heavy prefill portion on HBM GPUs and memory-intensive token generation on SRAM accelerators.
  * d-Matrix announced a partnership with NVIDIA for a target customer called Parasale (an inference cloud) combining Blackwell GPUs with d-Matrix Corsair SRAM accelerators.

### The Next Generation: 3D-Stacked DRAM
* **The Architectural "Endgame":** To overcome SRAM capacity limits without sacrificing speed, d-Matrix is building a "skyscraper" architecture by stacking custom DRAM directly on top of SRAM compute layers, rather than placing memory to the side like HBM.
* **Scaling Layers:** d-Matrix's roadmap starts with 1 layer of memory in their next-generation product, scaling to 4 layers, and eventually 8 layers (comparable to HBM stack heights).
* **Bridging the Axes:** This stacked approach allows d-Matrix to maintain deep X-axis interactivity while shifting upward on the Y-axis by expanding capacity for more users and larger context lengths.
* **Market Coexistence:** GPU-based HBM solutions and stacked-DRAM/SRAM solutions will coexist because frontier models trained on GPUs will continue to require GPU-level capabilities, while cost- and power-conscious deployments will utilize disaggregated architectures.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
