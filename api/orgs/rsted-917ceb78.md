## Ørsted CEO: Offshore Wind's Boom and Bust, and America's Pushback | Podcast | In Good Company

**TL;DR:** Ørsted CEO Rasmus Errboe details the company's evolution into a renewables leader, the $8B capital raise following a crippling US stop-work order, and the broader supply chain and interest rate pressures facing the offshore wind industry.

### Company Evolution & Strategic Pivot
* **Fossil Fuel Divestment:** Ørsted transitioned from a Danish oil and gas company (founded as DONG Energy in 2006) into a pure-play renewables leader, selling its oil and gas business in 2017.
* **The Growth Era (2015–2020):** During this period, the Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) for offshore wind dropped by roughly 70%, driven by rapid industrialization and scaling, particularly in the UK.
* **Strategic Retrenchment:** Early in his tenure, CEO Rasmus Errboe streamlined Ørsted's strategy by exiting floating wind, green fuels, and the European onshore business to focus purely on bottom-fixed offshore wind where the company holds a competitive advantage.

### Industry Headwinds & Cost Inflation
* **LCOE Reversal (2020–2025):** LCOE rose by roughly 50% due to rising interest rates, supply chain cost inflation, and unstable tender frameworks, resulting in failed tenders across the industry.
* **Market Differentiation:** Errboe noted that while Ørsted delivered on its European core business despite price spikes, the severe write-downs were concentrated in the US market.
* **Geopolitical Drivers:** The energy crises sparked by geopolitical events have cemented renewables as the cheapest long-term solution for European energy security and affordability, with electricity demand in Europe projected to nearly double by 2050 due to electrification and AI.

### The US Crisis & Capital Raise
* **The Stop-Work Order:** On August 22, 2024, just six months into Errboe's tenure and in the middle of a massive capital raise, Ørsted received a 1-page stop-work order from the US government on a project that was 80% complete.
* **Legal Action:** On September 4, 2024, Ørsted filed a lawsuit against the US government after evaluating permits obtained over 7 to 9 years.
* **Record Capital Raise:** Amid the US backdrop, Ørsted executed an €8 billion equity capital raise—the largest ever for renewables and in the Nordics—with €4 billion provided by its majority owner, the Danish state.
* **Self-Critique:** Errboe admitted Ørsted should have stepped back from its problematic US projects sooner and that management set the bar too high at the June 2023 Capital Markets Day by prioritizing volume over value.

### Supply Chain & Geographic Focus
* **European Manufacturing:** Over the past 3 years, the European supply chain has invested €14 billion in manufacturing capacity (factories, ports, vessels).
* **China's Role:** While Ørsted is not active in China as a developer or turbine sourcing OEM, the broader global offshore wind industry relies on Chinese components like monopiles, cables, and vessels.
* **Global Footprint:** Ørsted's current active markets outside Europe include Taiwan, Korea, Australia, and the US.

### Leadership & Turnaround Strategy
* **Four-Pronged CEO Focus:** Errboe focused on four core pillars during the crisis: setting a new strategic direction, building a new team, making critical capital allocation decisions correctly, and resetting internal/external communications to rebuild trust.
* **Organizational Impact:** Ørsted announced a 25% reduction in headcount to right-size the organization, which Errboe communicated transparently despite the toll on employee morale.
* **Team Sport Philosophy:** Errboe rejects the notion of the "lonely CEO," emphasizing that running the company is a collective team effort backed by strong organizational purpose.
