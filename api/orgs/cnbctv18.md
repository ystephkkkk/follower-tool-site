## Young Turks Reloaded In Davos | India, A.I. & The New Global Order

---

**TL;DR**
Davos 2026 highlights a shift toward a distributed global order where India is emerging as a primary engine for AI innovation and Europe is rapidly remilitarizing through deep-tech partnerships. Global investors are moving beyond speculative AI toward "agentic" applications and physical robotics, signaled by massive capital inflows into companies building foundational "brains" for autonomous systems.

### India’s Growth and Prosus’s Strategic Pivot
* **Investment Outlook:** Fabricio Bloisi (CEO, Prosus) emphasized that India is a top-three global market alongside Europe and Latin America, citing a 7.5% GDP growth rate and its position as the world's fourth-largest economy.
    * **Portfolio Value Goal:** Prosus aims for its Indian portfolio companies to reach a collective valuation of $50 billion within the next four years, up from its current $10 billion investment base.
    * **Profitability Milestones:** PayU is now profitable in India; Swiggy’s core food delivery business is profitable, though its "Quick Commerce" arm continues to require investment for market expansion.
* **AI Infrastructure:** Prosus is collaborating with the Indian government on the "AI for All" initiative.
    * **R&D Expansion:** Following the success of its AI house in Amsterdam, Prosus plans to launch a dedicated AI Lab and R&D center in India in 2026 to train local language models.
* **IPO Momentum:** Following a successful 2025 featuring IPOs for Meesho, Urban Company, and ixigo, Prosus anticipates several more Indian portfolio companies will go public in 2026.

### The Rise of Physical AI and General-Purpose Robotics
* **The "Brain" Gap:** Deepak Pathak (Co-founder, Skild AI) identified the lack of a versatile "brain" as the primary reason robots are not yet ubiquitous. Skild AI is developing "omni-bodied intelligence" to allow one AI model to control any hardware—from humanoids to quadrupeds.
* **Capital Intensity:** Skild AI recently closed a $1.4 billion Series C funding round at a valuation exceeding $14 billion, backed by Nvidia, Softbank, and Jeff Bezos.
* **Target Markets:** 
    * **Near-term:** Enterprise applications including manufacturing, inspection, data centers, and delivery.
    * **Long-term:** Consumer and home robotics to address labor shortages and aging populations.
* **India’s Hardware Opportunity:** India is well-positioned to become a robotics hardware hub by leveraging its existing automotive and electronics supply chain expertise.

### Defense-Tech and Global Security Innovation
* **Satellite Disruption:** Rafal Modrzewski (CEO & Co-founder, ICEYE) highlighted a shift in military intelligence using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) satellites. ICEYE has launched 64 satellites to provide real-time global monitoring.
* **Strategic Speed:** A recent 1.4 billion euro contract with the German government (Rheinmetall) will deploy a 40-satellite constellation within just two years—a fraction of the traditional decade-long procurement cycle.
    * **Tactical Advantage:** This "swarm" technology allows for troop monitoring every 20 minutes, compared to the previous 24-hour revisit rates.
* **Funding Climate:** The European defense-tech sector has seen a "transformational shift" since the onset of the war in Ukraine. Previously "unimaginable" funding is now available as Europe seeks to remilitarize and bypass traditional supply chain bottlenecks.

### Investment Discipline in Deep Tech
* **Valuation Skepticism:** While acknowledging that some AI companies are overvalued, Fabricio Bloisi argued that AI is not a bubble, comparing the current era to the internet in 2000—a foundational shift that will redefine all business processes over the next decade.
* **Founder Mentality:**
    * **Capital Management:** Founders warned against "spending sprees" after large raises. Rafal Modrzewski noted that while deep tech requires massive capital, discipline is required to avoid "toxic traits" associated with over-funding.
    * **First Principles:** Building successful deep-tech companies requires solving fundamental science problems rather than chasing financial exits.

> "AI over 10 years is going to change how everything works. It's like discussing in 2000, 'is the internet overvalued?' Probably some companies are, but in five to 10 years, everyone is going to run all their process through AI." — **Fabricio Bloisi**

> "Our dream is just any robot, any task, one brain. We would like to be the brain... like Android governs smartphones, Skild would power the brain behind [all robots]." — **Deepak Pathak**

> "We focus on disrupting the field of intelligence... we are really looking forward to deploy such large intelligence systems that nation-states would have enough information to know about threats before they happen." — **Rafal Modrzewski**
