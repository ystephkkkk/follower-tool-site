## How She Got Peter Thiel to Anchor Her First Fund (Without Pitching Him)

- Channel: How I Invest w/David Weisburd
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K4CL8dckwKw
- Published: 2026-05-11
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260511.html#video-K4CL8dckwKw
- Speakers: Keri Findley (Investor); David Weisburd (Host, How I Invest)

---

**TL;DR**
Keri Findley, founder of Tacora Capital, leverages a "ratings arbitrage" strategy to invest in highly illiquid credit assets that traditional institutions cannot hold, generating alpha by solving complex financing problems for venture-backed startups. Her reputation for rational, "age-adjusted" trading led Peter Thiel to anchor her first fund as his largest investment outside of Founders Fund, based on personal trust and her ability to structure unconventional risks.

### The Investment Philosophy: Risk vs. Illiquidity
*   **The Illiquidity Spectrum:** Findley differentiates her strategy by moving along the illiquidity spectrum rather than the risk spectrum. She targets assets that lack the ratings required by insurance companies or the liquidity needed by mutual funds.
*   **Ratings Arbitrage:** A core strategy involves "finding, creating, and analyzing" assets that the market misprices due to regulatory constraints.
    *   **2009 Mortgage Bond Case:** Findley bought triple-A bonds downgraded to "D" (default) for 60 cents on the dollar. By restructuring them into trusts, she could get 60% of the bond re-rated as "single-A" to sell to mutual funds at 95 cents, effectively acquiring the remaining high-yield "mez" bonds for nearly nothing.
*   **The Advantage of "No Scars":** Findley attributes her early success at Third Point to being a young trader during the 2008 crisis. Lacking the "battle scars" and emotional trauma of older traders, she could rationally analyze data (e.g., that 97% of mortgage defaults occur by month 60) to identify "fulcrum securities" others were too terrified to touch.

### Strategic Operations at Tacora Capital
*   **The "North Star" Portfolio:** Tacora maintains a diversified split across three primary sectors:
    *   **PropTech (1/3):** Real estate-related credit.
    *   **InsurTech (1/3):** Statutory capital and surplus notes for niche insurance products with low loss ratios.
    *   **FinTech (1/3):** Financial services, invoice purchasing, and receivables finance.
*   **Solving the Scaling Problem:** Tacora focuses on deals in the **$30 million range**, a "sweet spot" where they face less competition. Larger private credit firms (Ares, Apollo, Blackstone) have scaled so much they now require $150M+ deal sizes to move the needle, leaving the mid-market underserved.
*   **Venture Debt Alternative:** Tacora provides an alternative to dilutive equity. Instead of a Series A company giving away 20-30% of the business to fund a lending product, Tacora provides the warehouse line in exchange for a small equity piece (warrants) and interest.

### Relationship-Based Fundraising and the "Thiel Anchor"
*   **The Peter Thiel Partnership:** Findley secured Thiel as her anchor investor without a formal pitch. After building trust over several years through discussions on ideas and lending, Thiel offered to be her biggest investor, stating he was comfortable being the *only* investor if necessary.
*   **The Power of Trust:** Findley notes that Thiel invests more in the person and their ambition than the specific asset class. His family office’s proximity to her early work with SoFi allowed him to observe her "stewardship of capital" firsthand.
*   **Institutional Constraints:** Despite a stellar track record at Third Point, Findley found raising a first-time credit fund exceptionally difficult due to the high "quantum of capital" required to achieve necessary portfolio diversity (at least $100M+ to start).

### Risk Management and Counterparty Ethics
*   **Avoiding Hard Assets:** A key lesson from Fund 1 was to **never fund mobile equipment** (e.g., tractors, trucks). Findley discovered that when a company fails, physical assets in rural areas are easily stolen or hidden by disgruntled employees. She now focuses exclusively on financial assets like insurance notes and invoices.
*   **The "Ethical Counterparty" Rule:** Findley argues that credit "brings out the worst in people" during defaults. She prioritizes working with founders who have ethical VC board members (e.g., Andreessen Horowitz), as these VCs act as a "golden ticket" to ensure the company behaves professionally during restructuring to protect their own reputations.
*   **Portfolio Expectations:** Tacora constructs portfolios with roughly **15-21 deals**, expecting approximately one-third to wind down or default. The goal is to ensure the equity upside from the successful "winners" (which can return 9 or 10 figures) offsets any credit losses.

## Why Most VCs Misunderstand Peter Thiel’s Power Law

- Channel: How I Invest w/David Weisburd
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KwRdF4JExjA
- Published: 2026-04-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260423.html#video-KwRdF4JExjA
- Speakers: David Weisburd (Host, How I Invest)

---

**TL;DR:** High-performance venture capital is less about chasing "magical" unicorn frameworks and more about returning to the fundamentals of value investing: selecting singular, non-conforming founders, protecting their leadership at all costs, and focusing on the next 3-5x growth cycle rather than hindsight-driven power laws.

### The Foundations of Investment Selection
* **Moving Beyond the "Unicorn" Myth:** Eric Stromberg reflects on a pivotal conversation with Peter Thiel where Thiel simplified the Founders Fund mission: "We're just trying to make good investments." This shifted Stromberg’s focus from "magical" frameworks to business fundamentals like moats, business models, and market reality.
* **The "Idea Maze" and Value Investing:** 
    * **Analytical Screening:** The most efficient way to navigate the "idea maze" (a concept by Chris Dixon) is to think like a value investor—interrogating the business model and moats to understand the founder’s depth of knowledge.
    * **Dual Discovery:** This process simultaneously reveals if the founder is impressive and if the investor is someone the founder wants to work with.
* **AB Testing vs. First Principles:** 
    * **Local vs. Global Maxima:** While AB testing is a powerful tool for optimization, it typically leads to a "local maximum." 
    * **Category Definition:** To create a "power law" company (a category definer), a founder must operate from first principles, as there is no existing playbook to test against.

### The Founders Fund Doctrine: Idiosyncrasy and Loyalty
* **The "Never Fire a Founder" Rule:** A core tenet of Founders Fund is that they have never pushed a founder out of their own company. 
    * **Historical Context:** This stems from the "founding mythos" of PayPal, where board interference nearly destroyed the company during the merger of PayPal and X.com.
    * **Strategic Logic:** The most valuable companies (Nvidia, Meta) remain founder-led. Founders are required to "reinvent" the business continuously, a level of aggression professional CEOs rarely match.
* **Partner Idiosyncrasy:** The fund thrives by hiring partners with vastly different selection strategies:
    * **The Analyst:** Partners like Napoleon Ta use a data-driven, growth-fund style approach.
    * **The Authenticity Filter:** Partners like Brian Singerman may ignore spreadsheets entirely, focusing instead on whether a founder is "authentically driven" enough to stay for 15 years.
    * **The Hands-on Mentor:** Partners like Keith Rabois provide active mentorship and strategic foresight for founders who specifically seek that involvement.

### Decoding the "Great Founder" Archetype
* **The Tenacity Gap:** Most observers underestimate the level of persistence required by an order of magnitude. If a "normal" person thinks tenacity is a 10, the reality of a top-tier founder is a 100.
* **The "Neurodivergent" Filter:** Building a $100 billion company requires a level of non-conformity that is often found in neurodivergent individuals or "outsiders."
    * **Immigrant Success:** First-generation immigrants represent a massive percentage of venture-backed success because they are "outsiders" to society, allowing them to maintain the contrarianism necessary to succeed.
* **Mission vs. Pivot:** While some investors (like Mike Maples) prioritize founders who can build "movements" and pivot, Stromberg notes that his best investments (like Anduril or Crusoe Energy) rarely pivoted; they had a singular, mission-driven direction from "Day Zero."

### Market Dynamics and "Economic Invisibility"
* **Capital Concentration:** The venture market has become top-heavy. In 2010, the top 20 funds controlled 40% of the market; today, they control 75%.
* **The Growth Gap:** Massive $10 billion funds cannot justify $10 million checks because those investments cannot "return the fund." This creates a dislocation where high-quality companies become "economically invisible" to the largest platforms.
* **The Importance of Signal:** In oversubscribed "hot rounds," smaller investors like Stromberg’s Overlook Capital provide value through "credibility and conviction." Having a high-signal investor on the cap table acts as a mouthpiece for the company’s reputation during future recruiting and fundraising.
* **Bayesian Investing:** Savvy investors must avoid the ego trap of past decisions. An investment might be "wrong" at the Series A but "right" at the Series C based on new data. The "superpower" of top firms is the ability to ignore past misses and focus on the risk-reward ratio of the current day.

### Notable Quotes
> "You know what Eric, we're just trying to make good investments. It's really all we're trying to do here." — **Peter Thiel** (recited by Eric Stromberg)

> "When people think about what it takes to be a great founder, they're off at least by an order of magnitude of 10 in terms of persistence and tenacity... You have no chance as a normal human being with a normal mind." — **Eric Stromberg**

> "If you think I can run your business better than you can, I should not be investing in this company." — **Scott Nolan** (recited by Eric Stromberg)

> "The biggest mistake is not realizing you were wrong and then [not] investing at a later stage... What matters way more than convincing myself I was right is making the right decision today." — **Eric Stromberg**


## JD Vance’s Co-Founder on Space Defense, Hard Tech, and Solving Hard Problems

- Channel: How I Invest w/David Weisburd
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yvAj6LEAIYw
- Published: 2026-04-21
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260421.html#video-yvAj6LEAIYw
- Speakers: Colin Lintz (Co-Founder, Narya Capital); David Weisburd (Host, How I Invest)

---

**TL;DR**
Narya co-founder Colin advocates for a "regressive" venture capital strategy that returns to the industry’s roots of solving critical national problems in defense, space, and manufacturing through concentrated, first-principles investing rather than chasing speculative market trends.

### The Narya Investment Philosophy
* **"Regressive" Venture Capital:** Colin argues that to be progressive in the asset class, investors should return to the original mission of venture capital: helping the government, creating real GDP growth, and improving citizens' lives through advances in science and technology.
* **The Vance Vision:** Colin highlights J.D. Vance’s approach as a "first principles" investor capable of looking 5–7 years ahead.
    * **Focus Areas:** Advanced manufacturing, biotech, and defense in "off-the-beaten-path" geographies.
* **Concentrated Bets:** The firm maintains a highly concentrated portfolio of approximately 12 companies per fund.
    * **Strategy:** They focus on 3–4 "big bets" where they double and triple down, relying on a mesh network of strategic LPs and engineers for technical due diligence.
* **Avoiding Mimetic Behavior:** Narya intentionally avoids "busy" or trendy sectors like Crypto, NFTs, or Large Language Model (LLM) platforms. Instead, they seek out "hidden-in-plain-sight" problems that have been overlooked by the broader VC community.

### Hard Tech and the "Space Economy"
* **Business Model Innovation:** In "bits and atoms" businesses (hardware, pharma, energy), technical success is insufficient. Colin emphasizes that the key to venture-scale returns is finding founders who can translate complex tech into a business model with real gross margins.
* **Space as a Contested Domain:** Colin views space as the next "operating system for modern power," comparable to historical air and maritime rights.
    * **True Anomaly:** A key portfolio company founded by ex-Space Force/Military personnel focusing on Space Domain Awareness (SDA).
    * **Strategic Deterrence:** The goal is protecting critical orbital assets (comms and military systems) from adversaries like China, who are advancing rapidly in offensive space capabilities.
* **Cost Catalysts:** The transition from theoretical to practical space projects was driven by an order-of-magnitude reduction in launch costs, primarily led by SpaceX, allowing for the proliferation of small-sat constellations.

### Operational Discipline and Scaling
* **The Danger of "War Chests":** Colin warns against founders raising excessive capital to sit on the balance sheet.
    * **Distraction Risk:** Large cash reserves often lead to "feature creep," hiring bloat, and chasing "shiny objects" that distract from the core mission.
    * **The "Lean" Advantage:** Starving a balance sheet can create a necessary sense of urgency. Innovation should happen leanly to avoid the trap of sunk-cost fallacies in secondary products.
* **Product Sequencing:** Founders should only move to "Product Two" when they have reached market saturation or completed the mission of "Product One." Colin advises that having senior leadership focus on two products simultaneously is where companies typically "go sideways."

### Founder Archetypes and Market Trends
* **Industrial Acumen:** Unlike the stereotypical 22-year-old Silicon Valley founder, Narya often backs founders in their early 30s who possess deep "insider knowledge" of industrial sectors, such as tooling or manufacturing, often gained outside of major tech hubs.
* **The Holdco Model:** Colin highlights **Julie Bush (ex-Palantir)** and her approach to defense. Instead of chasing multi-billion-dollar "sexy" government programs, her model uses a "hub and spoke" structure to solve smaller, shorter-term government micro-projects at scale.
* **Depolarizing Through Building:** Colin rejects the concept of a "parallel economy" based on ideology.
    * **Ideology-Neutral Success:** The best founders are often apolitical, focusing on solving energy, health, or education problems that benefit all citizens regardless of political affiliation.

> "To be progressive in the asset class, you need to be regressive and return the asset class to its inception... to help our government, to help our companies create real GDP growth at scale, and to help our citizens." — Colin

> "Space is this next operating system for modern power... What does it mean to be sovereign in space? How do we create systems that work with our allies? These are the complicated questions we are delving into." — Colin

> "Signal to noise is not measured by how loud you are. If a founder is spending a lot of time on X or any other platform, they're not building. The best founders tend to be less active there." — Colin
