## Catching Up With Power Investors Howard Marks and Bruce Flatt | At Barron's

- Speakers: Bruce Flatt (CEO, Brookfield Corporation); Howard Marks (Co-chairman, Oaktree Capital Management)

**TL;DR**
The integration of Oaktree Capital Management under Brookfield Corporation combines massive global scale in infrastructure with specialized credit expertise, positioning both firms to navigate a highly optimistic market by prioritizing long-term capital compounding over short-term trading.

### The Strategic Integration of Brookfield and Oaktree

* **The Acquisition Path:** Brookfield initiated its partnership with Oaktree in 2019 by buying a majority stake and completed the acquisition of the entire company seven years later.
  * **Decentralized Model:** To preserve investment performance, Brookfield left Oaktree’s investment teams decentralized and retained the Oaktree brand name.
* **Mutual Benefits:** 
  * **Scale in Credit:** The transaction provides Brookfield's clients with institutional-scale credit solutions, filling a key capability gap in its infrastructure and renewable power offerings.
  * **Balance Sheet Alignment:** Oaktree gained access to Brookfield’s proprietary capital, allowing Oaktree to co-invest more heavily in its own strategies alongside clients.
* **Corporate Scale and Performance:** Brookfield manages $1.3 trillion in total assets. On a de-consolidated basis, Brookfield Corporation holds $400 billion in assets (including $160 billion in equity capital) and has achieved an annualized compounded return of nearly 19% over the past 30 years.

### Market Outlook: Navigating Optimism and Credit Cycles

* **Equities and Market Sentiment:** Howard Marks notes that the S&P 500 has more than doubled since October 1, 2022 (when the Federal Reserve pivoted to a more dovish stance), driven by high optimism.
  * **The Need for Caution:** Marks warns that high optimism leads to high prices and lower relative future returns over the intermediate term (2–4 years). Investors must actively prepare for less optimistic times.
  * **Bifurcated Valuations:** Bruce Flatt views the public market as split; while tech and high-profile AI IPOs trade at massive multiples, many high-quality, cash-flowing traditional businesses trade at low multiples, presenting attractive take-private opportunities.
* **Credit Markets and Interest Rates:** The credit market has experienced nearly 17 years of strong, uninterrupted progress, creating a generous borrowing environment with diminished skepticism.
  * **Federal Reserve Policy:** Marks advises against cutting interest rates, arguing that the economy is performing well and does not need stimulative measures that could rekindle inflation.
  * **Software Debt Concerns:** While the market is experiencing a "spike in worry" regarding software-related private credit, Marks believes actual default risks are overstated and that companies generally remain highly capable of servicing their debt.

### AI Infrastructure and Global Investments

* **The Reality of AI Buildouts:** While some fear an artificial intelligence bubble, Flatt notes that the physical constraints of building AI factories, data centers, and power grid connections prevent supply from keeping pace with demand.
  * **Capital Requirements:** Building these facilities requires massive capital commitments ranging from $20 billion to $250 billion. 
  * **Contract Security:** Brookfield mitigates downside risk by securing 25-year contracts with highly rated corporate and sovereign entities.
* **US-Canada Relations and Global Scale:** Brookfield has approximately $600 billion invested in the United States, making it one of the country's largest private investors. 
  * **Local Investment Strategy:** Brookfield operates as a local investor in 40 countries, relying on localized teams and strong national rules of law rather than high-level macro trade agreements.
  * **Leadership:** Flatt expressed strong support for Mark Carney (former Brookfield executive and current Canadian economic advisor/politician), praising his leadership capabilities in addressing Canada's economic issues.

### Reclaiming the "Ownership Mentality"

* **The Evolution of Private Markets:** Marks highlights the explosive growth of private credit—specifically direct lending for mid-sized buyouts—which grew from virtually nothing 15 years ago to a $1.7 trillion market today.
* **Shift Away from Financial Engineering:** In past decades, falling interest rates made it easy to generate returns through leverage and financial engineering. 
* **The Four Pillars of Real Returns:** Marks outlines that genuine, superior returns in the current macro environment must be driven by:
  * Buying assets for less than they are worth.
  * Applying the correct financial structure and leverage.
  * Adding intrinsic operational value to the business.
  * Navigating the asset to a premium valuation over the purchase price.

> "Investments aren't about getting rich overnight. They're about compounding wealth over very long periods of time." — Bruce Flatt

> "When [the market is] driven by optimism, you get higher prices relative to intrinsic value, which implies lower returns relative to the average... with everything you do, some part of your body has to be saying, 'Yes, but how do we prepare for less optimistic times?'" — Howard Marks

> "In the period ahead, I think there'll be a greater emphasis on an ownership mentality, on buying things at reasonable prices and adding value." — Howard Marks


## Brookfield's Bruce Flatt discusses the global markets at the Milken Institute Global Conference

- Channel: Brookfield

- Speakers: Bruce Flatt (CEO, Brookfield Corporation); Michael Milken (Chairman, Milken Institute); Larry Fink (CEO, BlackRock)

---

**TL;DR**
The global economy is entering a $10 trillion "rewiring" phase driven by AI and digital infrastructure needs, shifting the investment landscape from traditional assets to long-term "continuity" assets managed by private capital firms rather than debt-constrained governments.

### The $10 Trillion Infrastructure "Rewiring"
*   **Scale of Investment:** Bruce Flatt (CEO, Brookfield) estimates a $10 trillion global expenditure over the next decade to build out power grids, AI factories, data centers, and fiber networks. 
*   **Infrastructure Pivot:** The global economy is transitioning from legacy networks (highways and railways) to a digital backbone. 50% of the assets Brookfield owns today did not exist as investable asset classes 15 years ago.
*   **Supply Shortages vs. AI Bubble:** Larry Fink (CEO, BlackRock) argues that we are in a supply shortage rather than a bubble. The U.S. is currently short on power, compute capacity, chips, and memory. 
*   **New Security Paradigms:** The rise of drone warfare is necessitating a reevaluation of physical security for infrastructure, potentially moving critical $50B–$75B data center investments underground to protect against domestic and international threats.

### Stability and the Private Capital Advantage
*   **Asset-Liability Matching:** Michael Milken highlighted that recent bank failures (Silicon Valley Bank, First Republic) resulted from mismatched liabilities. In contrast, both Brookfield and BlackRock report that less than 1% of their managed assets are redeemable on a quarterly basis.
*   **The Role of Private Markets:** With governments facing mounting deficits, the private sector is now the primary engine for massive infrastructure builds. Long-term private capital acts as a stable intermediary that the two-year capital structure of the banking system cannot support.
*   **Solution-Based Investing:** Large firms are moving away from selling "products" and toward "solutions." This includes bespoke vehicles, such as one mentioned by Flatt that locks in capital for 27 years to match long-tail institutional liabilities.

### Global Market Democratization and Retirement
*   **Shift to Self-Directed Retirement:** BlackRock is working with countries like Saudi Arabia and India to move away from government-sponsored pensions toward personal, defined-contribution plans.
*   **The Japanese Catalyst:** Fink noted that the Japanese stock market's recent surge was directly tied to the doubling of tax exemptions for "NISA" (self-directed retirement accounts), proving that domestic capital participation drives market valuation.
*   **Wages vs. Capital:** Fink warns that in an AI-driven world, wages will not keep pace with the growth of capital. Broad economic success now requires getting individual citizens to invest in capital markets rather than keeping money in low-interest bank accounts.

### The Strategy of Long-Term Compounding
*   **The "Miracle" of 12%:** Bruce Flatt emphasized that consistent 12% compounding over long periods is more valuable than chasing volatile 35% returns. The goal is to own "great businesses" for decades rather than churning assets.
*   **Consolidation of Winners:** The panel predicted a "K-shaped" economy where one or two winners dominate each industry due to the massive CAPEX required to integrate AI. 
*   **Institutional Scale:** The era of $100 billion funds is ending; the market is moving toward a landscape of 30+ funds managing over $1 trillion each. These "mega-funds" require "continuity assets" they can hold indefinitely to avoid the friction of constant asset churning.

### Notable Quotes

> "Having your money in a bank account is one of the worst financial decisions of a lifetime... we are not going to be able to broaden economic success only by wages because wages in this AI world are not going to grow as fast as the potential of the AI growth." — **Larry Fink, CEO of BlackRock**

> "The greatest miracle of investing, finance, in fact, almost everything, is compounding... if you can earn north of 12%, nothing else matters. Don't try to earn 35%." — **Bruce Flatt, CEO of Brookfield Corporation**
