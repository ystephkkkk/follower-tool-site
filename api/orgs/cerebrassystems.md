## Andrew Feldman on Building Cerebras and the Future of Chips | Ep. 57

#### TL;DR
Cerebras Co-Founder and CEO Andrew Feldman and Benchmark Partner Eric Vishria discuss the grueling 10-year journey of building the world's largest AI chip company, the Wafer Scale Engine, overcoming nearly fatal technical hurdles, and rethinking the semiconductor supply chain to tackle incumbent giants like Nvidia.

### The Genesis of Cerebras & The 100x Strategy
* **The 2016 Insight:** As a computer architect, Feldman recognized that AI workloads would be exceptionally compute-intensive, requiring a radically different architecture rather than remodeled existing chips.
* **The Goliath Strategy:** Feldman and Vishria argue that attacking an incumbent like Nvidia with a 10% or 2x better product is a losing game because incumbents possess massive margin flexibility to cut prices and bundle products.
* **The 100x Rule:**
  * **Scale timeline:** A new compute company takes at least 5 years to reach scale.
  * **Incumbent compounding:** If an incumbent doubles every year ($2^5 = 32\text{x}$ over 5 years), a new entrant must target a **100x to 500x performance multiple** to successfully intersect the market ahead of them.
* **Control Your Own Stack:** To achieve radical innovation, Cerebras brought chip, board, system, software, and API development entirely in-house.
  * **No Off-the-Shelf Parts:** Building a chip the size of a dinner plate meant no commercial catalog items (like heat sinks or packaging tools) existed; they had to pioneer world-class packaging through years of failure.

### The Valley of Death: Surviving the 18-Month Nightmare
* **The Boardroom Reality:** For 18 months, Cerebras faced a continuous existential hurdle: burning **$8 million a month** while walking into board meetings every six weeks with the exact same update: *"Still can't make it."*
* **The Mantra:** The team operated on a strict engineering discipline: **"Only new mistakes."** Every failure underwent rigorous analysis so they never repeated the same error twice.
* **The Breakthrough:** In **July 2019**, after months of shattering wafers in seconds, then minutes, a test run maintained a flat temperature in a makeshift lab with a hole drilled in the wall for ventilation—solving a problem that had baffled computer architecture for 75 years.

### Demystifying the Semiconductor Supply Chain
* **The Modern Pyramid (TSMC & ASML):** A cutting-edge fab costs **$40 to $50 billion**, takes 4 to 5 years to build, and utilizes football-field-sized facilities powered by continuous concrete pouring.
* **The Lithography Monopoly:** ASML builds the $500M+ photolithography machines (50–60 feet long, 20 feet high) that etch transistors into silicon wafers—representing a true technological monopoly.
* **The Geopolitical Bottleneck:** Three decades of domestic policy pushed fabs and packaging vendors (like Amcor and ASE) overseas. Feldman advocates for waiving local ordinances to build robust US domestic fab capacity with partners like TSMC, Samsung, and GlobalFoundries.

### Data Center Constraints & Energy Realities
* **The Real Estate Mismatch:** AI moves at the speed of software while data centers move at the speed of real estate, creating a permanent lag.
* **Power and Grid Hurdles:** 
  * The US grid relies on 1940s-1950s technology.
  * Data center expansion is driving intense innovation in backup power, including jet-engine power adaptations (from Boom), fuel cells (Bloom Energy), and renewed interest in nuclear power.
* **The Water Fallacy:** Data centers utilizing closed-loop water systems consume between **4x and 7x less water** than California almond growers.

### Scaling Throughput & Disaggregation
* **Speed Creates Markets:** Just as fast internet transformed Netflix from mailing DVDs into a movie studio, instant frontier intelligence unlocks entirely new applications.
* **Disaggregation Partnerships:** Cerebras leverages its architecture to partner with other chipmakers (such as AMD and AWS), achieving up to **5x additional throughput** while maintaining constant speeds by splitting inference workflows.

### Google I/O: Astronomical Scale, High Throughput, and Ambient Search

*   **Massive Infrastructure CapEx:** Google is scaling its annual CapEx to an estimated $180 billion to $190 billion (up 6x from $31 billion in 2022) to support a global network running more than 1 million custom TPUs. 
*   **Unparalleled Token Growth:** Google’s monthly token processing jumped 7x year-over-year to 3.2 quadrillion tokens, with model APIs now processing 19 billion tokens per minute. The consumer Gemini app has reached 900 million active monthly users.
*   **Gemini 3.5 Flash:** Launched as the new default model for Gemini and AI Search, Flash focuses heavily on raw speed (boasting 4x faster output than competing frontier models) and tool orchestration over raw frontier intelligence benchmarks.
*   **Gemini Omni & Deep Multimodality:** Led by DeepMind’s Demis Hassabis, GDM is pushing deep multimodal video and audio generation (such as generating custom, physics-accurate claymation explainer videos of protein folding), positioning Google as the sole US frontier lab heavily chasing video and physical-world simulations.
*   **Verification via SynthID:** To counter synthetic misinformation, Google's SynthID has watermarked over 100 billion images and 60,000 years of audio. Major AI platforms, including OpenAI, Kakao, and ElevenLabs, have officially adopted the standard.
*   **AI-Native Search & Universal Cart:** Google Search is transitioning to a persistent model where user-defined AI agents continuously crawl web, social, and forum data in the background (e.g., tracking rental listings). Additionally, a "Universal Cart" will allow users to seamlessly add products, track prices, and purchase items across YouTube, Gmail, Search, and partner merchants like Nike, Target, and Shopify.

### The Agentic Shift and Consumer Hardware Pivots

*   **Anti-gravity 2.0:** Rebranded from the Windsurf acquisition, Google's new standalone, agent-first developer environment automates complex software engineering tasks by orchestrating fleets of code agents in parallel, pointing toward a future where code is entirely abstracted away from human developers.
*   **Gemini Spark:** Powered by Gemini 3.5 Flash, Spark is Google’s always-on personal agent that runs 24/7 on dedicated Google Cloud virtual machines. It automates cross-product actions like scanning Gmail inboxes, managing Calendar invites, and building live trackers in Google Sheets.
*   **Samsung & Google Audio Glasses:** Arriving this fall, these glasses use Gemini to whisper private audio assistance directly into the user's ear. Featuring frames co-designed by Warby Parker and Gentle Monster, the glasses opt for an audio-only interface to preserve battery life and maintain real-world user presence.

### Cerebras Systems’ Historic $95B IPO and Wafer-Scale Innovation

*   **Historic Valuation & Market Debut:** Cerebras Systems completed the largest US tech IPO since Uber in 2019, raising $5.5 billion, closing up 68%, and reaching a $95 billion market cap.
*   **The Wafer-Scale Bet:** Co-founder and CEO Andrew Feldman detailed the company’s highly contrarian architecture: bypassing traditional GPUs to build a single dinner-plate-sized wafer (58x larger than normal chips) packed with 40 to 50 gigabytes of ultra-fast SRAM rather than slower HBM or DRAM.
*   **Performance Leaps:** By keeping model memory entirely on-wafer, Cerebras runs inference workloads up to 15x to 20x faster than traditional GPU setups, achieving 1,000 tokens per second on trillion-parameter open-source models like Llama-3-based architectures.
*   **Massive Enterprise Validation:** Cerebras scaled from selling 12 systems in Gen 1 to thousands in Gen 3. In December 2025, they signed a landmark multi-year deal with OpenAI worth over $20 billion, followed by a major deployment term sheet with AWS in March.
*   **The Domestic Manufacturing Bottleneck:** Feldman emphasized that building semiconductor fabs is a decades-long endeavor. He estimates Elon Musk’s proposed domestic "Terafab" to be a 15-to-20-year project due to the immense complexity of manufacturing and advanced packaging, which is currently concentrated in Taiwan and South Korea.

### Key AI Personnel Moves and Legal Milestones

*   **Andre Karpathy Joins Anthropic:** The OpenAI co-founder and former Tesla FSD head joined Anthropic's pre-training team to use Claude to accelerate its own pre-training research. Karpathy noted that researchers who remain outside of frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) inevitably experience "judgment drift" as the cutting edge becomes too opaque.
*   **OpenAI Defeats Musk Lawsuit:** A federal jury unanimously rejected Elon Musk’s breach-of-contract lawsuit against OpenAI in just two hours, ruling that Musk waited too long to file under the statute of limitations. Feldman characterized the legal battle as a "giant distraction" that diverted focus from constructive development.
