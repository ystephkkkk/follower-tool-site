## The AI Semiconductor Boom and What Could End It with Stacy Rasgon | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 63

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=imrBSvXepYI
- Published: 2026-06-08
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260608.html#video-imrBSvXepYI
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Stacy Rasgon (Semiconductor Analyst, Bernstein)

---

**TL;DR**
The semiconductor sector has transitioned from a niche AI boom into a broad infrastructure super-cycle, with growth now shifting toward "bottlenecks" like memory and CPUs as AI evolves from content generation to autonomous "agentic" tasks. While valuation multiples have actually contracted due to massive earnings outperformance, the long-term sustainability of the boom hinges on the ultimate return on investment (ROI) for hyperscalers and the constraints of the U.S. power grid.

### Market Dynamics and the "Bottleneck" Rotation
* **Sector Acceleration:** The semiconductor sector (SOX index) is up roughly 60% year-to-date, driven by earnings growth rather than multiple expansion. In many cases, P/E multiples have actually decreased because earnings revisions are outpacing stock price gains.
* **Playing Constraints:** Investors are rotating away from pure GPU names like Nvidia to play "bottlenecks" in the supply chain:
    * **Memory (Micron/SanDisk):** Pricing has surged. One notable example includes a company guiding to quarterly EPS higher than its entire stock price just 18 months ago.
    * **Semicap Equipment:** The "Big Five" (Applied Materials, Lam Research, KLA, ASML, and Tokyo Electron) control ~70% of the market. They are the essential providers for the "layer cake" manufacturing process of advanced chips.
* **Agentic AI Driving CPU Demand:** A major shift is occurring from "Generative AI" (creating content) to "Agentic AI" (executing real-world tasks like travel booking or coding). 
    * **Computational Shift:** While GPUs handle the models, the orchestration of sub-agents and virtual machines runs primarily on CPUs.
    * **Attach Rates:** Historically, data centers used an 8:1 or 4:1 GPU-to-CPU ratio; newer AI racks are moving toward 2:1 or even 1:1, significantly boosting the total addressable market (TAM) for CPU makers.

### Nvidia: The "Big Bang" Continues
* **Accelerating Growth:** Nvidia’s revenue growth recently accelerated from 65% to 85%. Despite its massive market cap, the stock has recently lagged some peers due to institutional "weighting" limits and the investor rotation into bottleneck plays.
* **Revenue Disclosure:** New disclosures show Nvidia’s data center revenue is split approximately 50/50 between "hyperscalers" (Google, Meta) and "non-hyperscalers" (enterprise, sovereign AI, and "neoclouds").
* **The Obsolescence Argument:** Addressing Michael Burry’s thesis that rapid GPU iterations make old hardware obsolete, Rasgon notes that rental prices for older generations (Hopper, Ampere) are actually *increasing* because global demand for compute is so high that even less-efficient chips remain highly profitable.

### Competitive Landscape: AMD, Intel, and Broadcom
* **AMD’s Market Share Surge:** In the x86 server chip market, AMD has moved from 0.1% revenue share 10 years ago to the low-to-mid 40% range today. They are successfully taking "oodles of share" from Intel and have secured major GPU deals with Meta and OpenAI.
* **Intel’s Recovery:** Despite products that Intel admits are not yet fully competitive, they are being "bailed out" by the sheer magnitude of market demand. New leadership under CEO Lipu is focused on executing a turnaround in manufacturing yields (18A and 14A processes) and leveraging their "packaging" IP.
* **Broadcom’s Infrastructure Play:** Beyond its AI networking dominance, Broadcom is a major player in "Custom AI" chips (ASICs), co-developing Google’s TPUs. Their software acquisition of VMware is now viewed as a critical infrastructure layer for AI rather than a traditional SaaS application.

### Potential Threats to the Boom
* **The ROI Question:** The primary "doomsday scenario" is a lack of return on investment. If hyperscalers (spending $130B–$220B+ annually) fail to see a profit motive, capex will be slashed. However, current proxies show "token usage" and revenue for AI startups like Anthropic are currently going vertical.
* **The Power Constraint:** The US electrical grid is a major physical bottleneck. Lead times for massive gas turbines are roughly three years. 
    * **Geopolitical Paradox:** The U.S. has the chips but lacks the immediate power infrastructure; China has the power (via rapid coal expansion) but lacks access to high-end chips due to sanctions.
* **The "Air Pocket":** Historically, hyperscalers go through "build and digest" cycles. A digestion phase could cause a temporary stock correction, even if the long-term AI thesis remains intact.

## Why Energy Stocks Are Down When They Should Be Up with Bob Brackett | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 60

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dFGIXqpyVzA
- Published: 2026-05-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260518.html#video-dFGIXqpyVzA
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Investor and Host); Bob Brackett (Energy and Mining Analyst, Bernstein)

---

**TL;DR**
The energy sector is currently defined by a "conundrum" where massive geopolitical supply shocks (specifically the Iranian threat to the Strait of Hormuz) haven’t translated into sustained stock rallies due to market belief in a quick resolution and a confusing forward curve. However, rising electricity demand for AI and data centers is creating a structural long-term growth story for natural gas and copper.

### The Oil Paradox and Geopolitical Risk
*   **The Strait of Hormuz "Black Swan":** Bob Brackett notes that Iran’s ability to block the Strait captures 20% of global oil flows and 20% of natural gas. This is a larger supply threat than the Russia-Ukraine war, yet oil prices remain below previous peaks due to "market hope" that the situation will be resolved quickly.
*   **The Inventory Drain:** Global inventories are being drained at a rate never seen before to compensate for supply shortfalls. This creates a "coiled spring" effect for prices as governments will eventually need to restock strategic reserves.
*   **The Forward Curve Confusion:** The market is currently in **Backwardation** (future prices are lower than spot prices). 
    *   Investors often mistakenly use the forward curve as a price predictor, leading them to fear negative earnings revisions and avoid stocks like ExxonMobil despite strong current cash flows.
*   **OPEC Instability:** The UAE's exit from the OPEC cartel stems from a desire to grow their prolific onshore resources, which was restricted by quotas. This signaling suggests a weakening of the cartel's long-term ability to control prices.

### Natural Gas: The AI and Electricity Growth Story
*   **Surging Electricity Demand:** After 15 years of flat demand, US electricity needs are growing at 3-4% annually, driven by AI data centers and robotics. 
    *   **Scale:** A single one-gigawatt data center requires as much energy as a city of one million people.
*   **The Bridge Fuel:** While nuclear is 24/7 "green," it has long lead times. Natural gas turbines are the only short-term solution to meet massive AI-driven power needs.
*   **Investment Picks:** Brackett favors "pure-play" dry gas producers over diversified majors for those looking to play the gas surge.
    *   **EQT (EQT) and Expand Energy (EXE):** These are the largest US gas producers and benefit most directly from rising Henry Hub prices.
*   **Negative Pricing in the Permian:** In West Texas, natural gas is currently a "waste product" with negative prices because oil drillers have no pipe capacity to move the associated gas, forcing them to pay others to take it away.

### Mining: Copper Scarcity vs. Lithium Disruption
*   **The Copper Tailwind:** Unlike oil (which benefited from fracking technology), there is no new technology to "frack" for copper. 
    *   **Freeport (FCX):** Identified as the "copper champion" due to its massive assets in Indonesia and the US.
    *   **Demand Drivers:** EVs and grid infrastructure are "copper-hungry." One EV contains as much copper as an entire house.
*   **Lithium’s Technological Shift:** Lithium is moving from traditional hard-rock mining to "oilfield brines."
    *   **The Arkansas/Appalachia Resource:** Companies like Exxon and Equinor are exploring extracting lithium from the salty water produced during oil and gas drilling. This "low-cost" chemical process could disrupt traditional lithium miners.

### Strategic Midstream and The "Toll-Booth" Model
*   **Cheniere Energy (LNG):** Described as a "refrigerator" model. They buy US gas, liquefy it, and sell it globally on 20-year "take-or-pay" contracts.
    *   **Safety:** It is a pass-through model that removes commodity price risk, acting as a "toll-booth" for global energy.
    *   **Valuation:** Currently trades at an 8% free cash flow yield, which Brackett views as undervalued compared to other S&P 500 midstream companies.
*   **The Majors (Exxon/Chevron):** These remain the "gold standard" for project management. Brackett notes their 3% dividends are "untouchable" and were maintained even when oil prices went negative during COVID, unlike European peers (Shell, BP) which cut distributions.
