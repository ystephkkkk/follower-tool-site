## Democracies ignore the cost of living crisis at their peril - economist Isabella Weber

#### TL;DR
Economic policies that fail to protect people from cost-of-living crises and soaring essential prices fuel political extremism and threaten the survival of democracies. Economist Isabella Weber argues that "anti-fascist economics" requires putting a floor under human livelihoods by ensuring the affordability of basic essentials and reining in extreme corporate wealth concentration.

### The Cost-of-Living Crisis and the Threat to Democracy
* **Democratic Disillusionment:** **Isabella Weber** argues that when elites tell citizens that democracy is the greatest political system but claim elected representatives can do nothing about basic needs, people inevitably lose trust in democratic institutions.
* **Protests as a Warning:** Street protests over the cost of living (such as recent movements in France and widespread European unrest) signal that the status quo is intolerable and must be met with a democratic way out rather than allowing the extreme right to exploit the frustration.
* **Casual Acceptance of Suffering:** Mainstream economic management—specifically raising interest rates to cool labor markets—amounts to a "casual acceptance of existential pain" by deliberately threatening livelihoods, jobs, and communities.

### Redesigning Policy: Floors, Ceilings, and Essentials
* **Defining Anti-Fascist Economics:** The framework focuses on economic policies designed to prevent the rise of the extreme right by putting a floor under people's livelihoods and a ceiling on extreme concentrations of private and corporate wealth.
* **The Primacy of Essentials:** Following principles similar to postwar constitutions and thinkers like Harold Laski, **Isabella Weber** emphasizes that access to life's essentials—energy, housing, food, transportation, communication, and care—must be publicly managed or secured because private, exclusive control over them creates an inherent tension with democracy.
* **Sellers' Inflation:** The post-pandemic inflation wave was primarily driven by supply shocks (energy, shipping, computer chips) and corporate margin protection rather than simple excess demand. Corporate unit profits per national income in the US rose from **12% to 14%** pre-inflation to **18%** post-shock (and jumped further under Trump).
* **Targeted Emergency Tools:** **Isabella Weber** defends the selective, emergency use of price controls and windfall profit taxes to align corporate incentives with societal survival, citing an IMF study showing price controls were a dominant global response to recent energy crises.

### Rethinking Monetary Policy and Central Banking
* **Challenging Central Bank Orthodoxy:** **Isabella Weber** asserts that monetary policy is too important to be insulated from democratic control. Relying solely on interest rate hikes when facing supply-driven inflation creates structural failures.
* **The Dual Mandate History:** **Isabella Weber** highlights the 1970s push for full employment—advocated by figures like Coretta Scott King and backed by the Humphrey-Hawkins Act—proving that central bank mandates can and should incorporate employment goals alongside price stability.
* **Countervailing Powers:** Drawing on John Kenneth Galbraith and Karl Polanyi, **Isabella Weber** argues that market fundamentalism carries a "fascist virus" that imposes market order through state violence during crises; democracies must actively build countervailing powers instead.

### Managing Commodities, Food Security, and Trade
* **Reviving Public Buffer Stocks:** Noting rare historic agreement between Keynes and Hayek regarding commodity volatility, **Isabella Weber** proposes public buffer stocks and joint procurement entities (modeled on historical concepts like the World Food Board) to stabilize volatile food and energy staples.
* **Regional and Global Applications:** A G20 task force report for South Africa demonstrated how regional buffer stocks (for crops like cassava) and joint import procurement (for wheat, rice, and corn) can build resilience in Africa while incentivizing climate-friendly, water-efficient crop production.
* **Managed Trade vs. Free Trade:** **Isabella Weber** rejects the dichotomy between free trade and nationalist protectionism, noting that trade has always been managed via complex legal agreements. Trade policies should instead be restructured to prioritize working people and global climate goals.
