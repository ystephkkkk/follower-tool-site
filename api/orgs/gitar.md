## Redesigning How Software Gets Built With AI Agents — Sonar & McKinsey Panel

- Channel: AI Engineer
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XqF-IFHBCkM
- Published: 2026-10-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261007.html#video-XqF-IFHBCkM

### TL;DR
While 80-90% of companies have deployed AI agents, fewer than a third see measurable business impact because they fail to adapt their processes, tooling, and—most importantly—their people and roles. Research by Sonar and McKinsey reveals that scaling agentic workflows requires moving past isolated lighthouse teams, making implicit engineering knowledge explicit, and rebuilding developer trust through a gradual progression from AI reviews to auto-merge.

### 1. The Scaling Gap and Three Pillars of Maturity
* **The Adoption vs. Impact Mismatch:** **Prakhar (Moderator / McKinsey)**
  * **Widespread deployment:** 80-90% of organizations report deploying AI or agents in some format across their workflows.
  * **Low scale and impact:** Fewer than 33% of companies have scaled agents end-to-end or achieved measurable business results.
  * **Maturity Horizons:** Most organizations currently sit in Horizon 2 (human-guided and validated agents executing specific tasks), while very few reach Horizon 3 (fully automated end-to-end workflows with human oversight).
* **The Three Core Success Factors:** **Prakhar (Moderator / McKinsey)**
  * **Process workflow redesign:** Shifting away from traditional SDLCs to orchestrating end-to-end agentic workflows (e.g., guide, verify, solve).
  * **Tooling infrastructure:** Ensuring the presence of high-quality agents, robust harnesses, and adequate context layers.
  * **People and operating model (most overlooked):** Recognizing that traditional boundaries between software engineers, product managers, and designers are blurring, requiring a complete remodeling of organizational roles.

### 2. Organizational Transformation & The Limitations of Role Modeling
* **The Lighthouse Team Bottleneck:** **Tariq Shaukat (CEO, Sonar)**
  * **Initial expectations:** Tariq assumed that establishing lighthouse teams to model best practices would allow other teams to adopt new habits rapidly through observation.
  * **The reality:** Role modeling alone is insufficient; teams struggle to adopt the required new ways of thinking and understanding *why* they are using agents.
* **Objective-Driven Adoption:** **Tariq Shaukat (CEO, Sonar)**
  * **Avoiding token maxing:** Sonar's internal transformation (leveraging their 18-year-old open-source engineering roots) focused strictly on generating real value rather than vanity metrics.
  * **Deliberate orchestration:** Implementing the "guide, verify, solve" model internally and ensuring clear, measurable objectives from the start.

### 3. Bridging Startup Agility with Enterprise Platform Teams
* **Structural Advantages of Large Enterprises:** **Ali-Reza Adl-Tabatabai (CEO, Gitar / Sonar)**
  * **Pre-existing platform orgs:** Large companies possess a structural advantage via established platform organizations (such as those at Uber, Google, and Facebook) responsible for driving standards and developer productivity.
  * **Key platform principles:** 
    * Establishing baseline telemetry and metrics (tracking PR throughput, cycle time, quality, error rates, and developer sentiment).
    * Optimizing for the entire life cycle rather than isolated tools.
    * Protecting developer time, keeping them in the flow, and offloading grunt work to agents.
* **The Rise of the Generalist:** **Tariq Shaukat (CEO, Sonar)**
  * **Pushback against small teams:** Organizational resistance to smaller atomic units usually stems from hyper-specialized silos (e.g., front-end vs. back-end engineers).
  * **The era of the generalist:** The future belongs to software engineering generalists rather than narrow specialists, as agents handle deep siloed coding tasks.

### 4. Evolving Skill Sets: Explicit Knowledge & Critical Thinking
* **Making Implicit Knowledge Explicit:** **Tariq Shaukat (CEO, Sonar)**
  * **The software design shift:** Traditional software development relies heavily on unspoken, internal knowledge (e.g., team preferences, preferred dependencies, coding styles).
  * **Explicit specifications:** Developers must learn to externalize and articulate these implicit rules so agents and PRD evaluation agents can utilize them effectively.
* **Mastering Critical Thinking:** **Tariq Shaukat (CEO, Sonar)**
  * **Plausible over-confidence:** AI models always output high-conviction, plausible answers with zero self-doubt or hesitation.
  * **The human role as auditor:** Engineers must act like managers of interns—knowing exactly where to poke, what questions to ask, and how to interrogate AI output for flaws.

### 5. Building Trust: The Step-by-Step Automation Ladder
* **The Trust Progression Curve:** **Ali-Reza Adl-Tabatabai (CEO, Gitar / Sonar)**
  * **Step 1 - AI Reviews & CI Analysis:** Users start by relying on AI-generated code reviews and CI failure analyses to uncover actual bugs.
  * **Step 2 - Blocking on Issues:** High signal precision builds enough trust for teams to configure rules that block PRs when critical issues are found.
  * **Step 3 - Automated Fixes:** Developers enable auto-fix features, allowing the agent to generate PRs, fix CI failures, and iterate until builds turn green.
  * **Step 4 - Auto-Approval & Auto-Merge:** Teams establish rules to automatically approve non-critical code changes, eventually leaving agents to handle merge conflicts and complete fully autonomous end-to-end PR creation through merge.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Full-Scale Autonomy Timeline:** **Ali-Reza Adl-Tabatabai** predicts that within **the next year**, more and more teams—including those inside large enterprises—will rapidly approach fully autonomous, end-to-end PR creation through merge workflows.
