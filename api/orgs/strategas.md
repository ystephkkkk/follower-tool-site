## The AI Trade, Rising Rates, and Why the Market Is Still Standing | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 73

#### TL;DR
Despite a tumultuous mid-year environment marked by geopolitical conflict, rising interest rates, and massive AI capital expenditures, the U.S. stock market has proven remarkably resilient due to strong economic growth, robust underlying market internals, and a highly rotational tape.

### Market Resilience, Concentration, and the Macro Backdrop
* **Market Concentration:** **Jason Trennert** noted that market leadership remains highly concentrated, with the top 10 holdings of the S&P 500 making up 39% of the index, the tech sector accounting for 36%, and tech-adjacent companies pushing the total exposure past 50%.
* **Economic Strength:** **Jason Trennert** highlighted that PMIs indicate the U.S. economy is expanding rapidly, supported by the "one big beautiful bill" incentive allowing companies to write off the entire amount of capital investments made in the U.S.
* **Resilient Internals:** **Chris Verrone** pointed out that even as leading semis and hyperscalers experienced 30% to 50% corrections between mid-May and late July, market breadth expanded—moving from 50% of the S&P 500 above its 200-day moving average on June 2nd to 75% at the time of the episode.
* **China Demand Disconnect:** **Chris Verrone** observed a divergence where base metals like copper, zinc, and tin hit new highs due to AI infrastructure demand, while Chinese iron ore collapsed to 52-week lows, signaling weak domestic Chinese demand rather than a broader global slowdown.

### The Fed, Interest Rates, and Equity Tolerance
* **The New Fed Regime:** **Jason Trennert** and **Steve Eisman** discussed the shift under Kevin Warsh away from post-meeting press conferences and explicit forward guidance toward a pre-1994 style of monetary policy, ending the "free pass" of near-zero rates that disproportionately favored Wall Street over savers.
* **Competitive Bond Yields:** **Chris Verrone** and **Jason Trennert** admitted their previous threshold for a market-breaking interest rate was a 4.5% 10-year yield, but the market has shown it can absorb higher rates. Verrone compared the current environment to historical bubble periods like Japan in 1989 (JGB yields moved from 4% to 8%) and the Nasdaq in 1999 (10-year yields moved from 4% to 7%).
* **Bond Yield Range:** **Chris Verrone** noted a surprising statistic: over the 400 days since Trump's inauguration, the 10-year yield traded in an 85-basis-point range, making it one of the narrowest ranges in history.

### The AI Capex Debate and Software Software/HypersCALER Pressures
* **Hyperscaler Cash Flow and Financing:** **Jason Trennert** and **Steve Eisman** emphasized that hyperscalers (Meta, Amazon, Microsoft, and Google) are experiencing massive increases in capital intensity that cannot be sustained out of operating cash flow alone. 
    * **Google** raised $85 billion in equity for the first time since the early 2000s.
    * **Meta's** quarterly cash flow dropped to $785 million while expenses grew 55% against 28% revenue growth.
    * **Microsoft's** cash flow dropped around 25% year-over-year.
    * **Amazon** reported negative cash flow for the trailing 12 months.
* **LLM Ecosystem Risk:** **Jason Trennert** warned that 70% of AI hyperscaler revenue comes from just two major foundational model companies (OpenAI and Anthropic), creating systemic ecosystem risk if those startups encounter trouble.
* **Antitrust and Legal Distractions:** **Steve Eisman** and **Jason Trennert** drew parallels between current antitrust lawsuits against Meta and historical distractions faced by IBM (70s) and Microsoft (90s), noting that "the lawsuit is the punishment" and can distract management from core innovations.

### Global Markets: Japan vs. Europe
* **Japan Outperformance:** **Jason Trennert** and **Chris Verrone** expressed strong bullish views on Japan due to the emergence from decades of deflation, rising JGB yields reflecting healthy reflation, and a corporate governance shift away from market share toward Return on Equity (ROE) via mandatory delistings for low-performing book/ROE companies.
* **European Headwinds:** **Jason Trennert** characterized European economies as structurally un-dynamic and heavily reliant on struggling Chinese demand (notably autos and luxury bear markets), though European banks and select AI-adjacent infrastructure names (Schneider Electric, Siemens Energy) have reached new highs.

### Banking, Private Credit, and Sector Rotation
* **Global Banking Strength:** **Chris Verrone** highlighted that global bank stocks (U.S., European, and Japanese brokerages/banks) are trading at new highs despite regulatory shifts and private credit competition.
* **Private Credit as Regulatory Arbitrage:** **Jason Trennert** noted that private credit grew rapidly as a regulatory arbitrage following Dodd-Frank and Sarbanes-Oxley, which restricted traditional bank lending; however, the 7-year monetization timeframe in private equity and private credit is now creating liquidity constraints for institutional endowments (e.g., Notre Dame holding 50% in private/alternative assets).
* **Defensive Sector Recovery:** **Chris Verrone** noted that healthcare (pharma, biotech, life sciences like Thermo Fisher, Danaher, Illumina, and UnitedHealth) represents the strongest defensive leadership, while traditional consumer staples (Kraft Heinz, Hershey) are only recently attempting to bottom after a multi-year slump.

**TL;DR**
The 2026 midterm elections are trending toward a split government with Democrats favored to flip the House, while the Federal Reserve prepares for a "regime change" under Kevin Warsh focused on shrinking the $6 trillion balance sheet rather than immediate rate cuts.

### The 2026 Midterm Landscape
*   **Trump’s Approval Rating:** Currently sits at 38%. Clifton notes that Trump’s approval on the economy went negative for the first time in April 2025 following tariff proposals, turning a former political asset into a liability.
*   **The House of Representatives:** Democrats only need a net gain of three seats to take control. Clifton predicts they will likely succeed, transitioning the midterms into a referendum on the incumbent.
*   **The Senate Battle:** Republicans currently hold a 53-47 majority. Democrats need a net gain of four seats for total control (or three if they hold the VP tie-breaker). 
    *   **North Carolina:** Likely to flip to Democrats due to a popular former Democratic governor running.
    *   **Maine:** Market odds give Democrats a 60% chance to flip Susan Collins' seat, though her incumbency remains a formidable obstacle.
    *   **Ohio and Texas:** Expected to be highly competitive and expensive races, though Republicans remain slight favorites to hold.
    *   **The "Powell Factor":** Fed Chair Jerome Powell may delay his departure until after the midterms; if Democrats win the Senate, Trump would be forced to appoint a mainstream economist rather than a "MAGA" candidate for the role.

### The "Warsh Fed" and Monetary Policy
*   **Balance Sheet Focus:** Kevin Warsh is prioritizing shrinking the Fed’s $6 trillion balance sheet over interest rate adjustments. The goal is to reduce the money supply—which leads goods inflation by approximately 15 months—to eventually allow for lower rates.
*   **Privatizing Reserves:** The strategy involves "privatizing" the Fed balance sheet by easing bank regulations (e.g., liquidity coverage ratios), encouraging private banks to hold more Treasuries and absorb the liquidity currently provided by the Fed.
*   **Market Resilience:** Despite expectations shifting from rate cuts to a possible 25-basis-point hike, the market has remained resilient because the broader economy is stronger than anticipated.

### Tariffs and Fiscal Stimulus
*   **Headwind to Tailwind:** While tariffs were a market drag in early 2025, they have become a tailwind. The effective tariff rate dropped from 11% in October to 7% today.
*   **Net Fiscal Impact:** The U.S. has collected only $35 billion in net tariff revenue year-to-date while distributing $75 billion in corporate tax cuts and tariff refunds. 
*   **Currency Strategy:** The next phase of "Trump 2.0" trade policy likely involves an aggressive push to lower the value of the U.S. dollar to boost the impact of domestic investment incentives.

### The 2028 Presidential Outlook
*   **Democratic Frontrunners:** Gavin Newsom is the current betting favorite, followed by Pete Buttigieg, who is viewed as a "fighter" by the base. Other contenders include Governors Josh Shapiro (PA), Gretchen Whitmer (MI), and Wes Moore (MD).
*   **Republican Field:** 
    *   **J.D. Vance:** Has a significant head start because Trump is not restricting his campaign activities.
    *   **Marco Rubio:** Seen as a "superstar" who can articulate Trump’s foreign policy to the establishment, though his standing is tied to the success of the Iran conflict.
    *   **Ron DeSantis:** Remains highly active on national issues and is positioning himself for a populist run centered on affordability.

### Emerging Risks: AI Backlash and Regional War
*   **Data Centers as "The New Fracking":** Clifton warns of a growing political backlash against AI data centers due to their massive consumption of land, water, and electricity.
    *   **The Local Trade-off:** In Loudoun County, VA, data centers pay 40% of the education bill, saving average residents $2,500 a year in property taxes—a "public good" narrative the tech industry has yet to master.
*   **The Iran Conflict:** The war is proving unpopular with Trump’s base, who expected an "anti-war" presidency. 
    *   **Oil Prices:** The U.S. and China are both tapping reserves to keep gas below $5.00/gallon to prevent a political breaking point.
    *   **Defense Shift:** The conflict is accelerating a shift in investor preference toward "Defense Tech" (drones/software) over traditional large-cap hardware providers.

---

**TL;DR** 
The US stock market is experiencing historic concentration, with technology and semiconductors dominating the S&P 500 and capturing $27 billion in flows since March 30, while all other sectors saw net outflows. While Google and GE Vernova charts remain structurally strong, key mega-caps like Microsoft and Meta are flashing technical warning signs alongside a risky boom in retail-driven leveraged single-stock ETFs.

### The Technical Playbook: Strong vs. Weak Stock Charts

*   **Semiconductors (SOXX):** The absolute leader of the current market. 
    *   Semiconductors now make up 18% to 19% of the S&P 500, up from just 2% a decade ago. 
    *   While extremely overbought, the broader index fundamentally "lives and breathes" by this sector's daily performance.
*   **Strong Charts (Google & GE Vernova):**
    *   **GE Vernova (GEV):** Backed by the massive AI power-demand story, the chart shows an excellent upward trend and is currently undergoing healthy, buyable consolidation.
    *   **Google (Alphabet):** Possesses the strongest chart among the mega-cap tech giants. After becoming overbought, it is digesting gains constructively.
*   **Flashing Warning Signs (Meta, Microsoft, Oracle):**
    *   **Meta:** Currently looks more like a short. It has failed to make a new high in months, and its 200-day moving average is flattening and rolling over.
    *   **Microsoft:** Exhibiting a chart weaker than Meta's. It is retesting its spring lows and showing significant relative weakness.
    *   **Oracle:** The chart remains highly messy with a rolling-over 200-day moving average, making it a frustrating hold for investors.
*   **Neutral & Speculative Charts:**
    *   **Amazon:** Described as "middle of the road" with no clear directional technical edge.
    *   **Software ETF:** Showing a minor mean-reversion reprieve from deeply oversold levels, but it is not a structural buy. Todd Sohn recommends utilizing risk-limited options strategies rather than owning the underlying stocks.

### Extreme Concentration and the Diversification Illusion

*   **The Passive Indexing Trap:** Because the S&P 500 is market-cap weighted, passive index investors are deeply concentrated in just a few mega-caps (Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta). Tech is nearing 40% of the S&P 500, meaning indexing no longer provides true diversification.
*   **Extreme Flow Disparity:** Since the March 30 low, an extraordinary $27 billion has flowed specifically into tech ETFs. Meanwhile, all other sectors combined experienced a *negative* $4.4 billion in outflows.
*   **"Disguised" Factor ETFs:** Investors buying "Quality" or "Momentum" factor ETFs for diversification are actually buying the same mega-cap tech names. Because companies like Nvidia generate massive free cash flow, they heavily weight the "Quality" index, making it mirror the S&P 500.

### The Structural Shift: Out of Mutual Funds, Into ETFs

*   **The Death of Mutual Funds:** In a monumental secular shift, cumulative flows into mutual funds since 1984 are now net-negative, driven by a massive $5.1 trillion cumulative outflow from actively managed equity mutual funds. 
*   **ETF Market Dominance:** ETFs now represent 30% of daily US exchange volume on average, surging to 40% to 45% during high-stress market days as investors rapidly adjust exposures.
*   **Leveraged Retail Appetite:** Leveraged ETFs have grown into a $200 billion category. Retail investors are increasingly utilizing high-fee, leveraged single-stock ETFs (such as 2x Nvidia or Tesla), which did not exist prior to 2022. Because these funds must rebalance at the end of every day to reset leverage, they amplify daily market volatility.

### Sector Rotations: Haves, Have-Nots, and "Shriveling" Sectors

*   **The Demise of Consumer Staples:** Once the largest sector in the S&P 500 in the early 1990s at 19%, staples have shriveled to just 4.5% of the index. For scale, Nvidia's individual market cap is now double the size of the entire consumer staples sector.
*   **The Shrinking Healthcare Sector:** Healthcare has shrunk from 16% of the S&P 500 down to 8% (jokingly referred to as taking its own "GLP-1 weight loss drug"). While structurally set up for a potential mean reversion due to massive outflows, it remains highly frustrating to hold.
*   **Recessionary Discretionary:** Equal-weighted consumer discretionary indexes (excluding the massive market-cap distortions of Amazon and Tesla) show terrible performance, signaling that the average US consumer is under immense pressure.
*   **Thematic ETF Hazards:** Chasing single-theme ETFs (e.g., cannabis, space, clean energy) is historically detrimental. Thematic ETFs carry a brutal average three-year drawdown of 32%, and 70% of them exhibit a poor Sharpe ratio of less than 1.

## Jason Trennert on Populism, Policy & a Distorted Market System | The Real Eisman Playbook Episode 44

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7SBWYjtIKTI
- Published: 2026-02-02
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260202.html#video-7SBWYjtIKTI
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host & Senior Portfolio Manager, Neuberger Berman); Jason Trennert (Chairman & CEO, Strategas)

---

### TL;DR
Populism is driving a new economic phase marked by higher inflation and robust nominal growth, favoring hard assets and specific sectors like industrials. While the public market is expensive due to Fed backstops, significant risks are accumulating in opaque private markets, and new investment frontiers are emerging in AI and prediction markets.

### The Economic Landscape & Populist Drivers
-   **Strong 2026 Economic Outlook:** The economy is projected to be strong, making a recession unlikely.
    -   **Catalysts:** A "One Big Beautiful Bill" is expected to inject approximately **$150 billion** into the system through tax refunds and stimulus.
    -   **Supply-Side Boosts:** Provisions for increased capital spending allow immediate write-offs for building new plants.
    -   **National Events:** The 250th anniversary of the country and the World Cup in the US will further stimulate economic activity.
    -   **Forecast:** Earnings growth is anticipated at **14-15%**, and GDP at **3%** (plus **2.5%** inflation) yields **5.5%** nominal growth.
    -   **Sustainability Question:** Long-term sustainability beyond 2026-2027 will depend on the success of these capital investments.
-   **Populism's Deep Roots:** Populism has dominated the American political scene for the last decade, stemming from public dissatisfaction with social and economic experiments that haven't improved lives.
    -   **Trade & Jobs:** "Free trade" with countries fixing their currency (e.g., China) benefited big businesses but not average people, who prioritize jobs over cheaper goods.
    -   **Immigration:** Uncontrolled immigration is a significant concern for both economic (jobs) and cultural reasons.
    -   **Environmental Policy:** The environmental movement, particularly in Europe, "got out of hand" with unrealistic mandates (e.g., auto manufacturers backtracking on EV plans); fossil fuels remain essential for global energy access.
-   **Tariffs as Negotiation Tactics:** President Trump's tariff threats (e.g., **200%** on French wine) are viewed as part of a continuous "negotiation process."
    -   **Greenland & Rare Earths:** Greenland's rare earths are highlighted for their national security importance, especially for AI and economic growth, which the US could potentially acquire without purchase.
-   **Investment Implications of Populism:**
    -   **Higher Inflation:** Expect higher inflation due to increased government spending aimed at public appeasement.
    -   **Sector Focus:** Industrials, materials, and hard assets (gold, farmland) are expected to outperform as hedges against inflation.

### The Fed, Public Markets, and Investor Behavior
-   **Expensive Public Markets:** The market is currently "expensive" by historical standards, with valuations (P/E, P/B, P/CF) in the "top couple of percent" of all-time readings.
    -   **AI Justification:** While AI is a "game-changing technology" that offers some justification, other factors contributing to high valuations are considered "not so great."
-   **Negative Consequences of Quantitative Easing (QE):** An inflated Federal Reserve balance sheet and the Fed's consistent willingness to "backstop problems" (financial repression) are key drivers of current market expense.
    -   **Financial Engineering:** QE encourages companies to prioritize stock buybacks over real capital investments, as low interest rates make issuing bonds for buybacks more attractive than building plants.
    -   **Distorted Capital Formation:** Funds flow into companies that might not otherwise be viable, creating issues like private equity firms holding unsellable assets.
    -   **Enabling Fiscal Irresponsibility:** The Fed's role in monetizing debt allows politicians to "spend whatever they want," with a notable "lack of sophistication" in Washington regarding the national debt.
    -   **Moral Hazard:** Constant backstopping (e.g., Silicon Valley Bank) reduces market discipline, fostering a belief among investors that the Fed will intervene in a crisis.
-   **Institutional Investor Sentiment:** Institutional investors are "cautiously optimistic" and "fully invested," particularly in growth stocks, despite acknowledging underlying risks.
-   **Rise of Retail & Meme Stock Power:** The influence of retail investors has dramatically increased due to fewer publicly traded companies, online/commission-free brokerages, and rapid information sharing.
    -   **Consequence:** This environment can make investing "akin to gambling," often sidelining long-term investment strategies.

### Unseen Risks in Private Markets
-   **The Illusion of Low Volatility:** Institutions (e.g., pension funds, endowments) are attracted to private equity (PE) by its reported low volatility, largely because assets are "marked once a month" by the PE firms themselves ("marked to private equity").
    -   **Underlying Reality:** Despite the lack of daily price fluctuations, these highly leveraged private investments inherently carry "much higher" volatility, which simply remains unobserved.
-   **Drivers of PE Investment:**
    -   **Unrealistic Return Targets:** State pension plans, facing balanced budget requirements, often mandate "7%" returns when realistic projections might be "5%", pushing them into PE to bridge the gap and avoid benefit cuts or tax increases.
    -   **Uninformed Wealthy Investors:** Many affluent investors are unaware of the "extraordinary" fees associated with private equity.
-   **Current Challenges:**
    -   **Inter-firm Transactions:** A significant portion (**60-65%**) of all PE transactions are between PE firms themselves, raising questions about genuine value creation.
    -   **Stagnant Exits:** Roughly "30,000" companies acquired during periods of near-zero interest rates (2021-2022) are now "stuck" due to current higher rates and lower valuations. The IPO market, a traditional exit route, remains "still pretty poor."
    -   **Cash Flow Issues:** This lack of liquidity is causing "cash flow issues," leading to pension funds seeking to offload investments in secondary markets, typically "at a discount," indicating a "mispricing" of these assets.
    -   **Critique:** Private equity often "smacks of financial engineering" more than it genuinely "fixes" companies.

### Key Sector Opportunities & Emerging Trends
-   **Bullish on Financials:** The firm is "quite bullish" on the financials sector, anticipating benefits from expected Fed easing and potential deregulation under a Trump administration.
    -   **Regulatory Impact:** Previous bank regulations inadvertently spurred the growth of unregulated "private credit markets."
    -   **AI's Role:** AI is expected to "really help banks just to reduce cost."
    -   **Bank M&A Needed:** A wave of M&A among regional banks is critical, as escalating regulatory and technology costs disproportionately burden smaller institutions, favoring large players like JP Morgan (whose deposit share grew from **7% to 13%** pre- to post-crisis).
    -   **Cyber Threats:** Cyber threats are an "increasingly significant issue," with expensive cyber insurance forcing some banks to self-insure, further benefiting larger entities.
-   **AI as a Fundamental Shift:** AI is viewed as a "real thing," not a bubble, with "the sky's the limit" for its applications.
    -   **Arms Race Dynamics:** An "arms race" is underway among major public companies, driven by incentives to invest heavily, where CEOs face greater risk for *not* participating than for investing and potentially losing money.
    -   **Skepticism on LLM Scaling:** Some experts suggest that large language model (LLM) scaling may have "reached a dead end."
-   **Housing Market Insights:** While affordability is a major issue—wages have not kept pace with the "level of prices" since **2000**—there are "signs of life" in housing statistics.
    -   **Mortgage Rates:** Mortgage rates have declined "about 100 basis points" (now around **6% plus**), though supply issues persist.
-   **Jason Trenard's Investment Focus:**
    -   **Defense & Space:** Invests in defense stocks (aerospace and defense), particularly interested in the "space race idea" (e.g., anticipated SpaceX IPO).
    -   **Hard Assets:** Holds gold stocks (miners, GDXJ).
-   **Prediction Markets Convergence:** A growing trend (e.g., PolyMarket) where individuals can bet on various outcomes (financial, political).
    -   **Market Implications:** These markets could become deep enough for hedge funds to use for risk offsetting.
    -   **Regulation Urgency:** These markets are "ripe for insider trading" due to a lack of regulation, necessitating oversight.
