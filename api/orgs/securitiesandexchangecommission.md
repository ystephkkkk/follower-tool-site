## SEC Chair Paul Atkins testifies on oversight of the Securities and Exchange Commission — 2/12/26

- Speakers: Paul Atkins (Chair, Securities and Exchange Commission)

**TL;DR**
SEC Chair Paul Atkins signaled a fundamental shift in agency philosophy from "regulation by enforcement" to a growth-oriented framework focused on clear rule-making for digital assets, reducing the cost of public disclosures, and revitalizing the American IPO market.

### Digital Asset Regulatory Reform
*   **Transition from Enforcement to Clarity:** The SEC is moving away from a landscape of subpoenas and lawsuits toward a formal regulatory framework.
    *   **The Clarity Act:** Support for upcoming legislation to define regulatory boundaries and provide domestic certainty for crypto entrepreneurs.
    *   **Crypto Task Force:** Led by Commissioner Hester Peirce, the agency is developing a token taxonomy and federal framework to clarify obligations for builders.
*   **DeFi and Self-Custody:** The SEC is exploring how decentralized finance (DeFi) can integrate into markets without duplicative regulation.
    *   **On-Chain AML:** Exploration of standards like ERC-3643 to embed Anti-Money Laundering (AML) protections directly into smart contracts.
    *   **Property Rights:** Chair Atkins affirmed the right of individuals to maintain self-custody of their digital assets.
*   **Regulatory Harmonization:** Increased coordination with the CFTC to reduce the "regulatory dichotomy" and allow for the development of "super apps" that trade both digital and traditional assets.

### Revitalizing Public Markets and Capital Formation
*   **Addressing the "IPO Drought":** Chair Atkins noted a 40% decline in U.S. public companies since the mid-1990s and pledged to make IPOs more accessible to smaller firms.
    *   **Empowering Main Street in America Act:** Legislative support for tools that help small businesses grow and list publicly.
    *   **Market Concentration:** Concern that market cap is currently concentrated in a few massive firms, limiting diversification for average investors.
*   **Reforming Disclosures:** The SEC aims to fix a regime that currently costs public companies $2.7 billion annually in filing fees and legal costs.
    *   **Materiality Standard:** A return to anchoring disclosures in what is essential for a "reasonable investor," rather than "information overload" that obscures risks.
    *   **ESG Rollbacks:** Withdrawal of proposed ESG mandates for registered funds to focus on core financial missions.
*   **Accredited Investor Expansion:** Discussion on moving beyond wealth thresholds to allow knowledgeable professionals (e.g., finance professors) to participate in private markets.

### Enforcement Philosophy and Oversight Dissent
*   **"Cops on the Beat" vs. "Golden Age of Fraud":** Committee critics argued that slashing disclosures and dropping registration cases would allow bad actors to cheat workers and consumers.
    *   **Enforcement Data:** Chair Atkins attributed a delay in enforcement reporting to a recent 43-day government shutdown.
    *   **Refocusing on Fraud:** Atkins defended dropping certain cases as an end to "arbitrary enforcement," stating the agency is refocusing on "first principles": fraud, insider trading, and breaches of fiduciary duty.
*   **Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT) Reform:** Plans to reduce the CAT’s annual cost to under $100 million while addressing significant investor privacy and constitutional concerns.
*   **National Security and China:** Rigorous oversight remains for China-based issuers via the PCAOB; any firm failing to provide transparent audit access faces potential delisting.

### Technology and Market Infrastructure
*   **Artificial Intelligence:** Bipartisan support for "regulatory sandboxes" to test AI tools safely within the financial sector.
*   **Proxy Advisory Firms:** The SEC is reviewing the "duopoly" of foreign-owned proxy firms to ensure they do not exercise excessive leverage or drive ideologically motivated corporate decisions at the expense of investors.
*   **Agency Modernization:** Voluntary retirements and buyouts have led to a staffing reduction, which Atkins claims provides a path for younger, tech-savvy staff to rise within the agency.

## WATCH LIVE: SEC Chairman Paul Atkins Testifies Before Senate Banking Committee

- Speakers: Paul Atkins (Chair, Securities and Exchange Commission)

---

**TL;DR**
Chairman Paul Atkins is pivoting the SEC toward a pro-growth, "common sense" regulatory framework that prioritizes legislative clarity for digital assets and IPO reform, while facing sharp Democratic criticism over a significant decline in enforcement penalties and the dismissal of cases against high-profile firms.

### Digital Asset Regulation & "Project Crypto"
* **Shift from Enforcement to Legislation:** Atkins and Committee Chairman Tim Scott emphasized ending the "regulation by enforcement" era, favoring the **Clarity Act** to establish permanent "rules of the road" for digital assets.
    * **Project Crypto:** Atkins announced a joint initiative with CFTC Chairman Mike Selig to provide a "bridge" toward legislation, focusing on a **token taxonomy** to clarify regulatory obligations.
    * **Regulatory Certainty:** Atkins described the previous environment as "two fortresses [SEC/CFTC] on either side of a no-man's land," where innovators were caught in the crossfire.
* **Technical Modernization:** The SEC is exploring "innovation exemptions" and "sandboxes" to allow blockchain builders to test products under structured oversight.
    * **Self-Custody:** Atkins expressed support for the right of individuals to be custodians of their own digital assets, stating "property rights should determine where [assets] are held."

### Revitalizing Capital Markets: "Make IPOs Great Again"
* **Addressing Market Decline:** Atkins highlighted that the number of U.S. listed companies has fallen by **40%** since the 1990s, largely due to high compliance costs and complex disclosures.
* **Cost Reduction:** Public companies currently spend **$2.7 billion annually** on filing reports. Atkins aims to streamline these requirements, noting that 1,000-page "War and Peace" style disclosures often obscure rather than illuminate vital information.
* **Accredited Investor Reform:** Chairman Scott and Atkins discussed expanding the "accredited investor" definition to include those with financial knowledge (e.g., professors) rather than just those with high net worth.
    * **Goal:** To ensure that "it should not take wealth to create wealth" in American equity markets.
* **Proxy Advisory Oversight:** Atkins committed to reviewing the "duopoly" of foreign-owned proxy advisory firms (ISS and Glass Lewis), which critics argue act as de facto regulators by imposing ideological ESG requirements on corporations.

### Enforcement Trends and Contested Staffing
* **Plunging Monetary Penalties:** Ranking Member Elizabeth Warren and Senator Jack Reed cited a massive drop in SEC enforcement efficacy over the past year.
    * **Statistical Decline:** Senate data suggested disgorgement (money returned to victims) fell from **$6 billion to $108 million**, a **98% drop**.
    * **Staffing Reductions:** Reports indicate a **17% cut** in SEC staffing, with over 600 employees leaving the agency. Atkins attributed this to "voluntary retirements and buyouts" rather than forced cuts.
* **High-Profile Dismissals:** Democrats questioned the dismissal of cases against major crypto firms (Kraken, Coinbase, Gemini) and the withdrawal of charges against individuals granted clemency by President Trump (e.g., Trevor Milton, Devon Archer).
    * **Atkins’ Defense:** He argued the dismissals focused on "registration issues" rather than fraud, asserting that the SEC was previously attempting to apply ill-fitting securities laws to non-security assets.

### National Security and Emerging Technologies
* **China and Cross-Border Risk:** Atkins established a **cross-border task force** to monitor market manipulation by foreign issuers.
    * **Delisting Risk:** The SEC has suspended trading in 14 Asia-based issuers recently due to potential "pump and dump" schemes.
    * **Audit Integrity:** Concerns were raised regarding the reliability of financial reports from Chinese companies whose auditors answer to the CCP.
* **Artificial Intelligence (AI) Guardrails:** Senator Mark Warner raised concerns about "agentic AI" performing self-executing trades without human oversight.
    * **SEC Task Force:** Atkins confirmed the SEC has a specialized task force examining AI's role in market malfeasance and internal SEC tools for identifying fraud.

> "Disclosure documents of that length can do more to obscure than to illuminate... shortly after I left the SEC in the mid-1990s, there were more than 7,800 companies listed on US exchanges. By the time I returned as chairman, that figure had fallen by roughly 40 percent." — **Paul Atkins, SEC Chairman**

> "Chair Atkins is unleashing a golden age of fraud. The billionaires will cash in while millions of American businesses and families will end up paying the price... The Trump SEC has gutted oversight and enforcement tools." — **Elizabeth Warren, Ranking Member**

> "This means clarity instead of chaos, accountability instead of bureaucracy, and a government that serves the American people, not gets in their way... In the greatest country on the planet, it should not take wealth to create wealth." — **Tim Scott, Senate Banking Committee Chairman**
