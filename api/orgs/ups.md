## UPS CEO Carol Tomé Talks Company Growth | Bloomberg Talks

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MnW4R5skIc4
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261027.html#video-MnW4R5skIc4
- Speakers: Carol Tomé (CEO, UPS); Romaine Bostick (Host, Bloomberg)

---

- **TL;DR:** UPS reported solid third-quarter results with a return to U.S. volume growth, enabling the company to raise its full-year guidance and project profit and margin expansion heading into 2027 through disciplined, precision growth.

### Q3 Financial Results & 2027 Growth Outlook
- **Solid Third-Quarter Performance:** UPS beat several financial metrics and raised its full-year revenue and profit guidance following a return to volume growth among U.S. small-business (SMB) and enterprise customers. (Carol Tomé)
- **Holiday Peak Forecasts:** Based on customer forecasts, average daily volume is expected to jump approximately 25% from the third quarter to the fourth quarter during the peak holiday season. (Carol Tomé)
- **The 2027 Growth Formula:** Management outlined an "algebraic formula" for 2027 featuring volume growth and revenue-per-piece (RPP) growth outpacing cost-per-piece (CPP) growth, driving profit and margin expansion. (Carol Tomé)
- **Cost vs. Revenue Dynamics:** 
  - Domestically, revenue per piece grew 6.1% compared to a 5.5% increase in cost per piece. (Romaine Bostick / Carol Tomé)
  - Higher fuel costs and purchased-transportation expenses drove 100% of the cost-per-piece growth. (Carol Tomé)
  - Fuel surcharges accounted for roughly half of the RPP increase, while base rates and mix drove the other half. (Carol Tomé / Romaine Bostick)
  - The expected long-term gap between RPP and CPP growth is projected to range between 50 and 100 basis points. (Carol Tomé)

### Network Transformation & "Precision Growth"
- **Dispelling Volume Myths:** UPS is moving away from the belief that "all packages are created equal" and that volume solves all problems, opting instead for a leaner, more efficient network. (Carol Tomé)
- **Post-Amazon Pivot:** 
  - The plan initiated in early 2025 to reduce Amazon's share of UPS business is largely complete, positioning the company for a post-Amazon era of automated, efficient network growth. (Carol Tomé / Romaine Bostick)
  - The total addressable market is estimated at $923 billion from factory floor to front door, with the premium space valued at $535 billion globally and $150 billion in the U.S. (Carol Tomé)
- **Organizational Restructuring:** 
  - UPS recently reorganized to eliminate dual standards between U.S. and international operations, placing Nando Cesarone in charge of global operations to unify standards, automation, and service-level agreements. (Carol Tomé)
  - The company appointed two experienced P&L leaders to run the U.S. business and international healthcare/supply chain divisions. (Carol Tomé)

### Healthcare & Supply Chain Logistics Expansion
- **Complex Healthcare Leadership:** 
  - UPS has positioned itself as the global leader in complex healthcare logistics, with healthcare revenue projected to exceed $13 billion by the end of the year (up from under $6 billion six years ago). (Carol Tomé)
  - Assets include over 20 million square feet of dedicated healthcare logistics space across 273 buildings in 36 countries, featuring cold-chain capabilities, special labeling, and control towers. (Carol Tomé)
  - Profit margins in the healthcare delivery business sit in the low-to-mid teens. (Carol Tomé)
  - The global addressable market for complex healthcare logistics is approximately $90 billion, bolstered by direct-to-home pharmaceutical delivery for GLP-1 drugs. (Carol Tomé)
  - Authority in this space was earned during the pandemic by delivering 1 billion COVID-19 vaccines in the first year with 99.9% effectiveness and zero temperature excursions. (Carol Tomé)
- **Supply Chain Solutions & Forwarding:** 
  - The forwarding and logistics business saw 17% growth in the quarter, driven primarily by premium healthcare and data center construction. (Carol Tomé)
  - Data center construction materials imported from outside the U.S., warehoused, and delivered represent an addressable logistics market of over $15 billion. (Carol Tomé)

### Technology, Productivity & Labor Relations
- **Sensing vs. Scanning:** 
  - RFID sensing technology is being deployed across package cars, replacing manual loader scanning to eliminate misloads and drive unprecedented productivity. (Carol Tomé)
  - Productivity improvements are viewed as a continuous, virtuous cycle running through 2027 and beyond. (Carol Tomé)
- **Labor Strategy & Teamster Relations:** 
  - Part-time building roles experience high student turnover, which absorbs automation naturally without backfilling. (Carol Tomé)
  - Package drivers are the "brand people" and face of UPS, with leadership expressing no desire to automate or introduce autonomy to their roles. (Carol Tomé)
  - Carol Tomé plans to remain CEO through the negotiation of the next Teamsters contract, which expires on August 1, 2028, making her only the second CEO in company history (aside from the founder) to negotiate two union contracts. (Carol Tomé / Romaine Bostick)
  - Management noted lessons learned from the 2023 negotiations—specifically committing to being "very clear" on achieving a "win-win-win" desired outcome. (Carol Tomé)

### Global Trade & Macro Trends
- **Trade Lane Dynamics:** 
  - Inbound trade into the U.S. turned positive at the end of the quarter. (Carol Tomé)
  - Asia-to-Asia trade lanes grew over 8% during the quarter. (Carol Tomé)
  - Europe experienced a significant trade drop following the elimination of the de minimis exemption in July, though it is beginning to recover. (Carol Tomé)
  - Canadian border activity shows mild softness, which management characterizes as cyclical rather than structural. (Carol Tomé)

### Predictions & Forward Calls

- **Revenue Per Piece vs. Cost Per Piece Gap:** The gap between revenue-per-piece (RPP) and cost-per-piece (CPP) growth will range between 50 and 100 basis points. — Carol Tomé
- **Healthcare Addressable Market:** The addressable market for complex healthcare logistics globally is approximately $90 billion. — Carol Tomé
