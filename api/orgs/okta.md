## The AI Gold Rush: Models Are Shovels, Data Is the Gold | RAISE Summit 2026

- Channel: RAISE Summit
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NITnsKtHDu4
- Published: 2026-07-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260727.html#video-NITnsKtHDu4
- Speakers: Gareth Davies (CPO, Okta); Will Bryk (Co-Founder & CEO, Exa); Dmitry Panenkov (Founder, emma)

---

**TL;DR:** While AI models are commoditizing into a base layer like the Linux kernel, the true strategic value and bottleneck lie in accessing, governing, and securing proprietary data. Enterprises must navigate a landscape of shifting economics (multi-step agent workflows), data trust, and security challenges to successfully move AI from sandboxes to production.

### The Commoditization of Models vs. the Value of Data
* **Models as Commoditized Infrastructure:**
  * **Will Bryk (Exa):** Open-source and base models are already crossing a threshold of intelligence where they are "good enough" for most knowledge work, shifting real differentiation to the data products have access to.
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Compared foundation models to the Linux kernel—a trusted, shared commodity that everyone relies on, with true value created by building specialized layers on top.
* **The Scale of Available Data:**
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Noted that the web's data is only a fraction of the world's total data.
  * **Will Bryk (Exa):** Illustrated the scale gap by noting web data sits in the exabyte range (mostly video), while the world's total data is in zetabytes (roughly 1,000x more), representing an enormous, mostly locked pool of value.
* **Data Enrichment and Trust Barriers:**
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Compared raw enterprise data to unenriched uranium—it requires "reactors" (preparation, governance, and infrastructure) before it can safely be utilized.
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Emphasized that the biggest barrier to unlocking enterprise and consumer data is **trust**. People fear exposing their data and memory to external AI labs without choice, control, or fair compensation.

### Enterprise Adoption: Production vs. Sandbox Reality
* **Current Enterprise Maturity:**
  * **Alex Bouzari (DDN):** Argued that enterprise adoption is actively accelerating, citing real-world deployments where DDN serves as the data engine for Salesforce (increasing training productivity), RUI (in pharmaceuticals/genomics for drug discovery), financial services compliance, and sovereign government frameworks spanning departments from commerce to defense.
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Countered that broad enterprise adoption is still limited because much of enterprise data remains unstructured, unlabeled, and restricted by strict regulatory frameworks (especially in Europe).
* **The Shift to Agentic Workflows:**
  * **Gareth Davies (Okta):** Stressed that the industry is transitioning from simple chatbots to multi-step agents. While a standard chatbot makes one call, an agentic workflow makes dozens of calls, driving a massive increase in compute, data usage, and cost.

### Security, Governance, and Identity
* **The Security Gap:**
  * **Gareth Davies (Okta):** Highlighted that **48% of enterprises deploying AI in production are not securing their agents**, often leading developers to hard-code API keys and grant overprivileged static credentials. 
  * **Identity as Foundation:** Agentic security requires identity management to enforce granular access policies, govern reasoning chains, and monitor tool calls to internal and third-party MCP servers.
* **Sovereignty and Compliance:**
  * **Alex Bouzari (DDN):** Noted that sovereign AI implementations require air-gapping, multi-tenancy controls, and strict data localization to satisfy national and enterprise security requirements.
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Emphasized that legal compliance, data residency regulations, and secure data movement are non-negotiable pillars for enterprise and government AI architectures.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Will Bryk (Exa):** Predicts that the emerging agent economy will **"thousand-x over the next few years,"** enabling individuals and companies to monetize their proprietary data via agent-consumption APIs like ExaConnect.
* **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Predicts that Privacy-Enhanced Technologies—specifically **federated learning**—will reach maturity within the **next 5 years**, allowing organizations to collaborate and train models on distributed data without exposing or pooling base datasets.

## Okta's CEO is betting big on AI agent identity | Decoder

- Channel: Decoder with Nilay Patel
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UoWNdgZzwug
- Published: 2026-03-30
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260330.html#video-UoWNdgZzwug
- Speakers: Nilay Patel (Host, Decoder); Todd McKinnon (CEO, Okta)

---

**TL;DR**
Okta CEO Todd McKinnon is pivoting the company to become the primary identity and security layer for AI agents, betting that the "Agentic Enterprise" will expand the software market far beyond the current scope of human-centric SaaS.

### The "Sasspocalypse" and Market Expansion
* **Healthy Paranoia:** McKinnon acknowledges the threat of "vibe coding" (customers building their own tools with AI) but argues that enterprise software's true value lies in the "last mile"—thousands of integrations, 100% uptime, and a brand reputation that boards trust during a breach.
* **Capturing the Services Budget:** The total addressable market (TAM) is growing because AI allows companies to shift labor budgets (currently a ~$1.8T IT services market) into software budgets (~$1.2T).
* **The "Pie" Logic:** While some legacy SaaS tools may be disrupted, McKinnon believes the overall technology pie is expanding significantly, similar to the shifts seen during the internet and cloud revolutions.

### Strategy: The Blueprint for Agentic Identity
* **The New Primary Identity:** Okta is moving to treat AI agents as a distinct identity type—a hybrid between a human user and a system service. 
* **The Three Pillars of the Blueprint:**
    * **Onboarding & Inventory:** Providing enterprises with a centralized "system of record" for every agent running across their network, whether built internally or provided by vendors like Salesforce or Microsoft.
    * **Standardized Connections:** Moving toward an industry standard for how agents share permissions and access data across different software "silos."
    * **The "Kill Switch":** A security fail-safe that allows an admin to instantly revoke an agent’s access to all company data if it exhibits non-deterministic or "rogue" behavior.
* **The Revenue Opportunity:** McKinnon predicts that managing agentic identity could eventually become the largest single category in the $280B cybersecurity industry.

### Leadership and Organizational Agility
* **The "Change Quotient":** To adapt to 2026’s software landscape, McKinnon has pushed Okta to turn up its "dial of change." While a normal company might aim for 20% change and 80% stability, he is mandating a 60/40 split to force innovation.
* **Decision Framework:** 
    * **Filtering Decisions:** McKinnon focuses on "deciding which decisions to make," delegating smaller choices to maintain a high success rate on high-stakes strategic pivots.
    * **Trusting Instincts:** After years of slowing down for "expert advice," McKinnon is returning to founder-driven instincts to move faster in a volatile market.
* **Top-Down Mandates:** While preferring a bottom-up culture, McKinnon argues that massive technical shifts (like the pivot to AI agents) require top-down leadership to overcome organizational inertia.

### The Future of Infrastructure and Regulation
* **The "Unbundling" of Software:** Apps are being split into three layers: Data, Intelligence, and Interface. Okta aims to be the secure "connective tissue" between these layers.
* **Antitrust Concerns:** McKinnon suggests that if dominant players (like Microsoft or Amazon) use "monopolistic lock-in" to prevent third-party agents from accessing their data silos, government intervention or antitrust enforcement may be necessary to protect customer choice.
* **Fighting AI Fraud:** To counter AI-powered scams and bots, Okta is betting big on the digitization of "offline" identities, such as mobile driver’s licenses and biometric passports, as the ultimate verification layer for "real" humans.

> "Winning [the identity layer for AI agents] is job number one for our company... this new agent layer could be the biggest category in cyber by far." — **Todd McKinnon**

> "No one's ever maintained an agentically developed system for five years. No one's ever figured out how to make it scale... what’s going to happen is the software engineers are going to be figuring out how to make it work at scale." — **Todd McKinnon**

> "If the market doesn't work, if customers can't force the choice... then we have to rely on regulators to come in and fix it." — **Todd McKinnon**
