### Private Equity & Private Credit: The Software Overexposure Crisis
- **SaaS Buyout Boom (2017–2023):** 
  - Private equity firms focused heavily on SaaS companies due to predictable revenues, high margins, and high growth (90% of tech buyouts were SaaS; SaaS deal volume made up 25% of the total PE buyout market).
  - Specialist funds like Thomas Bravo, Vista Equity, and Silver Lake accounted for **60%** of total SaaS buyouts from 2017 to 2023.
  - At the 2021 peak, U.S. PE buyouts of tech companies hit over **$284 billion** (**$256 billion** for software), fueled by an exceptionally low-rate environment and heavy leverage.
- **Vulnerability of Major Software Deals:**
  - **Citrix Systems:** Buyout led by Vista Equity Partners for **$16.5 billion**; funded with **$8.55 billion** in debt (**52%** leverage). Vulnerable to AI.
  - **McCafee Corp:** Buyout led by Advent International and Talmera for **$14 billion**; funded with **$8.96 billion** in debt (**64%** leverage). Cybersecurity is better positioned to survive.
  - **Zendesk:** Buyout led by Hellman and Friedman and Permira for **$10.2 billion**; funded with **$5 billion** in private credit led by Blackstone Credit (**49%** leverage). Vulnerable to AI.
  - **Proofpoint:** Buyout led by Thomas Bravo for **$12.3 billion**; funded with **$4.6 billion** in first-lien loans (**37%** leverage). Cybersecurity insulation.
  - **Anaplan:** Buyout led by Thomas Bravo for **$10.4 billion**; funded with **$2.5 billion** in debt (**24%** leverage) via lenders including Blue Owl, Gollup Capital, Blackstone Credit, and Apollo Global. Vulnerable to AI.
- **Broader Sector Valuation Impact:** 
  - On average, broader software buyouts were financed with roughly **50% debt**.
  - Adobe serves as a public market proxy: it peaked at **$630** in February 2024 and dropped to **$240** (a **62%** decline), reflecting widespread valuation multiple compression due to AI disruption fears.

### The 2027 Refinancing Wall and Equity Wipeout Mechanics
- **Corporate Debt Structure vs. Mortgages:** Unlike mortgages that amortize principal, corporate loans require full refinancing at maturity, leaving companies exposed to variable-rate debt expenses.
- **The Mathematical Problem:** 
  - A hypothetical 2022 buyout of a $1 billion software company funded with 50% variable private credit debt ($500M debt, $500M equity) facing a 50% valuation drop due to the SaaS apocalypse is now worth $500M total.
  - Because debt claims must be satisfied before equity, the new valuation ($500M) matches the debt ($500M), reducing the equity value to **$0**.
  - To refinance in 2027, private credit will demand PE firms inject another $500M in fresh equity, leading PE firms to likely walk away and surrender ownership to lenders.
- **Early Precedents:** 
  - In 2024, Blue Owl and other lenders took control of **Pluralsight** (workforce development platform), wiping out Vista’s entire equity stake.
  - In August, **Medallia** (Thomas Bravo deal) was taken over by lenders, wiping out equity.

### The AI Hype Cycle Hits Structural Realities

* **Anthropic's Government Shutdown:** The U.S. government issued an export control directive suspending foreign national access (including Anthropic's own employees) to Fable 5 and Mythos 5, effectively shutting down its most advanced models.

* **The Emerging Bear Case for AI:** Despite sustained CAPEX growth—led by Nvidia’s accelerating Q1 2026 revenue growth of 85%—Eisman outlines six major structural concerns slowing the AI sector:
  * **Extreme Capital Intensity:** Software is shifting from a high-margin, low-capital business to a capital-heavy hardware model. Google is raising $85 billion in equity (its first equity raise since 2005) to fund its projected 2026 AI CAPEX of $180 billion to $190 billion, as cash flows can no longer cover these costs alone. Meta and Microsoft are rumored to be planning similar capital-raising moves.
  * **Absence of Moats:** Hyperscalers are projected to spend nearly $1 trillion on AI CAPEX this year, yet their models (Gemini, Anthropic, OpenAI) remain highly commoditized with little sustained differentiation.
  * **Deflationary Pricing:** OpenAI is reportedly considering lowering token prices, signaling margin erosion in a commodity market despite trillions in sector spend.
  * **Token Pricing Friction:** Tech firms are transitioning from cheap subscription models to usage-based token pricing to recoup costs. This is causing severe budget overruns; Uber reportedly exhausted its entire annual AI budget in just four months.
  * **Lack of ROI:** Clear financial returns on AI investment remain largely unproven relative to the massive infrastructure spend.
  * **Power and Energy Constraints:** Massive data center construction is facing local pushback and energy supply limits, slowing projected rollouts.
* **The Airline Analogy:** Eisman compares hyperscalers to capital-intensive, low-pricing-power airlines, while position-dominant suppliers (like semiconductor and power companies, or tech hardware suppliers like Arista and Cisco) represent the highly profitable "TransDigm" of the AI trade.

* **The Iran Memorandum of Understanding (MOU):** President Trump's announced deal with Iran triggered a strong relief rally, a 5% drop in oil prices, and pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back below 4.5%.

* **Federal Reserve Policy Pivot:** Under Kevin Warsh’s first presided meeting, the Fed kept interest rates unchanged. The central bank indicated that while rate cuts are off the table for now, rate hikes have returned as a viable policy option, prompting a negative market reaction.
* **Private Credit Liquidity Stress:** Highlighting cracks in the private credit market, BlackRock was forced to cap redemptions from its HPS corporate lending fund at 5% after investors attempted to withdraw 13% of its shares (up from 9.3% in Q1).
