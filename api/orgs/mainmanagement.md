## ETF Edge on crypto's rebound, the new ETFs to watch and what's different this time

- Channel: CNBC Television
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UbuxyWi0CXE
- Published: 2026-03-09
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260309.html#video-UbuxyWi0CXE
- Speakers: Simeon Hyman (Global Investment Strategist, ProShares Advisors); Kim Arthur (CEO, Main Management); Dominic Chu (Host, CNBC)

**TL;DR**
Despite ongoing geopolitical turmoil in the Middle East, cryptocurrency is maintaining its role as a strategic portfolio diversifier with low correlation to traditional assets, while the industry shifts from a "crypto winter" toward institutional maturity through new index-based ETFs and regulatory-compliant infrastructure.

### Cryptocurrency as a Portfolio Diversifier
* **Low Asset Correlation:** **Simeon Hyman** notes that Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies have shown "de minimis correlation" with equities and surprisingly low correlation with traditional safe havens like gold and silver.
    * During recent Middle East conflicts, Bitcoin has remained stable or risen slightly while equities have pulled back, reinforcing the diversification thesis.
* **The "Nuclear Winter" Bottom:** **Kim Arthur** suggests the market is currently in a "bottoming process" following a massive 50% drawdown in Bitcoin's price over the last year.
    * Recent price action (up 10% off the lows) is encouraging, signaling a potential thaw in the extended depressed period for the asset class.
* **Store of Value vs. Utility:** Speakers distinguished between Bitcoin as a "digital asset" or "store of value" and Ethereum/Solana as platforms with utility on the blockchain.

### ETF Innovation and Investment Strategies
* **Broad Indexing via CRYP:** ProShares recently launched the **ProShares CoinDesk 20 Index ETF (CRYP)** to provide an indexed approach to the top 20 cryptocurrencies.
    * **Quarterly Reconstitution:** Unlike typical equity indices that rebalance annually, CRYP rebalances quarterly to keep pace with the rapidly evolving crypto landscape.
    * **Capped Weighting:** The index caps Bitcoin and Ether at roughly 50% of the total weight (which would otherwise be 85% in a pure market-cap model) to capture the breadth of the ecosystem.
* **The Active Management Benchmark:** **Kim Arthur** argues that Bitcoin is the master benchmark for all crypto allocators.
    * If an active manager or an altcoin-heavy fund cannot outperform Bitcoin over a five-year horizon, the added turnover and risk of active management are rarely justified.

### The Stablecoin Infrastructure and the "Genius Act"
* **Reserve Asset Innovation:** ProShares launched the **ProShares Genius Money Market ETF (IQMM)**, the first ETF designed to comply with the "Genius Act" (clarity legislation for stablecoin reserves).
    * **Institutional Utility:** IQMM provides a 1940-Act regulated vehicle for the $250 billion stablecoin industry to use as a reserve, featuring same-day settlement and weekly distributions.
* **The Stablecoin Use Case Gap:** Current data shows that 80% of stablecoin activity is used for trading other cryptocurrencies. 
    * To achieve broad adoption, the industry must expand stablecoin use into traditional commerce and "buying stuff" outside the crypto ecosystem.

### Macro Outlook and Sector Rotation
* **Geopolitical Resilience:** Historical data from the Gulf Wars and the 12-day Iran war suggests that while markets initially pull back during Middle East conflicts, they typically rebound 25-30% in the following 6–12 months.
* **Shift to "Asset-Heavy" Plays:** **Main Management** has shifted its sector rotation strategy away from "asset-light" sectors (Technology, Communications) toward "asset-heavy" sectors:
    * **Basic Materials, Energy, and Industrials:** These sectors are benefiting from reshoring, manufacturing incentives, and the massive land and steel requirements for new AI data centers.
* **Market Sentiment:** **Kim Arthur** emphasizes that "fear is temporary, but greed is permanent," suggesting that regional economic interests (such as tourism and trade in Qatar and Dubai) will likely press for a swift resolution to current tensions.
