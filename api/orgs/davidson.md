## The Blue Wave Meets AI: What the Midterms Mean for Data Centers | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 84

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wo07fmJw0Es
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-wo07fmJw0Es
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Dan Ives (Analyst, Yorkville); Gil Luria (Analyst, Davidson)

---

### TL;DR
Following the November 4 midterm "blue wave," political pushback against data centers is forcing a slower, more disciplined AI CapEx cycle that favors cash-rich hyperscalers and Apple while exposing debt-heavy neoclouds like CoreWeave, legacy laggards like Intel and Oracle, and shrinking moats for frontier LLM providers.

### Post-Midterm AI Narrative & Data Center Politics
* **The "Blue Wave" CapEx Brake:** Following the November 4 midterms—where Democrats took both houses of Congress—bipartisan opposition to data centers has made US builds harder, slower, and more expensive (e.g., in Texas).
  * **Dan Ives (Yorkville):** Relentless capital raises (ATMs, bond offerings, converts) had drowned the tech market, and investors finally demanded a slowdown. If 10–15% of the 800–1,000 planned data centers don't get built, it hurts builders like Quanta but benefits installed-base equipment vendors like Dell, Cisco, and Arista (who have 18–24 months of visibility).
* **Hyperscalers Win Either Way:**
  * **Gil Luria (Davidson):** Every AI company reported better-than-expected AI results over the last three months, while non-AI segments lagged (PC sales down hurting Microsoft/Intel; search down hurting Google; retail down hurting Amazon/Shopify).
  * **The Collectible Effect:** Hyperscalers (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon) win regardless of CapEx pace—more AI accelerates revenue, while less AI accelerates free cash flow—and their existing data center footprint becomes more valuable ("like a collectible").

### Google Search, Oracle, Intel, and AMD
* **Google Search Slowdown:** Search revenue growth decelerated abruptly from 19% in Q1 to 12%.
  * **Gil Luria:** "This is the quarter we feared." Google admitted for the first time that AI search generates fewer ad dollars than traditional search. With over half of web traffic now coming from chatbots, advertisers may balk at paying cost-per-thousand for bot clicks. While OpenAI's ad business is currently ~$1B, advertisers could eventually shift 10–20% of budgets to ChatGPT as its ad load catches up.
  * **Dan Ives:** The market is pricing in a worst-case ad scenario; if Google demonstrates even a 30–40% success rate monetizing Gemini, the stock could move significantly higher.
* **Oracle's "High Wire Act":**
  * **Dan Ives:** Most cautious on Oracle, which has cut into "muscle and bone" via layoffs while executing non-stop debt and equity raises (including a secondary offering this week) to fund a massive $300B bet on OpenAI.
  * **Gil Luria:** Oracle sits between hyperscalers and neoclouds; its 50%-margin database business pays the interest, but its balance sheet was already strained by Cerner and other acquisitions.
* **Intel vs. AMD:**
  * **Gil Luria:** Intel is a "broken company" with gross margins in the 30s (vs. 50s–70s for peers). Even capable new CEO Lip-Bu Tan—who secured deals with the US government, Nvidia, Apple, Elon Musk, and the 14A node—cannot fix execution at every level. By contrast, AMD (led by CEO Lisa Su) operates in the same PC market with margins in the 50s and "didn't bat an eyelid."

### AI Moats, Frontier Models, and the "SaaSpocalypse"
* **Shrinking Frontier Moats:** Steve Eisman noted that Anthropic reportedly cut prices by 50% and saw Q3 revenue growth slow as "token maxing" ended.
  * **Gil Luria:** OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google DeepMind hold a talent moat (housing most of the ~100 people globally capable of advancing frontier AI, with the rest in China). However, their slice of the AI pie is shrinking for two reasons:
    * **Open-Weight Shift:** CTOs are moving 80–90% of routine AI tasks to cheaper open-weight models (running on Nvidia, Microsoft, or Amazon compute), reserving frontier models for mission-critical work like security, trade monitoring, and predictive maintenance.
    * **The Control Plane Buffer:** Enterprises are hiring Palantir, Microsoft, and Amazon to build orchestration layers and harnesses on top of LLMs so they don't hand proprietary alpha to Anthropic or OpenAI, who may eventually compete with them. Cybersecurity leaders (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto Networks, Zscaler) are also defending their turf.
  * **Dan Ives:** Models will commoditize, which is why Anthropic and OpenAI are aggressively pivoting to enterprise sales to reach $500B–$700B (or $1T) in revenue over 8–10 years.
* **The "SaaSpocalypse" Distribution:** ServiceNow rallied from 230 to 90 to 160 before missing guidance by ~50 bps and selling off sharply.
  * **Gil Luria:** Software performance follows a normal distribution: clear AI winners (Palantir, Snowflake, Datadog, CrowdStrike, Shopify, JFrog), AI-jeopardized laggards (UiPath, Pega, Appian, Nice, Five9), and a middle tier (ServiceNow, Salesforce, Adobe). Even if someone "vibe-codes" the Adobe suite, enterprises won't switch because they pay for workflows, data governance, and collaboration.

### Neoclouds, Apple, and Agentic AI
* **Neoclouds (CoreWeave vs. Nebius):**
  * **Gil Luria:** CoreWeave is "an Nvidia special purpose vehicle" created by Jensen Huang ("playing 3D chess") to create competition for his top five hyperscaler customers. Microsoft leases from CoreWeave for 3–5 years to avoid putting $300B into the ground (spending $200B instead). CoreWeave generates low single-digit returns while borrowing at a 9% blended rate (secured in the high 7s, unsecured now at 15%)—a negative carry where "equity holders own nothing."
  * **Steve Eisman:** CoreWeave executives have sold hundreds of millions of dollars in stock, marking the heaviest insider selling he has seen in 20 years.
  * **The Preferred Play:** Both Dan Ives and Gil Luria favor **Nebius**, citing minimal debt and experienced operators focused on profitability.
* **Apple as the Safe-Haven "Utility":**
  * **Dan Ives:** Under new CEO John Ternus (a hardware innovator), upcoming foldable ("duo") and AI-enabled devices will drive a multi-year upgrade cycle. Roughly 20% of the world will access AI through Apple's 1.5B iPhones and 2.5B iOS devices.
  * **Gil Luria:** Apple is the "safe trade" with a light CapEx burden, collecting 30% of every $20 ChatGPT subscription downloaded on iOS while others burn billions.
* **Agentic AI:** Steve Eisman noted that while consumers can use agents to book tickets or shop auto insurance (threatening brand moats like Progressive and Geico), banks like JP Morgan currently block Agentic AI from accessing accounts. Dan Ives expects regulatory and trust hurdles to delay normalization by a few years, while Gil Luria views it as a net positive that reduces friction and creates consumer surplus.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **CapEx & Free Cash Flow Timeline:** 2027 will likely be the peak year for AI CapEx, with hyperscaler free cash flow rebounding significantly in 2028, and chip supply/demand not reaching equilibrium until late 2028 or early 2029. — *Dan Ives*
* **Near-Term Tech Rally:** Tech stocks will see a significant run into year-end and over the next 6 to 9 months as winners separate from losers. — *Dan Ives*
* **The "Goldilocks" AI Cycle:** Instead of 70% debt-fueled data center growth next year, CapEx will moderate to a healthier, cash-flow-funded 20–25% annual growth rate over the next 3 to 5 years, extending the cycle and proving that chipmakers (Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD, Micron) and hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google) are undervalued. — *Gil Luria*
* **Agentic AI Normalization:** Agentic AI is still "a few years" away from becoming normalized due to regulatory and trust barriers. — *Dan Ives*
