## Why are electric vehicles still the ‘car of the future’?

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B3J_TTxIDjY
- Published: 2026-11-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261112.html#video-B3J_TTxIDjY
- Speakers: Robin Pomeroy (Host, Radio Davos); Laia Barbarà (Director, Clean Mobility and Infrastructure, World Economic Forum); Agustin Delgado (Chief Technology Officer, Iberdrola)

---

**TL;DR:** While electric vehicles (EVs) are cleaner and cheaper to run, the global transition is progressing unevenly due to upfront vehicle costs, charging infrastructure gaps, and regional policy divergences. 

### The Current State of EV Adoption & Fleet Turnover
* **Global Sales vs. Existing Fleet:** 
  * In 2025, fully electric cars and plug-in hybrids together accounted for 25% (1 out of 4) of new car sales worldwide.
  * Only about 5% of cars currently on the road are electric or plug-in hybrids, out of an existing global installed base of 1.3 to 1.4 billion cars (**Laia Barbarà**).
* **Regional Divergences:**
  * **China:** Operating as the global frontrunner/mecca of EVs, almost the entire new car fleet sold is electric, driven by strong policy alignment, subsidies, and aggressive export scaling (**Laia Barbarà**, **Elaine Buckberg**, **Paul Farrell**).
  * **Europe:** EV sales grew around 30% year-over-year, but the region faces policy friction trying to balance affordability, locally made vehicles, and job retention (**Laia Barbarà**).
  * **North America:** Adoption has experienced slower growth and fits-and-starts compared to Europe, though hybrid vehicle adoption continues to grow alongside shifting federal priorities (**Paul Farrell**).

### Consumer Psychology & Lifestyle Barriers
* **Upfront Cost vs. Long-Term Savings:** While EVs are cheaper to run (especially with home charging), high upfront purchase prices remain a primary hurdle for budget-constrained consumers (**Laia Barbarà**, **Paul Farrell**).
* **Range Anxiety & The "Extreme Use Case":** 
  * Only 1% to 2% of Americans drive a trip longer than 100 miles each way annually, yet consumers buy cars based on extreme use cases like annual family road trips (**Elaine Buckberg**).
  * Studies (such as research by Christian Kaps and Mike Toffel at Harvard Business School) show that actual EV owners utilize similar driving and charging patterns to internal combustion engine (ICE) vehicle owners once they have a vehicle with at least 250 miles of range (**Elaine Buckberg**).
* **Urban & Multi-Family Housing Challenges:** Apartment dwellers and city residents lacking home garages or dedicated parking rely heavily on accessible public curbside or supermarket charging (**Laia Barbarà**, **Elaine Buckberg**, **Paul Farrell**).

### Infrastructure & Energy System Integration
* **Grid Reliability and Investment:** 
  * The massive scale of electrification requires significant, costly investments in power grids, transmission operators, and charging networks (such as Iberdrola and BP Pulse's joint venture in Spain and Portugal) (**Agustin Delgado**).
  * Power systems require proper technical mechanisms and specialized workforces to maintain high reliability, underscored by wake-up calls like the past Iberian blackout (**Agustin Delgado**).
* **Charging Infrastructure Deployment:** 
  * Solutions range from home time-of-use tariffs (costing under €2 per 100 kilometers) to highway safety charging networks and fast-charging hubs at supermarkets (**Agustin Delgado**, **Elaine Buckberg**).
  * Government policies like California and Massachusetts' "right-to-charge" rules help condo and apartment owners install home chargers (**Elaine Buckberg**).

### Industry Strategy & Supply Chain Transformation
* **Resilient Portfolios:** Tier-1 suppliers like BorgWarner have spent the last decade building flexible, resilient portfolios spanning combustion, hybrids, and full battery-electric vehicles to adapt to varying regional paces (**Paul Farrell**).
* **Economies of Scale:** As EV production scales from niche to mainstream across a 90-million-unit annual passenger car market, manufacturing efficiencies, battery cost reductions, and technological maturity are driving down costs (**Paul Farrell**).
* **Global Competition:** The transition is heavily influenced by international competition between China, the U.S., and Europe, forcing incumbent industries to embrace new technologies rather than protect old ones (**Agustin Delgado**, **Paul Farrell**).
