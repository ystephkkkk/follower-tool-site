## How to find your arbitrage (from the guy who’s done it 3x)

#### TL;DR
Nick Kokonas reveals how he mastered multiple fields—from options trading and building the world-class restaurant Alinea to creating the software company Tock—by viewing every industry through the lens of time-scale arbitrage, intense daily execution, and adjacent-industry theft.

---

#### Trading Floor Origins & The Art of Arbitrage

* **The Reality of High-Stakes Trading:** 
  * **Capital Scarcity:** Nick Kokonas started trading with just $60,000 ($20,000 of his own money and $40,000 from a backer), which he describes as drastically undercapitalized for the floor.
  * **The 1-Year Goal:** Kokonas set a hard target to be trading on a badge within one year of taking a $5.25/hour runner job at the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, hitting his goal almost to the day.
  * **Day 1 Trial by Fire:** On his first day in the pits, the strict rule was that he had to complete at least one trade in a zero-sum, hostile, and chaotic physical environment to break the psychological seal.
* **Bypassing the 30-Second Gap:**
  * **The Time-Scale Arb:** Recognizing that a trade execution process took 30 to 40 seconds via paper tickets passed between New York and Chicago, Kokonas sought to bridge the gap directly.
  * **Regulatory Scrutiny:** By meticulously reading the voluminous CFTC rules—which lawyers and board members rarely read in full—he discovered that direct communication between identical firm tax IDs was not explicitly barred.
  * **Union Workarounds:** When the Merc blocked dedicated fiber-optic line permits to protect broker group interests, Kokonas bribed union cable workers with cases of Jameson to run the line.
  * **Peak Revenue:** The direct telecommunications link generated instant, zero-risk arbitrage profits, scaling to over $100 million in trading revenue at its peak.

#### Restaurant Innovation & Alinea's Blueprint

* **Open-Sourcing a Masterpiece:**
  * **Building in Public:** Before launching Alinea, Kokonas created the "Alinea Project" forum thread on eGullet, open-sourcing the business plan, name choices, and menu development.
  * **Marketing Leverage:** The public documentation generated hundreds of thousands of views and early demand, securing heavy press coverage from the *New York Times*, *Wall Street Journal*, and *Gourmet Magazine* before opening day.
  * **The Michelin Goal:** The explicit target was to build the best restaurant in America and make it so exceptional that the Michelin Guide (which had not yet expanded to the U.S.) would look bad for omitting it.
* **Operations, Margins, and Scale:**
  * **The Universal Restaurant Calculator:** Kokonas built a single-tab spreadsheet with roughly 80 cells to track every possible cost input, realizing that the low barrier to entry causes most restaurants to fail due to poor business fundamentals.
  * **Financial Success:** Unlike the popular narrative that restaurants only net 2-3%, Alinea scaled to roughly $20 million in annual revenue with 18% to 20% profit margins, ultimately selling in 2023 for an 8x return on initial investment (totaling roughly 27x to 28x overall with quarterly distributions).

#### Software & The Tock Revolution

* **Solving the Host Stand:**
  * **The KYC Problem:** Kokonas founded Tock out of frustration with OpenTable, which acted as an intermediary and left restaurants completely blind to who was walking through their doors (Know Your Customer).
  * **Dynamic Pricing:** Tock introduced variable pricing for time-slotted businesses, matching the reality that a Tuesday 9:00 PM table has fundamentally different demand than a Saturday 7:00 PM table.
* **The M&A Playbook:**
  * **Diligence Control:** When selling Tock during COVID-19, Kokonas, along with co-founder Jeff Kaplan and CFO Steve Bernaki, converted an 896-question diligence list into a Google Doc and color-coded it.
  * **Bypassing Intermediaries:** By refusing to act as passive passengers and completing the rigorous data request in just three days, they saved millions in advisory fees while keeping the deal on track to close before the buyer's Q1 S1 filing deadline.

#### Stealing from Adjacencies: Magic & Theater

* **Borrowing from Magicians:**
  * **Attention Control:** Instead of reading standard hospitality books, Kokonas studied magic books by authors like Nate Stanforth and Darren Brown to understand how magicians handle attention, sight lines, and audience positioning.
  * **Service Positioning:** Applying magical principles, Alinea servers were trained to stand *alongside* the diner rather than directly across from them, creating a collaborative "same team" dynamic.
* **The "Strange House" Venture:**
  * **Immersive Illusion:** Kokonas partnered with magician Nate Stanforth and game designer Alistair Lee to build *Strange House*—a 90,000-square-foot purpose-built experiential venue beneath Wall Street.
  * **Human Connection:** The project aims to combat societal isolation by leveraging theatrical staging, psychological illusion, and Disney-style room throughput to evoke childlike wonder and foster deep human connection among strangers.

### TL;DR
Jeff Harmon shares how founders can find marketing "glitches"—untapped channels of attention—to scale businesses, drawing on his experience building viral ad campaigns for brands like Poo-Pourri and Squatty Potty, and scaling Angel Studios into a top-10 global movie studio doing nearly $500M in revenue.

### 1. Finding Your "Glitch" in Marketing
* **Definition of a Glitch:** Look for areas where you have attention from people that nobody else is paying attention to, functioning as a tactical beachhead for growth.
* **The Ora Brush Origin Story:** 
  * Jeff Harmon helped a college professor sell a tongue cleaner sitting in boxes in his garage by buying a Facebook page called "Kisses" (1.3M followers) and pivoting it to Ora Brush.
  * Initial ads failed because they focused on product details rather than the happy ending/benefit for the customer (e.g., kissing without bad breath shame).
* **The YouTube Ad Arbitrage Glitch:** 
  * Leveraged YouTube's newly launched ad platform to run a $500 script-driven video (using a spoon-scraping bad breath demonstration inspired by an I Justine video) at less than a penny per view.
  * This early-mover advantage yielded a $2-$3 return for every $1 spent on total costs (ads, shipping, product), eventually leading to a $10M company exit.

### 2. Product Development and the "User Method"
* **Solve Your Own Problems:** Follow the "User Method" popularized by Jeff Schwarting—most top businesses are built by founders solving personal problems (e.g., Airbnb founders trying to pay rent).
* **Dr. Bob's Motivation:** The inventor of Ora Brush was a mission president in the Philippines trying to solve horrible breath among his missionaries.
* **Agentic Shopping Opportunities:** Shift attention away from Google search and optimize for AI agents (like Grockbot) where shopping happens via direct API integrations without users leaving the AI interface.

### 3. Crafting High-Conversion, Comedy-Driven Ads
* **The Harmon Brothers Formula:** 
  * Combine a rigid structure (problem, solution, 3 core elements, credibility, call to action) with professional comedians who understand how to build humor that drives the core message.
  * It is significantly easier to train a comedian to be a marketer than a marketer to be funny.
* **High-Stakes Creative Retreats:** Lock creatives, attorneys, and key stakeholders in a cabin for 2–3 days with competing script reads until a winning concept is forged and signed off.
* **Poo-Pourri and Squatty Potty Breakthroughs:** Transformed struggling or small-scale products into billion-dollar brands by leaning into absurd, high-contrast juxtapositions (e.g., a cute British girl talking about pooping on a toilet).

### 4. Disrupting the Movie Business with Angel Studios
* **The Angel Guild Model:** 
  * Built a community of ~3M members paying $12–$20/month who vote on incoming projects, receive two movie tickets per month, and actively fund/feedback-loop indie filmmakers.
  * Members have paid out $300M in profit shares to filmmakers, with Angel splitting profits 50/50 with creators.
* **The Chosen Pilot Experiment:** 
  * Bypassed traditional Hollywood gatekeepers by uploading a 19-minute pilot as a skippable YouTube ad, raising $11M from 17,000 investors via $800K in ad spend.
  * The resulting series achieved 200M+ global viewers and seven seasons of production.
* **Viral Stunts and Algorithm Warming:** 
  * Leveraged Kevin James's face in unlisted social channels and viral art-teacher personas to "warm up" algorithmic facial recognition before launching projects like *Solo Meal* and *Runner* (which secured 100M views via a comedic minivan/Owen Wilson campaign).
  * Executed a Super Bowl stunt by placing Kevin James in a tuxedo surrounded by influencers and cameras in the center of 12 empty seats without any direct commercial messaging.

### 5. Executive AI Workflows and Economics
* **Personal Vibe Coding:** Jeff Harmon uses a personal vibe coder and late-night Claude/GPT prompts running agents overnight to prototype software tools (such as automated movie trailer AB-testing links) before handing them off to the product team.
* **AI Harnesses:** Angel Studios utilizes internal brand-guide harnesses hooked into AI coding models to ensure automated output respects company rules.
* **AI and Employment Economics:** Dismisses the idea of mass AI-driven unemployment, arguing that when a tool makes teams 10x more effective, businesses expand headcount to capitalize on increased productivity rather than shrinking.

### TL;DR
High-performance coach Joe Hudson breaks down the mental, emotional, and nervous system patterns of elite tech founders with hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr, explaining how to untangle self-reliance, conflict avoidance, and negative self-talk through counter-intuitive emotional acceptance and weekly experimentation.

### Mind, Heart, and Nervous System: The Tri-Level Framework
* **The Three Levels of Transformation:** Real personal or professional change requires addressing three distinct systems rather than just intellectual understanding:
  * **The Mind (Prefrontal Cortex):** The easiest level for high-achievers to grasp intellectually, though knowing what to do rarely translates to doing it.
  * **The Heart (Mammalian/Emotional):** The emotional center where underlying fears and avoided feelings must be processed.
  * **The Nervous System (Reptilian):** The primal, physical layer (triggering a physical startle response to a Slack message or a late arrival) which changes not through a-has, but via repetition and expanding baseline pleasure.
* **The Self-Reliance Pattern:** Traced back to childhood where individuals learned they had to fend for themselves and others (e.g., dealing with critical or addicted parents), creating adults who:
  * Struggle to ask for help, own their desires, or announce when they are hurt.
  * Harbor a complicated relationship with authority—both rebelling against external authorities and refusing to fully step into their own.
* **The Golden Algorithm of Emotion:** The emotion you are trying to avoid is the exact emotion you end up inviting into your life by the way you try to avoid it.
  * *Example:* Avoiding the feeling of helplessness by over-grinding leads to burnout, poor decisions, and ultimately, a deeper state of helplessness.
  * *Solution:* Fall in love with the emotions you are avoiding; treat them as messengers and signals rather than enemies.

### Rewiring Self-Talk and Negative Thought Loops
* **Abusive Self-Talk Patterns:** High-achievers are frequently their own worst abusers, trapped in loops of overt criticism ("Why did you do that?"), endless pressure ("You've got to do this now"), or subtle undermining ("You can do it, Mike").
* **Reaction Over Content:** Trying to change the self-talk directly is inefficient; instead, change your *reaction* to the self-talk by treating it playfully or as bad management (e.g., responding to an internal critique with, "Oh, I see how scared you are. Prove it.").
* **Debunking the Narrative:** List the top 20 things the negative voice in your head says and find at least 7 concrete ways each is false (or ask friends to help prove it wrong).

### The Psychology of Founders and Conflict Avoidance
* **Conflict as Progress:** Avoiding conflict in a marriage or business is equivalent to ignoring a blinking warning light on a factory machine; conflict is the exact mechanism through which efficiency and alignment are gained.
* **Organizational Health Metrics:** Joe Hudson evaluates corporate health using just two metrics: how quickly an organization has hard conversations, and how quickly it resolves them.
* **Conducting Hard Conversations:** 
  * Do not solve the problem for the person (which is just a manifestation of CEO self-reliance); instead, ask them to present a plan to solve it by tomorrow.
  * Prioritize understanding their context via open-ended questions before delivering feedback.
  * Express your own vulnerability (e.g., "I feel like we've lost trust and that sucks for me") rather than making it entirely about attacking the other person.
* **The Psyche of Top 1% Performers:**
  * Exceptional fast-paced CEOs rarely second-guess themselves because the velocity of decision-making demands decisiveness.
  * Approximately 60% to 70% of high performers harbor an underlying paradox: they simultaneously feel superior to everyone else (conscious mind) and deeply broken (underneath), using achievement to prove they are not broken.

### Practical Weekly Experiments for Growth
* **The 10% Enjoyment Experiment:** Take what you are already doing and find a way to enjoy it 5% to 10% more without changing your actual actions, doing this roughly 5 times a day for a week.
* **The Do-Only-What-You-Enjoy Week:** Commit to doing only what you enjoy for a week; when forced to confront an unenviable task (like taking out the trash), figure out how to genuinely enjoy the process rather than enduring it.
* **The Gratitude Shift:** Consciously look around every day and thank 5 people for what they did for you to actively dismantle the self-reliance pattern.
* **Visualization of the Worst-Case Scenario:** Like samurai or stoics visualizing their own death or the destruction of their company, thoroughly feel the worst-case outcomes (such as homelessness or corporate collapse) so that fear no longer dictates your business decisions.

- **Founding Fathers' Expectations:** Noted that the Founding Fathers (such as Thomas Jefferson, age 33 during the Declaration) presided over a nation of just 3 million people and did not expect the country or its documents to endure or achieve global superpower status.
- **Leadership Axioms:** Frequently references Abraham Lincoln (praised for his grace, humility, and saving the Union) and George Washington as the ultimate benchmarks for leadership.

### Personal Philosophy & Philanthropy

- **The Giving Pledge:** Signed the Giving Pledge early on, intending to give away the bulk of his remaining wealth to philanthropic causes.
- **Anti-Alzheimer's Strategy:** Reads extensively and interviews authors and historians as a deliberate brain-training exercise to stay sharp in his later years.

  - Culminated in a single royalty payout meeting where an Activision executive slid a check across the table for **$4.5 million**.

### The CEO Hardcore Playbook: Incentives & Operations
*   **Option Drop:** Shared by Martin Basiri (Founder of Applyboard & Passage). 
    *   *Mechanism:* Rather than relying on static company values, Basiri uses instant equity rewards ("option drops") on the spot—awarding 50,000 options for great ideas or impromptu late-night work—to reinforce desired behaviors in real time.
*   **Friction Removal Service:** Used by OpenAI. 
    *   *Mechanism:* To combat bureaucratic bloat as the company scales, employees can email `friction@openai.com` with any administrative drag or roadblock, which a dedicated triage person then systematically investigates and eliminates.

### Communication & Focus Hacks
*   **One-Bit Communication:** Utilized by the Siemens CEO and Jeff Bezos.
    *   *Siemens Approach:* The CEO responds to emails with exclusively "okay" or "no," holding zero recurring calendar meetings; if more detail is needed, employees must talk to him immediately in person.
    *   *Bezos Approach:* Bezos famously forwarded random customer complaints to executives with a single question mark ("?"), signaling the executive team to investigate potential systemic machine failures rather than just addressing isolated anecdotes.
*   **Single-Tasking Enforcement:** Utilized by Peter Thiel at PayPal.
    *   *Mechanism:* Thiel forced extreme focus by physically walking out of the room if employees attempted to bring up any topic outside of their single assigned primary problem.
    *   *The Output Law:* Thiel's philosophy is that "focus is convex"—moving from 80% to 90% focus doesn't just add 10%, it doubles your impact and separates top performers from the rest.

### Resource Culling & Talent Cloning
*   **Continuous Culling:** Utilized by Jack Welch at GE.
    *   *Rules:* Every business division had to be #1 or #2 in its market within one year, or it was fixed, sold, or closed; additionally, Welch fired the bottom 10% of company performers every single year.
*   **Employee Cloning:** Utilized by Jimmy Donaldson (MrBeast).
    *   *Mechanism:* In Beast Industries' early days, new key hires shadowed Donaldson from the moment he woke up until he went to sleep for six months straight, turning them into exact mental proxies who knew how he would make every decision.

### The Cost of Greatness vs. "Just Being Great"
*   **The Kevin Kelly Framework:** As discussed via David Perell's interview with Wired co-founder Kevin Kelly, achieving historical greatness requires an extreme, unbalanced life. Kelly consciously surrendered the path of greatness to live a flexible, exploratory life as a "gadfly."
*   **The Mother Teresa & Historical Legacy Reality:** True long-term legacy is rarely bought through money or corporate success; centuries later, people forget billionaires, while remembering inventors, artists, and humanitarians.
*   **The Shift to Daily Character:** Shaan Puri and Sam Parr conclude that instead of chasing abstract, multi-generational corporate greatness (which carries a brutal personal price tag, such as missing out on young children's lives), the better target is "active greatness"—showing up with character, kindness, and excellence in everyday, micro-moments.
