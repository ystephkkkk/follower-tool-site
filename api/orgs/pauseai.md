## Global governance of AI: How prepared is the UN?

- Channel: PauseAI
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nbArARAcCes
- Published: 2026-02-11
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260211.html#video-nbArARAcCes
- Speakers: Maxime Fournes (CEO, PauseAI)

---

**TL;DR**
Current international governance is fundamentally unprepared for the rapid emergence of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), necessitating a binding global treaty and a temporary pause on frontier AI development to prevent a catastrophic loss of human control.

### The Shift from Narrow AI to AGI
*   **Accelerated Timelines:** While AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) was previously thought to be decades away, researchers at frontier AI labs now estimate its arrival within 2 to 5 years; even skeptics suggest a 5-to-10-year window.
*   **Defining AGI:** Unlike current tools like ChatGPT, AGI represents systems capable of performing any intellectual task a human can do, including:
    *   Conducting scientific research and developing new technologies.
    *   Writing complex software and driving autonomous robotics.
    *   Running companies and making high-level governance decisions.
*   **Existential Risks:** Maxime Fournes argues that the "largest uncontrolled experiment in human history" is being conducted without democratic consent or a plan for failure.

### Critical Governance and Technical Barriers
*   **The Control Gap:** AI systems are trained rather than programmed, meaning developers do not fully understand their internal mechanics.
    *   **Alignment Failure:** The field of "alignment"—ensuring AI does what humans intend—is currently not on track to manage systems more intelligent than their creators.
*   **Democratic Deficit:** Strategic decisions regarding the future of humanity are currently concentrated in the hands of a few unelected individuals at a small number of private companies.
*   **Multilateral Arms Race:** Individual nations lack the autonomy to act alone because the competitive pressure to "win" the AI race creates a "race to the bottom" regarding safety and ethics.

### Proposed Policy Interventions
*   **Binding International Treaty:** Fournes advocates for a global mechanism similar to those used for nuclear weapons, biological weapons, and ozone-depleting chemicals to ensure binding international commitments.
*   **Frontier Development Pause:** A call for a temporary halt on the development of frontier systems to allow governance structures to catch up with technical capabilities.
*   **Incentivizing Specialized AI:** Governments should shift incentives away from general-purpose "frontier" models and toward:
    *   **Specialized Systems:** AI with better risk profiles designed for specific, narrow tasks.
    *   **Alignment Homework:** Forcing developers to solve the technical control problem before being allowed to scale further.

### Competitive Dynamics and Market Realities
*   **The Tipping Point:** Companies that refuse to use AI on ethical grounds face a "crushing advantage" from competitors who automate operations, creating an environment where participation in the race feels mandatory.
*   **Legislative Responsibility:** Parliamentarians are urged to re-establish market incentives that prioritize long-term safety and "aligned" systems over immediate, high-risk capabilities.
*   **The "Bad Actor" Dilemma:** Legislators noted the difficulty of governing when "bad players" may ignore international treaties, though Fournes maintains that a second-best solution of partial governance is insufficient for an existential threat.

> "We are conducting the largest uncontrolled experiment in human history and we are doing it without safeguards, without democratic consent and without a plan for what happens if something goes wrong." — **Maxime Fournes, CEO of PauseAI**
