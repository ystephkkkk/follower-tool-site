## This AI Company Catches Fraud Across the Internet

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JF6XIixstmQ
- Published: 2026-03-31
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260331.html#video-JF6XIixstmQ
- Speakers: Jared Friedman (Managing Partner, Y Combinator); Karine Mellata (Co-founder, Variance)

**TL;DR**
Variance has emerged from three years in stealth with a $21M Series A to provide purpose-built AI agents that automate high-stakes risk, fraud, and compliance operations for Fortune 500 companies and major platforms.

### Core Product and Market Impact
* **Autonomous Risk Management:** Variance builds AI agents to automate complex content review, fraud detection, and identity verification (KYC/KYB) for organizations like GoFundMe and IAC.
* **The "Secret Weapon" Strategy:** The company spent three years in stealth because its tools deal with highly sensitive data; revealing specific detection methodologies could provide a roadmap for bad actors.
* **GoFundMe Case Study:**
    * **Fraud Detection:** Variance verifies fundraisers in real-time, specifically targeting "crisis-driven" scams where fraudsters impersonate victims of natural disasters or high-profile news events (e.g., the murder of Charlie Kirk).
    * **Automated Investigation:** The AI analyzes images, bios, and behavioral signals against terms of service, replacing manual reviews previously conducted by human analysts.
* **Complex Business Verification (KYB):** The system builds identity graphs to uncover shell companies and links to sanctioned countries or money laundering, tasks that were previously entirely manual due to data complexity.

### Technical Innovation: Beyond Rules and Classifiers
* **The Shift to AI Agents:** Karine Mellata argues that traditional "patchwork" systems—consisting of deterministic rules and static classifiers—are too slow for the dynamic nature of fraud. 
* **Self-Healing Systems:** Unlike traditional models, Variance’s agents can read standard operating procedures (SOPs) and reason over unstructured data, allowing the system to evolve as fast as the adversaries it tracks.
* **Solving the Data Problem:** 
    * **Unstructured Sources:** Variance processes petabytes of data from disparate sources, including internal databases, 100+ global business registries, and the open web.
    * **UI Interaction:** To access data locked in legacy systems, Variance agents can spin up browsers to "scrape" internal human-centric dashboards and reason over the information found there.
    * **The "Final Node":** Mellata identifies access to the open web as the critical missing piece in fraud automation, allowing agents to perform the "Googling" and judgment calls previously reserved for humans.

### AI-Native Operations and Team Culture
* **Hyper-Lean Engineering:** Despite managing massive enterprise workloads, the team consists of only 12 people, including just five software engineers.
* **AI Coding Maximalism:** The engineering team functions as "managers" of AI coding agents, utilizing three monitors each to maximize output. Mellata estimates their five-person team has the software output of a 25-person team.
* **Democratized Shipping:** Non-technical staff, such as the Customer Success Manager, use AI tools (like Cursor) to autonomously build and ship feature requests directly to customers without taxing the core engineering team.
* **In-Person Collaboration:** The team operates entirely in-person in San Francisco to maintain a high-ownership, product-focused culture.

### Real-World Consequences and Company Resilience
* **State-Sponsored Threats:** During recent elections, Variance identified complex fraud rings and narratives pushed by state-sponsored actors that isolated classifiers would have missed.
* **Preventing Violence:** The system has detected credible threats of physical harm, leading to interventions by law enforcement.
* **Founder Resilience:** In July 2024, as revenue was doubling, Mellata was hit by a truck, resulting in a broken spine and leg. Despite fears that the "bus factor of one" might end the company, the team maintained operations, and the crisis reinforced their commitment to the mission.
