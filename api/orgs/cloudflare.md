## OpenAI Foundation Co-Founder & President Greg Brockman and Cloudflare CEO Matthew Prince Talk AI...

- Speakers: Greg Brockman (President, OpenAI); Matthew Prince (CEO, Cloudflare); Ed Ludlow (Host, Bloomberg)

---

### TL;DR
OpenAI's advanced AI models helped uncover a critical, long-standing vulnerability in the PHP programming language—which runs roughly 70% of the world's websites—allowing companies like Cloudflare to protect web infrastructure before the flaw was publicly exploited.

### Discovery of the PHP Vulnerability
* **Two-Stage Detection:** **Greg Brockman** noted that a security researcher using OpenAI's models guided by deep expertise found a version of the bug in August, followed by an internal OpenAI model that fully autonomously found a more complete version of the exploit in September.
* **Model Autonomy vs. Human Effort:** **Greg Brockman** estimated that the researcher-guided effort was roughly 75% to 80% human expertise-driven, whereas the OpenAI internal find was set up with an environment utilizing the most capable model with significant compute behind it to run autonomously.
* **The Scale of the Flaw:** **Matthew Prince** emphasized that the PHP software containing the vulnerability runs on approximately 70% of the world's websites, representing a massive latent risk that had likely existed for years.

### Exploitation and Industry Defense
* **Current Attack Volume:** **Matthew Prince** stated that hackers are currently exploiting the known flaw at incredible rates, with Cloudflare observing millions of attempted attacks every single minute across its network.
* **Mitigation Requirements:** **Matthew Prince** stressed that once the exploit is known, attackers do not even need AI to use it; anyone running PHP must immediately update to the free, latest version of PHP to stay safe.
* **Coordinated Disclosure:** **Greg Brockman** and **Matthew Prince** highlighted that OpenAI responsibly coordinated behind the scenes for two months with Cloudflare, WordPress, the PHP community, and the government to ensure defensive protections were in place prior to public release.

### The "Defender Window" and Tech Debt
* **The Defender Advantage:** **Greg Brockman** explained the concept of a "defender window," where frontier AI labs possess capabilities ahead of what is broadly available, allowing defenders to peer into the future and secure their systems against vulnerabilities before attackers can leverage them.
* **Accumulated Tech Debt:** **Greg Brockman** noted that AI is exposing deep-seated tech debt that has piled up in software over many years, raising critical societal questions about how the internet can remain secure as vulnerabilities are found at an accelerating scale.
* **Precedent for Intervention:** **Greg Brockman** drew parallels to historical botnet cleanups authorized by court orders via the FBI, questioning how societal processes and authorized defenders might need to evolve to patch vulnerable internet systems remotely when site owners fail to act.

### AI Adoption and Proactive Agents
* **Rapid Growth:** **Greg Brockman** reported that adoption and usage of "dots" is "hockey sticking," unlocking surprising use cases across professional and personal lives.
* **Proactive Security Agents:** **Greg Brockman** shared an anecdote where an AI agent successfully intercepted a phishing email in a user's inbox, proactively warning both the user and their admin not to open it.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Greg Brockman** predicts that attackers are going to start using AI models broadly to try and exploit software flaws, leading to a steep ramp-up in attempted exploitations.

### Notable Quotes
> "AI is the real hero of this story. These flaws have existed for quite some time... AI didn't create the bug. AI found the bug that was there, and it's incredibly important that we've now found it, fixed it, and the software is significantly more secure because of the work that OpenAI did."
> — Matthew Prince

## OpenAI Helped Find Potentially Catastrophic Cyber Flaw

- Speakers: Greg Brockman (President, OpenAI); Matthew Prince (CEO, Cloudflare); Ed Ludlow (Host, Bloomberg Tech)

---

**TL;DR:** OpenAI’s AI models helped uncover a critical, long-standing security vulnerability in the PHP programming language—which runs roughly 70% of the world's websites—allowing Cloudflare and other partners to implement protections before public disclosure. 

### The PHP Vulnerability Discovery
* **Role of AI in Finding the Flaw:** 
  * **Initial Discovery:** In August, a security researcher using the `GPT-5.6 Sol` model (guided by expert intuition) identified an early version of the bug.
  * **Autonomous Discovery:** In September, OpenAI used its latest internal model with extensive compute in a set-up environment to autonomously discover the full, more complete version of the exploit.
* **Scale of the Underlying Software:** 
  * **Global Reach:** PHP runs on approximately 70% of the world's websites and web applications.
  * **Latent Risk:** The flaw had been hidden in the codebase for years, potentially exposed to and exploited by nation states or hackers prior to discovery.

### Mitigation and Industry Coordination
* **Responsible Disclosure:** 
  * OpenAI worked confidentially ahead of public release with Cloudflare, WordPress, the PHP community, and government partners to ensure protections were ready.
* **Cloudflare's Defense Efforts:** 
  * Cloudflare worked behind the scenes to secure customer networks before the flaw went public.
  * Once the exploit details became known, hackers began attempting attacks at rates of millions per minute across Cloudflare's network.
* **Immediate Remediation Required:** 
  * Matthew Prince stressed that servers running PHP must immediately update to the latest free version of PHP to stay safe, as the exploit no longer requires AI to execute and can be triggered with a couple of requests to a web server.

### The "Defender's Window" and Systemic Security
* **Advantage for Defenders:** 
  * **Frontier Model Lead:** Greg Brockman introduced the concept of the "defender's window," where frontier AI labs have access to cutting-edge capabilities that can find vulnerabilities far into the future before attackers can leverage them.
  * **Tech Debt:** AI is now exposing piles of accumulated technical debt and vulnerabilities across software built over many years.
* **Societal Governance Questions:** 
  * With AI set to uncover floods of new vulnerabilities, Brockman raised questions about how the Internet stays secure and whether systems need to evolve—such as exploring precedents where the FBI obtained court orders to clear malware from botnets, or establishing streamlined processes for defenders to scan and remotely patch vulnerable systems.

### OpenAI Product Adoption (Dots)
* **Adoption Metrics:** 
  * **Rapid Growth:** Adoption of "dots" is "hockey sticking," showing heavy usage across work and personal life.
  * **Proactive Protection:** Brockman cited examples of dots acting as a proactive safety layer, such as intercepting phishing emails and alerting both users and their administrators in real time.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Attacker Behavior:** Greg Brockman stated that attackers are going to start using models broadly to try to exploit these kinds of software flaws, and that OpenAI is already monitoring and seeing initial attempts.
* **Future Software Vulnerabilities:** Matthew Prince predicted that good actors with access to frontier tools from companies like OpenAI and Anthropic will actively comb through code and find a "bunch of flaws and a bunch of bugs," ultimately making the Internet more secure.
