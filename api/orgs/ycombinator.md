## This Is How You Should Actually Make Launch Videos

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs
- Published: 2026-10-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261023.html#video-v12aSIZIXUs
- Speakers: Farza Majeed (Founder, Hey Clicky); Aaron Epstein (Host, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Launch videos in 2026 no longer require expensive $20,000 commercial productions; instead, founders should treat every product demo as a mini-story and consistently ship raw, process-driven content on algorithmic social platforms.

### The Modern Launch Playbook: Mini-Stories Over Major Events
- **Shift from Single Events to Continuous Shipping:** Founders should abandon the outdated meta of coordinating a single, high-stakes launch (like a TechCrunch article or costly agency video).
  - Farza Majeed notes that a simple, authentic video saying "hi" and showing a product performs far better than a $20,000 produced New York Times video.
  - Success comes from treating launches as "mini-stories" and constant demos rather than high-risk rocket launches.
- **Demo-Driven Development:** Work backwards from a demo at every stage of product creation.
  - If a feature sounds good on paper as a video hook before building begins, it is worth creating.
  - The internal excitement of shouting *"Yo, check this out!"* to a co-founder is the exact raw energy that should be packaged for the internet.

### The Content Creation Process: Scripting and Hook Mechanics
- **Religiously Scripting Off-the-Cuff Videos:** Despite appearing natural, high-performing videos are meticulously planned in advance.
  - Farza writes a script days ahead, memorizes and visualizes lines in the shower or on the way to work, and uses text editors like *Freewrite* (freewrite.io) to refine the copy.
  - The core rule is evaluating the text against oneself: *Did I stop scrolling to read this sentence?*
- **The First 10 Seconds & Visual Hooks:** Setup should comprise only 10-20% of a one-minute video.
  - Start with a clear hook defining who you are, what you are doing, and why it matters.
  - Utilize recognizable visual anchors in the first frame (such as *Xcode* for developers) and zoom in immediately to capture attention while users are scrolling.
- **Embracing the "Cringe" Phase:** Getting past initial discomfort requires sheer repetition.
  - Farza admits that many produced videos initially felt terrible or "cringe" to his team before going viral (e.g., a video he thought was awful hit 3 million views on Twitter).
  - Founders must push through publishing imperfect content because audiences forget quickly and the reps build necessary taste and skill.

### Channel Selection and Conversion Realities
- **Prioritizing Platforms by Down-Funnel Conversion:** Vanity metrics (like total views) matter far less than actual user downloads and retention.
  - **Twitter/X:** Historically yields massive view counts (e.g., 3 million views) but features the lowest conversion rates for actual app downloads (less than 1%).
  - **YouTube:** Generates lower initial views (e.g., 20,000 views) but acts as a long-term library with extremely high conversion rates (roughly 20% download the app).
  - **Instagram:** Drives the majority of traffic (around 70%) for Farza's current company, *Hey Clicky*.
- **Targeting Niche Audiences on General Platforms:** Mass-market algorithmic feeds can successfully capture B2B or specialized buyers if directly addressed.
  - By directly calling out specific personas (e.g., "Are you the founder of a design agency?"), Farza generated 50 targeted design agency leads through TikTok videos averaging 4,000 views.

### Lean Production Stack and Team Structure
- **Essential Hardware and Software:** High-end production is completely optional, but audio quality is non-negotiable.
  - *Screen Studio* ($30) is used for smooth screen and cursor recordings.
  - An iPhone selfie camera is sufficient for native, authentic updates.
  - For cinematic quality, Farza uses a *Sony FX3 / FX30* paired with a *DJI mic*.
  - **Audio Warning:** Clean audio is mandatory; viewers will tolerate 720p video in a basement, but poor audio causes immediate drop-off. Never use built-in MacBook or AirPod microphones.
- **Early Team Composition:** Hiring for creative leverage early on frees technical founders to build.
  - Farza's first hires at *Hey Clicky* and *Buildspace* prioritized a filmmaker alongside engineers.
  - A dedicated filmmaker handles the execution (sets, shots, motion graphics, and editing), allowing the founder to focus on product, hiring, and growth.

> — Farza Majeed

---

### TL;DR
Magic Patterns co-founder and CEO Alex Danilowicz joined YC’s Aaron Epstein to demonstrate their AI design tool and review startup websites to help founders eliminate "vibe-coded" design flaws.

### Magic Patterns: Product & Origins
* **Company Background:** Founded in October 2023 by two non-designer front-end engineers, Alex Danilowicz and his co-founder launched as one of the very first prompt-to-design tools on the market alongside V0.
* **Pivot to Product-Market Fit:** Applied to YC with a text message analyzer before their YC partner advised them to focus on their core expertise: translating Figma mockups into code; after trying various pivots (component library editor, storybook on steroids, local host Chrome extension) over 9 months, they finally found traction when launching Magic Patterns.
* **Dogfooding Culture:** The team relentlessly dogfoods their own product internally, such as building their own loading indicators and using their own canvas tools before shipping.
* **Model and Feature Agnosticism:** Unlike competitors like Claude Design (which locks users into Anthropic models and Claude Code), Magic Patterns is model-agnostic and allows exports to Figma, cursor, cloud code, and any coding agent.
* **Team-Wide Adoption:** The tool democratizes design across non-designers, with everyone from the ops team to sales using Magic Patterns to create materials without outsourcing.

### The Magic OS Canvas & Workflow
* **Canvas 2.0 / Magic OS:** A newly released infinite canvas and operating system for context that allows product teams to bring together prompts, images, PRDs, and documentation.
* **Multi-Threaded Interaction:** Unlike traditional design tools or single-threaded AI chats where users must wait for an agent to finish, Magic OS supports multiple concurrent threads and artboards.
* **Model Context Protocol (MCP):** Initially released a year ago as a hackathon project, MCP usage has completely exploded and become a primary interaction method for enterprise users who want to connect context from Cursor, Claude Code, and PRDs directly to a visual canvas.
* **3D Game Experiences:** Demonstrates that modern code-based design is moving beyond flat 2D; users can now one-shot complex 3D WebGL experiences in the browser (such as a chocolate factory theme park in Roller Coaster Tycoon) by feeding component-based guardrails to the AI.

### Startup Website Design Reviews
* **Ego (Applied AI research lab building personal agents):**
  * *Critique:* Suffering from "vibe-coded" smells like unnecessary dark-mode red animations, blinking dots, an intimidating un-interactive eyeball, and hiding the core value proposition and waitlist CTA too far down the page.
  * *Insight:* Input forms or interactive elements should be immediately visible and expanded on the page rather than requiring hidden clicks or popups to maximize conversion rates.
* **Sentient OS (On-device LLM for Mac):**
  * *Critique:* Employs "scrolljacking" (altering normal trackpad/mouse scroll precision), which frustrates users and reduces conversion; uses trendy mid-2020s design defaults like Instrument Serif and Space Grotesque fonts and random bolding.
  * *Insight:* For local machine apps, trust, privacy, and security (such as emphasizing open-source, on-device processing) must be front and center on the hero section.
* **Saranovas (Institutional intelligence and decision infrastructure):**
  * *Critique:* Features excessive white space above the fold, vague enterprise copy, and forces users to click through target personas (enterprise, middle market, private equity) instead of immediately showing what problem the product solves.
  * *Insight:* Vibe-coded websites often read like investor pitch decks rather than customer-centric product pages.
* **Slashy (Intelligent inbox):**
  * *Critique:* The logo reads as "Sashy" due to the slash placement, and the demo video contains too much dense text for a user to process quickly.
  * *Strengths:* Clear value proposition above the fold, strong social proof from founders/investors, a trust and security navigation link, and high-quality footer spacing.

### Advice on Design Taste & Best Practices
* **Developer Design Taste:** Alex Danilowicz recommends reading *The Design of Everyday Things*, looking at beautiful art (like a Matisse exhibit), and realizing that design is not just pixel-perfect border radius, but putting the human and user experience first.
* **Cross-Functional Customer Support:** Every employee at Magic Patterns works in the customer support inbox daily to experience user frustrations firsthand and fix them immediately.
* **User Testing:** Founders must talk directly to users and ask for honest feedback without letting them know it is their own website to avoid overly polite bias.

Jared Friedman (Y Combinator Managing Partner) hosts the discussion on AI data centers, safety, and career lessons.

### Cisco's Transformation and Startup Playbook
- **Operating Model ("Rule of Thirds"):** Jeetu Patel balances leadership by dividing his team into three groups: at-scale Cisco operators who know how to maneuver the company, market-in system thinkers brought in from the outside, and co-founders/CEOs of acquired companies given oversized charters.
- **Wartime Mindset & Founder Mode:** Patel acts as a "late-stage co-founder" (arriving 36 years late) who pokes across departmental boundaries in sales, marketing, and engineering, fostering a culture of ownership where speed and scale coexist.
- **Adoption Strategy for 32,000 Engineers:** Cisco rejected efficiency-first narratives, guaranteeing employees that not using AI will make them lose their relevance, and initially provided unlimited tokens to encourage familiarization before efficiency.
- **Concrete Software Result:** OpenAI Codex integration and internal adoption led engineering teams to refactor 500,000 lines of legacy code down to 100,000–120,000 lines in just two weeks (a 5x improvement).

### AI Infrastructure and the Data Center Buildout
- **Explosive Hyperscaler AI Orders:** Cisco’s AI infrastructure business launched two years ago with a $1B year-one guidance target, but closed year one at **$2.3 billion** in orders, doubled to **$5 billion** target, and finished the fourth quarter at **$9.3 billion** in total orders.
- **Networking Stack Expansion:** Cisco covers scale-up networking (within racks), scale-out networking (across rows/data centers), and scale-across networking (connecting data centers hundreds of kilometers apart to function as a single computer), plus photonics/optics.
- **Data Center Economics:** A gigawatt of data center capacity costs **$35 billion to $50 billion**, with roughly 10% to 15% dedicated to networking.
- **Hardware vs. Software Planning Cycles:** Silicon operates on a 5-year planning cycle (IP like SerDes, manufacturing, SDKs), hardware takes 18 to 30 months, operating systems take 12 to 18 months, and models/apps operate on 3-month to weekly cycles.

### AI Agents, Security, and Overbuilding Myths
- **Current Agent Penetration:** Sub-2% of humans use agents in a power-user capacity today, yet infrastructure is already supply-constrained.
- **Agent Resource Consumption:** An average agent is **450% more consumptive on network bandwidth** than a human conducting the same exact task.
- **Agentic Security & Observability:** Addressing the "paperclip optimizer" and rogue agent problem, Cisco acquired Galileo (featuring former DeepMind and Uber talent) to build run-time guardrails, non-human identity management, algorithmic red teaming, and dynamic monitoring.
- **The "Overbuilding" Fallacy:** Patel categorically denies that AI infrastructure is overbuilt, contrasting it with the dot-com boom because current infrastructure is consumed instantly despite exponential supply growth, and agents will drive exponentially higher token demand.

### Personal Journey and Leadership Philosophy
- **Early Adversity:** Patel grew up in India with a high-stakes con man father, escaped domestic abuse with his mother at age 19 in 1991, and immigrated to the US where he waited tables at Sizzler for an average tip of $1.
- **Early Entrepreneurship & Lessons:** An intern at DocuLabs, Patel took out a $10,000 loan and a subsequent $250,000 loan at age 19 to buy out an investor and ran the services business for 17 years—a move he calls a mistake driven by ego rather than learning.
- **The Mentorship Pivot:** Rick Devenuti hired him at EMC (60,000 employees) telling him, *"You don’t have 17 years of experience. You have one year of experience 17 times over."* The pivot taught him the power of mentorship and scaling beyond a lifestyle business.
- **Career Scaling:** At Box under Aaron Levie, he helped scale revenue from **$200 million to $800 million**, before joining Cisco at CEO Chuck Robbins' invitation.

## Paul Graham On Startups, Ambition, and Great Founders

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bxp78i96S8
- Published: 2026-09-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260903.html#video-5bxp78i96S8
- Speakers: Vivian Shen (Visiting Partner, Y Combinator); Paul Graham (Co-Founder, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Startups today are tackling significantly more ambitious problems—such as curing cancer and launching intercontinental cargo rockets—while the core mechanics of building a company and the psychological profile of great founders have remained fundamentally unchanged over Y Combinator's 21-year history.

### Evolution of Startups and Ambition
* **Supposedly "Good Old Days":** Critics often claim YC has lost its edge since 2008, but early iconic companies like Reddit were far less serious than current batch companies building intercontinental ballistic cargo systems or tackling cancer.
* **Frighteningly Ambitious Ideas:** The 2012 essay concept of a "new Google" manifested via OpenAI, which bypassed head-on search competition by making obsolete the traditional web-page discovery model in favor of direct information delivery.
* **Serious Healthcare Ventures:** Current YC batches feature multiple diverse approaches to curing cancer, ranging from vaccines to on-demand cancer research ("death of a thousand cuts"), mirroring how YC alumnus Sid Sijbrandij of GitLab successfully attacked his own cancer.

### Founder Psychology and Formidability
* **Inborn Drive:** Great founders possess an innate, formidable drive that cannot be easily manufactured; the rare exceptions are young founders who have merely been trained by pushy parents or environments not to show their ambition.
* **Real-Day Motivation:** While billionaires-in-the-making, founders are driven day-to-day by immediate survival fears—such as a crashing server or an engine falling off a model train set—rather than financial wealth.
* **The "Formidable" Definition:** Coined from Jessica Livingston and Maya's private language, a formidable founder is simply someone who "gets what they want in any situation," aligning perfectly with investor interests.
* **The Anti-Credential:** Treating startups as a resume badge or cool credential is a severe mismatch, akin to applying to Harvard and being forced to study theoretical physics with no easy majors available.

### AI Surprises and the Definition of AGI
* **The Inverted AI Trajectory:** Rather than starting with a perfect fly brain and working up to humans, AI development started with a full-human but deeply flawed baseline ("an undergrad trying to bluster his way through a paper") and is working toward perfection.
* **The Jagged Frontier:** AI exhibits uneven capabilities, effortlessly solving famous open math problems while struggling with basic real-world tasks like restaurant opening times.
* **The Turing Test Redefined:** AGI is no longer viewed as a clear-cut finish line, but rather as a "smear" with certain AI capabilities well across the line while others lag behind.
* **GPU and Token Economics:** While salaries used to be the primary startup cost, companies now face massive token and GPU infrastructure bills, though inference costs consistently drop dramatically over time.

### Y Combinator Origins and Structure
* **Accidental Beginnings:** YC started out purely as an angel firm using standardized paperwork, initiating batches specifically to function as an alternative summer job for undergraduate students.
* **Batch Advantages:** YC provides crucial isolation relief by giving founders colleagues to work alongside, technical problem-solving support from peers, and the "YC GDP"—an immediate cohort of early adopters.
* **Growth and Scaling:** Despite growing from 40 startups in the early days to massive current batches, YC preserves the intimate environment by organizing companies into pods of roughly 70 startups.

## The World’s Largest Electric Aircraft Just Flew

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nM86DBOqgPM
- Published: 2026-09-01
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260901.html#video-nM86DBOqgPM
- Speakers: Gustaf Alströmer (Partner, Y Combinator); Anders Forslund (Co-founder and CEO, Heart Aerospace)

---

* **TL;DR:** Heart Aerospace has flown the world’s largest electric aircraft (a 100-foot wingspan, 25,000-pound takeoff weight, and $5 in electricity cost) as it builds the ES30 hybrid-electric airliner to revolutionize regional air travel.
* **Makers & Milestones**
  * **Company Evolution:** Founded 7 years ago out of Y Combinator W19 by Anders Forslund and Clara (starting with a 3D-printed model), Heart Aerospace now employs a 40-person team at its pilot plant in Los Angeles.
  * **Record-Breaking Aircraft:** The company built and flew the largest electric aircraft in the world by a factor of two, marking the first clean-sheet airliner flown in the US in any category in the last 18 years.
  * **First Flight:** The historic first flight took place on Wednesday, August 12th in Plattsburgh, New York.
* **The ES30 Aircraft & Economics**
  * **Capacity & Specs:** The upcoming ES30 will seat up to 36 passengers (most commonly configured for 30, giving everyone about 6 inches of extra legroom) and features an all-electric range of up to 125 miles, a hybrid range of up to 500 miles, and a 30-minute recharge time.
  * **Operating Economics:** Driven by rising oil prices, Heart's overall operating economics have improved from 33% better to 48% better compared to legacy aircraft.
  * **Conventional Design Strategy:** Unlike competitors building futuristic-looking craft, Heart deliberately designed the ES30 to look like a conventional turbo-prop to blend seamlessly into existing infrastructure across the US's 5,000 airports.
* **Electric Motor & Component Advantage**
  * **Simplified Engineering:** Electric motors have only one moving part (compared to thousands in jet engines), feature zero combustion, experience virtually zero wear, and create constant torque that allows them to run virtually silently when standing 100 feet away.
  * **In-House Manufacturing:** Heart designs and builds actuators, 6061 aerospace aluminum cylinder bodies, and 15 machine components in-house rather than relying on traditional aerospace suppliers.
* **Battery Technology & The Hybrid Solution**
  * **Cell Selection:** The team evaluates cells from Chinese, American, and Korean manufacturers in their battery lab to optimize flight cycles, safety, cost, and energy density.
  * **Energy Density Target:** While current cells on their lab table reach up to 370 Wh/kg, one manufacturer is producing a 400 Wh/kg cell expected within a couple of months.
  * **The Reserve Challenge & Hybrid Fix:** Because 1-in-1,000 US flights divert and require 45 minutes of loiter time plus a 100-mile alternate, battery-only aircraft would need 2/3 of their battery dedicated to reserves; Heart solved this by adding a simple, inexpensive turbo-prop hybrid engine for ranges up to 500 miles.
* **Go-To-Market & Capital Strategy**
  * **Early Validation:** Anders Forslund secured Letters of Intent (LOIs) from SAS and three Nordic airlines before having underlying technology.
  * **Inbound Customers:** United Airlines discovered Heart via an inbound message in their spam/info email folder after seeing their 400 kW motor.
  * **Capital-Raising Rule:** Anders Forslund advises founders that every time capital is needed, it must be tied to something material and physical that can be touched and shown.
* **Notable Quotes**
  * > "With a relatively small team, they're going after something that Boeing and Airbus have not been able to pull off." — Gustaf Alströmer
  * > "We're not trying to build a sports car. I'd rather build something that looks very conventional, but is kind of hiding its Superman cape under the hood." — Anders Forslund
  * > "Traditional aerospace is like 'well the the impact is always catastrophic. So let's minimize the probability.' We don't know anything until we know everything. Whereas if you look at 'hey how do we minimize impact to getting it wrong?' Then you can select a process that's a lot cheaper, that's a lot easier to iterate on." — Anders Forslund


## Michael Kratsios: Inside the White House's AI Strategy

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zLUZclThLhU
- Published: 2026-08-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260818.html#video-zLUZclThLhU
- Speakers: Michael Kratsios (Director, White House Office of Science and Technology Policy); Luther Lowe (Head of Public Policy, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
The White House strongly supports open-source AI as essential for U.S. technology leadership, while prioritizing regulatory clarity and a single national standard to help startups avoid a restrictive state-level patchwork. Michael Kratsios and Luther Lowe outline the federal strategy for science, technology, and AI at Startup School 2026.

### Washington's AI Strategy & Open Source
* **Commitment to Open Source:** The White House's national AI strategy (co-authored by Michael Kratsios, David Sacks, and Secretary Rubio in July 2025) explicitly commits to maintaining a vibrant ecosystem of both closed and open-source models.
* **Open Weights and Rumors:** Speculative executive orders restricting open-source models are countered by active administration policy; Kratsios praised industry advocacy—including open letters organized by Y Combinator—for providing the direct feedback loop necessary for sound policymaking.
* **Federal-Private Dialogue:** Government policies rely on real-time input from the startup community, tech ecosystems, and financial institutions to prevent decisions made in a Washington vacuum.

### Regulating a Fast-Moving Technology
* **Avoiding EU-Style Red Lines:** The White House explicitly avoids setting firm red lines or hard compute thresholds that fail to stand the test of time, citing the EU AI Act (passed before the invention of ChatGPT) as a cautionary tale of rigid regulation.
* **National Standards vs. State Patchworks:** A key priority for President Trump is preempting state-level laws to establish a single national framework for AI, shielding resource-constrained startups from navigating conflicting state regulations.
* **Born-Free vs. Born-in-Captivity Tech:** Regulation is categorized into "born-free" domains (like the early internet) which require preservation from over-regulation, and "born-in-captivity" domains (like commercial drones and supersonic flight) where targeted regulatory barriers and permit processes must be actively dismantled.

### Washington Perspective vs. Silicon Valley Realities
* **Balancing Competing Concerns:** Washington policymakers must simultaneously weigh the broader public perception of AI, data center expansion in local communities, healthcare applications, and labor market impacts alongside raw compute demand.
* **Supporting "Little Tech":** The administration acknowledges the dominance of large tech players and utilizes traditional stakeholder mechanisms, Requests for Information (RFIs), and federal antitrust enforcement (such as ongoing DOJ and FTC cases against Google, Apple, Facebook, and Amazon) to ensure smaller startups retain a seat at the table.
* **Intellectual Property and Creators:** While direct model outputs (e.g., generating unauthorized trademarked assets like Mickey Mouse) remain restricted by statute, the White House prefers to let market-driven revenue-sharing agreements and licensing deals mature organically rather than over-legislating the space prematurely.

### The Inside View of Federal Policy & Science
* **Federated Decision-Making:** Technology policy is distributed across multiple agencies (defense, health, energy, science); the White House acts as the central convening authority to align competing agency equities before policy is executed.
* **The "Science: A New Golden Age" Report:** Released in early 2026, this report re-evaluates the post-WWII Vannevar Bush model, noting that R&D funding has inverted—with the private sector and philanthropy now driving roughly 70% of research compared to the federal government's 30%.
* **Autonomous Scientific Discovery:** The report emphasizes that artificial intelligence will fundamentally transform material science, pharmaceuticals, and chemistry via autonomous cloud labs and robotic experimentation loops.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AI in Science (Michael Kratsios):** Scientists in material science, pharmaceuticals, and chemistry will conduct research dramatically differently because of artificial intelligence **within 2 to 3 years**.
* **Quantum Computing Goal (President Trump via Michael Kratsios):** The Department of Energy is directed to build a scientifically relevant quantum computer **by 2028**.

## Y Combinator CEO on Founder Psychology in the Age of AI

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fsTtKywmWlU
- Published: 2026-08-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260812.html#video-fsTtKywmWlU
- Speakers: Anish Acharya (General Partner, a16z); Garry Tan (President and CEO, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
AI is transforming company building by enabling tiny teams equipped with hundreds of agents and markdown-file "employees" to operate at a scale that once required massive organizations. Garry Tan and Anish Acharya discuss how this shift redefines founder psychology, renders traditional SaaS models vulnerable, and demands a return to first-principles ambition and earnest obsession.

### 1. Silicon Valley Culture & Founder Psychology
* **The Cost of Chasing Trends:** Garry Tan reflects on his early career mistakes in 2003—such as joining Microsoft's Windows Mobile team instead of staying with web programming or joining early Palantir founders Joe Lonsdale and Stefan Cohen—attributing those errors to chasing what was "hot" rather than following direct experience and first-principles knowledge.
* **The "Don't LARP" Ethos:** Tan emphasizes that building what matters requires earnestness, courage, and true obsession rather than mimicking surface-level tech culture or following Wall Street map-making.
* **Y Combinator's Role as "Birthright for Tech":** Founded by Paul Graham, Jessica Livingston, Trevor Blackwell, and Robert Morris, YC broke down traditional pedigree barriers by relying on simple applications (12 questions plus a 1-minute video) to identify global builders and bring them to San Francisco (celebrating 7,000 participants in their startup school).
* **The Loneliness of Founders:** A core value of YC is providing a peer community where founders can drop the facade of "killing it" and deal honestly with the solitary burden of business failure.

### 2. The Economics of Company Building in the AI Era
* **The Death of Traditional SaaS:** Pure per-seat, 10-20x ARR multiple SaaS models face severe vulnerability over the next 5–10 years unless they anchor deep moats in data or network effects.
* **Hyper-Leaned Agentic Companies:** Founders can now build from zero to $15 million ARR in approximately four months with just 2–3 people supported by hundreds of AI agents and reusable markdown "skill files."
* **Token Maximization for CEOs:** Tan notes that frontier model lab constraints on cost and compute can be bypassed by power users running local agentic frameworks (like OpenClaw or Hermes agent) with massive token windows (800k to 1 million tokens), effectively letting a founder "live in 2028 today" at a cost of $50,000 to $100,000 a year.
* **The Markdown File as an Employee:** Every business process (sales, marketing, customer support, engineering QA) can be automated by turning iterative interactions into a permanent markdown file, code, and test suite run via cron jobs.

### 3. Organizational Design & Eliminating Bureaucracy
* **Reversing Corporate Bloat:** Historical bureaucratic middle management (such as the 7-level friction Tan experienced working on Windows Mobile in Redmond) was a 100--year distraction from true "founder mode."
* **Algorithmic Conflict Management:** Startups can leverage agentic systems and automated meeting-transcript parsing (as seen with Brex executive Pedro's "crab trap" setup) to surface ground truth, resolve internal friction, and eliminate emotional politics.
* **Toyota Production System Parallel:** Just as Japanese auto manufacturing empowered line workers to change the assembly line because they held the most context, AI erases traditional API lines and middle-man bottlenecks, allowing the people doing the work to directly improve it.

### 4. Local Politics and Civic Engagement
* **Act Local to Fix Society:** Tan's civic activism in San Francisco—spurred by failures around public safety, crime against elders, and attempts to remove algebra from middle schools—demonstrates that builders must engage in local governance.
* **The Success of Local Reform:** Through grassroots organizing, community advocacy, and platforms like `giryslist.org`, local tech and resident coalitions successfully pushed back against ineffective policies, leading to safer neighborhoods and highly popular local leadership under Mayor Lurie.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **SaaS Horizon:** Pure per-seat SaaS models will not exist in their current form within **5 to 10 years** — Garry Tan.
* **The 2027 Harness Wars:** The year **2027** will be defined by the "harness wars" driven by falling compute costs and consumer AI adoption — Garry Tan.

## How To Design In The Agent Era

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P06RgnUKX_I
- Published: 2026-08-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260807.html#video-P06RgnUKX_I
- Speakers: Stephen Haney (Founder, Paper); Aaron Epstein (General Partner, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Stephen Haney, founder of AI-native design tool Paper, joins YC General Partner Aaron Epstein to demonstrate how Paper uses HTML/CSS rendering to bridge human design taste with agentic workflows, while warning founders against generic AI design "slop."

### The Genesis and Vision of Paper
* **Founding & Team Size:** Paper was started as a solo side project by Stephen Haney out of a love for creative tooling and founder-market fit, and has grown to a team of **12 designers and engineers**.
* **Agent-Native Rendering Engine:** **Stephen Haney** explains that Paper uses HTML and CSS as its actual rendering engine rather than a custom rendering engine.
    * **Developer & Agent HandOff:** This constraint-free system creates a seamless handoff for both human developers and AI agents, because agents natively understand HTML/CSS from their training data.
    * **Token Efficiency:** This approach saves tokens, runs faster, and produces fewer hallucinations.
* **The New Agent Stack:** **Stephen Haney** describes the modern builder setup as combining Cursor, Claude Code, and GitHub with Paper acting as the visual interface to communicate with agents.
* **Company Traction:** According to Ramp credit card subscription data cited by **Aaron Epstein**, Paper is the first company since Figma to make a measurable dent in the design tool market, growing rapidly and ranking alongside Sketch.

### Core Features and Demos
* **Paper Shaders:** Fully open-source WebGL animations designed by studying physical paper to model natural randomness. 
    * **Marketing Strategy:** **Stephen Haney** notes that Paper treats its shader library and custom fonts (like the upcoming **Paper Mono**) as its marketing budget instead of buying traditional ads.
    * **Real-World Usage:** **Aaron Epstein** notes that Y Combinator uses these shaders for acceptance tickets and Startup School branding.
* **Image Generation & Vectorization:** Paper features an image generation library using four different models simultaneously via a "variety pack," alongside color extraction and vectorization tools.
* **Desktop App & MCP Server:** Built following the December 2025 cloud code explosion, featuring an MCP server that allows agents like Cursor and Conductor to directly render and manipulate frames on the canvas.
* **Paper Snapshot:** A Chrome extension allowing users to pull live websites directly into the Paper canvas as editable layers instead of taking static screenshots.

### Deconstructing AI Design "Slop"
* **The Importance of Differentiation:** **Stephen Haney** and **Aaron Epstein** emphasize that using default, unedited AI output makes startups look generic, untrustworthy, and indistinguishable from millions of other projects.
* **Common AI Design Tells:**
    * **Excessive Font Weight & Sizes:** Models over-use bold weights and incorporate 5 to 8 different font sizes.
    * **The "Four Horsemen":** Side-swatches of color on every card, purple gradients, all-uppercase tiny headers with wide letter spacing, and badge pills with unnecessary small icons.
    * **Widgets for Widgets' Sake:** Pointless decorative numbers or small UI elements added merely to fill space.
    * **Default Dark Mode:** Forcing dark mode on every MVP regardless of whether it fits the product.
* **Quick Remediation Rules:** **Stephen Haney** advises designers to pull font weights back to regular or light, restrict type hierarchies to a maximum of **three font sizes**, and ruthlessly delete non-communicative decorative elements.

### Live Redesigns: Legion Health, Sciteex, and More
* **Legion Health Review:** 
    * **Aaron Epstein** notes the original design suffers from generic pill elements and weak alignment, lacking the trustworthy fit-and-finish required for mental health medication management.
    * **Live Fix:** Using Paper Snapshot and conductor, **Stephen Haney** generates multiple layout variations, cleans up font weights, and removes overlapping text elements.
* **Sciteex Review:**
    * **Aaron Epstein** points out that the field infrastructure platform's value proposition is buried below the fold, overshadowed by heavy purple glows and vibe-coded elements.
    * **Live Fix:** Simplifying the layout to three font sizes and removing bold weights immediately elevates the design's perceived trustworthiness.
* **The New Curation Workflow:** **Stephen Haney** explains that builders can leave comments on a design, let agents run overnight generating hundreds of variations, and curate the best ideas into a unified final product.

### Founder Lessons and Future Outlook
* **Previous Ventures:** **Stephen Haney** previously founded Modulz (focused on design-to-dev handoff) and created Radix UI, which was eventually sold to Work OS.
* **Operational Philosophy:** **Stephen Haney** stresses focusing on bottlenecks first, avoiding over-complicated strategies, and maintaining a strict rule of **talking to a user every single day** for the first year (a culture now instilled across Paper's entire 12-person team).
* **Roadmap:** Paper's upcoming focus for the next **6 months** includes building out table stakes features like components, comments, and prototype organization tools for designers using vibe-coding playgrounds.
