**TL;DR:** Arjun Bhatnagar, CEO of Cloaked, has raised $400 million to pioneer "data poisoning," a privacy strategy that uses masked, unique credentials for every transaction to disrupt the ability of AI and data brokers to track individual identities.

### The Core Philosophy: Data Poisoning
* **The Vulnerability of a Single Number:** Bhatnagar highlights that a single phone number can reveal an individual's Social Security number, address, family members, and credit card history because it acts as a universal identifier.
* **The "Poisoning" Strategy:** Rather than just deleting old data, Cloaked "poisons the well" by providing users with thousands of unique phone numbers, emails, and credit cards.
    * **Disrupting Identity:** By giving different information to every merchant (e.g., Domino’s vs. Sephora), there is no clear consensus for surveillance systems to build a profile.
    * **Contextual Relevance:** Data brokers value current, contextual data; Cloaked renders this data useless by ensuring it doesn't map back to a central identity.
* **The Privacy Paradox:** Bhatnagar notes that "people like to feel known, but they don't want to feel surveilled," aiming to balance personalization with safety.

### Product Capabilities and Security Infrastructure
* **Three-Pillar Protection:** The platform is designed to manage the full lifecycle of personal data:
    1. **Clean up the Past:** Scanning the dark web and removing existing data.
    2. **Defend the Present:** Using AI agents to screen calls; 40% of the millions of calls Cloaked handles daily are identified as AI scams.
    3. **Protect the Future:** Generating real-time masked credentials (emails, phone numbers, credit cards, and soon physical addresses).
* **Technical Architecture:** To prevent a mass breach, Cloaked does not use a single "multi-tenant" database.
    * **Unique Infrastructure:** Every user has their own unique database and infrastructure.
    * **Zero-Knowledge Encryption:** The company cannot access user data; even if the system is breached, an attacker would have to hack every individual user's encrypted database separately.
* **Financial Services Integration:** Cloaked allows users to create merchant-specific credit cards with hard spending limits (e.g., a card exclusively for Spotify capped at $15/month).

### Explosive Business Growth and Market Scale
* **Funding Milestones:** The company has raised over **$400 million**, including a $375 million Series B in 2024.
* **User Adoption:** 
    * **Consumer Base:** Grown from 20,000 to nearly 400,000 paying subscribers in approximately one year.
    * **Pricing Model:** Primarily B2C, with users paying roughly $10/month or $100/year.
    * **Enterprise Shift:** Expanded from zero to over 50 enterprise accounts in four months, driven by employees wanting the tool for business use.
* **Strategic Partnerships:** 
    * **NFLPA Partnership:** Cloaked has a deal to provide privacy services to every NFL athlete to protect them from doxing, physical threats, and financial fraud.
    * **Sports as a Standard:** After working with the Chicago Cubs, Cloaked views professional sports as the "gold standard" for testing high-stakes security.

### Founder Insights: High-Stakes Leadership
* **The $1.5M Marketing Gamble:** In late 2023/early 2024, Cloaked was nearly out of cash. Bhatnagar decided to spend the final $1.5 million on performance marketing to prove the "story" of their growth. The gamble worked, leading to their massive Series B.
* **Storytelling Over Decks:** Bhatnagar advises young founders to "ditch the deck." He practiced by sending voice memos to investors while driving, refining his ability to explain the AI privacy crisis without visual aids.
* **AI Origin Story:** The idea for Cloaked began in 2020 when Bhatnagar built a personal AI that accidentally began "automating" his relationship, sending "I love you" texts and memes to his girlfriend without his knowledge. This made him realize the terrifying lack of control individuals have over personal data used by AI.

### TL;DR
By replacing traditional paper check-outs with immediate QR-code mobile payments integrated into point-of-sale (POS) systems, fintech unicorn **sunday** has scaled to over $5 billion in transaction volume across six countries, saving restaurants an average of 12 minutes per table turn. 

***

### The Product and the 12-Minute Table Turn
* **Frictionless Checkout:** sunday utilizes table-placed QR codes linked directly to a restaurant’s POS system, allowing diners to split bills, calculate tips, and pay in under 10 seconds without waiting for a physical check.
* **12-Minute Table Savings:** sunday’s data confirms that digital checkout saves an average of 12 minutes per table turn. This acceleration helps high-volume restaurants seat extra walk-ins during peak shifts (e.g., weekend brunch and Friday/Saturday nights).
* **Empowering Servers:** By eliminating the administrative burden of running credit cards and splitting checks, servers gain time back to upsell, interact with diners, and elevate the hospitality experience.
* **POS Integrations:** The platform integrates natively with 28 major full-service POS platforms—including Toast, NCR, Micros, and Square—offering a specialized checkout experience that outperforms watered-down, built-in features from major POS competitors.

### Scaling Through Post-Pandemic Adaptation
* **Pandemic Roots:** Co-founded in April 2021 by US CEO Christine de Wendel and European restaurateur Victor Lugger, the product was originally beta-tested as a touchless safety solution for Lugger's Big Mamma restaurant group.
* **The Menu Pivot:** While early adoption was driven by contact-free menus, 90% of sunday’s restaurant clients have returned to physical paper menus post-COVID, choosing to use sunday exclusively for the payment portion of the meal.
* **Adoption Metrics:** Across 3,500+ restaurant groups, adoption rates range from 35% up to 80% in top-performing spots like Lettuce Entertain You's Chicago venues. High adoption is driven by proactive server onboarding and integration with restaurant loyalty programs.
* **Fine Dining & Discreet Payments:** Despite early assumptions that QR codes might diminish luxury service, Michelin-starred and high-end venues (such as the Seven Reasons group in DC) use the app to facilitate fast, highly discreet payments for time-sensitive corporate lunches and private dinners.

### The "Shift Work" Philosophy and Operational Legitimacy
* **Mandatory Restaurant Shifts:** As part of onboarding, every sunday employee—including software engineers—is required to work shifts at a partner restaurant (such as Big Mamma's high-volume venues).
* **Building Empathy and Trust:** Experiencing the high-stress reality of front-of-house operations on a busy Saturday night helps tech teams build empathy and ensures they understand the real-world impact of system downtime.
* **Product Feedback Loop:** Real-world training shifts yield direct product iterations. sunday hosts a dedicated Slack channel where employees submit operational insights and immediate "light bulb" UI/UX improvements from their floor shifts.
* **Cross-Industry Application:** De Wendel strongly advocates that corporate executives in any sector experience frontline customer realities (e.g., working in e-commerce warehouses or answering customer support calls) to foster humility and deep brand alignment.

### Serendipity and De Wendel’s Executive Journey
* **An Accidental Co-Founding:** The partnership with Victor Lugger was sparked by a seasonal holiday card sent by de Wendel in 2020 after she relocated from Europe back to Atlanta. Seeing she was in the US, Lugger called her to pitch spinning off his restaurant's checkout tech.
* **Career Pivots:** De Wendel initially aimed to work in international diplomacy—failing the Foreign Service exam three times—before pivoting to business consulting at Bain & Company, where she built her foundational strategic toolkit.
* **Scaling Pedigree:** Before sunday, de Wendel managed high-growth tech operations in Europe, serving as a key executive at fashion e-tailer Zalando (scaling the French division to IPO) and Chief Operating Officer at home-improvement marketplace ManoMano.
* **Growth Trajectory:** After securing $125 million in seed and Series A funding in 2021 and growing from 0 to 400 employees in its first year, sunday aims to double its transaction volume to $10 billion in the next year while targeting market expansion in California, Texas, and Florida.

AI is transforming healthcare from an art into a computational information science, driving massive efficiencies across diagnostics, clinical trials, and patient care. To capture this value, innovators and investors must navigate a complex regulatory landscape, bypass fragmented legacy data oligopolies, and prioritize immediate, high-utility business models over purely theoretical science.

---

### Redefining Medicine as an Information Science

* **The Healthcare Data Paradox:** Despite the U.S. spending 20% of its GDP on healthcare and generating one-third of the world's data—including complex medical records, lab results, imaging, and omics data—patient outcomes have not improved proportionally over the last 10 to 20 years. 
* **Key Acceleration Drivers:** Morgan Cheatham, M.D., highlights three core pillars driving the modern healthcare investment thesis:
    * **Computation:** Transitioning medicine into a highly structured information science rather than a subjective clinical art.
    * **Precision Medicine:** Moving away from standard "one-size-fits-all" blockbuster drugs toward tailor-made therapeutics designed for specific patient mutations.
    * **Prevention:** Prioritizing tools that keep patients healthy rather than simply treating them once they fall ill.
* **Reducing Administrative Friction:** AI is not replacing doctors or nurses; instead, it is absorbing back-office friction and non-clinical tasks, allowing providers to focus directly on patient care.

---

### Investment Strategies and Viable Business Models

* **Avoiding "Science Experiments":** Investors are shifting away from long-term, speculative biotechnology bets toward companies with clear, immediate paths to revenue.
    * **Direct-to-Clinician Sales:** Companies like Open Evidence bypass slow, low-margin B2B hospital procurement processes by selling AI-driven literature syntheses directly to doctors at the point of care.
    * **AI-Enabled Prescriptions:** Portfolio companies like Doctronic are legally executing automated medication refills and telemedicine routing in states like Utah, capturing direct transactional revenue.
* **Where Value Accrues:** On the services and technology side, value is generated directly inside the exam room where the patient-physician relationship is established. In biotech, physical therapeutic assets remain the ultimate currency, as major biopharma companies continue to focus on acquiring physical drugs rather than software platforms alone.

---

### Navigating Regulatory Windows and Data Oligopolies

* **A Pivotal Regulatory Window:** Bradley Tusk notes that while broad public and legislative sentiment toward AI has grown increasingly restrictive—such as New York considering a data center moratorium and federal executive orders targeting frontier models—the FDA and CMS remain highly innovative and permissive regarding healthcare AI.
    * **Urgency for Startups:** Companies requiring regulatory approvals should act rapidly, as shifting presidential administrations could pivot policies or roll back current clearances.
* **Overcoming the "EMR" Oligopoly:** Legacy Electronic Medical Record (EMR) systems like Epic remain highly fragmented and siloed, causing clinical bottlenecks and lost patient records.
* **The Rise of Consumer Health Records:** With the explosion of consumer wearables, personal genomic sequencing (now costing under $1,000), and accessible LLMs like Claude and OpenAI, consumers are increasingly building their own longitudinal health trackers, bypassing institutional data bottlenecks.
* **Balancing Privacy and Progress:** To achieve rapid drug breakthroughs and diagnostic systems, society may need to tolerate lower privacy thresholds. Many patient communities willingly share their health profiles to accelerate cures for rare diseases.

---

### The Next Frontier: Diagnostics, Trials, and AI Prescriptions

* **Computational Diagnostics:** AI's value lies in detecting patterns invisible to human clinicians. 
    * *Example:* Researchers are using routine mammography data to detect calcifications in arteries, identifying early-stage cardiovascular disease in women long before traditional symptoms manifest.
* **Real-Time Clinical Trials:** Traditional clinical trials suffer from severe data lags, often taking months to aggregate and clean data from international sites. Dr. Shalabh Gupta highlights the transition toward "digital twins" and real-time data analytics, which allows companies to monitor trial metrics instantly and terminate non-viable trials early to save capital.
* **The 3-Year Vision:** Panelists predict a future dominated by automated biometric-to-prescription systems, localized healthcare policies that integrate diagnostics with treatment, and the ubiquitous societal adoption of highly effective preventative therapies like GLP-1s.

## Is the Global Economy at a Turning Point? Carlyle CEO Harvey Schwartz Explains

- Channel: Forbes
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VMh8a5FJ8Lw
- Published: 2026-06-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260604.html#video-VMh8a5FJ8Lw
- Speakers: Harvey Schwartz (CEO, Carlyle); Steve Forbes (Chairman & Editor-in-Chief, Forbes)

---

**TL;DR**  
The global economy is undergoing a historic inflection point as 80 years of post-WWII globalization and geopolitical cooperation reverse in favor of national security, onshoring, and energy diversification. While geopolitical tail risks remain difficult for markets to price and private credit faces structural adjustments, the underlying financial system remains robust and presents vast opportunities for private capital.

---

### The Rewiring of the Global Economic Model

*   **Reversal of Multi-Decade Trends:** The fundamental trends that defined the global economy for the past 80 years—globalization, post-WWII geopolitical cooperation, and frictionless mobility of capital and labor—are slowing down or reversing, with technology being the sole exception that continues to accelerate.
*   **The Shift to Energy Diversification:** Global discussions have pivoted from "energy transition" to "energy diversification" because energy security is now treated strictly as an issue of national security.
*   **Onshoring and Deglobalization:** 
    *   **Supply Chain Resilience:** The 1990s focus on "just-in-time" inventory and global supply chain efficiency is being replaced by onshoring and localized economic growth to eliminate international dependency.
    *   **The Cost of Rebuilding:** While geopolitical cooperation can be dismantled quickly, physically unwinding and rebuilding complex global supply chains is a capital-intensive, multi-year process.
*   **The Demand for Private Capital:** Ballooning government deficits mean public funds cannot support this global transition. The trillions of dollars required for energy diversification and onshoring over the next 10 to 15 years must come from private sources, including banks, private equity, and private credit.

### Regional Growth and Market Dynamics

*   **Japan's Structural Pivot:** After 30 years of stagnation and deflation, Japan is actively executing a multi-pillared growth strategy.
    *   **Capital Activation:** The government is encouraging citizens to move wealth out of bank savings accounts and into investable assets, alongside reforming corporate behaviors and investing in national defense.
    *   **Investor Sentiment:** Sentiment toward Japan has turned highly positive as capital moves freely to chase these structural reforms.
*   **Carlyle Portfolio Performance:** Carlyle manages approximately $500 billion in assets, with portfolio companies employing 750,000 people globally.
    *   **Key Indicators:** Portfolios show solid EBITDA growth and stable (though slowing) hiring.
    *   **The AI Spending Squeeze:** Heavy investments in IT and AI implementation are currently squeezing out other capital allocation options within businesses.

### Pricing Risks: Geopolitics vs. Financial Systems

*   **The Difficulty of Pricing Geopolitical Risk:** Geopolitical tail risk is nearly impossible for markets to price accurately, leading to stark pricing anomalies.
    *   **Disproportionate Reactions:** The collapse of Silicon Valley Bank moved financial markets roughly 10 times more than Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. This demonstrates that markets over-respond to immediate financial system shocks while struggling to price long-term geopolitical shifts.
*   **A Robust Financial System:** Unlike the pre-2008 era, the post-crisis regulatory work done over the last decade has left the core global banking and financial systems highly resilient.

### The Reality of Private Credit and Systemic Risk

*   **An Essential Capital Source:** Since the year 2000, the number of public companies globally has decreased by half. Private credit has stepped in to provide the essential capital required for private middle-market companies to scale.
*   **Correction of the 2021–2022 Bubble:** During 2021 and 2022, a bubble formed in private credit as assets were acquired at highly inflated multiples. 
    *   While companies with weak business models and poor capital structures will struggle, high-quality businesses with challenged balance sheets are expected to successfully navigate the transition.
*   **No Impending Systemic Domino Effect:** The private credit market does not pose a systemic risk to the global economy. 
    *   **Lack of Crisis Triggers:** A systemic crisis requires a combination of excessive leverage, high liquidity, deep interconnectedness, and panic—variables that do not align in the current private credit landscape.

---

> — **Harvey Schwartz**

> "If we were sitting here at this conference in the '90s, we would all be talking about just-in-time inventory and the need to be globally competitive... You can unwind geopolitical cooperation very quickly, it's much harder to unwind and redo all that supply chain work."  
> — **Harvey Schwartz**

> "The one thing about systemic risk is you need a handful of variables. You need lots of leverage, lots of liquidity, lots of interconnected activity, and you need panic... [Private credit] doesn't really pass the test."

## Ray Dalio Explains the 5 Forces Shaping the Future of the Global Economy

- Channel: Forbes
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BoFw7Sz0Ego
- Published: 2026-06-04

---

**TL;DR** 
Bridgewater Associates Founder Ray Dalio warns of an impending sovereign debt crisis and a historic shift in global power, advising investors to combat extreme market concentration and geopolitical risk by building portfolios with at least 15 uncorrelated assets.

---

### The Five Forces Shaping the Future
To guide global macro investing, Dalio utilizes a historical framework based on five overlapping forces that have driven shifts in wealth and power over the last 500 years:

*   **The Debt, Money, and Economic Dynamic:** The cycle of credit expansion, asset appreciation, and eventual debt accumulation. When debt growth outpaces income growth, debt servicing costs inevitably squeeze out essential spending.
*   **The Internal Political Cycle:** The friction between the political left and right, which directly impacts taxation, wealth distribution, and policy stability.
*   **Geopolitics:** The external shifts in global power and international conflict.
*   **Acts of Nature:** Historically, pandemics, climate shifts, and natural disasters have killed more people and altered more world orders than wars or economic crises.
*   **Technology:** Human innovation and invention, spearheaded today by the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI).

---

### Geopolitics: China's Strategy and Changing Global Perceptions
Following a recent trip to China, Dalio highlighted major shifts in global power dynamics and economic strategy:

*   **Declining US Deterrence:** There is a growing global perception that domestic political divisions and cost-of-living concerns will prevent the US from entering a direct military conflict to defend allies or counter Chinese expansion.
    *   Dalio compares this shift to the British Empire's loss of the Suez Canal, which permanently altered the global perception of British military dominance.
*   **The Deployment of Chinese Capital:** China earned approximately $1.2 trillion from exports last year. How Chinese entities reinvest this massive capital pool globally—particularly in regions like Africa—will heavily impact international capital markets.
*   **The "Art of War" Strategy on Taiwan:** Rather than a direct military invasion, China is expected to employ gray-zone warfare, leveraging economic pressure, political influence, and strategic deception. This strategy takes advantage of the belief that the US cannot comfortably fight a two-front war.
*   **Divergent Views on AI:** 
    *   **The US View:** Highly commercialized, focused on profitability, capital formation, and dominant market valuations.
    *   **The Chinese View:** Viewed as a public utility like electricity or running water. The goal is to raise overall national productivity rather than secure high corporate profit margins.

---

### The Sovereign Debt "Heart Attack"
Despite the markets ignoring rising debt levels, Dalio maintains that a systemic credit correction is inevitable.

*   **The Cardiovascular Analogy:** Dalio compares the current global economy to an unhealthy individual with plaque building up in their arteries. Just because they have not suffered a heart attack yet does not mean the underlying behavior is safe.
*   **The Yield Curve and Central Banks:** The demand for US dollar-denominated debt is weakening. 
    *   Global central banks are actively selling US dollar debt and purchasing gold.
    *   Sustaining these debt levels requires offering higher real returns, which puts upward pressure on the long end of the yield curve.

---

### The Investor Game Plan: The Power of 15 Uncorrelated Bets
In an environment dominated by FOMO (fear of missing out) and extreme market capitalization concentration in tech and AI, Dalio advises investors to prioritize portfolio construction over chasing momentum.

*   **Uncorrelated Diversification:** The holy grail of investing is holding **15 good, uncorrelated bets**. 
    *   Mathematically, diversifying into 15 independent cash flows can reduce overall portfolio risk by up to 80% without reducing expected returns.
*   **Targeting Real Assets:** To hedge against monetary inflation and debt-market weakness, investors should look toward global opportunities and physical commodities, such as copper and other critical minerals.

---

* **The "Cook-en-Route" Experiment:** Wonder initially utilized 450 Mercedes Sprinter vans to cook food outside customer homes. While customers loved the quality, the model was too capital-intensive and difficult to scale.
* **The Hard Reset:** In early 2023, Lore pivoted the entire company to a brick-and-mortar "hub" model after seeing just three weeks of superior data from a test location.

* **Capital Strategy:** Lore uses a "magic number" (66.66) to determine funding needs based on target valuation.
