## John Deere CEO: Autonomous Tractors and Farming's AI Era | Podcast | In Good Company

**TL;DR:** John Deere CEO John May discusses transforming a 190-year-old tractor manufacturer into an AI and precision-technology company, highlighting autonomous farming by 2030 and significant input savings for farmers.

### Smart Industrial Strategy & Customer Focus
- **Customer-Centric Reorganization:** John May explained that John Deere shifted away from building the best standalone machinery (like planters) to focusing entirely on the customer’s production systems and overall profitability.
  - Realigned the corporate structure around crop production teams (corn, soybean, wheat, cotton, sugar) rather than traditional product lines or geographic regions.
  - Delayered the company from 11 corporate layers down to 7 to accelerate decision-making speed.
- **Precision Inputs & Sustainability:** By utilizing AI and computer vision adapted from autonomous driving technology, John Deere machinery drastically reduces waste.
  - **Herbicide Reduction:** Computer vision allows sprayers to differentiate between a weed and a healthy crop, spraying only the weed and cutting herbicide application by **60%**.
  - **Nitrogen Savings:** Instead of free-flowing nitrogen into a trench, computer vision targets the exact seed location during planting, saving **2/3** of the nitrogen cost.

### Autonomous Farming & Technology Integration
- **Autonomous Equipment Rollout:** 
  - Launched a commercial fully autonomous tillage solution in 2023.
  - The portable technology stack works across tractors, combines, and sprayers, allowing equipment to run 24 hours a day without an operator in the cab.
- **Precision Planted Infrastructure:** Modern planters have replaced hydraulics with electric motors, allowing independent row control and micro-adjustments based on soil fertility (e.g., planting 30,000 seeds in one row and 24,000 in another).
- **Redefining the Farmer's Role:** May emphasized that autonomy moves farmers out of the cab to focus on higher-value tasks like planning, monitoring multiple machines, and optimizing system performance.

### Data, Subscriptions, and Business Model Evolution
- **Subscription & Pay-As-You-Go Models:** John Deere introduced Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) and pay-by-the-acre options to help farmers who cannot afford full capital equipment purchases access the latest technology. Take rates have doubled year-over-year.
- **The Operations Center Ecosystem:** Since putting telematics gateways on machines in 2012, John Deere has amassed 15 years of data across 1 million connected machines. 
  - The cloud-based Operations Center houses machine and agronomic data.
  - Farmers can securely share data with third-party input providers (such as Syngenta) to generate customized planting prescriptions sent directly to their machinery.

### Market Conditions and the Farm Income Cycle
- **Global Trade & Supply Disruptions:** Farm income is currently down due to a global convergence of trade flow disruptions, market exits (such as Russia and Ukraine), and geopolitical conflicts (such as the war in Iran affecting fuel and fertilizer costs).
- **Managing Inventory:** To navigate the downturn, John Deere has intentionally underproduced retail demand to stabilize used equipment inventories globally.

### Leadership Philosophy and Background
- **Rooted Beginnings:** May grew up in Caribou, Maine—where the local school system shut down four weeks a year for potato harvesting—and attended college using federal Pell Grants.
- **Corporate Culture:** Stresses a foundational philosophy of putting the customer first and ensuring employees treat everyone with integrity and humanity.
- **Evolution at Deere:** A 30+ year veteran of the company who previously ran operations in China (2004–2007) and took over as CEO in 2019.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- John May stated John Deere’s ambition to make US corn production fully autonomous (tillage, planting, spraying, and harvesting without operator engagement) by **2030**.
- John May predicted that **2026** will mark the bottom of the current farm income and equipment-purchasing cycle, after which the industry will cycle back to a normal market.
