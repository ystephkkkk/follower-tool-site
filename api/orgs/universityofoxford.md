## What if Fixing the System Isn’t the Answer?

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qk6XhFXlghA
- Published: 2026-06-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260623.html#video-Qk6XhFXlghA
- Speakers: Paulo Savaget (Associate Professor, University of Oxford); Hilary Cottam (Social Entrepreneur and Author)

---

**TL;DR** 
Complex systems cannot be "fixed" like static machines because their challenges are deeply intertwined and constantly evolving; instead, leaders should utilize "workarounds"—creative, low-cost entry points that leverage existing networks, rules, and resources to trigger massive systemic shifts.

***

### The Fallacy of "Fixing" Complex Systems

*   **The Machine Metaphor Failure:** Standard problem-solving assumes systems are like broken machines where a single faulty component can be replaced. In complex systems, this approach fails because problems are deeply intertwined and lack a single root cause.
    *   **Intertwined Systems:** Attempting to solve one issue, such as obesity in healthcare, quickly pulls in food systems, metabolic conditions, and even agricultural fertilizer cycles. 
    *   **Evolving Challenges:** Problems shift as we try to address them. For example, the nature of poverty today is fundamentally different from poverty 100 years ago, and urban poverty looks completely different from rural poverty.
*   **The Power of Entry Points:** If a root cause cannot be isolated, leaders must find accessible "entry points" (often visible symptoms of a problem) to build momentum, learn, and scale change.
*   **Hacker-Inspired Methodology:** Computer hackers excel at finding entry points in systems designed to lock them out. Non-technical leaders can adopt this "hacky" mindset to bypass barriers in education, healthcare, and environmental protection without trying to break down the walls of the system.

### Three Categories of Workarounds

Paulo Savaget identified three distinct patterns of workarounds based on over 100 global case studies:

*   **The Piggyback (Relationships):** This approach involves crossing systems to take a "free ride" on an infrastructure that was never designed for your purpose.
    *   **The ColaLife Example:** In Zambia, life-saving diarrhea medicine was unavailable in remote regions, yet Coca-Cola reached everywhere. The organization ColaLife designed medicine packaging to fit precisely in the unused space between bottles in decentralized Coca-Cola crates.
    *   **Systemic Impact:** This tiny-budget workaround increased medicine uptake from less than 1% to approximately 50% in targeted regions, ultimately prompting policy changes at the World Health Organization (WHO).
*   **The Loophole (Rules):** This approach identifies and exploits legal or procedural gaps for a noble cause.
    *   **Bypassing Academic Paywalls:** Academic research is heavily subsidized by public/philanthropic funds, yet journals charge high fees for access. Savaget and other academics use platforms like *ResearchGate* to upload preprints of their articles. This bypasses paywalls while keeping the work citable.
*   **The Next Best (Resources):** This involves creatively recombining or repurposing discarded or unconventional resources.
    *   **Acoustic Monitoring for Deforestation:** Social entrepreneur Topher White addressed illegal logging in Borneo by repurposing discarded cell phones. Powered by solar energy and placed in tree canopies, the phones "listen" to the forest.
    *   **Real-Time AI Intervention:** An AI program distinguishes the sound of chainsaws from ambient forest noise within a two-kilometer radius and automatically sends a geolocation text alert to local forest patrols.

### Designing, Scaling, and Adapting Workarounds

*   **The Extraordinary and the Mundane:** Successful innovators either find mundane uses for extraordinary technology or extraordinary uses for mundane resources.
    *   **Mundane Use of High-Tech:** Zipline utilized drone technology—initially hyped for commercial retail delivery—for the mundane but life-saving task of transporting blood for transfusions across Rwanda's challenging terrain.
    *   **Extraordinary Use of Low-Tech:** Topher White's use of trash-bin smartphones to halt illegal logging showcases how simple, discarded tools can solve high-level environmental crises.
*   **How Workarounds Scale:** A workaround is not a static silver bullet; it is an entry point. For a workaround to scale, innovators must build momentum, engage with unexpected stakeholders, and adapt. ColaLife succeeded because they redesigned their packaging to serve as a liquid-measuring cup and collaborated with the Zambian Ministry of Health to alter regulations.

### Q&A: Accountability, Hostile Systems, and Cultural Context

*   **The Risk of Relieving Government Accountability:** Somto (Global Shaper, Africa) raised concerns that institutionalizing workarounds might allow failing governments to avoid building permanent public infrastructure. Savaget noted that while this "dark side" exists, many workarounds naturally evolve into the new, highly adaptive default systems of the future.
*   **Cultural Equivalents:** Audience members noted that workarounds are highly prevalent in the Global South, operating under regional terms:
    *   **Jugaad (India):** Resourceful, improvisational innovation born out of severe constraints.
    *   **Jeitinho and Gambiarra (Brazil):** Ingenious ways of bypassing rigid systems using minimal resources.
*   **Navigating Hostile Systems:** Gustavo (Global Shaper, US) asked how to navigate systems that actively close loopholes (e.g., medical debt forgiveness). Savaget explained that closing one loophole usually opens others. He cited Uber's strategy of growing rapidly under unregulated "sharing economy" definitions to become "too big to ban" before regulators could react.

### Notable Quotes

> "We are told that we have to fix a system... But this is not how complex systems work... There is no root cause to these problems because not only the problems are intertwined, but the problems change as we try to address them." — Paulo Savaget

> "Hackers taught me that we are wired as humans to look at a system and find an obstacle and think that we have to remove this obstacle. But we constantly find ourselves banging our heads against the wall... [Instead], you don't need to break the walls or the gates to find a way in. You can piggyback." — Paulo Savaget

> "It's not trying to fix a system, it's trying to shift them. It's trying to stir them up. It's trying to adapt, learn, and evolve along with the changes that happen in the system." — Paulo Savaget


## Demis Hassabis, Alison Noble and Paul Nurse discuss the future of science with AI

- Channel: Nobel Prize
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N8LEewvzmUA
- Published: 2026-06-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260607.html#video-N8LEewvzmUA
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (Nobel Prize Laureate & Co-founder, Google DeepMind); Alison Noble (Professor of Biomedical Engineering, University of Oxford); Paul Nurse (Nobel Prize Laureate & Geneticist)

---

**TL;DR** 
Artificial intelligence is driving a monumental shift toward "science at digital speed," enabling researchers to bypass decades of manual labor through computational breakthroughs like AlphaFold. While this transition promises a golden age of scientific discovery, it requires urgent global collaboration to solve the AI "alignment problem," navigate the rise of general intelligence, and preserve creative human inquiry in the lab.

### Science at Digital Speed: The AI Impact So Far
* **The AlphaFold Breakthrough:** In 2020–2021, Google DeepMind used AlphaFold to predict the structure of all 200 million proteins known to science in approximately one year. 
    * *Immediate Solutions:* AlphaFold was highly accurate and capable of folding an average protein in just a few seconds.
    * *Democratizing Global Access:* In partnership with the European Bioinformatics Institute (EMBL), the database was made open-source. Today, over 3 million researchers from 190 countries use these structures via simple keyword searches, eliminating the need for expensive wet-lab equipment.
* **The Three Pillars of Digital Speed:** Demis Hassabis defines the rapid evolution of science through AI across three dimensions:
    1. *Computational Speed:* Resolving highly complex molecular structures in seconds instead of years.
    2. *Rapid Dissemination:* Bypassing the traditional 10-year timeline for physical experimental methods (like CRISPR) to permeate the scientific community.
    3. *Agentic AI:* Moving into an era where AI doesn't just process data but actively generates scientific hypotheses and designs experiments.

### The AI-Assisted Laboratory and the "Virtual Cell"
* **Eliminating Laboratory Drudgery:** Paul Nurse emphasizes that a vast majority of wet-lab work consists of repetitive tasks like pipetting. AI is already transforming daily lab efficiency:
    * *Image Analysis:* Processing complex biological imagery now takes minutes instead of days.
    * *Literature Surveys:* Large language models can consolidate massive amounts of scientific literature in seconds to jumpstart research.
* **Fostering Creative "Taste":** Freeing PhD students and postdocs from manual lab tasks allows them to focus on high-level intellectual work, such as choosing the right questions, establishing rigorous controls, and formulating innovative hypotheses.
* **The 30-Year Dream of the "Virtual Cell":** Hassabis and Nurse discuss the long-term goal of simulating a living cell to understand life’s complex emergent behavior.
    * *The "Sloppy" Biology Challenge:* Nurse warns against trying to simulate cells using rigid physics-based calculations (e.g., hundreds of differential equations), noting that cellular processes are naturally "floppy and sloppy." This plasticity is precisely why adaptive, deep-learning models are better suited to biology than traditional math.
    * *Incremental Simulation:* Hassabis proposes modeling smaller, self-contained sub-units—such as the cell nucleus—as a pragmatic starting point.

### Navigating AGI Timelines and the Compute Divide
* **The Singularity Horizon:** Hassabis states that society is currently in the "foothills of the singularity," predicting that Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)—systems that surpass human-level capability—is only a few years away.
* **The Science vs. Engineering Gap:** Market forces have accelerated commercial AI engineering (such as consumer chatbots and LLMs) faster than the scientific community can analyze how these "black box" neural networks function.
* **The Academic Compute Divide:** Alison Noble notes that academic researchers struggle to compete with massive tech firms spending tens of billions on high-performance computing. 
    * *The Open-Source Solution:* Hassabis suggests academic researchers should bypass the "fear of missing out" on building frontier models. Instead, they can utilize smaller, highly capable open-source models (like Google’s Gemma 4) which are small enough to run on a single laptop but match the frontier capabilities of the previous year.
    * *Restructuring Academic Rewards:* Noble highlights that current academic publishing cultures penalize scientists who slow down to deeply analyze and stress-test AI systems, prioritizing being "first" over scientific depth.

### Existential Risks, Alignment, and Philosophy
* **Four Core Post-AGI Risks:** Hassabis identifies the key societal challenges of superintelligent AI:
    * *Bad Actors:* Rogue states or individual bioterrorists weaponizing general-purpose AI.
    * *The Alignment Problem:* The unsolved technical challenge of ensuring autonomous systems remain constrained by human-designated guardrails once they surpass human intelligence.
    * *Economic Inequality:* Ensuring the massive wealth and medicinal benefits generated by AI are distributed equitably to all nations.
    * *Human Meaning and Purpose:* Resolving the philosophical crisis of human identity when machines can outthink humans.
* **De-conflating Intelligence and Consciousness:** Hassabis argues that consciousness and intelligence are separate properties. He advises utilizing AGI first to study neuroscience and better define what consciousness actually is before attempting to cross the "second Rubicon" of building conscious machines.
* **A Renaissance for Philosophy:** The panel emphasizes that philosophy of science is more critical than ever. New ethical frameworks, virtue models, and epistemological tools are urgently needed to help humanity define "understanding" when relying on complex, un-provable AI simulations.
