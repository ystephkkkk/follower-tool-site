### TL;DR
Krishna Guha and Steve Eisman break down the rise of the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, attributing it to a combination of AI debt issuance crowding out Treasuries, war-driven oil prices, a massive 6% GDP deficit, and Kevin Warsh’s removal of Fed forward guidance guardrails.

### Kevin Warsh's Shift in Fed Policy
* **New Leadership Approach:** 
  * **Supply-Side Ideology:** Krishna Guha notes that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh views himself as a supply-side change agent, moving away from the 20-year consensus of demand-side New Keynesian economics championed by his predecessors (Ben Bernanke, Janet Yellen, and Jay Powell).
  * **Anti-QE Stance:** Warsh believes quantitative easing (QE) was massively overdone and bloated the central bank balance sheet, arguing that QE should be restricted strictly to market seizure emergencies like the Global Financial Crisis or the pandemic.
* **Balance Sheet Reduction Plans:** 
  * **Current Scale:** The Fed's balance sheet sits between **$6.5 trillion and $7 trillion**, down from a peak of **$9 trillion**.
  * **Tactical Caution:** Warsh has established an external expert task force on the balance sheet, but Guha emphasizes he must proceed carefully to avoid triggering a spike in long-term interest rates by dumping bonds prematurely.

### The Perfect Storm Driving the 10-Year Yield to 5%+
* **Competing Borrowers & AI Boom:** 
  * **Hyper-Issuance:** A surge of debt issuance by AI hyperscalers is competing directly with U.S. Treasuries.
  * **Structural Shift:** Bond investors are pricing in a multi-year AI investment boom that has transitioned the global economy from a world of excess savings to one of high investment demand chasing a limited pool of capital, alongside a federal deficit running at **6% of GDP** (within a **$40 trillion** market).
* **Energy Shocks:** 
  * **Refined Products:** While Brent and WTI crude fluctuate, refined products like diesel and jet fuel have surged drastically—with diesel prices essentially doubling in a short window—creating a lasting energy cost impulse.
* **Removal of Guardrails:** 
  * **Death of Forward Guidance:** Warsh has largely abandoned broad strategic communication and the Fed's "reaction function playbook," returning the central bank toward early-Greenspan opacity. Guha argues this removal of market guardrails has left bond investors to navigate uncertainty alone, causing yields to spike.

### Scott Bessent's Bond Buyback Program
* **Operation Twist Execution:** 
  * **Scale Disconnect:** Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent initiated a buyback program initially set at **$4 billion per operation**, later scaled to **$6 billion**, aimed at retiring long-term high-yield bonds while financing them by issuing short-dated Treasury bills.
  * **Butter Knife to a Gunfight:** Given the **$40 trillion** Treasury market size, Guha characterizes the initiative as bringing a butter knife to a gunfight, noting that Bessent ultimately backed off after biting off more than he could chew.
* **Credibility & Regulatory Shifts:** 
  * **Loss of Market Confidence:** Intervening in the debt market made the U.S. government look like a struggling emerging market, damaging credibility.
  * **Regulatory Reforms:** The administration and the Fed are exploring reforms to Liquidity Coverage Ratios (LCR) and banking regulations to encourage major banks to hold fewer Fed reserves and more Treasury debt.

### Economic Strains & The Rubicon for Rates
* **Two-Speed Economy:** 
  * **AI Resilience:** AI investment is largely interest-rate insensitive because projected returns (e.g., **500% to 10,000%**) dwarf 6% to 9% borrowing costs. High-end consumer wealth effects driven by AI stock gains are currently propping up consumer spending.
  * **Traditional Strain:** Ground zero for rate pain is the housing market, where 3D-impact mortgage rates sit around **7%**, while mom-and-pop "old economy" businesses face severe crowding out.
* **The Stock Market Threshold:** Guha warns that while a **5-handle** on the 10-year yield strains non-AI sectors, the ultimate breaking point to watch is whether the stock market can withstand current yields, as a sell-off would trigger a devastating negative wealth effect for consumers.

### Historical Rating of Fed Chairmen
* **Alan Greenspan:** 
  * **Macro Management (9-10/10):** Highly praised for macroeconomy handling.
  * **Regulation (3-4/10):** Heavily faulted for deregulation and the philosophy that markets are self-equilibrating, planting seeds for the Global Financial Crisis.
* **Ben Bernanke:** 
  * **Crisis Intervention (High Mark):** Credited with saving the global economy from a second Great Depression through aggressive financial system stabilization and pioneering QE/forward guidance tools that were later reused during COVID-19.
* **Janet Yellen:** 
  * **Labor Recovery (Very Good Record):** Praised for recognizing deep structural issues post-GFC and keeping rates low longer to foster a fuller labor market recovery, though she did not face a moment of maximum peril.
* **Jay Powell:** 
  * **COVID & Inflation Catch-up (Extraordinary Credit):** Credited with preventing a 2020 financial collapse via aggressive private-sector backstops and achieving a rare soft landing by bringing inflation down without crushing employment, despite initial delays in recognizing post-pandemic inflation driven by trade wars and Middle East conflicts.

## Private Credit's Clock is Ticking w/ Glenn Schorr & Ken Worthington | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 72

### TL;DR
Steve Eisman, joined by Glenn Schorr of Evercore and Ken Worthington of JP Morgan, explores the looming refinancing crunch in private credit software loans, the mechanics of retail broker cash yield optimization via agentic AI, crypto use cases versus Bitcoin’s store-of-value thesis, and a booming second-quarter earnings season for major investment banks.

### Retail Brokers, Cash Yields, & Agentic AI
* **Brokerage Cash Revenue Model:** 
  * Retail brokers (such as Charles Schwab and LPL) generate a substantial portion of their profits from "lazy cash" — uninvested customer sweep balances from which the firm captures interest spread or fees.
  * **Ken Worthington:** Notes that if an investor manages their cash actively (e.g., in money market funds), the broker's fee is significantly lower than the spread earned on lazy cash.
* **Agentic AI Threat & Friction:**
  * **AI Efficiency vs. Profitability:** Agentic AI could theoretically automate cash optimization by paying bills and sweeping idle funds into higher-yielding products based on spending habits, potentially squeezing broker cash net interest income.
  * **Regulatory & Systemic Hurdles:** Worthington notes that systemic safeguards and regulators are unlikely to allow frictionless, micro-optimized checking-account swapping across financial institutions without triggering stability risks.
  * **Execution Reliability:** Worthington points out that AI agents are currently incorrect the majority of the time when managing money and must reach near-perfect reliability before high-net-worth investors will relinquish control.
* **Resilient Financial Models:**
  * **Glenn Schorr:** Prefers large diversified banks (e.g., JP Morgan) and wealth managers (e.g., Morgan Stanley) that bundle primary checking accounts with comprehensive services like trust, estate, and tax planning, making customer switching friction high.
  * **Charles Schwab Valuation Debate:** Eisman questions whether Schwab's business model long-term equilibrium should rely on customer inaction, while Worthington maintains a **Buy** recommendation on Schwab, arguing that clients retain the option to manually optimize and firms can package their own agentic services.

### Private Equity & Private Credit
* **Private Credit Inflows & Redemptions:**
  * **Glenn Schorr:** Inflows into wealth-channel direct lending products have nearly dried up, while redemption requests remain elevated, with most funds exceeding the 5% per quarter limit.
  * Schorr notes that while underlying software investments continue to generate cash flow and margins, fundraising momentum has stalled.
* **Private Equity Monetizations & Valuations:**
  * PE performance has underperformed public markets for the first time since 2008–2009, struggling to keep pace with the Magnificent Seven.
  * While some managers (e.g., **KKR**) reported record monetizations, others face sluggish exit markets despite record highs in public equity indexes, creating pressure from LPs demanding liquidity.
  * **Vintage Multiples Challenge:** Assets purchased during the 2020–2021 zero-interest-rate environment at high multiples are facing extended holding periods as companies grow into their valuations.
* **The Software Refinancing Time Bomb:**
  * **Steve Eisman:** Highlights the public "SaaS apocalypse" (e.g., ServiceNow down over 50% from its peak) and asserts that private equity software company valuations must similarly be down roughly 50%.
  * **Refinancing Timeline:** Eisman predicts a major reckoning starting in late 2027 and accelerating in **2028 and 2029**, when approximately **$270 billion** of software maturities held by financial sponsors come due for refinancing.
  * **Lender vs. Sponsor Standoff:** Schorr notes that restructuring conversations involve a spectrum of outcomes, from a few keys handed back to lenders, to stalemate negotiations, to opportunistic credit funds stepping in with higher yields and stricter terms to take over positions.

### Crypto, Stablecoins, & Bitcoin's Relevance
* **Crypto Ecosystem & Token Proliferation:**
  * **Ken Worthington:** Views crypto not strictly as a fiat currency hedge, but as an asset class where blockchain technology and token utility co-exist and proliferate (citing blockchains launched by Coinbase and Circle).
  * **Bitcoin vs. Alt-Chains:** Worthington distinguishes Bitcoin as a single-use store of value facing concentration risk, whereas blockchains like Ethereum and Solana support active application development and token trading.
* **Stablecoins & Payment System Friction:**
  * **Steve Eisman:** Argues that payment incumbents like Visa and Mastercard are elite, deeply entrenched operators unlikely to cede territory to new entrants, making Circle's push into the core payment system extremely difficult.
  * Worthington points to cross-border remittances, 24/7 trading settlement layers, and foreign-market dollarization as viable near-term use cases for stablecoins like USDC alongside ongoing traditional experimentation.

### Big Bank Earnings Review
* **Second-Quarter Results Overview:**
  * **Glenn Schorr:** Characterizes big bank earnings as stunning, noting that fundamental drivers are operating at near-peak efficiency.
  * **Investment Banking & Trading Performance:** Investment banking revenue surged **38%**, and trading revenue jumped **47%** (with equity trading revenues up approximately **68%** and margin balances on borrow up **50%**).
  * **Asset Management & Expenses:** Asset and wealth management posted **7%** organic growth amid all-time high markets, while operating leverage remained positive with expenses well-controlled.
  * **Deposit Competition:** Deposit costs are ticking up due to real competition, representing the only mixed fundamental area in an otherwise exceptionally strong quarter.

## The Real State of the American Consumer w/ Three Evercore Analysts | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 65

- Channel: Steve Eisman

- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Michael Binetti (Consumer Analyst, Evercore); David Palmer (Consumer Analyst, Evercore)

---

**TL;DR** 
The K-shaped recovery is evolving into a "limping" middle class, forcing a mass consumer migration away from high-priced fast food and legacy carb-heavy brands toward value-driven platforms like Walmart. Consequently, brands with execution power or luxury appeal (such as Walmart and Ralph Lauren) are thriving, while legacy food manufacturers, mismanaged retail giants like Nike, and the frozen housing market face severe structural headwinds.

### The Cracking Middle Class & The Flight to Value

* **The Limping Middle Class:** While high-income consumers (the top quintile driving nearly 40% of US retail spending) remain insulated by the wealth effect, the middle-income cohort has experienced growing financial stress over the past 12 months.
* **Fast Food Price Resistance:** Lower-income consumers (earning under $50,000) are dropping out of the restaurant market. Fast food chains raised prices by roughly 40% post-COVID while wages rose only 24%, triggering severe consumer backlash and pushing average same-store sales growth to negative 1%. 
    * **Casual Dining Outperforms:** Chains like Texas Roadhouse and Longhorn Steakhouse are winning share because they limited price hikes to roughly 20%, maintaining a stronger relative value proposition.
* **Walmart’s Trade-In Flywheel:** Higher-income households (earning $150,000+) are actively trading down to Walmart, utilizing Walmart Plus for everyday essentials. Walmart, Costco, and Amazon now command a combined 24% of all US retail sales.
* **The Frozen Housing Market:** Existing home sales are severely depressed, with roughly 30 million homes "locked up" due to sub-3.5% mortgage rates and stagnant capital gains tax thresholds. This housing freeze has caused a four-year decline in home improvement demand, dragging down the retail sector.

### Brand Winners & Losers in Specialty Retail

* **Ralph Lauren's Premium Pivot:** Ralph Lauren successfully engineered a brand turnaround by pulling back from promotional off-price channels (like TJ Maxx) and department store couponing, raising average unit prices by 60% since 2018. They have recaptured Gen Z and aspirational buyers by shifting marketing dollars to high-end lifestyle experiences, including the Polo Bar and Ralph’s Coffee.
* **Nike's Direct-to-Consumer Misstep:** Nike over-pivoted to direct-to-consumer (D2C) e-commerce, damaging wholesale relationships and losing critical retail shelf space. This strategic vacuum allowed competitors like Hoka, On Running, Adidas, and New Balance (now an $8 billion brand) to rapidly capture market share. Turnaround efforts under returned veteran leadership are moving slowly.
* **Lululemon's Product Vacuum:** Lululemon is struggling to sustain its premium market share after the departure of its lead designer. Rather than maintaining brand consistency, the company chased temporary product trends (e.g., belt bags), allowing newer competitors like Alo Yoga and Vuori to win over premium athletic wear consumers.
* **Department Store Stabilization:** Macy's is successfully consolidating its retail footprint toward a highly profitable core of roughly 400 stores. Due to disciplined inventory management post-COVID and strong luxury performance from Bloomingdale's, mid-tier department stores are stabilizing and are no longer automatic short targets for investors.

### Packaged Food Headwinds & Restaurant Turnarounds

* **A "Very Dark Time" for Legacy Food:** Major legacy packaged food brands (such as Campbell Soup, Kraft Heinz, General Mills, and Conagra) are facing structural decline. Campbell Soup projects 2027 input cost inflation of up to 6%, which will be highly difficult to pass through to an inflation-weary consumer.
* **The Protein & Satiety Revolution:** Consumers are rapidly shifting away from legacy carb-heavy products (e.g., sugary breakfast cereals) to protein-rich, satiety-focused foods. 
    * **The GLP-1 Effect:** While GLP-1 weight-loss drugs act as a direct ~0.5% headwind to total food consumption, the broader cultural shift toward wellness, portion control, and protein is a massive threat to traditional food portfolios.
* **Restaurant Stock Outlooks:**
    * **Starbucks (Positive):** Poised for recovery under new CEO Brian Niccol. Same-store sales grew 7% in North America last quarter, though the company must now translate these sales into meaningful earnings flow-through.
    * **Chipotle (Neutral/Watch):** Despite a 20% stock correction and executive turnover, Chipotle remains fundamentally strong. The deployment of automated kitchen throughput equipment in half of its stores by year-end is projected to add 2 to 4 percentage points to same-store sales.
    * **Underperformers:** Wendy's, Pizza Hut, and Olive Garden are heavily exposed to price-sensitive cohorts and face persistent traffic declines.

### Big Box Operational Playbooks & Technology Integration

* **Home Depot vs. Lowe's:** Home Depot is favored over Lowe's due to its early, aggressive investments in expanding its "Pro" (professional contractor) distribution network, effectively widening its addressable market. Lowe's is currently copying this playbook but carries significantly higher debt leverage (over 3x debt-to-EBITDA).
* **Auto Parts Normalization:** Auto parts distributors (O'Reilly, AutoZone) remain highly resilient as the average age of the US vehicle fleet climbs to 13 years. However, their stocks are experiencing short-term corrections as same-store sales growth decelerates from peak pandemic levels back toward historic norms.
* **The Reality of Retail AI:** While AI remains in its early stages, top-tier retailers are successfully deploying it in two primary areas:
    1. **Logistics Optimization:** Using predictive modeling to streamline supply chains from Asia to US distribution centers.
    2. **Hyper-Personalized Marketing:** Transforming online storefronts (such as Macy's) from static displays into personalized digital vending machines based on individual user data.
    3. **Agentic Shopping Resistance:** Approximately half of consumers state they do not trust "agentic AI" to shop on their behalf—a skepticism that closely mirrors early-stage consumer resistance to mobile phone shopping twelve years ago.

## Is Private Equity Destroying the Life Insurance Industry? | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 64

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8lsoSdCuFfI
- Published: 2026-06-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260615.html#video-8lsoSdCuFfI
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Tom Gallagher (Life Insurance Analyst, Evercore)

---

**TL;DR** 
While private equity (PE) firms assume more illiquidity and concentration risk than traditional life insurers to harvest an extra 100 basis points of yield, they are not taking reckless credit risks. Despite sector-wide de-risking, public life insurance companies continue to trade at depressed single-digit multiples due to lingering legacy product anxieties and a reluctance to aggressively buy back stock.

---

### The Role and Risk Profile of Private Equity in Life Insurance

* **The PE Playbook:** Large private equity firms view life insurance entities as permanent capital vehicles. 
    * Firms like Apollo (Athene), KKR (Global Atlantic), Carlyle (Corebridge stake), and Blackstone use these platforms to fuel growth in their core asset management businesses, collecting fees of 40 to 50 basis points on the assets under management.
* **Yield and Asset Differences:** Alternative managers typically achieve yields of 6.0% to 6.5% on private credit portfolios, compared to the 5.0% to 5.5% achieved by traditional life insurers.
    * This additional 100 basis points of yield is primarily achieved by taking on illiquidity risk and investing in structured securities (such as CLOs, asset-backed securities, and aircraft leasing) rather than traditional direct corporate bonds.
* **Portfolio Concentration:** Unlike traditional insurers that run on a conservative "zero-loss expectation" model, alternative managers are willing to take on concentrated positions that are roughly twice the size of those held by traditional companies.
* **Segmented Credit Risk:** While credit quality remains stable among the largest alternative players, higher investment risks are more visible within mid-sized, alternative-backed annuity players (e.g., Security Benefit Life and Sammons Group).

### Reinsurance and Capital Arbitrage

* **Regulatory Relief:** Reinsurance is widely used by US life insurers to bypass highly conservative state statutory regulatory regimes, unlocking capital tied up by onerous discount rates and reserve requirements.
* **Jurisdictional Shifts:** Bermuda has matured to become nearly as conservative as the US statutory framework. As regulatory arbitrage options shrink in Bermuda, some insurers are turning to more lenient and less transparent jurisdictions, such as the Cayman Islands.
* **Captive Reinsurance Permits:** State regulators permit "internal captive reinsurance"—where a company reinsures its own book of business—as an accommodation to prevent local insurers from abandoning their domestic regulatory jurisdictions entirely.

### The Legacy Liabilities: Variable Annuities, SGUL, and Long-Term Care

* **Variable Annuities (VA) Mismatch:** Historically, insurers sold 20-year payout guarantees (6% to 7% annual rollups) but hedged them with short-term (three-month) equity options. During the 2008 Great Financial Crisis, volatility spiked, rendering renewals cost-prohibitive and exposing naked liabilities.
* **Secondary Guarantee Universal Life (SGUL):** Insurers priced these high-interest-rate guarantee policies under the assumption that policy lapse rates would hover around mid-to-high single digits. Instead, because guaranteed rates far outpaced post-crisis market rates, lapse rates fell to less than 1%, triggering massive charges (e.g., Lincoln's $2 billion charge in 2022).
* **Long-Term Care (LTC) Deficit:** Priced in the 1990s and early 2000s under flawed assumptions (8% to 10% lapse rates and 18-month average claims), LTC blocks imploded due to falling interest rates, longer lifespans from Alzheimer's care, and <1% lapse rates.
    * **Accounting Delay:** Because older accounting rules did not require immediate margin impairment adjustments until the entire block became unprofitable, major detonations took nearly two decades to manifest, highlighted by General Electric's $15 billion charge in 2018.

### Valuation Disconnects, Consolidation, and the Buyback Dilemma

* **Depressed Multiples:** Despite extensive de-risking, many public life insurance companies trade at incredibly low forward price-to-earnings (PE) ratios:
    * **Brighthouse:** 3.3x
    * **Lincoln National:** 4.6x
    * **Corebridge Financial:** 5.6x
    * **Equitable Holdings:** 5.8x
* **The Buyback Hesitation:** Despite trading at deep discounts where stock repurchases offer an obvious return on investment, companies avoid "going private" or executing extreme buybacks to avoid shrinking their market caps into obscurity, which would render them irrelevant to benchmark-driven institutional investors.
* **Strategic Consolidation:** The proposed Equitable-Corebridge merger highlights a scale-driven play to internalize asset flows. By acquiring Corebridge, Equitable can divert $55 billion in annual fixed-income flows away from external managers (like BlackRock and Blackstone) and into its own asset manager, AllianceBernstein (which is 69% owned by Equitable).
