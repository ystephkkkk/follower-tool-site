### TL;DR
Masayoshi "Masa" Son predicts artificial intelligence will become 10,000 times smarter than humans within a decade, viewing his past decades of investing merely as a warm-up for the coming superintelligence and physical robotics era. Meanwhile, Blackstone leadership confirms that AI infrastructure spending and enterprise portfolio implementations are driving real economic growth despite higher corporate borrowing costs.

### AI Adoption in Enterprise Portfolios
- **Broad Portfolio Impact:** Rodney Zemmel notes that AI value creation has expanded beyond initial software and services hypotheses into industrials (e.g., Chamberlain's digital access business), healthcare data/customer interactions, and consumer decision-making.
- **The Activation Barrier:** Zemmel explains that deploying AI at scale requires overcoming a difficult activation barrier that takes time and close coordination:
  - **Leadership Requirement:** Zemmel states business impact only occurs when led by business and technology working in a tight, iterative partnership rather than being tech-led alone.
  - **Strategic Focus:** Zemmel advises CEOs to focus on just one to two initiatives rather than trying to implement everything everywhere at once.

### Economic Weather Report and Infrastructure Spending
- **Corporate Borrowing Costs & Growth:** Winfield Sickles reports that 10-year Treasury yields have pushed through 5.3% (the highest level since 2002) driven by growth, and corporate borrowing costs are higher, but growth drivers from the AI infrastructure boom remain intact.
- **Spending Scale and Cloud Revenue:** Sickles notes hyperscaler spend reached upwards of $800 billion this year and will likely exceed $1 trillion next year, with cloud revenue up between 50% to 120% last quarter across major names.
- **Broader Economic Indicators:** Sickles highlights that Q3 GDP estimates are running around 3% (above US trend), and logistics businesses are now seeing growth driven by the downstream impact of AI infrastructure build-outs.

### Masayoshi Son's Vision for Superintelligence and Robotics
- **The 50-Year Conviction:** Masa Son recounts crying on the street at age 17 upon seeing a microprocessor photo, realizing it would eventually surpass human brain power. Now 69, he views his entire track record (Alibaba, Yahoo, Arm, Vision Fund) as a warm-up.
- **Ecosystem Investing Strategy:** Son explains that SoftBank avoids picking just one segment, instead investing across the entire AI ecosystem (LLMs, chips, data centers) as a highly involved major shareholder (holding 20% to 80% stakes) to create portfolio synergy.
- **Physical Robotics Production:** Son reveals that SoftBank is quietly mass-producing robots via a network of more than 30 portfolio robotics companies, aiming to free humans from tiring daily chores.
- **Personal AI Usage:** Son reports using AI as a brainstorming "thought partner" for 3 hours a day on weekdays and up to 7 hours a day on weekends, stating he cannot live without it.
- **Investment Philosophy:** Son advocates for the 70% rule: "Forget about short-term, small money. Think big... If the wish is so big, dream is so big, you still get 70% achievement."

### Investment Perspectives and AI Safety Debates
- **Speed Over Slowing Down:** Son firmly rejects slowing down AI development due to alignment or deception risks, arguing that technological progress is the solution, and society must teach AI agents "good intent" and etiquette just like automotive rules.
- **Steve Schwarzman on Contrarian Investing:** Steve Schwarzman notes that Masa Son has "nerves of steel" and bets all his chips when he identifies a multi-decade technological trend.
- **Long-Term Societal Benefits:** Schwarzman shares his early support for AI safety (funding Oxford in 2019 and MIT in 2018) and expresses confidence that humanity will solve safety challenges while unlocking profound medical advancements, individualized education via smartphones, and wealth-increasing robotics.
