### TL;DR
Yann LeCun, Founder and Chair of Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI), explained why Silicon Valley is overly "LLM-pilled" and detailed his $1 billion seed-funded bet on "world models" and physical AI—funded entirely without Silicon Valley VCs. He also warned that the concentration of AI power among a few US and Chinese companies poses a major threat, advocating for open-source foundation models to ensure global AI diversity and sovereignty.

### The Limits of LLMs & Silicon Valley's Blind Spot
* **The "LLM-Pilled" Echo Chamber:** LeCun argued that while large language models (LLMs) are useful and commercially successful, they are not a path to human-like intelligence or superintelligence.
  * **Discrete Symbols Only:** LLMs rely on self-supervised learning to predict the next item in a sequence of discrete symbols (human language, computer code, mathematics), which leaves physical AI as a "complete blind spot."
  * **Pixel-Level Failure:** Generative models cannot accurately predict the physical world because most real-world information is unpredictable at the pixel level (e.g., predicting what an unseen part of a room looks like as a camera rotates).
* **Flawed Agentic Systems:** Current agentic systems apply trained recipes without being able to predict the consequences of their actions, which LeCun called an unreliable and unsafe way to build agents.

### AMI's $1 Billion Bet on World Models
* **Spinning Out from Meta:** AMI (Advanced Machine Intelligence) began as an internal project at Meta. While CEO Mark Zuckerberg and CTO Andrew Bosworth were supportive, Meta's broader AI organization increasingly focused on LLMs, and AMI's industrial applications fell outside Meta's core interests.
* **Record Seed Round ("A Coconut"):** AMI raised **$1 billion** in seed funding—likely the largest seed round outside the US—as a French company.
  * **Investor Breakdown:** **40%** from European sources, **33%** from the US, **27%** from Asia (including one fund from Dubai), and **$0 (0%)** from Silicon Valley VCs.
  * **Why No Silicon Valley VCs:** LeCun noted the round was oversubscribed, and Silicon Valley VCs wanted control they were not going to get.
* **Technology & Commercial Focus:** LeCun has worked on world models for **20 years** (advocating publicly for **10 years**, with real breakthroughs beginning **5 years ago** via self-supervised learning on video).
  * **No Language:** AMI is staying away from language entirely, focusing instead on video, audio, and sensor data.
  * **Industrial Applications:** Target use cases include physical AI, industrial process control, predictive maintenance, and anomaly detection.
  * **Robotics:** While robotics will be a big business, LeCun noted that current robotics companies do not know how to make robots smart enough to be useful, and domestic robots will require conceptual AI breakthroughs.

### AI Safety, Concentration of Power & Open Source
* **Cybersecurity Transition:** In response to moderator Shelly Banjo's questions on AI risks, LeCun said he is not worried about ad-supported chatbots, but expects a short period where AI favors cyber attackers over defenders before all future software is hardened with AI.
* **Concentration of Power:** LeCun's primary concern is that citizens' information diets are increasingly mediated by AI controlled by a handful of companies on the US West Coast or in China, which he called "extremely dangerous."
* **The Case for Open Source:** Open-source foundation models are essential for countries to achieve AI sovereignty and for organizations to build assistants that reflect diverse languages, cultures, and value systems.
  * **Meta's Open-Source History:** LeCun was influential in convincing Meta leadership to open-source **Llama 2**, which jump-started the global AI ecosystem. However, **Llama 4** was a disappointment, prompting Zuckerberg to reboot Meta's AI effort and briefly pull back from open-sourcing.
  * **China's Lead:** LeCun lamented that the most powerful open-source AI systems are currently coming out of China, though he noted that because they are open source, they can be unconditioned and fine-tuned for any purpose.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Cybersecurity & Software Safety:** There will be a short transition period where AI tools facilitate cyberattacks and favor attackers over defenders, followed by an era of "unprecedented safety of software systems" as all future software is hardened with AI. — *Yann LeCun*
* **Domestic Robotics Timeline:** Useful domestic robots are "not for tomorrow" and will not be a very short-term business, as they require several conceptual breakthroughs in AI. — *Yann LeCun*
