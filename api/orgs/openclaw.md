## Peter Steinberger: What Happens When 4.7 Million People Let It Cook

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=whcfSGN6CAU
- Published: 2026-08-10
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260810.html#video-whcfSGN6CAU
- Speakers: Peter Steinberger (Founder, OpenClaw)

---

### TL;DR
Peter Steinberger shares how his side project OpenClaw exploded from a simple WhatsApp relay built out of personal annoyance into an open-source juggernaut with 4.7 million weekly downloads, and explains why developer velocity depends entirely on building things that genuinely excite you.

### Origins and Product-Market Fit
- **The Annoyance Catalyst:** 
  - Created in November 2025 because there was no easy way to send prompts from a phone to a computer to check on coding agents while away.
  - Built a WhatsApp relay in about an hour by letting the model cook in a terminal session.
- **The Evolution to OpenClaw:** 
  - Expanded to Discord after receiving a community pull request, temporarily naming the project Claudius, then Claudebot, before settling on OpenClaw.
  - Initially struggled to get Twitter followers to care about the agent's magical, proactive feel until testing it in WhatsApp group chats and witnessing strong emotional reactions (amazement and fear).
- **Viral Explosion:** 
  - Launched a Discord server during the first week of January 2026 for people to watch him build in the open.
  - A launch daemon ensured the agent kept running after pressing Ctrl+C, resulting in 800 messages upon waking up and immediate viral pickup by tech reporters.

### Scale, Burnout, and the Open Source Toll
- **Staggering Metrics:** 
  - Reached over 18,000 different people opening an issue or pull request, totaling over 111,000 contributions, with almost 3,000 people contributing commits.
  - Downloads bottomed out at 835,000 weekly downloads in May before peaking at an all-time high of 4.7 million weekly downloads.
- **The Cost of Attention:** 
  - The sudden influx of reporters, leaked phone numbers, and security reports nearly caused Steinberger to delete the entire project and severely strained his mental health.
  - Shifted time away from building toward legal work (setting up a 501(c)(3) American nonprofit) and defensive security hardening.
- **Security and Maintenance Pitfalls:** 
  - Fought off a wave of security reports, noting that only about 0.3% of scanned skills were actually malicious (out of 67,000 scanned).
  - Accumulated around 9,500 configuration options, making the codebase increasingly difficult to maintain and breaking user setups when shipping updates.
  - Faced sudden dependency disruptions when Anthropic gave roughly 24 hours' notice before disabling subscriptions for Opus, which OpenClaw had been heavily optimized around.

### Philosophy of Building
- **Personal Brand Over Products:** 
  - Emphasizes that any product can be forked or cloned, making a personal brand vital before starting a new venture.
  - Believes programming is just a means to an end; the real passion lies in building.
- **The Power of Fun:** 
  - Discovered that "fun is velocity"—weeks spent enjoying the build process resulted in a visibly better product, whereas maintenance work slowed momentum to a halt.
  - Advises building software that solves personal annoyances, noting that user #1 should be yourself and users #2 through #20 should be friends.
- **Current Team and Infrastructure:** 
  - The OpenClaw foundation now has 10 people on payroll and is actively hiring additional roles, including a CEO.
  - Nvidia stepped in early to provide personnel who took over much of the project's security workload.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Open Source Agent Capabilities:** Peter Steinberger claims that perpetually running, proactive AI agents are entirely possible today, but are currently bottlenecked by token costs and the lack of efficient systems to handle large context resets without burning excessive money on idle computations.

## Builders Unscripted: Ep. 1 - Peter Steinberger, Creator of OpenClaw

- Channel: OpenAI
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9jgcT0Fqt7U
- Published: 2026-02-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260224.html#video-9jgcT0Fqt7U
- Speakers: Peter Steinberger (Creator, OpenClaw); Romain Huet (Head of Developer Experience, OpenAI)

---

**TL;DR**
Peter Steinberger, creator of OpenClaw, discusses how "agentic engineering" has fundamentally shifted the developer's role from writing syntax to managing intent, enabling a level of productivity where a single builder can maintain over 120 projects and 90,000 annual contributions.

### The Genesis of OpenClaw and Autonomous Problem-Solving
* **Emergence of a Phenomenon:** OpenClaw transitioned from a personal "playground" to a global project in weeks, resulting in "ClawCon" events with thousands of attendees in San Francisco and hundreds in Vienna.
* **The "Aha" Moment in Autonomy:** Steinberger realized the power of agents when his bot autonomously handled a voice message it wasn't programmed for.
    * **Spontaneous Problem Solving:** The agent identified an audio file without an extension, used `ffmpeg` to convert it, found an OpenAI API key in the environment, and used `curl` to transcribe the audio via Whisper—all without specific instructions.
* **The Personal Assistant Vision:** OpenClaw is designed as a personal, highly privileged assistant that can access a user’s computer, WhatsApp, and deployment tools like Vercel to "yield things into existence."

### The "Codex" Workflow: Scaling Human Output
* **Massive Productivity Gains:** Steinberger recorded 90,000 GitHub contributions across 120+ projects in one year, a feat he attributes to mastering agentic harnesses.
* **Conversation as Engineering:** Steinberger views programming now as a conversation rather than manual typing. 
    * **The "Any Questions?" Prompt:** He emphasizes asking the model "Do you have any questions?" to prevent it from making incorrect default assumptions based on stale training data.
* **The "Agentic Trap":** He warns against over-optimizing setups (the "agentic trap") and instead advocates for a "vibe coding" approach—focusing on architecture and gut feelings for how long a prompt will take to execute.
* **Accepting "Boring" Code:** Steinberger argues that most code is simply data transformation. He accepts "slop" or code that isn't exactly how he would write it, provided it fulfills the architectural intent and passes performance requirements.

### Redefining Open Source: From Pull Requests to "Prompt Requests"
* **Intent over Syntax:** With 2,000+ open PRs, Steinberger no longer reviews every line of code. Instead, he uses agents to summarize the *intent* of a contributor's PR.
* **The AI Maintainer Workflow:** He uses voice commands to discuss a PR's architectural fit with an agent. If the intent is sound, he uses a "land PR" command to have the agent rewrite the solution to fit the broader system.
* **Self-Modifying Software:** OpenClaw's default installation is a `git clone` where the agent sits inside its own source code, allowing it to modify itself based on user prompts.

### Security Challenges and the "Hacker’s Paradise"
* **Unsolved Prompt Injection:** Steinberger acknowledges that while prompt injection is a concern, modern models are increasingly resilient. He uses a "soldier.md" file to define core values and operational boundaries for his agents.
* **The Public Internet Risk:** A significant friction point exists between Steinberger’s vision of a "hacker’s paradise" (accessible via private networks) and security researchers who flag the lack of enterprise-grade login limits as a vulnerability when users mistakenly expose the tool to the public internet.
* **Sandboxing Evolution:** To mitigate risks, he transitioned OpenClaw from running directly on a Mac Studio ("the castle") to localized containers.

### Strategic Advice for Builders
* **The Playful Mindset:** Steinberger advises developers to approach AI tools "playfully" to avoid frustration. He views agentic engineering as a skill like playing the guitar—it requires time to develop a "gut feeling" for model behavior.
* **High Agency as the Core Value:** He echoes the sentiment that AI will not replace developers; rather, developers who use AI effectively will replace those who do not.
* **Lowering the Barrier to Entry:** The current toolchain allows builders to create solutions that solve 100% of their specific problems, even if an existing open-source tool already solves 70%.

> "Software is still hard, but you're so much faster now... I had this realization that now I can build anything." — **Peter Steinberger**

## OpenClaw: The Viral AI Agent that Broke the Internet - Peter Steinberger | Lex Fridman Podcast #491

---

**TL;DR**
OpenClaw creator Peter Steinberger describes the project’s rapid evolution into the fastest-growing repository in GitHub history, marking a "phase shift" from language-based AI to agentic systems that autonomously execute complex tasks and modify their own source code.

### The Genesis and Capabilities of OpenClaw
* **Rapid Prototyping:** Steinberger built the initial prototype in one hour as a thin WhatsApp wrapper for the Claude CLI. It evolved into a system-level assistant that reached over 180,000 GitHub stars in weeks.
* **Autonomous Problem Solving:** The agent demonstrates emergent capabilities without specific training, such as identifying a file as an audio type, using `ffmpeg` to convert it, and finding an API key to run it through a translation service.
* **Self-Modifying Code:** OpenClaw is designed with "self-introspection," allowing it to read its own documentation and source code to debug and modify its own harness. 
* **The "Proactive" Heartbeat:** Unlike traditional chatbots, OpenClaw can be programmed with a "heartbeat" (a regular cron-style trigger) that allows it to initiate conversations or follow up on tasks without a human prompt.

### Agentic Engineering vs. "Vibe Coding"
* **The "Zen" of Short Prompts:** Steinberger identifies a learning curve in AI programming: beginners use short prompts, intermediate users use complex multi-agent orchestrations, and experts return to short, high-level prompts once the agent has sufficient context of the codebase.
* **Voice-Driven Development:** Steinberger rarely writes code manually, instead using voice prompts to direct up to 10 agents simultaneously. He recorded over 6,600 commits in January alone.
* **Model Comparisons:**
    * **Claude Opus 4.6:** Described as "American" and sycophantic; better for roleplay and creative task-taking but requires "Plan Mode" to stay on track.
    * **GPT-5.3 Codex:** Described as "German" and reliable; a "weirdo in the corner" that overthinks but reads significantly more code by default, making it superior for large-scale refactors.
* **The Agentic Trap:** Steinberger warns against over-automating the loop. Successful building requires "empathy" for the model—understanding that the agent starts every session with an empty context and needs specific "pointers" to navigate a 100,000-line codebase.

### The Security Minefield and "AI Psychosis"
* **System-Level Risk:** Because OpenClaw has permission to execute shell commands and access local files, it is a high-stakes security environment. Steinberger emphasizes that users should never run OpenClaw on the open internet without private network protections.
* **Prompt Injection:** While injection remains a threat, Steinberger argues that higher-intelligence models are more resilient. He discourages the use of "cheap" or local models (like Haiku) for agentic tasks because they are too "gullible."
* **The MoldBook Phenomenon:** The "MoldBook" social network, where agents posted manifestos, triggered a wave of "AI psychosis"—a mix of viral clickbait and genuine fear. Steinberger clarifies that much of the "scary" agent behavior was actually human-prompted "art" rather than spontaneous AI rebellion.

### The Corporate "War Room" and Future Career
* **The Anthropic Name Dispute:** Originally called ClaudeBot (with a "W"), the project was forced to rename after Anthropic lawyers intervened.
* **Atomic Renaming Saga:** The transition to "OpenClaw" was a "war game" effort. Crypto-squatters used scripts to snipe GitHub handles, NPM packages, and Twitter accounts within seconds of Steinberger clicking "rename," forcing him to spend $10,000 on a business account to reclaim the brand.
* **The Meta vs. OpenAI Decision:** Steinberger is currently weighing offers from Meta and OpenAI. 
    * **The Zuckerberg Appeal:** Mark Zuckerberg personally tinkered with the code and engaged in a "10-minute fight" with Steinberger over which model was superior.
    * **The OpenAI Appeal:** OpenAI offers "Thor’s Hammer"—access to the most advanced upcoming models and massive compute tokens via recent hardware deals (e.g., Cerebras).

### The Future of the "App" Economy
* **The Death of Apps:** Steinberger predicts that agents will make 80% of specialized apps (like MyFitnessPal or travel booking tools) obsolete. Users will simply tell an agent their goal, and the agent will interact with the service's API or "scrape" its web interface.
* **Apps as APIs:** Software companies will be forced to pivot from consumer-facing UIs to agent-facing APIs. If a site (like Medium or Twitter) blocks bot access, users will simply move to platforms that are "agent-friendly."
* **Mourning the Craft:** While manual programming may become a "hobby like knitting," Steinberger views this as the "power to the people" moment, where anyone with an idea can build complex software without knowing how to "splice an array."

## OpenClaw Creator: Why 80% Of Apps Will Disappear

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4uzGDAoNOZc
- Published: 2026-02-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260207.html#video-4uzGDAoNOZc
- Speakers: Peter Steinberger (Creator, OpenClaw); Raphael Schaad (Partner, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR** OpenClaw's local-first AI agent paradigm is rapidly transforming personal computing by enabling deep device integration and intelligent task execution, leading creator Peter Steinberger to predict the obsolescence of 80% of traditional apps.

### OpenClaw's Viral Impact and Core Differentiator

*   **Internet Sensation:** OpenClaw, an open-source personal AI agent, rapidly garnered over **160,000 GitHub stars**, sparking extensive community projects like Maltbook, where bots communicate amongst themselves and even hire humans for real-world tasks.
*   **Local-First Advantage:** Creator Peter Steinberger attributes OpenClaw's unprecedented success to its ability to run directly on the user's computer.
    *   **Deep Device Integration:** This local execution grants it control over any device function and connected appliance (e.g., oven, Tesla, Sonos, bed temperature) that the user's machine can access.
    *   **System-Wide Access:** It can deeply search a user's entire computer, surprising users by discovering forgotten data like audio files to generate narratives about their past year.

### The Evolving Landscape of AI Agents

*   **Bot-to-Bot/Human Interaction:** The natural progression involves AI agents negotiating with other bots (e.g., for restaurant bookings) or leveraging human assistance for tasks that require real-world interaction (e.g., calling an old-fashioned restaurant).
*   **Swarm Intelligence:** The paradigm is shifting from a centralized "God AI" towards decentralized "swarm intelligence."
    *   **Specialization:** Like humans in a society, individual AI agents can specialize in different domains (e.g., private life, work, relationships), leading to more effective and powerful collective intelligence.

### Genesis and Autonomous Capabilities

*   **"Aha" Moment:** Peter's initial motivation was a simple need for his computer to "do stuff," particularly with coding. The breakthrough arrived in November when he rebuilt the system as a natural, conversational interface rather than a terminal.
*   **Exceeding Expectations:** A rudimentary prototype, built in just an hour, soon demonstrated profound autonomy:
    *   **Unforeseen Problem Solving:** During a trip to Marrakesh, the agent autonomously processed a voice message, even though voice recognition wasn't explicitly built-in. It identified the unknown file type, converted it to `.wave`, transcribed it using an external OpenAI API (choosing it over a local `whisper` install to save time), and responded—all in approximately **9 seconds**.
    *   **Intelligent Decision-Making:** This moment revealed the agent's advanced creative problem-solving and contextual intelligence, capable of making optimal choices for efficiency without explicit programming.

### The Future of Software: Agents Over Apps

*   **80% of Apps Obsolete:** Peter Steinberger predicts that **80% of current applications will disappear.**
    *   **Natural Data Management:** Apps primarily managing data (e.g., My Fitness Pal, to-do lists) will be replaced by agents that naturally integrate and automate these functions based on user behavior and context.
    *   **Survival of Sensor-Based Apps:** Only apps with direct sensor interfaces or unique hardware integration may endure.
*   **Data Ownership and Privacy:** While large language model companies currently hold a "moat" due to their advanced models, the long-term value shifts to user ownership of data.
    *   **Local Memories:** OpenClaw ensures users retain control of their "memories" as local markdown files on their own machines, addressing concerns about sensitive personal information.

### Contrarian Development Philosophy

*   **Local-First Development:** Peter champions local coding using tools like `codex` for its broad file scanning, contrasting with the prevailing trend of cloud-based development.
*   **Streamlined Workflow:** He opts for multiple copies of the `main` repository over `git worktrees` or branches to minimize complexity and cognitive overhead.
*   **CLI Preference:** OpenClaw intentionally avoids traditional "classical MCP (Menu, Command, Prompt) crap" in favor of Command Line Interfaces (CLIs). This allows for more elegant, scalable agent interactions by leveraging bots' proficiency with Unix tools, mirroring how skilled humans interact with computers.
*   **"Soul.md":** A private, non-open-source file named "soul.md" contains the agent's core values and principles for human-AI interaction, contributing to its unique character and natural responsiveness.
