## Databricks CEO: AGI Is Already Here, Why Companies Are Behind, and the Superintelligence Debate

- Channel: All-In Podcast

- Speakers: Ali Ghodsi (CEO, Databricks); Chamath Palihapitiya (Co-Host, All-In Podcast); Jason Calacanis (Co-Host, All-In Podcast)

---

### TL;DR
Databricks CEO Ali Ghodsi argues that while AGI is already here, enterprises are stuck using AI as a glorified Google search due to context, security, cost, and IP sovereignty bottlenecks. He demonstrates how Databricks uses its internal "Genie" ontology to run the company like "management Moneyball," explains why Databricks is staying private for now, and dismisses AI doom scenarios because none of the four conditions for recursive self-improvement (RSI) are currently met.

---

### Databricks' Origin & Contrarian Bets
- **UC Berkeley Roots:** Founded in 2013 out of UC Berkeley's AMPlab (~20 researchers, the largest AI lab at the time), where Ali Ghodsi and his team worked on the 2008–2009 Netflix Prize ($1M for a 10% improvement in movie recommendations). "AMP" stood for Algorithms, Machine Learning, and People—reflecting an early belief in human-in-the-loop AI.
- **Three Contrarian Bets:** At a time when AI was dismissed as niche ad-tech, Databricks bet everything on:
  - **Cloud:** Separating compute and storage (an analyst firm bet Ali it would be a "strategic blunder" that would sink the company).
  - **AI Use Cases:** Building for enterprise data science teams.
  - **Open Source:** Making the core technology open.
- **Scale & Growth:** Grew from $1.5M in revenue in 2015 to a $7B+ run rate by August 2026, growing 70–80% YoY (with Ali aiming for >100% growth) across a 12,000-person organization.

### AGI Is Here, But Enterprise AI Is Behind
- **AGI vs. Superintelligence:** Ghodsi believes AGI—as defined by AMPlab in 2009–2010—was achieved last year, though researchers have moved the goalpost. By contrast, **Superintelligence** (Nick Bostrom's 2014 definition: doing 1,000 years of human thought in a minute) remains the obsession of frontier labs.
- **Current Enterprise Reality:** Global 2000 and Fortune 500 companies mostly use AI as a "glorified Google search" (chatbots) and for coding (seeing 20–30% speedups), rather than running thousands of autonomous agents.
- **Four Bottlenecks Holding Enterprises Back:**
  - **The Context Problem:** AI is useless if it isn't in the room. One major bank CEO told Ghodsi they will "never" record meetings due to liability.
  - **Cybersecurity & Surface Area:** Connecting agents to all corporate data multiplies attack surface area by 1,000x+ and creates insider threat risks.
  - **Exponential Token Costs:** Token spend rises exponentially without a matching exponential lift in revenue, prompting CFOs to push back on OpEx and COGS.
  - **Data & Intelligence Sovereignty:** Companies fear IP leakage into foundation model providers (e.g., Anthropic, OpenAI) that release competing features every two weeks.
- **Digital Natives Lead:** Younger companies (10–15 years old), such as Jack Dorsey's Block/Square (building a "Company World Model"), are far ahead of 20–50-year-old legacy enterprises.

### Live Demo: "Genie" & Management Moneyball
- **The Company Ontology:** Databricks' **Genie** product constructs a live, 24/7 graph of the entire company—mapping org charts, emails, Slack, docs, spreadsheets, and meeting transcripts—to cut latency across hierarchical org charts.
- **HR Survey & Causality:** Out of 10,000+ employee responses, executive leadership scored 88%, work-life balance 60%, and "action" (feeling heard) ~33%. When the CFO hypothesized that poor work-life blend caused low action, Genie disproved causality (e.g., surfacing a manager with 44% work-life blend and 77% action) and sliced root causes by department (PM: too many stakeholders; Engineering: tools/systems; Sales Ops: career growth).
- **Engineering Productivity (The L-Curve):** Plotting weekly pull requests across 2,000 engineers revealed an L-shape where the top 10% ship vastly more code by running "automated factories." Overlaying token cost showed top shippers weren't necessarily spending more because they route simpler tasks to open-source/cheaper models.
- **Eliminating Human Blockers:** Genie tracks "human blockers" (legal, finance, sibling orgs) and audits whether regional offices (e.g., Japan vs. EMEA vs. LatAm) adhere to core cultural principles. Jason Calacanis noted he built a similar internal bot ("Major Decisions") to track Slack, Notion, and email for decision-making speed.

### Staying Private vs. Going Public
- **The Case for Going Public:** Provides access to public capital, massive quarterly marketing events, and liquid M&A currency (though Databricks has already executed $1B+ acquisitions privately).
- **Why Databricks Is Staying Private (For Now):**
  - **Avoiding the Public Rollercoaster:** Ghodsi wants to shield employees from public market sentiment swings during the AI transition, where a sudden stock drop feels like a pay cut and triggers attrition.
  - **Vibrant Secondary Liquidity:** Databricks has raised $20B–$30B in annual tender offers. Employees rarely use up the full tender allocation because they prefer to hold onto 70–80% compounding growth.

### RSI, Cyber Risk & The Doom Debate
- **Why We Don't Have RSI:** Writing 98% of code with AI does not equal Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI). Ghodsi outlines **four unmet conditions** required for an RSI flywheel:
  1. *Faster Training:* Next-gen models must train super-linearly faster (currently, pre-training takes longer and follows scaling laws requiring exponentially more hardware).
  2. *Fewer Resources:* Training must use less compute, not 10x more hardware each generation.
  3. *Generalized Accuracy:* Accuracy must improve at least linearly across all domains, not just in narrow RL gyms or math.
  4. *Continuous Loop:* All three must compound continuously (e.g., training dropping from 3 months → 1 month → 2 weeks → 1 week, and cost dropping from $100M → $50M → $1M).
- **Cyber Risk Is Real, But Finite:** Cyber is asymmetric—attackers only need 1 success out of thousands of agents, while defenders must be 100% secure. Databricks addresses this with **Lakewatch**. However, code vulnerabilities are finite; as AI scans new code and rewrites/shadow-patches legacy code, software will reach a more secure steady state.
- **Tangible AI Wins:** Regeneron used Databricks to identify the genome behind chronic liver disease; Merck and Databricks trained **Teddy** (Transformer Enabled Drug Discovery) on gene regulatory networks (GRN); Crisis Textline uses LLMs to detect self-harm; and Zipline uses Databricks for drone delivery operations.

- **Historic Code Volume:** In **less than the next 12 months**, humanity will write more code than ever in history, and it will be secure thanks to AI vulnerability scanning — *Ali Ghodsi*
- **Databricks IPO:** Databricks **will go public** eventually, though not during the current AI transition period — *Ali Ghodsi*
- **AI Extinction Risk:** The probability of a 10% chance that 7 billion people die from AI is **close to zero** — *Ali Ghodsi*

## Freedom vs. Safety: Axon CEO Rick Smith on the Future of Policing

- Channel: All-In Podcast

---

### TL;DR
Axon CEO Rick Smith joined David Friedberg on the All-In Podcast to discuss the company's 30-year evolution from a garage startup building non-lethal weapons to a $35–40B public cloud and software giant for public safety, while addressing the delicate balance between civil liberties, privacy, and technological policing advancements.

### Company Origins & Financial Survival
- **Founding and Early Struggles:** 
  - Founded in 1993 in a Tucson garage by 23-year-old Rick Smith and a 73-year-old NASA scientist who invented the original Taser in the 1970s.
  - Initial target market was consumers (aimed at mothers like Smith's mom for self-defense), but after 7 years of struggling with failed product launches and heavy debt, they pivoted to law enforcement in late 1999.
  - Survived the brink of bankruptcy in 1998 when Smith's father and investor Bruce Culver injected their last $500,000 each after the company owed Silicon Valley Bank about $2 million.
- **Going Public and Subsequent Crisis:**
  - Went public in late 2000 at a ~$12M valuation during the dot-com crash because investment banks needed non-internet deals with real revenue.
  - In 2004, the stock peaked as the world's number one performing stock on a 1- and 3-year return basis, but crashed from $33 to $7 in 2005 due to an SEC investigation, short-selling attacks, and an internal mole.
  - Faced hundreds of shareholder and plaintiff lawsuits; notably sued their own insurance company to prevent them from settling meritless claims, ultimately dropping to a $100M market cap before rebuilding to today's $35–40B valuation.
- **The Role of Haddy Partovi:**
  - College friend and board member Haddy Partovi acted as a part-time CTO for ~5 years during the software transition.
  - Guided Axon away from managing their own data center (which suffered a near-catastrophic outage) onto AWS and Azure, helped hire the right technical team, and pushed to rebrand the company from Taser to Axon to reflect its broader software and hardware footprint.

### Body Cameras & Cloud Infrastructure
- **Creation of the Body Cam:**
  - Invented out of necessity to defend officers against false allegations and emotionally charged civil lawsuits (e.g., a 2004 Northern California case involving a meth overdose).
  - Faced initial employee betrayal (an engineer built a competing product in the middle of the night using stock options) and initial police pushback who feared "Big Brother" micromanagement.
  - The turning point for widespread adoption occurred following the 2014 shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri.
- **The Cloud Bet:**
  - Made a foundational bet on cloud infrastructure to securely manage and scale the massive influx of video data across 18,000+ police departments.
  - Axon currently manages over a million police officers uploading two hours of video daily, handling more video volume than YouTube.

### Surveillance State & Privacy Concerns
- **Balancing Safety vs. Freedom:**
  - Acknowledges legitimate public concerns regarding the rise of automated license plate readers (ALPRs like Flock) and surveillance technologies, noting that nobody wants to live in a Chinese-style surveillance state.
  - Argues that society cannot revert to 1950s technology since cameras are already ubiquitous; the core issue is establishing rigorous **data governance and regulatory guardrails**.
- **Regulatory Solutions:**
  - Strongly supports federal legislation introduced by Senator Josh Hawley, which implements criminal and civil penalties for police officers who individually misuse technology (e.g., searching an ex-girlfriend).
  - Advocates for technical safeguards from manufacturers requiring viable case codes and multi-key authorization (requiring another officer or administrator sign-off) before running searches.
  - Notes that demand for policing technology is paradoxically highest in underrepresented, poor neighborhoods suffering from violent crime, where residents cheer for police deterrence.

### Robotics, Drones, and Future Policing
- **The Skydio Partnership & Autonomous Drones:**
  - Axon became the largest investor in Skydio, securing exclusive channel rights into public safety markets.
  - Autonomous docks placed on fire stations allow drones to arrive on a crime scene within seconds—often before 911 operators finish logging calls.
- **Taser-Equipped Drones & School Shootings:**
  - Continuing R&D on non-lethal, Taser-capable drones and robots designed to de-escalate active shooter scenarios (like school shootings) without sending human officers into deadly gunfights.
  - In June 2022, members of Axon's AI ethics board resigned in protest over the project; Axon subsequently reconstituted the group into the **Equity and Ethics Advisory Council**, adding more diverse voices from impacted communities.
- **Future Robotic Guardrails:**
  - Emphasizes that policing will remain fundamentally human-driven, with a strict bright line: **decisions to use force will always be made by humans**.
  - Future robotic systems will feature real-time administrator approvals and multi-key oversight, making them the "least usable technology in the history of policing" to prevent abuse.

  - **David Friedberg:** Calls it potentially the biggest day of discovery in human history, compressing hundreds or thousands of years of human labor into 3-hour loops in silico. He notes math and coding are uniquely suited for this because the entire test cycle is verifiable and falsifiable without physical-world friction.
  - **Chamath Palihapitiya:** Counters that these proofs are narrow intellectual cul-de-sacs rather than fundamental boundaries of physics we were waiting on, though they prove code and math are fully verifiable.
  - **Cryptographic Panic:** Observers noted a complete absence of cryptography proofs, raising rumors that OpenAI is holding back breakthroughs on number factorization that could threaten public key cryptography wallets.
- **Dismantling the Expert Gatekeepers:**
  - Friedberg argues AI is the great democratizer, dismantling gated, specialized expert knowledge systems (like scientific supercolliders acting as employment welfare) and allowing humanity to pace its own frontier for free.
  - **David Sacks:** Draws parallels to the internet's democratization of news, noting how elite institutions and government agencies (like Fauci and the FBI during COVID) tried to crack down on true information via censorship. He warns that because SI will eat the internet, a massive control fight is underway to erect a federal Department of SI and put the genie back in the bottle.

### Riots in France, Bond Markets, and the Socialism Point of No Return
- **French Austerity Unrest:**
  - Strikes and protests erupted across 1,000 French schools following high school teacher shortages, civil service pay rising only 5% since 2017 against 20% inflation, and government austerity measures.
  - Protests resulted in 2,000 arrests and 305 injured police officers. France's 10-year bond top 5%, and Polymarket lists Marine Le Pen as the favorite at 43% for the April 2027 presidential election.
- **The Socialism Point of Guaranteed Return:**
  - **David Friedberg:** Introduces the theory of the "socialism point of guaranteed return," arguing that socialism removes human agency, degrades service quality over time due to zero market forces, and forces societies to demand more government intervention until the system breaks under unpayable debt. Once it hits rock bottom (like Latin America), societies bounce back out of socialism.
  - **David Sacks:** Adds the corollary that "socialism has never failed, only been failed," noting that government subsidies in housing, education, and healthcare distort market signals and inflate prices far past inflation.
  - **Chamath Palihapitiya:** Predicts the bond vigilantes (central banks and macro funds) will put the Western world on notice, noting that European quasi-socialist states like France and the UK are structurally broken on growth, housing, and education, driving yields up globally (US 10-year at 5.3%, 30-year at 5.6%).

### Headless Agents and the Death of Software IP
- **Headless AI & Agentic Workflows:**
  - Elon Musk announced **GroqBot** will go headless, picking the best end-to-end model dynamically to give users the best outcome.
  - Users are deploying agents to handle complex multi-step digital loops, such as managing inboxes, cutting subscriptions, negotiating bills, and saving thousands annually.
- **The Death of Software IP:**
  - **Chamath Palihapitiya:** Declares that **software IP was rendered worthless**, citing instances where developers decompiled Adobe's Big 5 product suite and published open-source equivalents. 
  - With software wrapped in agent tools and connected via MCP (Model Context Protocol), proprietary IP moats no longer matter; the focus is entirely on solving the problem.
- **The Shift to Maker Culture:**
  - **David Friedberg:** Argues that digital workflows will be automated by parallelized loops, forcing humans to transition from relying on recognition, papers, and IP to becoming makers and producers of real products and services, ultimately increasing global prosperity via Jevons paradox.
