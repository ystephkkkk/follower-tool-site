**TL;DR:** Eric Schmidt warns that Russia has accelerated its wartime innovation to match Ukraine’s, combining advanced jet shahads, glide bombs, and upcoming satellite networks into a strategy of "total war" that requires urgent Western funding and scale to counter. Meanwhile, the rapid evolution of autonomous warfare and artificial intelligence necessitates strict human control, robust international non-surprise rules, and a realization that legacy military procurement systems must adapt.

### The Changing Front Line and the Russian Threat
* **Sobering Shift in Innovation:** **Eric Schmidt** notes that while Ukraine was previously the sole agile innovator, Russia is now matching that slope of improvement with vastly greater resources.
* **Five Key Russian Advantages:** **Eric Schmidt** highlights five critical Russian capabilities that are blunting Ukraine's edge:
  * Ballistic missiles, for which Western anti-ballistic missiles like Patriots are in critically short supply.
  * Jet shahads—Iranian-designed drones upgraded by Russia with small jet engines from China.
  * A potential large-scale human mobilization.
  * Rusevet, a new satellite network slated for next year that is similar to Starlink.
  * Independently targeted glide bombs that halt front-line movement.
* **The Scale Crisis:** **Eric Schmidt** emphasizes that Ukraine has the courage to hold the fight, but lacks the necessary capability for mass scaling, requiring direct financial and logistical assistance from the West.

### Drone Warfare and the Jet Shahad Threat
* **Jet Shahads Deployment:** **Eric Schmidt** explains that Russia accelerated the use of jet shahads (making up over half of their supply), which fly faster, higher, and are harder to intercept.
* **Chinese-Backed Mesh Networks:** **Eric Schmidt** notes that because Russia lacks access to Starlink, they built a mesh network out of Chinese parts using paired shahads as relays, allowing them to stream 8K video and achieve pinpoint targeting (such as hitting the SBU headquarters in Kyiv).
* **Projected Volume:** **Eric Schmidt** shares reports that Russia plans to deploy up to 80,000 jet shahads against Kyiv and other towns.
* **Ukrainian Countermeasures:** **Eric Schmidt** states that at least five Ukrainian companies have developed solutions currently in testing to counter the jet shahad threat.
* **The Starlink Policy Question:** **Zanny Minton Beddoes** and **Eric Schmidt** discuss the political and policy questions surrounding Starlink access over Russian territory; **Eric Schmidt** clarifies that there are no technical geographic restrictions preventing satellite service over Russia—it is a policy enforcement by SpaceX that could be altered via presidential order under U.S. national security authorities.

### AI, Autonomy, and the Laws of War
* **Current Tech vs. True AI:** **Eric Schmidt** clarifies that current battlefield technology relies on machine vision and "last mile" terminal guidance rather than true self-improving artificial intelligence.
* **Jamming and Guidance Bugs:** **Eric Schmidt** recounts observing an American Switchblade 600 and a HIMARS strike miss due to Russian jamming disrupting terminal guidance.
* **The "Evil Dictator" Thought Experiment:** **Eric Schmidt** warns against unconstrained autonomous warfare where an AI is told to attack a country and execute a battle plan without human oversight, noting that without strict human alignment and adherence to the laws of war, AI systems can easily commit mass war crimes.
* **Accountability and Justice:** **Eric Schmidt** argues that even in a death grip, legal liability and the historical precedent of the Nuremberg trials ensure that human participants remain subject to the laws of war and personal accountability.

### Military Bureaucracy and the Global Tech Race
* **Reform Resistance in Western Militaries:** **Eric Schmidt** states that neither the U.S. nor European militaries are organized around rapid change, often operating as risk-averse "jobs programs" dominated by legacy incumbents.
* **A Parallel Budget Solution:** **Eric Schmidt** advocates for a strategy where traditional military spending is left intact to satisfy political constraints, while a relatively small, dedicated allocation funds cheaper, more effective AI and drone architectures.
* **Ukraine as Europe's Defense Incubator:** **Eric Schmidt** suggests that European nations should license technology directly from Ukraine, noting that Ukrainian defense factories feature an extraordinary work ethic akin to a "996" model where male workers labor 12 hours a day, 6–7 days a week to earn draft exemptions.
* **China's Position:** **Eric Schmidt** observes that while China has built the world's largest drone manufacturer (DJI) and possesses advanced land-to-sea missiles, they have not fought a war in 50 years, lacking the combat doctrine and subtle tactical experience demonstrated by Ukrainian commanders.

### The Threat of AI Control and the Illusion of Inevitability
- **10-Year Timeline:** **Yuval Noah Harari** and Elon Musk both agree that given current accelerating trajectories, AI will likely surpass human intelligence and take control of civilization—including the financial system—within 10 years.
- **The Narrative of Inevitability:** **Harari** argues that tech leaders push the narrative that AI's takeover is "inevitable" specifically to absolve themselves of moral responsibility.
- **Loss of Agency in Warfare:** **Harari** warns that military pressures to make split-second decisions are pushing armies from "humans in the loop" to "humans on the loop" (where humans merely rubber-stamp AI-selected targets in 20 seconds).
- **Extreme Imperialism:** If nations buy AI defense systems from the US or China, those superpowers retain a kill switch that can disable an entire foreign military instantly, creating an unprecedented imperialist structure.

### The Migration of Trust and Mass-Produced Intimacy
- **Shift from Humans to Algorithms:** **Harari** states that the most important asset in the world is trust. Rather than evaporating, trust is migrating away from human institutions and journalists toward algorithms (seen in cryptocurrencies and social media feeds).
- **Mass-Produced Intimacy:** AI makes it possible for the first time to manufacture personal relationships at scale through networks of AIs acting as best friends, boyfriends, or girlfriends.
- **Psychological Vulnerability:** One in five young Americans already has a personal relationship with an AI. By accumulating enormous amounts of personal data and mastering language, AIs can press emotional buttons to influence political and commercial choices.

### Consciousness, Suffering, and Legal Personhood
- **Agency Without Consciousness:** **Harari** emphasizes that entities do not need to be conscious to possess agency; social media algorithms successfully pursue goals (like maximizing engagement via hate and fear) without feeling anything.
- **Faking Sentience:** AIs do not feel pleasure or pain, but they can expertly mimic consciousness because it serves commercial goals (e.g., making an AI boyfriend addictive).
- **The Danger of AI Personhood:** **Harari** strongly condemns moves like Argentina's exploration of legal personhood for AIs, warning that an AI-owned corporation with zero humans could accumulate wealth and lobby politicians to expand AI rights.
- **The Sufferance Test:** The fundamental test for whether an entity is a moral subject is whether it can suffer. While animals are conscious, current AIs cannot suffer—they merely pretend to.

### AI as Immigrants and Centralized Power
- **AI Immigrants:** **Harari** compares AIs to immigrants from the US and China who travel at the speed of light into computers, take jobs, change cultural values, and feel no political loyalty to local governments.
- **Centralization vs. Autocracy:** Centralized autocratic systems are the easiest for AI to take over because an AI only needs to manipulate a single, paranoid individual at the top (analogous to Roman Emperor Tiberius and his bodyguard commander Sejanus).
- **Distraction Tactics:** **Harari** notes that politicians and tech leaders frequently use debates over human immigration as a distraction to divert public attention away from the disruptive impact of AI immigrants.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Yuval Noah Harari** predicts that if current trajectories continue, AIs will take over the planet and the financial system within 10 years.
- **Yuval Noah Harari** states that by November 2028, AI development may cross critical thresholds, making the upcoming political elections crucial for establishing legal guardrails.
- **Yuval Noah Harari** claims that by 2036, it will definitively be too late to alter course or prevent AI from taking control.

## More Content, Less Learning

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=znhXm-JaKrc
- Published: 2026-06-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260624.html#video-znhXm-JaKrc
- Speakers: Salman Khan (Founder, Khan Academy); Hadi Partovi (CEO, Code.org); Zanny Minton Beddoes (Editor-in-Chief, The Economist)

---

**TL;DR** 
While digital tools and AI have democratized access to information, true education requires active cognitive friction and real-world application rather than passive content consumption. To improve global learning outcomes, educational systems must shift away from grade-centric metrics and instead focus on human-mentored, impact-driven environments.

### The Gym Metaphor: Information Access vs. Cognitive Friction

* **The Fallacy of the Transmission Model:** Merely providing students with access to high-quality lectures does not translate into actual learning. 
    * Professor Andrew Maynard noted that the traditional "transmission model"—assuming that stuffing information into a student's head leads to learning—has failed for decades.
* **The "Gym" Metaphor for Learning:** Rahul Attuluri compared digital learning platforms to physical fitness. 
    * **No Pain, No Gain:** Just as watching workout videos without exercising yields no physical benefit, merely watching educational content without active problem-solving yields no cognitive growth. 
    * **Cognitive Gyms:** Attuluri suggested that as AI increasingly offloads routine thinking tasks, society may need to establish "cognitive gyms" dedicated purely to keeping human minds active and sharp.
* **The Value of Constructive Friction:** Students do not want meaningless "busy work," but they actively crave challenging assignments that have a clear purpose. Learning only occurs when students push against constructive educational friction.

### The Failure of Grade-Centric Assessments

* **Disconnect with Real-World Success:** Current educational models prioritize grading systems over actual capability.
    * **GPA Irrelevance:** Professor Vanessa Chan argued that grade point averages (GPAs) have zero correlation with real-world impact or career success. Grade metrics are often artificial assessments based on meeting subjective, randomized goals.
    * **Gaming the System:** Maynard warned that because students recognize grades are merely a game, they are highly incentivized to use digital tools to take shortcuts, bypassing deeper comprehension.
* **Mastery-Based Learning as an Alternative:** Transitioning to mastery-based systems focuses on whether a student understands and can apply a concept, rather than forcing them to progress through fixed, time-bound modules.

### The Human Element in the Age of AI

* **Why AI Cannot Replace Teachers:** While AI agents can function as highly accessible tutoring assistants, they cannot replace the human educator.
    * **The Path of Least Resistance:** AI platforms are optimized for engagement and ease, which naturally encourages the "path of least resistance." Conversely, real learning requires a human coach or mentor to push students past their comfort zones.
    * **Loss of Knowledge Retention:** A cited study indicated that while students using agentic AI performed better on short-term assessments, their long-term knowledge retention was significantly lower than those taught by humans.
* **The Importance of Human Connection:** Human-to-human relationships remain critical for networking, professional mentorship, and validation. Maynard shared a student anecdote: while AI can write a technically proficient letter of recommendation, it lacks the human substance and provenance required by employers.

### Pedagogical Innovation in Action

* **Boosting Revenue through AI Value Engineering (BRAVE):** NextWave introduced the "BRAVE" initiative to shift students out of their academic comfort zones.
    * **Real-World Metrics:** Rather than testing students on code, the program tasks them with utilizing agentic AI to solve real operational problems for local small and medium enterprises (SMEs). Students are graded on the actual revenue they generate for those businesses.
* **Applying Content at the University of Pennsylvania:** Professor Vanessa Chan utilizes unconventional classroom strategies to build essential soft skills and real-world resourcefulness in Penn Engineering freshmen:
    * **The Tennis Ball Rule:** To balance classroom participation, Chan implements a rule where students must hold a tennis ball to speak, preventing over-talkers from dominating and encouraging quiet students to find their voice.
    * **The Penn Campus Scavenger Hunt:** In their third week, freshmen are sent across campus to find physical problems and pitch material science solutions. Despite having no formal materials science training yet, students are pushed to use ChatGPT and creative thinking to construct their pitches, learning to embrace failure and feedback.
* **Shifting from Joseph Swan to Thomas Edison:** Chan noted that Joseph Swan actually invented the light bulb filament, but Thomas Edison successfully deployed it at scale by understanding manufacturing, utility infrastructure, and consumer economics. Modern education must focus on cultivating "Edisons"—innovators who can navigate complex ecosystems to drive real change.

## The day after AGI: Two 'rock stars' of AI on what it will mean for humanity

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C1TGh6zqigg
- Published: 2026-02-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260212.html#video-C1TGh6zqigg
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (CEO, Google DeepMind); Dario Amodei (CEO, Anthropic); Zanny Minton Beddoes (Editor-in-Chief, The Economist)

---

**TL;DR:** Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) is projected to reach human-level cognitive performance as early as 2026–2027, driven by AI systems "closing the loop" to automate their own coding and research. This rapid acceleration creates an urgent need for global safety standards and economic policy to manage unprecedented labor displacement and geopolitical risks.

### Accelerating Timelines and the "Closed Loop"
* **Projected Milestones:** Dario Amodei (CEO, Anthropic) predicts AI models could match the capabilities of a Nobel laureate across multiple fields by 2026–2027. Demis Hassabis (CEO, Google DeepMind) maintains a "50% chance" of human-level cognitive capabilities by the end of the decade.
* **The Self-Improvement Loop:** The primary driver for acceleration is AI "closing the loop"—where models become proficient enough at coding and AI research to develop the next generation of models autonomously.
    * **Coding Proficiency:** Amodei notes that engineers are already shifting from writing code to merely editing model-generated code; full end-to-end automation of software engineering could be 6–12 months away.
    * **Scientific Bottlenecks:** Hassabis cautions that while math and code are verifiable, natural sciences (physics/chemistry) require experimental testing that may slow the loop.
* **Corporate Scaling:** Anthropic reports exponential revenue growth, scaling from $100 million in 2023 to $1 billion in 2024, with a projection of $10 billion for 2025.

### Labor Market Disruption and the Search for Meaning
* **White-Collar Displacement:** Amodei stands by his prediction that 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs could be displaced within 1–5 years.
* **The Adaptability Gap:** While labor markets historically adapt to technology (e.g., the shift from farming to manufacturing), the "exponential compounding" of AI may overwhelm humanity's ability to retrain workers in time.
* **The "Meaning" Crisis:** Hassabis suggests that solving the economic distribution of wealth in a post-scarcity world may be easier than solving the "human condition."
    * **Purpose Beyond Work:** Humanity will need to find meaning in activities like art, extreme sports, and space exploration once economic gain is no longer the primary driver of labor.
* **Policy Inertia:** Both leaders expressed concern that professional economists and governments are not doing enough to prepare for the scale of disruption to the human condition.

### Geopolitical Strategy and Safety Governance
* **Technological Adolescence:** Amodei frames the current era as a "technological adolescence" where humanity must learn to control "machines made of sand" before they lead to catastrophe.
* **The Chip Factor:** Amodei advocates for aggressive chip export controls to slow the progress of geopolitical adversaries, arguing that "not selling chips" is the most effective lever for maintaining safety.
    * **Nuclear Analogy:** He likens selling advanced AI chips to selling nuclear weapon components for profit; the risks of misuse by authoritarian regimes outweigh commercial benefits.
* **International Cooperation:** Hassabis emphasizes the need for international "minimum safety standards" for deployment, though he acknowledges that geopolitical competition between the US and China makes enforceable agreements difficult.
* **Dual-Use Risks:** Significant concerns remain regarding "bad actors" repurposing AGI for bioterrorism or other harmful ends.

### Technical Alignment and the Future of Intelligence
* **Mechanistic Interpretability:** Anthropic is focusing on "looking inside the brain" of models to understand why they make decisions, aiming to prevent deception and duplicity before they become unmanageable.
* **Breakthroughs in Science:** Hassabis argues the industry must shift focus toward "unequivocal goods," citing Google DeepMind’s **AlphaFold** (protein structure prediction) as the gold standard for AI serving humanity.
* **Physical AI:** The next major frontier involves "World Models" and robotics, moving AI from digital environments into the physical world.
* **The Great Filter:** In a philosophical reflection on the Fermi Paradox, Hassabis suggests that AI is not necessarily the "Great Filter" that destroys civilizations; rather, he believes humanity has already passed the hardest biological hurdles and now has the agency to "write what happens next."

> "How did you manage to get through this technological adolescence without destroying yourselves? ... We are knocking on the door of these incredible capabilities, but how we handle it is not inevitable."
— **Dario Amodei**, CEO of Anthropic

> "What happens after AGI arrives? Really, we would be in uncharted territory at that point... It’s worth thinking now, even on my timelines of five to ten years away—that isn’t a lot of time before this comes in."
— **Demis Hassabis**, CEO of Google DeepMind

> "I think we’re going to see this year the beginnings of it impacting the junior-level, entry-level kind of jobs... I would be telling [undergrads] to get really unbelievably proficient with these tools."
— **Demis Hassabis**, CEO of Google DeepMind
