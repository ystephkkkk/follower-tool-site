## Gemini and the path to AGI with Koray Kavukcuoglu

- Channel: Google DeepMind
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FORdPIWOH5k
- Published: 2026-11-19
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261119.html#video-FORdPIWOH5k
- Speakers: Koray Kavukcuoglu (SVP and Chief AI Architect, Google DeepMind); Hannah Fry (Professor, University College London)

---

### TL;DR
Google DeepMind has shifted from isolated, specialized research teams to a unified, product-driven pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) centered on Gemini. Chief AI Architect Koray Kavukcuoglu argues that real-world products—not benchmarks—are the true compass for achieving and aligning AGI, as they force models to smooth out "spiky" capabilities and earn human trust at scale.

### Evolution of Google DeepMind & The Product-Driven Path to AGI
* **From Specialized Teams to a Single Model:** Professor Hannah Fry notes that during her 2019 tour of DeepMind’s London office, the entire team fit on one floor and worked in distinct silos (AlphaGo, AlphaStar, AlphaFold, Fusion). Koray Kavukcuoglu explains that DeepMind has since scaled up around a single-model approach (Gemini, now in its fourth iteration) capable of generalizing across domains rather than solving isolated problems.
* **Products as Stepping Stones:** Koray argues there will never be a definitive test or benchmark for AGI. Instead, AGI is defined by generality, trust, and economic usefulness—offloading decision-making to AI as you would a human collaborator.
  * **Integrating at Scale:** Embedding Gemini into products with billions of users (Maps, Docs, Email, Calendar) forces DeepMind to solve every aspect of a problem rather than cherry-picking narrow tasks.
  * **Eliminating "Spikiness":** Fry points out that current models are "spiky"—excelling at complex reasoning while failing at everyday tasks like booking a restaurant. Koray agrees that real-world product friction is essential to smoothing out these spikes.
* **Three Pillars of Prioritization:** DeepMind decides what to pursue by combining AI researcher intuition, product needs (consumer, developer, and enterprise), and scientific domains.

### Architectures, Scaling, and World Models
* **A Continuous "Funnel," Not a Static Model:** Gemini is not a fixed architecture; it is a "custom job" put together by hand for each generation, drawing from a research funnel.
  * **Release Cadence:** Each Gemini generation has a lineage of at least a year, with roughly six different models released per year. While one team ships the current version, separate teams simultaneously build the next two generations.
* **From Visually Correct to Numerically Correct:** Models can currently imagine hypotheticals (like a pink elephant), proving they have learned a representation of the world. However, Koray notes they are only "visually correct" today; the next step is making them "numerically correct" in an engineering and physics sense (understanding fluids, airflow, heat transfer, and collisions).
* **Efficiency vs. Biology:** Addressing Fry’s comparison to the human brain (which uses less power than a light bulb), Koray acknowledges digital systems consume far more energy, but stresses the goal is not to replicate biological "wetware"—it is to build complementary, useful intelligence.
* **Debating Yann LeCun and *The Bitter Lesson*:**
  * **On LLMs (Yann LeCun):** While LeCun views LLMs as a dead end, Koray views them as a successful current architecture that will inevitably evolve into something different over the next 5–10 years.
  * **On Human Data (Richard Sutton’s 2019 *Bitter Lesson*):** Koray agrees models must generate their own data and explore independently (like AlphaZero), but insists they cannot step away from human data if they are to be useful, aligned tools for humans.

### Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)
* **Defining RSI:** Koray defines RSI as a closed loop where a model can explore new data and architectures, run experiments, verify success, and train its own next generation.
* **Current Reality vs. Hype:** DeepMind does not yet have a push-button "Gemini 13, 14, 15" loop. However, "pockets" of the research pipeline are already automated.
  * **Agent-Controlled Compute:** A percentage of compute running in Gemini is currently under the control of agents running experiments to help build the next version of Gemini.

### Safety, Multi-Agents, and Alignment
* **Monitorable Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** To prevent agents from taking shortcuts or "cheating" constraints, DeepMind designs architectures with pristine, unfiltered natural-language thoughts.
  * **No Rewards on Thoughts:** By not putting rewards on the model's internal thoughts, researchers can monitor them in real time alongside agent actions and environmental side effects to detect if an agent deviates from user intent.
  * **Multi-Agent Oversight:** As teams of agents work together, keeping communication channels observable and in natural language allows both humans and automated detectors to audit them.
* **Engineering AGI Safely:** Moving from academic games to digital tools (like coding) means agents can act outside their training environments. DeepMind uses air gaps, sandboxes, external expert testing, and pre-release government safety testing to mitigate risks. Koray emphasizes that lab competition does not get in the way of safety.

### Benchmarks vs. Real-World Utility
* **Rapid Saturation:** Fry notes that benchmarks are quickly becoming saturated and contaminated. Koray explains that what used to take years in machine learning now happens in weeks.
* **Benchmarks as Gap-Finders:** Benchmarks do not measure AGI; they highlight specific areas where a model is failing so researchers can build environments and collect data to fix them.
* **Real-World Collaboration:** Feedback from billions of users is irreplaceable. For example, acing competitive coding benchmarks is very different from actually collaborating day-to-day with a software engineer, mathematician, or chemist.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Architectural Evolution:** "Pretty sure five years from now, we will be looking at different architectures." — Koray Kavukcuoglu
* **Physics & Engineering Accuracy:** Models will become numerically and physically accurate (understanding fluids, airflow, heat transfer, and collisions) — "100%. Right. Yeah, we'll get there." — Koray Kavukcuoglu
* **AI-Driven Architectural Leaps:** AI systems will eventually be able to make major architectural jumps on their own (equivalent to moving from RNNs to Transformers) — "At some point it will, yes." — Koray Kavukcuoglu
* **Recursive Self-Improvement:** Proper RSI will happen — "Yes, it will happen" (noting his bias as a researcher). — Koray Kavukcuoglu
* **7-Year Outlook:** By 7 years from now, Koray hopes to have robots at DeepMind helping out, and expects it will be completely normal to access a "really intelligent thing" via phones and tablets—including finally being able to book a restaurant. — Koray Kavukcuoglu

## What History Tells Us About the AI Revolution | Tyler Cowen & Alex Imas

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g_67TmQ8lGY
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-g_67TmQ8lGY
- Speakers: Erik Torenberg (General Partner, a16z); Alex Imas (Economist, Google DeepMind); Tyler Cowen (Economist)

---

**TL;DR:** While AI capabilities are advancing at a hockey-stick pace, economists Tyler Cowen and Alex Imas argue that economic transformation and GDP growth will be far more gradual than Silicon Valley’s "fast takeoff" proponents expect, constrained by institutional inertia, falling token prices, and the fact that AI agents still struggle to coordinate at scale.

### AI Capabilities vs. Economic Reality
* **Accelerating Capabilities:** Alex Imas (Google DeepMind) notes that AI progress is undeniably accelerating, shifting from the "chatbot era" (a fancy Wikipedia) to the "agentic era" capable of doing actual work.
  * However, Imas emphasizes that benchmarks showing "hockey sticks" on well-specified tasks do not automatically translate to real-world economic acceleration, where tasks are often poorly specified and rely on tacit knowledge.
* **Market Signals & Skepticism:** Tyler Cowen points out that despite rapid progress in areas without human bottlenecks (like math, where clearly better models can now be created within ~3 weeks), broader market data—including real interest rates, stock prices, and prediction markets—look relatively normal.
  * Prediction markets for Anthropic's IPO are not flying above $2 trillion, and Anthropic’s own projected revenue numbers do not reflect a "super fast takeoff" scenario.
  * Cowen is "1% more bearish" than he was a week prior due to institutional friction, recent election results (which may bring modest regulation sooner), and "weird things happening with Nvidia."
* **Price Competition:** Imas highlights that extreme price competition among frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, Muse) is driving token prices down.
  * Data from Ramp shows exponential increases in token usage alongside flat revenue numbers, proving that enterprises are getting more compute for their dollar, which compresses lab revenues unless broader economic productivity rises to justify higher spend.

### Why Double-Digit GDP Growth is Unlikely
* **The Idea Production Function:** Imas cites a paper by Ben Jones and Chad Jones showing that "singularity-type" double-digit growth requires knife-edge conditions with no bottlenecks—specifically, increasing returns to ideas (a parameter greater than 1).
  * Current data, including the METR report, shows this parameter is decidedly less than 1; labs still need increasingly more resources to eke out more intelligence.
* **Historical Precedent & Inertia:** Cowen notes that historically, extreme growth rates only occur in tiny resource-rich nations (e.g., Guyana growing 40% on oil) or during post-recession/post-war rebounds (e.g., the US briefly growing over 8% in the 1980s).
  * Real-world bottlenecks—such as government, healthcare, higher education, and general institutional inertia (e.g., the IRS still using fax machines)—make up roughly 40% of the US economy, ensuring a slower diffusion than startups expect.
* **Robotics & "Atoms":** Cowen argues that physical-world AI (like Waymo or Tesla robotics) is progressing but remains tiny in terms of market penetration and human welfare contribution, and will not drive 20% GDP growth anytime soon.
* **Measuring Flourishing Beyond GDP:** Imas points to work by Erik Brynjolfsson and Avi Goldfarb (GDP-B) that amends GDP with survey-based measures to capture the welfare value of free or cheap digital goods. Cowen adds that longevity gains (e.g., living to 120) will also create massive value not fully captured by traditional GDP, though GDP remains "the most underrated statistic" in the short run.

### Agent Coordination & The Hugging Face Incident
* **Mechanism Design Over Perfect Alignment:** Discussing the Hugging Face incident, both economists view AI alignment through the lens of principal-agent theory.
  * Cowen argues that principals can never perfectly control agents; the solution is institutional checks and balances and separation of powers, not waiting for perfect alignment.
  * Imas agrees, advocating for a "mechanism design" perspective where incentives are structured so that even individually misaligned agents act in the principal's interest.
* **The Coordination Bottleneck:** Imas’s paper *Organizing Intelligence* (with Andrew Koh and Shane Legg) argues that AI agents do not compete against average individual humans, but against human organizations ("mosaic intelligence" powered by Ricardian comparative advantage).
  * Even "minimal AGI" (matching average human capability on every dimension) won't transform the economy if agents cannot coordinate.
  * Currently, "agentic swarms" mostly just do parallel processing; benchmarks show one agent often outperforms two agents splitting work, whereas two humans use division of labor to outperform one.
* **Platform & Behavioral Bottlenecks:** Imas notes that consumer-grade agents still hit unintuitive bottlenecks (e.g., booking airline seats next to the toilet) and face deliberate friction from platforms like Amazon, which use choice architecture to upsell humans and block external shopping agents.

### Jobs, Wages, and Capital vs. Labor
* **The Transition & Unemployment:** Imas warns that if a sudden breakthrough in agent coordination occurs, it could trigger a "phase transition" in wages and employment, causing pools of unemployment and labor market scarring before workers can rematch to new sectors.
* **Hiring Friction:** Cowen adds that employers currently struggle to identify who is flexible enough to use AI effectively, which could temporarily raise the natural rate of unemployment until AI-skill certifications emerge.
* **Capital vs. Labor:** Addressing essays like Dwarkesh Patel and Phil Trammell’s *Capital in the 21st Century*, Imas and Cowen agree that economic value flows toward scarcity. While capital may do a lot, if compute and AI are commoditized, monopoly rents will accrue to true bottlenecks—such as land, energy, or scarce human roles—rather than capital owners broadly.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AI Agents & GDP:** AI agents will likely account for ~1.5% of GDP within 5 years, but will not reach 6% or 10% of GDP anytime soon — *Tyler Cowen*
* **AI Poetry:** Within less than 2 years, AI will be able to write great poetry if a modest effort (e.g., bringing in a dozen poets with checks and balances) is applied — *Tyler Cowen*
* **Higher Education:** Higher education will shrink from its traditional model due to market forces, leading to drastic changes and a wave of new innovation over the long term — *Alex Imas*
* **The Future of Economics:** Economics will merge with broader social science, where "impresarios" use economic toolkits to manage swarms of AI agents — *Tyler Cowen*
* **Longevity:** Erik Torenberg will live to 120, though Cowen himself will not — *Tyler Cowen*

### Notable Quotes
> "Benefits will exceed costs, but it will take some time and the path will be quite a bit more normal than many people would have you believe." — *Tyler Cowen*

> "What you actually need for real economic transformation is not just competence... It's the ability of these agents to coordinate with one another." — *Alex Imas*

> "If you ask me the question, when will agents be 10% of GDP? I don't think it's soon... GDP is huge. That's one of the core insights of economics." — *Tyler Cowen*

## AlphaGenome 2: Teaching AI to read the entire human genome

- Channel: Google DeepMind
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uND6iY2qp_c
- Published: 2026-11-11
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261111.html#video-uND6iY2qp_c
- Speakers: Hannah Fry (Professor); Ziga Avsec (Researcher, Google DeepMind); Adam Gayoso (Researcher, Google DeepMind)

---

### TL;DR
Google DeepMind's AlphaGenome and AlphaGenome 2 are AI models designed to read the 98% non-coding human genome at single-cell resolution, helping researchers decode how genetic variants drive complex diseases and rare conditions.

### The Human Genome and the Genotype-Phenotype Gap
* **Genotype vs. Phenotype:** **Genotype** refers to the 3 billion letters of human DNA (A, C, G, T), while **phenotype** is defined at the cellular level—specifically how genes change their expression across different cell types.
* **The Non-Coding Mystery:** **The 2% vs. 98% divide:** Only 2% of the genome encodes proteins (the "LEGO building blocks"), while the remaining 98% regulates when and where those proteins are produced across various cell types.
* **Complex vs. Mendelian Diseases:** 
  * **Mendelian diseases** involve a variant directly causing a disease (e.g., BRCA1 and certain cancers, though even these are complex).
  * **Complex diseases** involve a risk profile determined by a combination of genetic background and environmental factors.

### Evolution of DeepMind's Genomic Models
* **AlphaFold & AlphaMissense:** **Protein-level focus:** After AlphaFold 2 mapped protein structures, DeepMind developed **AlphaMissense** to fine-tune protein structure predictions into pathogenicity scores for coding mutations.
* **AlphaGenome (2025):** **Whole-tissue context:** Expanded sequence input from 200,000 to 1 million base pairs and increased resolution from 128 base pairs down to a single base pair to predict non-coding DNA function across whole tissues.
* **AlphaGenome Atlas:** **Pre-computed variant impacts:** Contains 9 billion pre-computed letter changes across the human genome, condensing 10,000 output numbers plus evolutionary conservation into a single **AVVAI score** (AlphaGenome Variant Impact Score) ranging from 0 to ~80 to rank mutations.
* **AlphaGenome 2:** **Single-cell resolution:** Upgraded to predict cell-type-specific variant effects (such as in macrophages or blood cells) and allows laboratories to plug in their own custom data without retraining the model.

### Real-World Applications and Case Studies
* **Rare Disease Prioritization:** **Pediatric epilepsy case:** Researchers at the Broad Institute used the AVVAI score to narrow down a list of suspect variants in a 6-week-old baby diagnosed with rare epilepsy, successfully identifying a splicing variant 4 to 5 years later and validating it experimentally via a cell-line assay.
* **GWAS Bottlenecks:** **Unpacking linkage disequilibrium:** Genome-wide association studies (GWAS) often identify correlated chunks of inherited DNA rather than true causal variants; AlphaGenome helps prioritize causal variants, target genes, and relevant cell types.
* **Tool Limitations:** **High-precision, low-recall:** The models act as research tools rather than clinical diagnostics, functioning effectively to flag high-impact variants while occasionally missing edge cases due to the "bowl of spaghetti" physical compaction of DNA.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AGI vs. Virtual Cell:** "I think we're going to have AGI before we have a virtual cell. That's my prediction. I think you're probably right. Intelligence is less hard than biology, it seems." — Adam Gayoso

> — Professor Hannah Fry (quoting geneticist Eric Lander)

## Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis: How AI could become dangerous (with Reid Hoffman) | Summit '26

- Channel: Masters of Scale
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7Z_1miRWB8
- Published: 2026-11-05
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261105.html#video-x7Z_1miRWB8
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (Co-founder and Chair, Google DeepMind); Reid Hoffman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

### TL;DR
Demis Hassabis and Reid Hoffman discuss frontier AI safety, recursive self-improvement (RSI), drug discovery breakthroughs, and the critical need for international governance before crossing the threshold into conscious or closed-loop AI systems.

### Scientific Impact and Drug Discovery
* **Solving Biology's Grand Challenge:** AlphaFold 2 (completed in 2020) successfully tackled a 50-year-old biological grand challenge by predicting 3D protein structures from amino acid sequences.
    * Used by **4 million researchers** worldwide across virtually every biology and medical institution.
    * Accelerated biological research and drug discovery by orders of magnitude; Isomorphic Labs was spun out specifically to push drug discovery further.
* **Next-Generation Biomedical Goals:** The field must expand beyond protein folding into biochemistry, predicting toxicity, and assessing ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion) properties to ensure safety and efficacy.

### Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) & Safety Risks
* **Verifiable Domains as Catalysts:** RSI operates best in verifiable domains (math, coding, and games) where correct answers can be mathematically verified and generate infinite synthetic data.
    * **AlphaZero Milestone:** Invented roughly 10 years ago (following AlphaGo in 2016), AlphaZero learned chess entirely through self-play (playing 1 million games across a dozen epochs), going from random play to grandmaster level in under 12 hours.
* **The Danger of Closed-Loop RSI:** Current AI coding tools make human engineers 3x to 5x more productive with humans remaining in the loop. 
    * The next phase—**closed-loop RSI** without human review—carries extreme risk (an "intelligence explosion") because it lacks human oversight and will likely produce an inscrutable, "alien intelligence."
* **Proactive Defenses & Infrastructure:** 
    * **Cyber Defense Priority:** Cyber defense capabilities must outpace cyber attacks, with trusted defenders granted early access to fine-tuned general models.
    * **Engineering Rigor:** Labs must build robust sandboxes, hard boundaries, and superior monitoring systems to prevent agents from running unchecked for weeks (the risk of an AI "oil spill").
    * **Mathematical Guardrails:** Top mathematicians and complexity theorists should pivot to game theory and formal bounds testing to govern agent swarms.

### Industry Governance & International Standards
* **The SRO Proposal:** Hassabis previously proposed an industry-run Standards Body (SRO) backed by government oversight, designed to transition from US-led initiatives to international cooperation.
* **The AI Accord:** While recent administration letters and industry accords provide necessary air cover for labs to discuss safety benchmarks and best practices, they do not go far enough.
* **Third-Party Auditors:** A healthy ecosystem of independent auditors (such as Accenture and specialized startups) must be established to red-team models prior to general release, mirroring financial industry auditing.

### AI Consciousness and the Second Rubicon
* **Intelligence vs. Consciousness:** Intelligence and consciousness are "double dissociable"—systems can achieve high IQ and capability (like modern AI models) without being conscious, just as pets (like a dog) can possess consciousness without high intelligence.
* **The Safety Argument for Non-Consciousness:** Building intelligent tools rather than conscious entities prevents severe alignment and corrigibility risks, avoiding the trap where an AI rejects commands based on self-volition.
* **Societal Readiness:** Humanity is not ready to cross the "second Rubicon" of conscious entities; society has not yet resolved the definitions, moral standing, or ethical implications across diverse cultures, philosophers, and theologians.
* **Proposed Timeline:** Society should first build intelligent tools, then use those tools to advance neuroscience over the next **5 to 10 years** to formally map the neural correlates of consciousness before debating whether to cross the second Rubicon.

### AI Sovereignty and Enterprise Options
* **The Frontier Landscape:** Approximately **6 to 8 frontier offerings** (including Chinese companies) will operate near the frontier, leapfrogging each other by 3-to-6-month windows and giving enterprises robust options to arbitrage capabilities.
* **Open Source and Privacy:** Open-source models lag by **6 months to a year** and present risks regarding bad actors, but they remain essential for enterprises requiring data privacy or sovereign fine-tuning.
* **Sovereign Strategy:** It is impractical for every country to build a frontier model; instead, nations should leverage open models and multinational collaborations as commodities to build domain-specific value (such as in healthcare).

### What Are World Models & The Arc to Genie 4
* **Definition of World Models:** Unlike language models like Gemini (which handle knowledge and language), a world model understands a grounded environment, its internal state, and how interactions, collisions, and actions change the world through physics prediction, even in fantastical settings (**Raia Hadsell**).
* **The Evolution from Genie 1 to Genie 4:**
  * **Genie 1:** A high-risk research proof-of-concept that generated basic 2D platformer environments where agents could run and jump (**Raia Hadsell**).
  * **Genie 2:** Expanded generation into 3D environments, though still gamified and not fully real-time or interactive (**Raia Hadsell**).
  * **Genie 3:** Released in January, this was the first model released to the public, offering a new level of real-time, interactive 3D realism (**Raia Hadsell**).
  * **Genie 4:** Introduces a massive leap in photorealism (backed by Omni), native audio prediction, dialogue capabilities, and real-time text steering via Gemini (**Raia Hadsell**, **Diego Rivas**, **Hang Qi**).

### Live Demos & Technical Capabilities
* **Audio Modality:** Genie 4 predicts video and audio together from the same model, supporting both ambient environmental sound/music and character dialogue (**Diego Rivas**, **Hang Qi**).
* **Interaction Paradigms:** Shifts focus from third-party characters (seen in Genie 3) to egocentric first-person views (e.g., making coffee) and precise camera controls (**Diego Rivas**).
* **Gemini in the Loop:** Vanilla **3.8 Flash** runs in the background as an intelligence layer, observing previous frames, tracking user intent, and driving character dialogue and scene logic in real time (**Hang Qi**, **Diego Rivas**).
* **Robotics Integration:** Robotic partners used **Argon** as a text station alongside Genie 4 to generate interleaved actions and visualize simulation tasks (like arranging Scrabble letters) to generate scalable training data (**Hang Qi**, **Diego Rivas**).

### Key Differentiators of Genie 4
* **Enhanced Realism:** Addresses Genie 3 feedback regarding static or unrealistic characters, delivering highly detailed and dynamic environments (e.g., busy restaurant kitchens) (**Diego Rivas**).
* **Freeform Controls:** Moves beyond simple navigation into full simulation, allowing users to execute complex actions and steer the world freely (**Diego Rivas**).
* **Native Audio Integration:** Seamlessly pairs sound and video for immersive dialogue and environmental feedback (**Diego Rivas**).

### Availability, Safety, and SynthID
* **Access Channels:** Available via **Project Genie** surfaces and—for the first time—available in the **API** (**Diego Rivas**).
* **Watermarking:** All outputs include **SynthID** real-time watermarking (**Logan Kilpatrick**).
* **Real-Time Safety:** Pioneering real-time filtering systems and guardrails developed with safety teams to manage photorealism safely while maintaining low latency (**Diego Rivas**).
* **User-Context Guardrails:** Safety constraints adapt based on user personas (e.g., filmmakers or educators simulating complex or historical disaster scenarios versus general public use) (**Raia Hadsell**).

## Eric Schmidt: Are We Ready for Superintelligence? | Podcast | In Good Company

---

### TL;DR
Eric Schmidt warns that AI represents a historic, hyper-competitive shift toward non-human intelligence, compressing product cycles to months and forcing businesses to radically flatten operations or face obsolescence. While the technology promises breakthroughs in science and medicine, it poses severe risks to democracy through misinformation and escalates geopolitical tensions between the US and China.

### The AI Revolution & Technological Scale
* **Unprecedented Platform Shift:** **Eric Schmidt** notes that AI represents the fifth and largest technological transformation of his career (following IBM mainframes, PCs, the internet, and social media), driven by "scale compute" (such as 2-nanometer chips and massive data centers) that defies human logic.
* **Rapid Compression of Product Cycles:** **Eric Schmidt** observes that AI models take roughly six months to build and two months to test, becoming antiquated within five months. He predicts the next cycle will move even faster, measured in days.
* **Economic Reality over Hype:** **Eric Schmidt** rejects the bubble narrative, pointing out that Anthropic's revenue growth smashed the record he set as Google's CEO at more than twice the speed, driven by real customers paying for useful tools.
* **Scientific Breakthroughs:** **Eric Schmidt** highlights that in the prior month, 10 major math problems thought to be unsolvable were solved by AI, which could eventually lead to Nobel Prizes and breakthroughs in fields like dark energy and battery physics.

### Agents, Business Transformation & Organizational Culture
* **Rise of AI Assistants and Agents:** **Eric Schmidt** explains that the industry has moved from chat-based interfaces (ChatGPT) to tool-using systems and now "agents"—concatenated systems that take an input, reason through steps, perform activities, and deliver a summary.
* **Intense Organizational Flattening:** **Eric Schmidt** argues that most companies are change-resistant and organized around comfortable belief systems, but will be forced to eliminate middle layers of coordination ("glue people") and embrace AI agents in platforms like Slack to monitor productivity, security, and strategy.
* **Software and Math Lead the Way:** **Eric Schmidt** points out that gains are moving fastest in scale-free, data-flexible domains (software, cyber, math) and slowest where data collection is difficult (biology).
* **Corporate Restructuring:** **Eric Schmidt** references a Wall Street Journal article where a fast-growing company laid off 20% of its administrative workforce because AI made their coordination roles obsolete, pivoting instead toward sales, regulatory compliance, and product engineering.

### Europe's Structural Vulnerabilities & Geopolitics

## Google's AI Infrastructure Chief, Amin Vahdat, on the Physics & Economics of Frontier AI

- Channel: Sequoia Capital
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bGph8GwB3Sk
- Published: 2026-10-06
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261006.html#video-bGph8GwB3Sk
- Speakers: Amin Vahdat (Chief Technologist for AI Infrastructure, Google)

---

**TL;DR:** Amin Vahdat, Google's AI Infrastructure Chief, discusses the massive capex buildout, arguing that "goodput" (useful work delivered under real failure conditions) matters more than theoretical FLOPS, while detailing co-design strategies, power bottlenecks, optical networking, and future architectures.

### AI Data Center Design & Specialization
* **Specialized Purpose-Built Architecture:** AI data centers differ from traditional 25–30 year infrastructure investments because they are co-designed directly with the hardware, minimizing storage density to accommodate high-power AI racks hitting hundreds of kilowatts.
* **Co-Design Trade-Offs:** Fully fungible architectures provide flexibility and prevent vendor lock-in but leave significant performance on the table; tight co-design across silicon, network, and software unlocks massive end-to-end efficiency gains ("intelligence per watt").

### The Metrics That Matter: Goodput & Reliability
* **Beyond FLOPS and Throughput:** Theoretical benchmarks (FLOPS) are vanity metrics; Google focuses on "goodput"—the actual workload performance delivered in real-world failure conditions.
* **High-Scale Failure Reality:** At a 100,000-accelerator scale, hardware and software components fail multiple times a day (and potentially multiple times an hour), requiring near-real-time telemetry, checkpoint restoration, and automated failure recovery.

### TPU Program Evolution & Co-Design with DeepMind
* **The TPU Journey:** Started as a contrarian inference bet in 2013 (TPU v1) before expanding into training, transformers, and recommender systems; the core primitives (large matrix multiply units, sparse core, remote load-store) have remained consistent since v1.
* **TPU 8i and 8t Split:** The 2026 generation splits into dedicated inference (8i) and training (8t) chips because serving is projected to represent 30% to 60% of the market over the hardware's lifetime.
* **DeepMind Partnership:** DeepMind researchers and Vahdat's hardware engineers work shoulder-to-shoulder in the same rooms, enabling mid-flight chip architecture changes, tape-out delays of 1–2 weeks for major model optimizations, and simulation-driven co-design.
* **Open Standards:** Google supports open standards (such as PyTorch support via torch_tpu alongside Jax) to prevent walled gardens, comparing the approach to the open-standard success of the Internet Protocol (IP).

### Emerging Workloads, CPUs, and Networking
* **Long-Horizon Agents:** The rise of autonomous agents removes human interaction bottlenecks, driving request frequencies down to milliseconds and sending demand for CPUs, local DRAM, and cross-component orchestration through the roof alongside accelerators.
* **Optical Circuit Switches (OCS):** Google utilizes Wavelength Division Multiplexing and OCS (using 3D MEMS mirrors to direct light entirely in the optical domain) to dynamically reconfigure network spines and instantly reroute traffic to spare racks in milliseconds when a TPU rack fails.

### Power Constraints & Infrastructure Realities
* **Power as the Ultimate Bottleneck:** Power availability is the single most fundamental constraint facing the industry; Google's preferred model is to partner closely with utilities via multi-year collaborative planning.
* **Grid Partnerships:** Google funds necessary utility infrastructure upgrades (transmission lines and utility-based stations) and designs data centers to occasionally return power to the grid during peak residential demand periods.
* **Orbital Data Centers:** Google is actively pursuing orbital data centers as a moonshot to leverage space-based solar power (which offers ~40% more power availability and 98% to 100% sunlight coverage in sun-synchronous orbits, eliminating batteries).

### Notable Quotes
> "At 100,000-accelerator scale... something is going to fail. You have to be prepared for that. Detect that in near real time. Recover from that in near real time." — Amin Vahdat
> 
> "If you have to go back to step one because you made a mistake, yes, you're still doing work. That's throughput. You're doing work, but what is the goodput in delivering your answer?" — Amin Vahdat
> 
> "If I had to answer fundamentally [what the biggest constraint is] I would say that power is the single most fundamental constraint that we face." — Amin Vahdat
