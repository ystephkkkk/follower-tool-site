## AI Has a Power Problem: Why the U.S. Power Grid Can't Keep Up | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 69

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z6-ManuYFPo
- Published: 2026-07-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260720.html#video-Z6-ManuYFPo
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Investor); Ben Kallo (Analyst, Baird)

---

**TL;DR**
The U.S. power grid is the primary bottleneck for AI expansion, requiring roughly 30 gigawatts of new capacity annually through 2035 to meet surging demand from data centers, manufacturing onshoring, and aging infrastructure.

### The Looming Grid Crisis & Data Center Demand
* **The Scale of the Problem:** The U.S. grid currently generates roughly 150 gigawatts (GW) of electricity. Estimates suggest the country needs between 100 and 350 GW of new capacity by 2035.
    * **Annual Growth:** The grid needs to add approximately 30 GW of capacity per year over the next decade.
    * **The "Lumpy" Buildout:** Data center development is constrained by labor shortages and power availability. While many projects are announced, actual construction is lumpy due to permitting and interconnect delays.
* **Technological Shifts:** The industry is moving toward an 800-volt architecture for new Nvidia chips, which requires more sophisticated power electronics and grid equipment.
* **Beyond Data Centers:** While AI gets the headlines, the grid is under stress from "onshoring" manufacturing, the electrification of homes/vehicles, and the decommissioning of 75-year-old coal plants.

### GE Vernova (GEV) and the Infrastructure "Super Cycle"
* **The "Long Tail" Business:** GE Vernova is a primary beneficiary of the grid expansion. Their Power segment, which sells massive natural gas turbines, is currently booked out through 2030–2031.
* **Electrification Segment:** Beyond turbines, GEV sells high-voltage equipment and transformers. They are successfully cross-selling this "nuts and bolts" equipment directly into data center projects.
* **Nuclear Outlook:** GEV is partnering with Hitachi on Small Modular Reactors (SMRs). 
    * **Timeline:** These 300MW reactors are unlikely to move the needle on earnings until the late 2030s. The first project in Ontario is targeted for 2035.
* **Wind Power:** Currently viewed as a "call option" or "self-help" story, as the company works through legacy offshore wind projects that have been financially taxing.

### The Tesla and SpaceX Strategic Outlook
* **The Tesla Energy Story:** The energy business—specifically "MegaPacks" (large-scale batteries for the grid)—now accounts for 20% of Tesla’s operating income and is growing at 30% annually.
* **The SpaceX Acquisition Theory:** Analyst Ben Kallo predicts SpaceX may eventually acquire Tesla.
    * **Strategic Control:** Elon Musk has expressed a desire for 25% ownership to control Tesla’s AI development; an acquisition via SpaceX (where he has high ownership) would facilitate this.
    * **Speed of Decision-Making:** Combining boards would allow for faster execution on joint projects like the proposed 100 GW solar module facility.
* **Autonomous Driving:** Despite years of delays, Tesla’s "vision-only" (camera-based) approach allows for faster scaling than competitors like Waymo, as Tesla harvests data from its entire existing vehicle fleet.

### Broader Mobility, Solar, and Raw Materials
* **EV Market Competitors:** 
    * **Rivian:** Positioned as the "first follower" of Tesla. The upcoming R2 model (approx. $45,000) is viewed as a compelling alternative to the Tesla Model Y.
    * **Lucid:** Currently viewed more as a technology provider than a mass-scale manufacturer, buoyed by Saudi Arabian investment.
* **The Solar Dilemma:** Kallo avoids residential solar due to high customer acquisition costs ($10,000 per home). 
    * **First Solar (FSLR):** Stock performance is currently tied to looming U.S. tariff decisions on Chinese components. If strict tariffs are implemented, there is a projected $100 upside to the stock.
* **Critical Minerals:** MP Materials (Rare Earths) is highlighted as a critical play for domestic supply chains, as China currently controls 90%+ of the magnet market required for EV motors and data center HVAC fans.

## How Silicon Valley Took Over the Defense Industry with Peter Arment | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 61

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VElWGgso2FY
- Published: 2026-05-25
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260525.html#video-VElWGgso2FY
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Peter Arment (Aerospace and Defense Analyst, Baird)

---

**TL;DR**  
The U.S. defense sector is undergoing a massive shift as $66 billion in venture capital disrupts an over-consolidated, slow-moving military-industrial complex, while a parallel nuclear renaissance is driving overlooked growth for specialty suppliers.

### The Great Defense Shake-Up: Silicon Valley vs. Legacy Primes

* **The Atrophied Legacy Base:** The U.S. defense industry consolidated from over 60 prime contractors in the 1980s to just five today, following the Pentagon's famous 1993 "Last Supper" meeting. 
    * **Cost-Plus Inefficiencies:** These five primes capture 50% of the Department of Defense's procurement budget but operate on slow "cost-plus" models that prioritize complex, ultra-expensive "exquisite" platforms rather than speed and volume.
    * **Munitions Depletion:** Recent conflicts have exposed extreme supply chain vulnerabilities. The U.S. is currently firing $4 million Patriot interceptor missiles to take out cheap $20,000 Iranian-designed Shahed drones, and restarting atrophied supply chains (like Stinger missiles) takes years.
* **The Silicon Valley Influx:** Between 2020 and 2024, venture capital and private equity funneled $66 billion into defense tech upstarts.
    * **Commercial-First Mindset:** Companies like the privately held Anduril Industries (valued at $14 billion) build tactical hardware and autonomous aircraft on their own dime before pitching to the Pentagon, bypassing slow government R&D cycles.
    * **The Pentagon Shift:** Under leadership beneath Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, including business figures like Emil Michael and Stephen Feinberg, the Pentagon is actively pushing for a faster, commercial-style acquisition process.

### The Sum-of-the-Parts Case to Break Up Lockheed Martin

* **The F-35 Drag:** The F-35 fighter jet represents 30% of Lockheed Martin’s (LMT) revenue. While it is the world's premier fighter, it costs $80 million+ per plane and $100,000 per hour to operate. It took over 20 years to build a global inventory of just 1,100 aircraft.
* **The Threat of Drone Mission Creep:** By the 2030s, cheap autonomous drones will threaten the F-35's market share. For short-distance defense scenarios (e.g., the 117-mile strait between China and Taiwan), cheap, expendable drone swarms are far more practical than manned stealth jets.
* **Unlocking 50% to 100% Value:** Analyst Peter Arment argues that breaking Lockheed Martin into three standalone public entities would unlock massive shareholder value:
    * **Missiles & Missile Defense Co:** Would trade at a premium multiple due to skyrocketing global demand for air defense.
    * **Space Co:** Consisting of highly classified, high-multiple national security satellite portfolios.
    * **RemainCo (Aeronautics & Sikorsky):** A slow-decaying, high-dividend cash cow suited for value investors.
* **Northrop Grumman's Superior Portfolio:** Among the major primes, Northrop Grumman (NOC) holds the most durable portfolio, with long-term contracts for the B-21 stealth bomber, the Minuteman nuclear ballistic missile replacement, and a third of the F-35's internal systems.

### Non-Prime Public Stocks and Drone Disruptors

* **AeroVironment (AVAV):** The pure-play drone manufacturer behind the "Switchblade" loitering munition ($150,000 per shot). AVAV recently merged with BlueHalo, doubling its size and adding directed-energy (laser) weapons like the LOCUST system, which can fry a drone's electronics from 2 kilometers away in 2.5 seconds.
* **BWX Technologies (BWXT):** Beyond nuclear propulsion, BWXT is a major player in "Group 3" tactical drones (sub-$100,000 units with a 1,500-mile range). These provide crucial "standoff capability," allowing forces to launch precision strikes from a safe distance without risking high-value aircraft.
* **Curtiss-Wright (CW):** A highly successful private equity roll-up of critical component manufacturers. It functions as a pure-play supplier for missile defense, space launch, and aerospace, trading at a premium valuation of roughly 40x EBITDA.

### The Nuclear Renaissance Powering Specialty Suppliers

* **Power Grid Pressure:** Artificial intelligence, data centers, and the European energy crisis have triggered a major nuclear energy revival. Over 50% of the 94 commercial nuclear reactors in the U.S. are currently applying for 20-year lifetime extensions (pushing operational lifespans from 60 to 80 years).
* **Curtiss-Wright’s Near-Monopoly:** The company produces the massive, high-margin reactor coolant pumps ($30 million each) required for Westinghouse’s modern AP1000 nuclear plants. 
    * **The Revenue Opportunity:** With 14 AP1000 plants in development globally (each requiring four pumps), Curtiss-Wright has a highly profitable $1.7 billion pipeline locked in, yielding 25% to 30% operating margins.
* **BWX Technologies’ Navy Moat:** BWXT holds a functional monopoly on manufacturing the nuclear reactors used to power the U.S. Navy's submarines and aircraft carriers. The company is also poised to benefit from commercial Small Modular Reactors (SMRs) funded by tech hyperscalers seeking clean, off-grid power for data centers.

### Commercial Aerospace and the Diluted Recovery of Boeing

* **The Aerospace Fleet Duopoly:** Global air traffic correlates strongly with GDP (historically growing at ~5%), meaning the global fleet of commercial aircraft doubles every 15 years.
* **Boeing's "Bean-Counter" Legacy:** Following its 1997 merger with McDonnell Douglas, Boeing’s management prioritized cost-cutting. Rather than spending 5 years and billions of dollars developing a clean-sheet plane to counter Airbus, they opted to put larger engines on the legacy 737 frame, resulting in the flawed 737 Max software and subsequent crashes.
* **Diluted Free Cash Flow:** Boeing was forced to issue massive amounts of equity to shore up its balance sheet during the crisis. While it was once projected to reach $40 in free cash flow per share, dilution has capped that potential to the low $20s. However, because there are no expensive new airplane development cycles on the horizon, Boeing remains a strong multi-year cash-generation story with a target price of $300 to $400.

## Catching a Falling Knife: The Truth About Software Stocks Today | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 54

- Channel: Steve Eisman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mf_rRVQrePg
- Published: 2026-04-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260413.html#video-Mf_rRVQrePg
- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Rob Oliver (Software Analyst, Baird)

---

**TL;DR:** The software sector is facing a massive valuation reset as generative AI threatens to dismantle traditional moats by lowering the cost of code creation and disrupting seat-based revenue models. While valuations have been cut in half, institutional investors remain sidelined, waiting for evidence that incumbents can innovate faster than they are disintermediated.

### The "Bear Case" for Software
*   **Cost of Creation:** The emergence of "vibe coding" and AI-assisted development has plummeted the cost of creating software, potentially allowing enterprises to build bespoke internal tools rather than paying for expensive merchant software.
*   **Seat-Based Model Risk:** Most enterprise software is priced per "seat" (per user). If AI allows companies to operate with significantly fewer employees, the total addressable market for seat-based licenses shrinks.
*   **Erosion of Pricing Power:** Historically, companies like Adobe and Salesforce raised prices based on innovation. There is deep concern that AI-native startups or open-source models will strip incumbents of their ability to dictate pricing.
*   **Disintermediation:** Large Language Model (LLM) providers like OpenAI and Anthropic may eventually circumvent traditional application vendors by selling directly to enterprises, absorbing the functionality that specialized software used to provide.

### Dramatic Valuation Reset
*   **Multiples Collapse:** Average Price-to-Sales multiples across the sector have dropped from 6.5x to roughly 3.5x over the last two years. 
*   **Historic Lows:** Iconic companies are trading at unprecedented valuations:
    *   **Salesforce:** Currently trading at ~12x free cash flow.
    *   **Adobe:** Trading at roughly 9x forward free cash flow (traditionally a much higher multiple).
*   **Shift in Capital Allocation:** To combat falling stock prices, companies are pivoting from growth-at-all-costs to mature financial strategies. Almost all companies on Oliver's list are now actively buying back stock (e.g., Salesforce’s $25B buyback) or issuing dividends.
*   **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC):** While still high, SBC as a percentage of revenue is trending downward. Investors are increasingly skeptical of "adjusted earnings" that add back these costs, pushing for more transparent GAAP reporting.

### Identifying Potential Winners & Moats
*   **Vertical Software Resilience:** Companies that serve specific, highly regulated end markets—such as **Tyler Technologies** (local government) or companies in the insurance/healthcare space—are viewed as safer bets. Their moats are built on proprietary data and deep operational integration rather than just "code."
*   **Incumbents as Innovators:** Successful incumbents like **ServiceNow** are not "potted plants." They are aggressively acquiring AI startups and integrating agentic solutions to prove they can remain the "system of record" for global enterprises.
*   **The "Golden Age" of Applications:** Despite the market rout, Oliver suggests we are entering a golden age for what software can do. The challenge for investors is distinguishing between companies that will be "rationalized" (cut) by IT departments and those that will harness AI to accelerate growth.

### Buy-Side Sentiment & Private Equity
*   **The "Grim Reaper" Market:** Investor sentiment is described as "shell-shocked." Institutional holders who have owned these names for decades are hesitant to add positions until they see several quarters of stable retention and renewal rates.
*   **Private Equity Exposure:** Approximately 25-30% of direct lending books are currently in software companies bought by private equity between 2018 and 2022. While these companies (like Citrix) are durable, their mark-to-market valuations are significantly lower than their purchase prices.
*   **Wait-and-See Approach:** The market is currently using a "blunt instrument" to sell off the entire group. Stock picking has largely ceased as investors wait for a "pathway of confidence" that the cloud transition's winners won't be AI's losers.
