## Austan Goolsbee Is Worried the Economy Is Overheating

### TL;DR
Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee expresses concern that persistent inflation and a robust AI infrastructure build-out could be overheating the economy, while supporting a re-evaluation of Fed communication tools like the dot plot and forward guidance.

### Inflation Drivers and the State of Monetary Policy
* **Current Policy Stance:** Goolsbee notes that whether policy is restrictive depends on underlying inflation; with inflation running above 3%, real rates are lower than desired, leaving him nervous about inflation over the past six months.
* **Decomposing Inflation:** Goolsbee attributes a significant portion of past inflation shocks to temporary factors like tariffs and oil price shocks, but warns that ongoing services inflation and recent warm readings mean these pressures cannot be perpetually dismissed as "transitory."
* **Supply Shocks & Uncertainty:** The modern economy faces frequent supply shocks combined with demand-side pressures, creating unusual labor market dynamics such as low hiring and low firing, which requires central banks to incorporate higher baseline uncertainty into their frameworks.

### The AI Boom and Overheating Risks
* **Data Center Build-out:** High investment in AI infrastructure is creating localized resource crunches—such as shortages of land, construction contractors, and HVAC workers in Cedar Rapids, Iowa—competing with other sectors for labor and materials.
* **Aggregate vs. Sectoral Pressures:** While AI investment is creating intense sectoral demand, Goolsbee notes that it only qualifies as macroeconomic overheating if the impulse broadens out to drive up wages and prices outside its immediate lane, potentially signaling that current interest rates are not restrictive enough.
* **Productivity Paradox:** While successful AI-driven productivity growth could raise the neutral rate ($r^*$) and improve potential output long-term, recent data has shown six consecutive months of relatively weak productivity growth.

### The Post-COVID Disinflation and Bond Market Signals
* **Immaculate Disinflation:** Goolsbee credits aggressive Fed rate hikes with preserving the 2% inflation anchor—noted by TIPS inflation compensation remaining steadily anchored at 2.3% CPI—and preventing a catastrophic wage-price spiral during the post-COVID shock.
* **Bond Yields and Issuance:** Rising long-term bond yields reflect a combination of inflation expectations, expectations of higher-for-longer Fed rates, and increased Treasury issuance, rather than a generalized freakout over U.S. credit default.

### Re-evaluating Fed Communications and Frameworks
* **Skepticism of $r^*$:** Goolsbee dismisses $r^*$, calling it the Fed's "Sasquatch" because it is completely unobservable and unhelpful for determining the next policy move.
* **Embracing Less Forward Guidance:** Goolsbee supports reducing explicit forward guidance that ties policymakers' hands regarding future rate moves at specific meetings, arguing it adds unnecessary market volatility.
* **Rethinking the SEP and Dot Plot:** Goolsbee is critical of the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) and dot plots, noting that they often damage Fed credibility when multi-year predictions fail and that the median dot does not represent a cohesive individual's reaction function.
* **Task Forces on Communication:** All communication tools—including press conferences, meeting frequency, and dot plots—are on the table for review by the Fed's outside task forces, which Goolsbee views as a healthy post-zero-lower-bound rethink.
* **Reaction Function vs. Guidance:** Goolsbee emphasizes that while pre-committing via forward guidance is unhelpful, communicating a transparent reaction function—how the Fed reads incoming data like wages and prices—remains vital.

**TL;DR**
Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee warns that while the labor market remains stable, persistent and rising inflation—particularly in services—is a major concern that may require the Federal Reserve to maintain a restrictive stance and re-evaluate forward guidance.

### Labor Market: "Stable Without Being Good"
* **Low-Hire, Low-Fire Environment:** Goolsbee describes the current job market as stagnant rather than deteriorating.
    * **Hiring Rates:** Current hiring levels are comparable to the depths of a recession.
    * **Layoff Rates:** Conversely, layoff rates remain at historic lows typical of an economic boom.
* **Overall Stability:** Despite the "cross-current" in data, Goolsbee notes that unemployment (4.3%) and vacancy rates have remained steady for over a year, suggesting the market is not "falling apart."

### Inflation Concerns and the Dual Mandate
* **The "Wrong Way" Trend:** Goolsbee expressed significant concern that inflation has moved away from the Fed's 2% target over the last three months after five years of being elevated.
* **Service Sector Pressure:** While energy and tariffs contribute to price hikes, Goolsbee highlighted that services inflation is uncomfortably high and not driven by temporary external shocks.
* **Addressing Stagflationary Risks:**
    * **Framework:** In the event of threats to both employment and price stability, the Fed must determine which side is deviating more and the projected duration of that deviation.
    * **Policy Options:** Goolsbee emphasized that "everything should always be on the table," suggesting a stern response might be necessary if inflation expectations become unanchored.

### Monetary Policy and Forward Guidance
* **Skepticism of "Jawboning":** Goolsbee remains skeptical of using forward guidance (words) to influence markets when interest rates are not at the "zero lower bound."
* **The "Data Dog" Approach:** He advocates for a comprehensive review of all economic indicators—including food, energy, and private sector data—rather than focusing strictly on core inflation.
    * **Quote Reference:** He expressed agreement with Chair-designate Kevin Warsh’s view on utilizing a diverse set of inflation measures.

### AI and Productivity Risks
* **Short-Term Overheating:** While AI promises long-term productivity gains, Goolsbee warns against "counting chickens before they hatch."
* **The Wealth Effect:** High expectations for AI can drive up stock market wealth, leading high-end consumers to increase spending before actual economic potential is realized.
* **Supply Chain Bottlenecks:** Massive investment in AI infrastructure is already creating "crowding out" effects, such as:
    * Skyrocketing land prices.
    * Shortages of skilled labor (e.g., electricians).
    * Increased competition for computer chips.

### Notable Quotes

> "I'd characterize it as we've been stable without being good... The hiring rate is low enough it would correspond to kind of the depths of a recession, but at the same time, the layoff rate is as low as would be at the peak of a boom." — **Austan Goolsbee**

> "I want to be one of the data dogs, and one of the main rules of the data dogs is sniff every piece of data that hits the floor because it might be food." — **Austan Goolsbee**

> "Expectations of future productivity growth, the more hyped they are, the more they can contribute to inflation in the short run and they can force the hand of the central bank." — **Austan Goolsbee**
