## What does the next training paradigm look like?

- Channel: Dwarkesh Patel
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=20p5-kQXF_Q
- Published: 2026-06-26
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260626.html#video-20p5-kQXF_Q
- Speakers: Dwarkesh Patel (Host, Dwarkesh Patel Podcast); Sasha Rush (Associate Professor, Cornell Tech)

---

**TL;DR**  
AI labs are betting that training models on massive reinforcement learning environments will lead to AGI, but true progress requires moving beyond temporary in-context learning to "continual learning"—permanently distilling real-world deployment experiences back into a model's weights.

### The AI Lab Bet and the "Grindability" Bottleneck

*   **The RLVR Bet:** AI labs are betting heavily on Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). They believe training AIs on millions of verifiable tasks across thousands of diverse RL environments will yield general, resilient problem-solving agents.
*   **The Argument for In-Context Scale:** Optimists argue that current model limitations—such as data inefficiency and lack of continual learning—can be bypassed by scaling context windows. If an agent can hold six months of on-the-job experience within its context window, permanent weight updates during deployment may not be strictly necessary.
*   **Verifiability vs. Grindability:** While tasks like computer use are highly verifiable (e.g., checking if a transaction went through), progress has been slow because they are not easily "grindable."
    *   **Grindability** requires running thousands of parallel, deterministic rollouts against a replayable simulator from the exact same starting point (such as spinning up identical software containers to train coding agents).
    *   **The Web Simulation Barrier:** Real-world computer environments (like Amazon or Gmail) cannot be easily spammed with parallel bots without getting blocked, making training highly labor-intensive until AIs can reliably code high-fidelity clones of these applications.

### The Limits of RLVR Generalization

*   **Reset-Free Environments:** Critical human domains (such as starting a business, winning court cases, or running political campaigns) lack repeatable simulators or rapid feedback loops. These real-world rollouts require interacting with non-stationary environments where data is scarce and unstructured.
*   **Horizon Degradation:** The assumption that RLVR training will naturally generalize to massive horizons is highly contested. 
    *   Dwarkesh highlights a quote from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, who noted that models trained on short-context horizons often suffer performance degradation when served at long contexts.
    *   If models cannot easily generalize from short-horizon training to long-horizon execution, they will struggle when dropped into complex, open-ended real-world tasks.

### The Waste of Deployment Compute & Continual Learning

*   **Inference Compute Waste:** Currently, 30% to 50% of an AI lab's compute budget is spent on inference (deployment), which plays absolutely no role in updating or improving the base model's weights.
*   **The In-Context Learning Bottleneck:** Keeping all new knowledge in the context window (via the KV cache) is not a scalable way to learn. It fails to mimic human intelligence, which relies on compression, metaphor, and conceptual abstraction.
*   **Overcoming the Weight Update Challenge:**
    *   **On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD):** A technique where a base model is trained to match the predictions of a "teacher" model that has accumulated long-context experience during a session. 
    *   **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Deficit:** Traditional SFT forces a model to memorize conversational transcripts with perfect fidelity, risking forgetting or overwriting unrelated base knowledge.
    *   **The OPSD Advantage:** OPSD provides a dense supervision signal without requiring an outer-loop verifiable reward. Like RL, it concentrates parameter updates only on what is necessary to achieve the correct outcome, preserving the model's broader capabilities.

### "Dreaming" and the 2027 Vision for AI Improvement

*   **Dreaming (Test-Time Training):** To overcome real-world data scarcity, future models may use massive compute to build internal simulations of reality and "dream"—rehearsing skills and reinforcing successful strategies against self-generated environments.
    *   This is inspired by *EfficientZero*, an RL model that achieved human-level sample efficiency on Atari by playing dozens of simulated games in its "head" for every single step taken in the real game.
*   **The 2027/2028 Paradigm:** By late decade, the primary driver of AI capability will shift from pre-training updates to collective, real-world deployment experiences:
    1.  **Deployment:** A highly capable agent trained on RLVR is deployed into the real world.
    2.  **Weekly Review:** The agent co-works with a human for a week. At the end of the week, the user provides a performance review.
    3.  **Distillation:** If the review is positive, the model distills that week of experience back into its weights using OPSD and dreaming.
    4.  **Networked Intelligence:** Instead of static software updates, AIs will become dynamically smarter every day by learning from the aggregated experiences of millions of users across the global economy.

Global geopolitics is defined by a fundamental divide between continental powers (like Russia and China) that seek security through territorial conquest and exclusive spheres of influence, and maritime powers (like the US and UK) that leverage geographic moats and international alliances to pursue wealth through open trade.

***

### The Geopolitical Framework: Elephants vs. Whales

*   **Geopolitics vs. Grand Strategy:** Strategic thinker Sarah Paine defines geopolitics as the influence of geography on politics. Grand strategy is the integration of all instruments of national power—diplomacy, military, finance, agriculture—to achieve long-term security.
*   **The "Moat" Requirement:** Maritime powers ("whales") can defend themselves primarily at sea, whereas continental powers ("elephants") cannot. This geographic reality dictates their entire military, economic, and political architectures.
*   **The Three Great Geopoliticians:**
    *   **Alfred Thayer Mahan (Maritime Theory):** Argued that global power stems from trade-generated wealth. True maritime power requires a protective moat, a dense internal transport grid, reliable sea egress, a dense coastal population, and stable government institutions (such as transparent, regular elections). Both Russia and China lack these.
    *   **Halford Mackinder (Heartland Theory):** Argued in 1904 that Eurasia is the "pivot area" (the Heartland)—a massive natural fortress insulated from sea power by mountains, deserts, and ice. He believed whoever controls this Heartland could dominate the world.
    *   **Nicholas Spykman (Rimland Theory):** Asserted in 1943 that US security depends on keeping the "rimland" (coastal Eurasia) out of enemy hands. He argued that US security relies on a maritime shield and strong continental allies to act as bases of operation.

***

### The Continental Paradigm: Security Through Conquest

*   **The Zero-Sum Approach:** Lacking protective moats, continental powers face constant border threats. They view security as a zero-sum game of territorial expansion: they must either absorb their neighbors or risk being invaded by them. 
*   **Primary Mission of the Army:** Unlike maritime armies designed for overseas expeditions, a continental power's army has three internal priorities: protecting the ruling regime from its citizens, garrisoning the empire to prevent defection, and defending the borders.
*   **The Catastrophic Cost of Land Wars:** Because land wars are fought on home soil, continental powers suffer devastating military and civilian casualties compared to maritime nations.
    *   *World War II Military Deaths:* Land powers suffered staggering losses (8.5 million Soviet soldiers, 1.3 million Chinese) compared to maritime powers (326,000 British, 295,000 Americans).
    *   *World War II Civilian Deaths:* The contrast is even sharper, with over 25.5 million Soviet and 11 million Chinese civilian deaths, while maritime home fronts remained virtually untouched.
*   **Rules for Continental Strategy:**
    *   **No Two-Front Wars:** Avoid being ganged up on by multiple neighbors.
    *   **No Great-Power Neighbors:** Take on neighbors sequentially, destabilizing the rising ones and ingesting the failing ones.
    *   **The "Jackal State" Maneuver:** Sow discord among neighbors and step in to take the spoils after they have weakened one another.

***

### The Maritime Paradigm: Prosperity Through Trade

*   **The Positive-Sum Approach:** Secured by their ocean moats, maritime powers prioritize national prosperity over raw territorial defense. They treat the world's oceans as a shared "commons" and focus on market access to build compound wealth.
*   **Grand Strategy for "Elephant Hunting":** To defeat aggressive continental powers without fighting land wars on their terms, maritime powers historically employ a six-rule strategy:
    1.  *Keep the home economy growing* to fund long-term efforts.
    2.  *Prevent the continental power from "foraging"* by using naval blockades to cut off their trade.
    3.  *Rent an "elephant"* by funding and arming a continental ally to fight the main land campaign.
    4.  *Fight in peripheral theaters* where sea access is highly efficient and land transit is not.
    5.  *Do not attack the enemy's main land force directly* as an opening move.
    6.  *Commit land forces only after the enemy is severely weakened* and surrounded by a grand alliance.

***

### How Industrialization and Technology Favored Sea Trade

*   **The Shifting Currency of Power:** The Industrial Revolution changed the source of national power from land ownership (which provided agricultural commodities and peasant conscripts) to commerce, industry, and global trade networks.
*   **The Suez Canal Shift:** Opened in 1869, the canal routed trade away from overland routes like the Silk Road. 
    *   *The Supertanker Revolution:* During the Suez Canal's closure from 1967 to 1972, shipping companies adapted by building massive supertankers. Moving oil around the tip of Africa in a supertanker cost roughly $4 per ton, compared to $13 per ton in a smaller ship traveling through the canal.
*   **Containerization:** In the mid-20th century, Malcom McLean revolutionized maritime commerce by standardizing shipping containers. This single innovation plummeted loading costs from $5.83 per ton to under $0.20 per ton and vastly reduced port times.
*   **The Failure of Land-Based Trade (Belt and Road):** Xi Jinping’s land-based Belt and Road Initiative cannot compete with maritime transit.
    *   *Capacity limits:* The longest trains carry roughly 600 containers and face delays from differing rail gauges. In contrast, modern container ships carry over 21,000 containers, with cargo values exceeding $1 billion.
    *   *Security issues:* Maritime shipping can simply detour around conflict zones. Land transit requires end-to-end military control through some of the most unstable regions on Earth.

***

### Modern Global Friction and the Post-WWII Order

*   **The Invisible Victory:** Maritime order is functionally invisible because its objectives are negative—preventing piracy, stopping territorial aggression, and preserving freedom of navigation. Success means nothing happens, making it difficult to prove the value of the defense spending that secures it.
*   **Alliances as an Insurance Policy:** Following World War II, a generation of leaders built global institutions (the UN, NATO, IMF, and WTO) to substitute ruinous land wars with diplomatic and legal dispute resolution. 
*   **The Putin and Xi Challenge:** Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping represent a return to the zero-sum continental mindset. They seek to break down international institutions, hollow out alliances, and return the globe to warring, exclusive spheres of influence.
*   **The "Chemotherapy" of Sanctions:** While economic sanctions rarely trigger immediate collapse, they act as economic chemotherapy. By stripping 1% to 2% of GDP growth annually, they compound over generations. This creates stark regional wealth disparities—such as the gap between North and South Korea—and limits a rogue state's strategic piggy bank.

## Building AlphaGo from scratch – Eric Jang

- Channel: Dwarkesh Patel
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X_ZVSPcZhtw
- Published: 2026-05-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260515.html#video-X_ZVSPcZhtw
- Speakers: Dwarkesh Patel (Host, Dwarkesh Patel Podcast); Eric Jang (AI Researcher)

---

**TL;DR** 
Rebuilding AlphaGo with modern AI tools demonstrates how Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) provides a mathematically superior training target that sidesteps the credit assignment problem plaguing modern LLM reinforcement learning, while highlighting board games as an ideal, non-cheatable sandbox for benchmarking automated AI research agents.

---

### The Architectural Primitives of AlphaGo and Modern Go Bots

*   **Trump-Taylor Rules:** Unlike traditional Chinese or Japanese scoring rules which contain subjective human ambiguities regarding "dead" stones, the mathematically unambiguous Trump-Taylor rules are used to train computer Go systems because they can be resolved perfectly and deterministically by a computer.
*   **The PUCT Action Selection Formula:** AlphaGo utilizes PUCT (Predictor Upper Confidence with Trees) to navigate the game's massive branching factor (historically starting at 361 moves per turn). 
    *   **Importance Sampling:** PUCT scales its exploration bonus using the neural network's policy prediction ($P$). This trick prunes the search space from 361 moves down to the 3 or 4 most promising actions, making tree search highly tractable.
*   **ResNets vs. Transformers:** In low-compute, small-data regimes, Residual Networks (ResNets) still outperform Transformers.
    *   **Convolutional Inductive Bias:** ResNets naturally benefit from spatial locality (convolutions), which matches local Go board battles.
    *   **Global Feature Pooling:** While vision transformers natively capture global context, modern Go engines like *KataGo* achieve similar results by manually injecting globally aggregated features into convolutional layers, bypassing the massive data requirements needed to train global attention from scratch.
*   **Massive Compute Reductions:** In 2020, David Wu of Jane Street released *KataGo*, which achieved a 40x reduction in the compute required to train a superhuman Go bot tabula rasa. 
    *   Due to faster GPU hardware (such as NVIDIA Blackwell and Ada Lovelace architectures) and improved libraries, a superhuman 9x9 Go bot can now be trained on a single consumer GPU in under 24 hours for less than $100.

### The Policy Iteration Loop and Search Distillation

*   **The Core Self-Play Loop:** AlphaGo Zero's learning engine relies on a four-step iterative process executed during every move:
    1.  **Selection:** Walk down the existing MCTS tree from the root, selecting actions that maximize the PUCT formula.
    2.  **Expansion:** Once a leaf node is reached, dynamic game engine rules are evaluated to append its children.
    3.  **Evaluation:** The neural network evaluates the leaf node, outputting a win probability ($V$) and raw policy probabilities ($P$). AlphaGo Zero completely discarded computationally expensive random "rollouts" to the end of the game, relying solely on this single network evaluation.
    4.  **Backup:** Walk back up the path, incrementing visit counts ($N$) and updating the mean action values ($Q$) of parent nodes.
*   **Distillation as the Improvement Operator:** During self-play, MCTS acts as a policy improvement operator. The final search yields a highly optimized visit distribution ($\Pi$). The raw policy network ($P$) is then trained to directly predict $\Pi$.
    *   This distillation forces the raw network's instant, "shoot-from-the-hip" intuition to match the performance of a computationally intensive 1,000-step lookahead search.
*   **Value Function Grounding:** MCTS can collapse if the value network's predictions are inaccurate. To ground the model early in training, developers should warm-start the network using expert human datasets (like AlphaGo Lee) or co-train on smaller 9x9 boards to build robust endgame intuitions before transitioning to 19x19 boards.

### Why AlphaGo’s RL is Fundamentally Superior to LLM RL

*   **The Credit Assignment Problem:** Modern LLM alignment (via RLHF or policy gradient methods like REINFORCE) struggles with credit assignment. If a model generates a 100k+ token trajectory and receives a correct answer at the end, the algorithm must naively guess which of those 100k tokens actually drove the success—a dynamic described as "sucking supervision through a straw."
*   **Local step-by-step Supervision:** MCTS sidesteps this entirely by providing a highly informative, low-variance training target at *every single step* of the trajectory. 
    *   Rather than up-weighting all tokens in a winning game and down-weighting those in a losing game, the model trains on the soft probability distribution ($\Pi$) generated by a local planner.
*   **The Cold Start Trap:** In a naive RL setting with a large action space (such as an LLM vocabulary of 100k+ tokens), an untrained model has a near-0% chance of randomly stumbling upon a winning multi-step trajectory. 
    *   **Log-Scale Pass Rates:** Supervised learning provides massive information gains in this low-pass-rate regime, whereas naive RL learns almost nothing because its signal is bounded by the flat entropy of binary coin flips. 
    *   MCTS circumvents this by using local, step-by-step search to iteratively climb out of the 0% success rate trap, even when the model cannot yet visualize a complete winning path.

### The Future of Automated AI Research and Self-Improvement Loops

*   **Squeezing Performance via Automation:** Eric Jang successfully used Claude (referred to as Opus 4.6/4.7 in the transcript) as a highly capable, grad-student-level assistant. 
    *   **Open-Ended Optimization:** The model successfully executed complex, end-to-end experiment pipelines, including writing PyTorch code, diagnosing gradient bottlenecks, managing data-loader augmentations, and analyzing training anomalies.
    *   **Interactive Plotting:** By configuring a custom "experiment" skill, Jang could instruct the model to run experiments, generate custom charts, compile comprehensive reports, and propose structural hypotheses.
*   **The Lateral Thinking Bottleneck:** While current frontier models are exceptional at local hyperparameter optimization, they fail at macro-level research direction. Current systems struggle to perform lateral thinking—such as stepping back from a failing research track, identifying a fundamental assumption error, and returning to first principles to rewrite the core infrastructure.
*   **Go as an Outer Loop for Self-Improvement:** To train automated AI research agents, the AI community needs fast, cheap, and uncheatable outer verification loops. 
    *   **A Standardized Sandbox:** Go serves as a perfect environment. An agent's research modifications (such as testing a new auxiliary loss or architecture) can be verified objectively via its win rate against *KataGo*. 
    *   This provides a reliable, scalable training ground for automated scientists before deploying them to noisier, more expensive real-world tasks like drug discovery or robotics.

## David Reich – Why the Bronze Age was an inflection point in human evolution

- Channel: Dwarkesh Patel
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sRKBGVFVYAw
- Published: 2026-05-08
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260508.html#video-sRKBGVFVYAw
- Speakers: David Reich (Professor of Genetics, Harvard University); Dwarkesh Patel (Host, Dwarkesh Patel Podcast)

---

**TL;DR:** Overturning the scientific consensus that human evolution halted after the agricultural revolution, Harvard geneticist David Reich and collaborator Ali Akbari demonstrate that natural selection dramatically accelerated during the Bronze Age, driving massive shifts in immunity, metabolism, and a full standard deviation increase in genetic predictors of cognitive performance.

---

### The Bronze Age as an Evolutionary Inflection Point

*   **Accelerated Natural Selection:** Genomic data from the last 10,000 years reveals that natural selection did not slow down after humans transitioned to agriculture; instead, it rapidly accelerated. 
    *   This evolutionary "quickening" peaked during the Bronze Age and Iron Age (between 5,000 and 2,000 years ago), a period characterized by massive cultural, technological, and demographic shifts.
*   **The Evolutionary Mismatch:** The transition to farming (~11,000 years ago) was only the initial phase of a longer biological disruption. The true genomic shock occurred 5,000 years ago with the onset of the Bronze Age.
    *   **Environmental Pressures:** Skyrocketing population densities, urbanization, and living in close proximity to domesticated animals exposed human populations to unprecedented pathogen loads and social stresses, forcing rapid genetic adaptation.
*   **Immune Selection Reversals:** Pathogen pressures during this era drove rapid genetic adaptations that subsequently reversed when environmental conditions changed.
    *   **Tuberculosis Risk (TYK2):** The TYK2 variant, a major risk factor for severe tuberculosis and multiple sclerosis, rocketed up in frequency between 8,000 and 6,000 years ago, but reversed and plunged in frequency 3,000 years ago—potentially when tuberculosis became endemic in the population.
    *   **Iron Storage (Hemochromatosis):** Selection for pathogenic iron build-up also inverted around this period, showing strong positive and negative selection patterns in northern Europe that were largely absent in southern Europe.

### The Selection of Cognitive Performance and Metabolic Traits

*   **Cognitive Performance Surge:** Over the last 10,000 years, natural selection pushed the genetic predictor of cognitive performance up by approximately one full standard deviation.
    *   **Bronze Age Clustering:** Almost all of this cognitive selection occurred between 4,000 and 2,000 years ago. In contrast, there is virtually zero evidence of selection for cognitive performance over the last 2,000 years.
    *   **Hunter-Gatherer Baseline:** The genetic predictor for cognitive performance in European hunter-gatherers of the Mesolithic era is estimated to be three standard deviations below the modern European mean.
*   **Interpreting "Years of Schooling" Genetics:** Because modern IQ tests and schools did not exist in antiquity, researchers utilize modern polygenic scores for "years of schooling" as a proxy, which correlates highly with cognitive performance.
    *   **Executive Function:** Rather than pure intelligence, the selected genetic variant combination is highly correlated with traits like executive function, walking pace, delayed gratification, and maternal age at first birth.
    *   **Cross-Continental Validation:** To rule out geographical artifacts, the team compared European ancient DNA trajectories with modern Chinese Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS). They found a highly significant five-to-six standard deviation correlation, proving the selected cognitive variants are biologically robust across isolated populations.
*   **Declining Risk of Obesity:** Genetic markers linked to body fat, body mass index (BMI), waist-to-hip ratio, and type 2 diabetes risk have declined by about one standard deviation over the last 10,000 years.
    *   **Thrifty Gene Hypothesis:** Hunter-gatherers relied on "boom-and-bust" metabolic cycles, gorging on fat-storing foods to survive weeks between successful hunts. 
    *   **Stable Food Access:** The rise of agriculture made food access more stable day-to-day (despite periodic seasonal famines), removing the evolutionary advantage of high fat-storage capacity and selecting against obesity-related alleles.

### Methodological Breakthroughs in Ancient Genomics

*   **Scaling Ancient DNA:** Previous studies failed to identify directional selection due to small sample sizes—the largest previous study in 2024 identified only 21 selected genetic positions. 
    *   Reich's laboratory scaled ancient DNA processing to sequence over 5,000 ancient genomes per year, building a dataset of 16,000 ancient individuals (and 22,000 total genomes when including modern samples).
*   **The Relatedness Matrix Method:** Ali Akbari developed a statistical technique to isolate true natural selection from the genetic noise of major migrations (such as the Yamnaya steppe migration 4,500 years ago, which replaced up to 80% of Europe's DNA).
    *   **Isolation Modeling:** The algorithm treats history as an archipelago of isolated sub-populations. It measures genetic relatedness across the entire genome to establish a baseline of genetic drift, then identifies specific loci where allele frequencies changed in a consistent direction across all isolated pockets of space and time.
*   **Validation via the UK Biobank:** The team cross-referenced their selection signals with 500,000 sequenced modern genomes in the UK Biobank.
    *   **Signal Enrichment:** As their selection statistic increased, the enrichment of variants known to affect actual physical traits rose five-fold, proving that their statistical discoveries are functionally active in biology.
    *   **Loci Count:** The scan identified 479 independent genetic positions with 99% confidence of being under active selection, and roughly 3,800 positions with 50% confidence.
*   **Overcoming the Microbial DNA Bottleneck:** Ancient bone samples typically contain up to 99% microbial DNA from post-mortem bacteria and fungi.
    *   **In-Solution Enrichment:** The lab washes ancient samples over artificially synthesized DNA "baits" targeting 1 million highly variable, biologically informative human sites. This pulls out the human DNA and discards the microbial sequences, making sequencing highly cost-effective.

### A Copernican Revision of Neanderthal Ancestry

*   **The Mitochondrial Paradox:** The standard genetic model asserts that Neanderthals and Denisovans are sister groups that split from modern humans 700,000 to 800,000 years ago. 
    *   However, Neanderthal mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) and Y-chromosomes split from modern humans only 350,000 to 450,000 years ago, sharing a much more recent ancestor with us than with Denisovans.
*   **The Wavefront Expansion Hypothesis:** To resolve this paradox, Reich proposes that an anatomically modern population carrying Middle Stone Age (Levallois) tool technology expanded out of Africa or the Caucasus 200,000 to 300,000 years ago.
    *   **Wavefront Swamping:** As this pioneering modern population pushed into Europe, they interbred with local Denisovan-like archaic humans. Due to low pioneer densities at the wavefront, the expanding population was genetically swamped, leaving modern Neanderthals with 95% archaic nuclear genomes.
    *   **Lineage Persistence:** However, because of patrilineal or matrilineal social structures—combined with social selection against archaic mates—the expanding population systematically retained its modern Y-chromosome and mtDNA.
    *   **Cultural Continuity:** This explains why Neanderthals shared modern human stone tool technologies, fire use, and maternal/paternal DNA lineages despite being genetically clustered with Denisovans.
*   **Deep African Substructure:** Modern human genomes show that our African ancestors split into at least two distinct lineages over 1 million years ago, subsequently remixing 200,000 to 300,000 years ago.
    *   This African remixing event was contemporaneous with the expansion that formed the Neanderthals, establishing Neanderthals not as archaic outgroups, but as close paternal and maternal cousins of modern humans.

**TL;DR**
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang argues that Nvidia’s moat is built on "accelerated computing" rather than mere chip manufacturing, leveraging a 20-year head start in the CUDA ecosystem to deliver 50x performance gains through algorithmic co-design. ### The Business of "Electrons to Tokens"
*   **Fundamental Model:** Huang defines Nvidia’s core mission as the transformation of "electrons to tokens," where the "token" (a unit of intelligence) is the new valuable molecule of the digital economy.
*   **"As Much as Needed, As Little as Possible":** Nvidia’s philosophy is to focus only on the "insanely hard" parts of the stack while partnering for everything else.

*   **The Software Explosion:** Contrary to fears that AI will commoditize software, Huang predicts the number of software instances will skyrocket as AI agents—not just humans—become the primary users of tools like Excel and Synopsys.

*   **Capital Commitments:** Reports indicate Nvidia has between $100 billion and $250 billion in purchase commitments with foundries (TSMC) and memory suppliers (SK Hynix, Micron).

*   **Bottleneck Resolution:**

### Competitive Edge: CUDA vs. ASICs (TPUs)

    *   **Algorithmic Leaps:** Nvidia achieved a 50x jump from Hopper to Blackwell—far exceeding Moore’s Law—by simultaneously optimizing processors, fabric (NVLink), and libraries.
*   **TCO and Market Reach:** Huang claims Nvidia offers the best Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) and "performance-per-watt," challenging competitors like Amazon (Trainium) and Google (TPU) to prove their cost-advantage claims via public benchmarks like MLPerf.
*   **The Investment "Miss":** Huang admits a previous mistake in not realizing that early AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) required massive investment from suppliers. Nvidia is now correcting this by participating in multi-billion dollar funding rounds for major labs.

### Geopolitical Strategy and the China Debate
*   **The Danger of Conceding Markets:** Huang strongly opposes total export bans on China, arguing that conceding 40% of the world's technology market forces China to build its own ecosystem (e.g., Huawei’s record growth) and accelerates domestic chip innovation.
*   **Energy as an Offset:** While the U.S. has superior 3nm/2nm chips, Huang notes that China’s abundance of energy and manufacturing capacity allows them to compensate for using older 7nm technology by simply "ganging up" more chips.

    *   **Standardization Risk:** If Chinese-developed open-source models are optimized for domestic hardware, the "American tech stack" loses its global dominance as AI diffuses to the Global South.

## Dylan Patel — The Single Biggest Bottleneck to Scaling AI Compute

- Channel: Dwarkesh Patel
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDG_Hx3BSUE
- Published: 2026-03-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260313.html#video-mDG_Hx3BSUE
- Speakers: Dylan Patel (Founder, SemiAnalysis); Dwarkesh Patel (Host, Dwarkesh Patel Podcast)

---

**TL;DR**
The primary bottleneck for scaling AI compute is shifting from short-term constraints like power and data center construction to the long lead-time semiconductor supply chain, specifically EUV lithography tools and High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). Rapid scaling is currently driving a massive "memory crunch" that will significantly increase the cost and decrease the performance of consumer electronics as resources are diverted to AI.

### The Economics of Hyperscale CapEx and Lab Scaling
* **Forward-Looking Spend:** A significant portion of the $600 billion combined CapEx from the "Big Four" (Amazon, Meta, Google, Microsoft) is being spent on "setup CapEx" for 2027–2029.
    * **Long Lead Items:** This includes down payments on turbines, power purchasing agreements, and land acquisition rather than just current-year chips.
* **The Inference Revenue Trap:** AI labs like Anthropic and OpenAI are entering a phase where revenue growth requires massive incremental compute.
    * **Gigawatt Scaling:** Anthropic likely needs 4 gigawatts of inference capacity just to sustain its current revenue growth trajectory, separate from its R&D and training needs.
* **OpenAI vs. Anthropic Strategy:** OpenAI has been more aggressive in securing long-term (5-year) compute deals with neo-clouds like Coreweave and Oracle. 
    * **Flex Capacity:** Anthropic’s more conservative approach has left them potentially paying higher spot-market margins or relying on revenue-share agreements with Google and Amazon to bridge the compute gap.

### Logic and the ASML Bottleneck
* **ASML as the Ultimate Linchpin:** While power and data centers are bottlenecks today, by 2028–2029, the bottleneck falls to the production of EUV (Extreme Ultraviolet) lithography tools.
    * **Tool Math:** It takes roughly 3.5 EUV tools to manufacture 1 gigawatt’s worth of NVIDIA’s "Rubin" chips. 
    * **Supply Constraints:** ASML produces only ~70-80 tools per year, with plans to reach ~100 by the end of the decade. This physical limit creates a hard ceiling on global AI chip production.
* **Underinvestment in Fabs:** There is a mismatch between data center construction (which takes ~8 months) and semiconductor fab construction (which takes 2-3 years).
    * **The AGI Disconnect:** Dylan Patel argues that the broader semiconductor supply chain is not yet "AGI-pilled"—manufacturers do not fully believe in the trillion-dollar demand projections, leading to persistent under-capacity.
* **The Performance Gap:** Moving back to older process nodes (like 7nm) to bypass tool shortages is inefficient because the performance gulf between generations is compound. 
    * **Beyond Flops:** The 20x performance difference between chip generations often comes from networking, interconnects, and memory bandwidth rather than just raw transistor counts.

### The Memory Crunch and Consumer Impact
* **The HBM Penalty:** High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is essential for AI but requires 3 to 4 times the wafer area of standard DRAM to produce the same amount of memory.
    * **Demand Destruction:** To supply the AI industry, memory vendors must "destroy" consumer demand. This is leading to a halving of production for low-to-mid-range smartphones and PCs.
* **Consumer Cost Spikes:** Memory price increases could add $150–$250 to the Bill of Materials (BOM) for high-end devices like the iPhone.
    * **The "Hate AI" Factor:** Patel predicts consumer resentment toward AI will grow as PCs and smartphones stop getting better and instead become more expensive and less capable due to diverted components.
* **Fab Lag:** No meaningful new memory fabs will come online until late 2027 or 2028, ensuring the memory crunch remains a persistent drag on scaling.

### Geopolitics: US vs. China Scaling
* **Fast vs. Slow Takeoff:** Patel posits a "Fast timelines = US wins; Long timelines = China wins" framework.
    * **US Advantage:** Currently, the US and its allies control the leading-edge logic supply chain (3nm/2nm). If AI capabilities advance rapidly, the US can leverage this hardware lead into an insurmountable software/intelligence lead.
    * **China’s Strategic Scale:** China is building a fully vertical, indigenous supply chain. While they lack EUV today, they may achieve massive scale with DUV (Deep Ultraviolet) and 7nm multi-patterning by 2030, potentially out-scaling the West if the intelligence race plateaus or takes longer.
* **Distillation Barriers:** As US labs move toward agentic, multi-step "reasoning" models, it becomes harder for Chinese competitors to "distill" or copy American model outputs, further widening the gap.
