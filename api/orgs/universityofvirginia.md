**TL;DR:** Speaking at the Federalist Society’s 2026 National Lawyers Convention, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio argued that lasting political and legal change requires building institutions over decades rather than just winning elections, while defending an "America First" foreign policy that rejects the authority of international bodies like the International Criminal Court and prioritizes preventing Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon.

### Background & Introduction
* **Biographical Overview:** Danielle Rubio, a third-year law student at the University of Virginia and daughter of Marco Rubio, introduced her father, noting he was born in Miami, Florida, in 1971 to Cuban immigrants.
* **Political & Senate Career:** After working as a land use and zoning attorney, city commissioner in West Miami, and Speaker of the Florida House, Rubio served nearly 14 years in the U.S. Senate, where he led efforts on the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act, Hong Kong democracy legislation, and pressure on the Cuban regime.
* **Cabinet Appointment:** Nominated by President Donald Trump in November 2024, Rubio was unanimously confirmed by the Senate 99–0 on January 20, 2025, and sworn in on January 21, 2025, as the 72nd Secretary of State and National Security Advisor.

### Institutional Power & Lessons from the Federalist Society
* **The 1982 Conservative Crisis:** Rubio drew parallels to early 1982, when President Ronald Reagan’s approval rating fell 25 points from 68% to 43% by March 1982—four points below Jimmy Carter at the same point—amid a recession and unemployment approaching double digits (the worst in 40 years).
* **Origins of the Federalist Society:** Against that backdrop in April 1982, four law students organized a federalism symposium in New Haven, Connecticut, attended by roughly 200 people, where 46-year-old professor Antonin Scalia diagnosed that conservatives had been "outgunned" federally for half a century despite Republicans winning three of the last four presidential elections and appointing seven of nine Supreme Court justices.
* **Beyond Elections:** Rubio emphasized that winning elections only determines who holds office for a season, whereas true political power resides in universities, the press, NGOs, corporate boardrooms, and think tanks.
    * He credited the Federalist Society with understanding that lasting transformation takes a generation or more, built by recruiting young talent into pipelines of power rather than relying on two-, four-, or eight-year election cycles.

### International Law, Sovereignty, & American Identity
* **Rejection of "Weaponized" International Law:** Rubio argued that global elites and unelected bureaucrats use international law as an instrument to weaken U.S. sovereignty, citing criticisms of U.S. military strikes on cartel boats in the Caribbean, the capture of Nicolas Maduro, the 2022 overturning of *Roe v. Wade*, and ACLU petitions to the UN regarding U.S. immigration enforcement.
* **Sanctioning the ICC:** Rubio defended President Trump’s decision to sanction the International Criminal Court (ICC), calling it a "fake" and unaccountable tribunal that attempts to supersede sovereign constitutions.
* **American Expansion & Character:** Rejecting what he called the "spirit of Marx" that seeks to abolish distinctions between nations, Rubio highlighted America's historic ambition—growing from 13 states in 1776 to a continental empire of nearly 3 million square miles by 1848 (just 72 years later).
* **Assimilation & Citizenship:** Responding to Harvard Law 2L Tasha Dambacher, Rubio distinguished between coming to live and work in America versus coming to *be* an American, noting that "Americans by choice" who immigrate legally often possess a deeper appreciation for U.S. freedoms than native-born citizens.

### Foreign Policy, Economics, & The Iran Conflict
* **Putting America First:** Answering University of Texas student Mariah Sraom, Rubio stated that the U.S. government's primary duty is to work for Americans, noting he had replaced international flags at the State Department with American flags for "America 250." He added that he and President Trump will travel next week to the Philippines for the ASEAN conference and to China for the APEC conference.
* **The Iran Threat:** Responding to William & Mary Law 2L Lucy Griffin on what constitutes an American victory in Iran, Rubio stated that Iran's radical Shia clerical regime has waged war on the U.S. since 1979 through proxies—citing Hezbollah's 1982–83 barracks bombing that killed over 280 U.S. Marines, the early 1990s bombing of a Jewish center in Buenos Aires, militias in Iraq, the Syrian civil war, and Houthi attacks.
* **Defining Victory:** Rubio defined victory as ensuring Iran *never* acquires a nuclear weapon. He argued that current U.S. economic pressure is severely weakening the regime without requiring direct military action, noting that while Iran shoots at ships in the Strait of Hormuz, 13 to 14 million barrels of oil still pass through every night.

### Q&A: Faith, Youth, & Economic Reality
* **Faith in the Legal Profession:** Answering Florida State College of Law 3L Morgan Hughes, Rubio stated that faith is "who you are," not just what you do, and argued that Judeo-Christian values of human dignity underpin the U.S. criminal justice system's presumption of innocence.
* **Addressing Youth Anxiety & Economics:** Responding to Berkeley Law 3L Schnair Gansburg on why young Americans are drawn to political extremes, Rubio acknowledged that younger people today are financially further behind than their peers were 20, 30, or 40 years ago, and that most Americans judge the economy by their ability to afford daily needs rather than the stock market. He argued that the free market is a tool that must serve people and provide dignified work, not the other way around.
