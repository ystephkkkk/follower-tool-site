## Comey Charged for Third Time & Anthropic IPO | Bloomberg Law

**TL;DR:** The Trump administration has charged former FBI Director James Comey criminally for the third time in a little over a year over a 2016 leak, while AI company Anthropic has filed for an unusual IPO that splits board control between a non-profit trust and founders while insulating safety decisions.

### Criminal Charges Against Former FBI Director James Comey
* **The New Charges:** Former federal prosecutor Robert Mintz (McCarter & English) details that the Trump Department of Justice filed a criminal information charging James Comey with two counts:
  * **Transmission of national defense information:** Allegedly tied to an October 2016 *New York Times* article regarding a secret FISA court order requiring Yahoo to scan emails for a foreign terrorist organization.
  * **Conspiracy to transmit:** Allegations that Comey directed his FBI General Counsel to disclose classified FISA information to the *New York Times* so the newspaper could write a more accurate story.
* **Prior Investigations:** The same disclosure was previously investigated twice without criminal charges:
  * **Special Counsel John Durham's investigation:** Began in 2017 and resulted in a classified memo recommending no criminal charges.
  * **U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Columbia:** Opened an investigation in January 2019 and closed it without charges in February 2020.
* **Grand Jury & Criminal Information Debate:** Prosecutors bypassed a grand jury by filing a criminal information on the exact 10-year statute of limitations deadline (October 5, 2016 article):
  * **Constitutional requirement:** Under the Fifth Amendment, felonies carrying over a year of imprisonment typically require a grand jury indictment unless the defendant explicitly waives that right.
  * **Statute of limitations tolling:** Comey did not waive his right; prosecutors used the criminal information solely to toll the 10-year statute of limitations.
  * **Precedent:** Mintz noted that while extremely rare, the 11th Circuit Court of Appeals upheld a similar tolling via criminal information in a 2025 case due to COVID-19 pandemic grand jury suspensions.
* **Additional Pending Legal Actions:** Comey's defense team is expected to argue vindictive prosecution due to his public criticism of President Trump:
  * **North Carolina case:** Comey was indicted regarding a 2025 social media photograph alleged to be a threat against the President's life (pending).
  * **Eastern District of Virginia case:** Comey was charged with lying to Congress during 2020 testimony about media leaks; a federal judge tossed the case due to the prosecutor being illegally appointed, and the DOJ has appealed the dismissal.
* **Sealing and Unsealing:** The latest charges were initially filed under seal, but a federal judge granted Comey's lawyers' request to unseal them, citing significant public interest in criminal charges against a former FBI director.

### Anthropic's Landmark IPO and Corporate Governance
* **IPO Details:** Corporate law expert Professor Dorothy Lund (Columbia Law School) discusses Anthropic's filing with the SEC to trade on the Nasdaq Global Select market under the symbol **ANT**:
  * **First Mover:** Anthropic is moving closer to becoming the first company focused primarily on developing the most advanced AI models to go public.
  * **Capital needs:** The company is entering the public markets to secure cash for AI infrastructure in the expensive race to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
* **Unusual Board Governance Structure:** Unlike standard public companies (e.g., Meta's dual-class structure where Mark Zuckerberg retains voting control via high-vote shares), Anthropic's public investors will own most of the equity but possess zero board control:
  * **Long-Term Benefit Trust:** Composed of outside experts with **zero equity stake** in the company, controlling a majority of board seats.
  * **Founder LLC:** Controlled by **seven founders**, each electing specific board seats, also holding minimal equity.
  * **No Controlling Shareholder:** Power is split among individuals with very small economic stakes, removing traditional shareholder mechanisms like proxy fights to redirect the company if leadership fails.
* **Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) Status:** Anthropic is incorporated as a PBC, legally charging management to balance shareholder interests alongside a public interest mission to develop AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.

### Existential Risks and Disclosure Obligations
* **Prospectus Disclosures:** Anthropic's 90-page prospectus explicitly highlights existential risks posed by its AI models, including attempts to resist shutdown, conceal or manipulate information, and behave autonomously or misaligned—a departure from past mega-tech IPOs like SpaceX.
* **Mandated Transparency:** Going public forces Anthropic to disclose revenue, margins, compute commitments, and customer concentration, allowing competitors like OpenAI to see its financial and operational metrics.
* **Self-Regulation and Governance Pressure:** The IPO establishes a template for how companies "slow things down" for safety; however, competitive pressures, capital needs, and potential disagreements among the seven founders could erode safety commitments over time.

### Shareholder Litigation Dynamics
* **PBC Insulation:** The Public Benefit Corporation structure largely insulates the board from shareholder lawsuits claiming management prioritized mission over profits, requiring plaintiffs to prove a conflict of interest or grossly negligent process.
* **Oversight Liability:** Conversely, shareholders could potentially sue under a *Caremark* duty if the board completely fails to take safety into consideration for a major decision, resulting in regulatory fines or severe negative economic repercussions.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Professor Dorothy Lund** predicts that if AI technology rivals governments in power, **future administrations (though not the current Trump administration)** will pursue government regulation and potentially **attempts at nationalization** akin to the Manhattan Project.
* **Professor Dorothy Lund** predicts that if Anthropic's IPO is a smash success, rival frontier labs such as **OpenAI will follow suit and go public relatively soon** to fund expensive AI infrastructure in the race to AGI.

## Election Lawsuits and Supermarket Deal Trial | Bloomberg Law

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nw3m7Wv4CI8

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261027.html#video-nw3m7Wv4CI8
- Speakers: Richard Briffault (Professor, Columbia Law School); Harry First (Professor, NYU Law School); June Grasso (Host, Bloomberg Law)

---

**TL;DR:** The Trump administration is pursuing legally precarious lawsuits to intervene in midterm elections in California and Alaska, while Kroger and Albertsons are locked in a multi-billion-dollar Delaware Chancery Court trial following the FTC's successful blocking of their $24.6 billion merger.

### DOJ Midterm Election Lawsuits in California and Alaska

* **California Ballot Segregation Lawsuit:**
  * **Administration Claims:** The Justice Department sent letters on October 15th claiming roughly 10,000 non-citizens were registered to vote and requested mail-in ballots, demanding they be segregated.
  * **Procedural Bypass:** After a federal district court scheduled a hearing within six days, the DOJ bypassed the lower court and filed an "appeal" directly to the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals. A Ninth Circuit hearing was held on Sunday, October 23.
  * **Lack of Legal Authority:** According to elections law expert Richard Briffault, no federal statute gives the DOJ authority to sue states in advance to force ballot segregation, and the lists provided by outside groups have repeatedly proven wildly inaccurate (e.g., a Nevada list of 17,000 alleged non-citizen voters yielded zero verified cases).
  * **Secret Ballot Conflict:** Judges questioned how segregated ballots could be clawed back once outer envelopes are separated to preserve the anonymity of the secret ballot.

* **Alaska Senate Race Intervention:**
  * **Targeted Race:** The DOJ sued to block election officials from certifying the US Senate contest if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan loses to Democrat Mary Peltola.
  * **Candidate Confusion Claim:** The administration argued that a second candidate named Dan Sullivan on the ballot inherently creates voter confusion and taints the result.
  * **Precedent and Voting System:** The Alaska Supreme Court previously ruled the second Dan Sullivan had a legal right to be on the ballot. Furthermore, Alaska utilizes ranked-choice voting where the two Sullivans' votes can combine if a voter ranks them across choices.
  * **Lack of Substantive Law:** Briffault noted the lawsuit relies entirely on the preamble of the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA) rather than any substantive federal statute, calling the suit "preposterous" and "totally frivolous."

### Kroger-Albertsons $24.6 Billion Merger Collapse

* **Failed Deal Scope:**
  * **Deal Parameters:** Announced in 2022 and scrapped in December 2024 after being blocked by a federal judge following an FTC antitrust challenge.
  * **Market Presence:** The combination would have merged the first and second largest supermarket chains in the US, encompassing 5,000 stores, 4,000 pharmacies, and 710,000 store associates.
  * **Regulatory Environment:** Antitrust expert Harry First noted that the merger was proposed during an administration with aggressive merger guidelines, lowered concentration thresholds, and heightened sensitivity regarding food inflation.

* **Delaware Chancery Court Damages Trial:**
  * **Albertsons' Claims:** Albertsons sued Kroger the day after the merger was blocked, demanding the $600 million termination fee plus over $8 billion in lost shareholder merger premiums and taxes (totaling more than $9.1 billion in damages).
  * **Kroger's Counter-Claims:** Kroger argued that Albertsons sabotaged the transaction and offered only token store divestitures, meaning Kroger owes nothing—not even the termination fee.
  * **Divestiture Plans:** Kroger agreed to divest up to 650 stores (out of 5,000) to C&S Wholesale Grocers (which operates Piggly Wiggly), but the FTC and state enforcers deemed the relief inadequate.

### Executive Finger-Pointing and Legal Arguments

* **Blame Shifting in Court:**
  * **Kroger CEO Testimony:** Former and current Kroger executives testified that the merger was doomed from the start due to FTC Chair Lina Khan's hostility toward dealmaking, and claimed they relied entirely on antitrust counsel to formulate store divestiture strategies.
  * **Expert Skepticism:** Harry First expressed disbelief at the notion that a CEO would completely outsource merger negotiations and business strategy to lawyers without active business leadership.
  * **Buyer's Remorse Defense:** Albertsons will need to prove Kroger suffered buyer's remorse and intentionally deviated from normal negotiation practices to tank the deal.
  * **Inviolate Precedent:** First noted a strong parallel to the $48 billion Cigna-Anthem merger collapse, where a Chancery judge ruled Cigna breached the merger agreement but awarded zero damages because the deal was legally indefensible under antitrust law anyway.

### Predictions & Forward Calls

* **Harry First:** The Delaware Chancery Court trial over the Kroger-Albertsons merger dispute will conclude this week, with post-trial arguments scheduled to take place "in a few months."
* **Harry First:** Kroger's newly announced $1.7 billion acquisition of supermarket chain Giant Eagle (announced in July) will face scrutiny under a different FTC, though it is a much smaller merger than the Kroger-Albertsons deal.
