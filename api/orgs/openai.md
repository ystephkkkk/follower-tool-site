* **OpenAI’s IPO Plans:** Altman confirmed OpenAI will go public at some point. While there are real downsides to being public during a volatile, high-stakes period for AI safety, he believes it is directionally good for the broader world—not just private-market investors—to have exposure.
* **Investor Demand:** Pushing back on Albergotti’s suggestion of investor skepticism around Anthropic’s upcoming IPO, Altman said skepticism comes mostly from journalists while investors remain bullish. ### Supply Chain Bottlenecks & Memory Shortages
* **"Double Counting" in Chips:** Clarifying his closed-door remarks to Samsung executives, Altman explained that historically there was one main chip provider, making demand forecasting simple. Now, multiple chipmakers are talking to the silicon supply chain assuming OpenAI, Anthropic, and others will buy *all* of their chips.
* **Shifting Constraints:** At massive scale, OpenAI’s bottlenecks have shifted unpredictably between memory, rack assembly, data center capacity, and random components like PCB laminate.
* **Consumer RAM & $7T Capex:** Addressing backlash over his comments on home computer RAM, Altman said he wants people to have plenty of RAM for home tinkering. However, he noted OpenAI warned years ago about a coming memory squeeze and the need for $7 trillion in capex. Because the industry underbuilt, DRAM prices have jumped roughly 5X to 7X.
  * **Historical reluctance:** Memory vendors have been hesitant to commit capex because the business has historically been "invest $20 billion and make $19 billion" through booms and busts, though Altman expects production to ramp up significantly now.
  * **Cloud vs. Edge:** In a supply shortage, Altman argued it is more optimal to share memory in the cloud at 100% utilization rather than having it sit idle in edge devices.

### AI Backlash, Data Centers, and Politics
* **Why Voters Hate Data Centers:** Following a Democratic "blue wave" in which all but one data center referendum bill passed, Altman blamed the backlash on a terrible industry "sales pitch"—promising cures for cancer and cheap goods in exchange for humans losing agency and "liv[ing] like a Labrador."

He dismissed fears that debt-funded build-outs are a "house of cards," pointing to unprecedented actual revenue growth and margins, while acknowledging there will be some "foam on the top."
* **Political Stance:** Altman reaffirmed his July 2025 comment that he is "politically homeless" and that Democrats have lost the culture of innovation, though he intends to work with whoever is elected. He noted he last spoke with Donald Trump at the China state dinner.

### Security, Safety, and Product Lock-In
* **Espionage & "Pragmatic Centrists":** Addressing past security incidents (including hidden cameras/microphones in conference rooms and foreign agents trying to extract pre-training secrets over drinks), Altman said OpenAI takes a "pragmatic centrist" approach to safety—neither building in total secret nor pretending AI is like any other technology.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** OpenAI is using its technology to build new models faster, but is *not* pushing a button for unmonitored rapid self-improvement. Altman said holding back on RSI is a deliberate tactic to keep humans in control.
* **External Evaluators:** OpenAI is working with embedded evaluators, including METR. Altman could not confirm METR's report that Anthropic doubled its capability development speed, noting progress is still too "jagged" across tasks to generalize.
* **Dots & Data Lock-In:** Responding to Albergotti’s concern that OpenAI's *Dots* product stores user memory in the cloud without export or edit controls, Altman agreed in principle that users should own and be able to export their data (as they can with ChatGPT memory). However, he noted that *Dots*' nuanced understanding of a user's life is harder to export than an iPhone camera roll (100,000 photos) or iMessage.

### The Next Wave: AI for Science
* **Beyond Chatbots and Coding:** OpenAI recently expanded free compute for academic researchers. Altman highlighted the progression from chatbots to coding agents to "AI for science"—solving historically difficult math theorems and accelerating physics, chemistry, material science, and chip design.
* **Compressing Progress:** Altman’s goal is to use AI and compute to compress 10 or 100 years of scientific discovery into a single year.

## OpenAI’s Cofounder & Research Chief Explain Self-Improving AI

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_aA1-R70E8
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261117.html#video-h_aA1-R70E8
- Speakers: Sarah Wang (General Partner, a16z); Mark Chen (Chief Research Officer, OpenAI); John Schulman (Chief Scientist, Thinking Machines Lab)

---

### TL;DR
Frontier AI labs have entered a "Centaur era" where AI acts as "research interns" roughly 25–50% of the way toward full recursive self-improvement (RSI). While AI is already accelerating research—delivering an estimated 2x speedup—achieving full RSI requires overcoming bottlenecks in "research taste," downstream evaluation, and compute, even as automated research emerges as one of the most promising tools for solving AI alignment.

### Defining RSI and the "Centaur Era"
* **The Current State of Acceleration:** According to a November 5 Metr report cited by a16z general partner Sarah Wang, AI research at Anthropic has seen a roughly 2x speedup in the rate of progress over the past year, driven by human researchers using AI models to accelerate their workflows.
* **RSI as an Accelerator:** Mark Chen, Chief Research Officer at OpenAI (who has been with the company for over eight years and co-leading research with Jakub Pachocki since 2025), defines RSI primarily as using AI—such as Codex—to accelerate pre-training and reinforcement learning (RL) and shift the capability curve.
* **"AI Research Interns":** Chen notes that OpenAI has built "AI research interns" today, measured by the amount of compute managed end-to-end by Codex to run scoped experiments, monitor runs, and automate parts of the research pipeline.
* **The AGI vs. RSI Spectrum:** Chen views both AGI and RSI as spectrums rather than single moments in time. While he estimates the industry is roughly 80% of the way to AGI, he places current capabilities at only 25–50% along the RSI spectrum.
* **The Centaur Workflow:** John Schulman, co-founder and Chief Scientist of Thinking Machines Lab and co-founder of OpenAI, describes the current phase as the "Centaur era," where an expert human working with AI outperforms either alone.
  * *Concrete Example:* Schulman shared his workflow using Fable (with settings like UltraCode turned up) to run experiments for several days. While the AI generates reasonable experiments and ideas, the end result often fails until Schulman steps in to read the results, ask questions the AI missed, and redirect the project.

### Bottlenecks to Full RSI: Taste, Verification, and Compute
* **The "Research Taste" Gap:** Schulman defines research taste as knowing which ideas are important long-term, generalizable, and composable with other ideas—as opposed to routine execution or hyperparameter optimization.
  * *Knowledge vs. Experience:* While AI can read and memorize all historical literature, it lacks the human experience of making predictions, being wrong, and absorbing those lessons.
  * *Baking in Intuition:* To bridge this gap, Schulman expects companies will use RL from human feedback (RLHF) to collect data from researchers on which proposed ideas are good, effectively baking human intuition into models.
* **Verification and Evaluation:** Chen identifies measuring success as one of the defining challenges of RSI.
  * *Pre-training vs. Downstream:* Pre-training is straightforward to hill-climb because the field relies on a single metric: perplexity. Downstream, however, evaluation benchmarks have increasingly shorter shelf-lives, forcing labs to focus heavily on automating the creation of new, harder evals.
* **Compute Constraints:** Unlike math—where hypotheses can be tested purely through reasoning—AI research resembles experimental physics or biology, requiring physical experiments.   * **False Duplicate Safeguards:** If a finding is marked as a duplicate, the system creates a reminder to confirm it no longer works once the primary bug lands in the tree.

### Evolution of Agentic Architecture: From Loops to Codex
- **Initial Paradigm Shift:** 
  - Early AI interactions required manual human intervention (e.g., copying and pasting code like FizzBuzz).
  - The introduction of reasoning combined with tool use (such as in the o3 launch) created the foundational "agentic loop."

- **Solving Long-Horizon Work via Compaction:**
  - Early models hit context window limits and stopped prematurely.

- **From Outer Loops to Native Persistence:**
  - Developers initially used manual scripts or brute-force loops (like Geoffrey Huntley's "Ralph loop" or using `continue` statements) to keep models working.
  - Introduced `--goal` in GPT-5.5 to track objectives outside the core context window.
  - With GPT-5.6, persistence was trained directly into the model, eliminating the need for `--goal` hacks. Wharton professor Ethan Mullica notably tested this by having Codex play *Slay the Spire 2* for five hours straight.

### Multi-Agent Workflows & Best Practices
- **Scaling from Single Agents:**
  - Eric (DX team) advises starting with a single agent and clear verification methods for tasks before scaling up to teams.
- **Autonomous Coordination (GPT-6):**
  - GPT-6 series models can coordinate and collaborate natively, breaking down massive tasks (like full codebase refactors or game ports) by spinning up subagents (e.g., Ultra) autonomously.
- **Agent Geometries and Effort Scaling:**
  - **Effort Scaling:** Dynamically assigns reasoning effort and models using subagents (e.g., Sol managing integration, Astra handling difficult test failures, and Luna taking on simple busy work).
  - **The Pair Pattern:** Deploying a pair of subagents with explicit communication boundaries (e.g., one agent handling capture tech in an old game engine, the other unpacking/visualizing the scene in Blender).
  - **Thread Managers:** Using ChatGPT app threads to spin up and manage separate conversations for discrete tasks (like a separate chat for every PR in a review queue) while keeping the manager informed.
  - **Departmental Roles:** Structuring long-running projects (like video games) by assigning agents distinct lanes (art director, playtester, programmer, producer).
- **Pitfalls to Avoid:**
  - **Middle Management Tax:** Overcomplicating agent hierarchies with too many rules causes agents to stall seeking approval.
  - **Context Drift & The Work Log:** To counteract short-term memory loss and constant human nagging/steering, prompt Codex to maintain a compact work log containing the overall goal, running work, and how recent steering relates to both.

### Scaling to the Next Generation: Agent Teams
- **Massive-Scale Collaboration:**
  - Moving beyond a handful of agents to thousands (e.g., 10,000 agents) to tackle massive, complex problems.
- **The Navier-Stokes Breakthrough:**
  - OpenAI utilized an internal advanced model alongside Codex to solve the long-standing Millennium Prize Navier-Stokes problem using **10,000 agents**, generating **2.7 million messages** and **130 billion output tokens**.
- **Agent Team Architecture:**
  - **Unified Goal with Diversity:** Initialized multiple teams with different versions of a problem to encourage varied approaches and prevent duplicative effort.
  - **Cross-Team Communication:** Agents share findings and ask questions across different teams.
  - **Dynamic Orchestration:** As hypotheses were explored and new questions arose, teams were dynamically created, steered, or redirected toward promising lines (e.g., all teams being diverted to the unforced Euler result once found).
  - **Broader Impact:** Using this internal model architecture, OpenAI resolved more than **100 longstanding problems** across mathematics and continues making discoveries in math and science.
