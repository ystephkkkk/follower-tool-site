## OpenAI’s Noam Brown: Why Game Theory Didn’t Lead to General Intelligence (Live at No Limit Finals)

- Channel: Sequoia Capital
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tRaFbDFBl_A
- Published: 2026-11-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261124.html#video-tRaFbDFBl_A
- Speakers: Noam Brown (Researcher, OpenAI)

---

### TL;DR
OpenAI researcher Noam Brown left computational game theory behind after discovering that human-data modeling—not mathematical rationality—is the key to general intelligence, leading him to help build o1’s test-time reasoning. Today, AI exhibits "jagged intelligence" that puts us in a "centaur period" of human-AI collaboration, though rapid recursive self-improvement leads Brown to advise promising students to drop out and join AI labs immediately.

### From Poker to Diplomacy: The Crisis of Faith in Game Theory
* **Early Poker Foundations:** Brown began studying AI for poker in grad school in 2012, winning the MIT Pokerbots competition (which offered $10,000 in prize money) in both 2014 and 2015 using Game Theory Optimal (GTO) self-play via Counterfactual Regret Minimization (CFR).
* **Tournament Strategy:** In a 60-minute, multiplayer, non-instant-runoff format like Sequoia's No Limit tournament ($100,000 prize pool), Brown noted that while K-means clustering plus 30 minutes of self-play can yield a low-fidelity GTO bot, an exploitation-based approach that targets opponent weaknesses is likely superior.
* **The Limits of Rationality in Diplomacy:** In 2019 (around GPT-2's release), Brown targeted Diplomacy because it required natural language negotiation and cooperation. He realized game theory fails here because it assumes all players are rational—illustrated by the Ultimatum Game, where offering $0.01 out of $100 is game-theory optimal, yet humans will reject it out of spite.
* **Modeling Human Irrationality:** Winning Diplomacy bots had to be trained on massive amounts of human data to imitate human behavior and irrationality. Host Alfred Lin connected this to Daniel Kahneman’s *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, noting that behavioral economics shows consistent "bugs" in human brains.
* **The 2022 Pivot:** Realizing game theory was not the path to general intelligence caused a "crisis of faith" for Brown in early 2022, prompting him to pivot away from his core expertise toward general-purpose reasoning and reinforcement learning (RL).

### The Reasoning Breakthrough & OpenAI's o1
* **Challenging the Pre-Training Dogma:** When Brown joined OpenAI in 2022, much of the AI field believed simply scaling up pre-training would solve everything. Brown pointed out that GPT-4 couldn't play Go, chess, or poker, and scaling pre-training alone to play Go confidently would require roughly 6 orders of magnitude more compute.
* **Thinking Before Acting:** Unlike early 2012–2013 poker bots that acted instantly due to short tournament clocks, humans pause to think through tough decisions.
* **Scaling Test-Time Compute:** The key unlock for OpenAI's o1 was applying RL to chain-of-thought reasoning. Training models to think longer before responding proved to be a general-purpose way to scale test-time compute across any domain, rather than relying solely on pre-training scale.

### The "Centaur Period" and Jagged Intelligence
* **Evaluating New Models:** Brown tests every new model by asking it to build a poker bot. While GPT-6 Astro can now make money playing online poker, it still cannot build a full-scale poker bot on its own (though it can build an excellent river solver in a single prompt).
* **Where Models Still Fail:** Models 5.5 and 5.6 were the first to 10x–20x Brown's bot-building speed, but current models still lack long-term planning and organization. For example, they will waste hours trying to optimize the numerics of CFR, failing to realize CFR is robust to small numerical errors.
* **Jagged Intelligence & Prompting:** Alfred Lin highlighted that AI intelligence is "jagged"—superhuman in some areas and weak in others. Humans add value by recognizing these gaps and breaking complex tasks into bite-sized problems the model can solve.
* **The Centaur Period:** Brown argued that most professions, including AI research, are entering a "centaur period" where human-plus-AI teams outperform either working alone.

### Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) & Talent Strategy
* **A Historical Loop:** Brown framed RSI not as a new concept, but as the story of all human technological progress—from telescopes to moving from punch cards to compilers, and now using LLMs to filter pre-training data quality (a practice already underway in 2022–2023).
* **What's Different Now:** Today's RSI loop is distinct due to the generality of the technology and its speed. While bottlenecks remain in compute and the "long tail" of tasks models are bad at, progress has accelerated over the past 6 months.
* **Drop Out Now:** Given the steep rate of progress, Brown’s most controversial advice is that promising students should drop out of school and join AI labs immediately. He noted he has hired several dropouts who skipped their senior year of college and achieved exceptional success.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Steep AI Progress:** The AI field will continue to see a "very steep rate of progress over the next year or two" — *Noam Brown*
* **General-Purpose Game Evaluation:** While LLMs cannot yet play real-time strategy games or *Zelda: Wind Waker* at a superhuman level, "Give it a year, who knows?" — *Noam Brown*
* **World Series of Poker (WSOP):** Within the next 5 years, a single AI agent has a 20% chance of winning the WSOP against humans—not via GTO (due to high variance), but only if it becomes superhuman at reading human faces and detecting bluffs — *Noam Brown*

## Investigating Linux Vulnerabilities with GPT-6 Cyber

- Channel: OpenAI
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MpsO8Ds8uyo
- Published: 2026-11-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261123.html#video-MpsO8Ds8uyo
- Speakers: Kyle Zeng (Cyber researcher, OpenAI); Clint Gibler (Applied cyber research lead, OpenAI)

---

**TL;DR**
OpenAI’s GPT-6 Cyber model, paired with a custom vulnerability research pipeline, dynamically validated over 1,000 Local Privilege Escalation (LPE) exploits in the 40-million-line Linux kernel—resulting in 948 confirmed vulnerabilities and candidate patches—demonstrating how AI can massively scale human security expertise while reducing the burden on open-source maintainers.

### Pipeline Architecture & Scale
* **Targeted Code Auditing:** Because not every line of the 40-million-line Linux kernel is security-relevant, Kyle Zeng’s pipeline first identifies security-relevant code regions—generating over 5,000 audit targets—before deploying analyst agents to flag potential issues.
* **Dynamic Validation Over Static Reasoning:** The analyst agents initially produced 2,038 unvalidated candidate findings. Triage agents then launched virtual machines (such as the latest Ubuntu) to dynamically trigger the bugs and generate working reproduction scripts that prove an unprivileged user can gain root access.
* **Operational Visibility:** Zeng built a custom dashboard to track the 5,000+ audit targets, 2,000+ candidate findings, vulnerability reports, suggested patches, and the token usage and API credit spending at each stage.
* **Scaling Human Expertise:** In his pre-AI career competing in events like Pwn2Own, TyphoonPwn, and Kernel CTF, Zeng demonstrated around 10 LPE exploits—noting that 20 LPEs was considered a massive career milestone before AI. The pipeline’s 948 confirmed LPEs represent a dramatic amplification of his domain knowledge.

### Redefining Vulnerability Counting & Patching
* **Variant Analysis Complicates Bug Counting:** Traditionally, separate variants of a code pattern were counted as distinct vulnerabilities with individual patches. Because the AI pipeline automatically performs variant analysis, a single generated patch can fix multiple variants at once.
  * While the pipeline produced more than 1,000 working exploits, OpenAI reported 948 vulnerabilities because they were resolved with 948 patches.
* **Maintainer-Focused Patching:** Clint Gibler emphasized that maintainers do not want a million separate patches; they want the minimal set of code changes—or strategic architectural fixes—that solve the most problems and save maintainer time.
* **Open Research Question:** Zeng noted that patching at a common sink to fix multiple variants can sometimes introduce performance regressions, meaning maintainers occasionally prefer multiple patches at the source of the vulnerabilities. Determining when and where to patch remains an open research question.

### Maintainer-First Collaboration & Quality Control
* **Three-Step Quality Process:** Before submitting any patch upstream, Zeng’s team applies three safeguards to minimize noise and ensure compatibility with Linux kernel community conventions:
  * **Variant-Aware Generation:** Generating patches that address multiple potential vulnerabilities simultaneously.
  * **Adversarial Testing:** Having the model actively attack its own generated patches to see if it can bypass the fix. Gibler highlighted that this adversarial loop is valuable for both AI-generated and human-written patches, as the system successfully found issues in both.
  * **Manual Review & Deduplication:** Humans review every patch and verify it has not already been patched upstream or discussed publicly.
* **Iterative, Human-Driven Process:** Maintainer feedback has been positive, with many patches merged. Gibler stressed that building the pipeline was not a binary test of AI capability, but an iterative, human-expert-driven process—adjusting the pipeline’s style and conventions based on maintainer feedback and project-specific requirements.
* **Looking Ahead:** Zeng noted that the pipeline primarily found easier-to-discover bugs, and that uncovering deeper vulnerabilities will require better models or smarter pipelines, alongside continued collaboration between the security community and maintainers.

### Notable Quotes
> "These are not hypothetical vulnerabilities or the model says, 'Oh, I think I found something here.' This is something that the output, you can just run it and observe that it works." — Kyle Zeng

> "It isn't really like, can AI do this or not do it. It's more of like a human expert-driven iterative process where you can actually get meaningfully better results." — Clint Gibler

> "Ultimately, as AI is becoming more and more capable at cybersecurity, I think it really requires the security community as well as maintainers everywhere working together to secure the critical software we all rely on." — Clint Gibler

## Why OpenAI Acquired a Biotech Startup

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RwYz8XhZiu0
- Published: 2026-11-23

- Speakers: Joy Jiao (Life sciences lead, OpenAI); Ashoka Rajendra (Life sciences team, OpenAI); Jay Rughani (Investor, a16z)

---

**TL;DR:** OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio to pair its frontier reasoning models (like GPT Rosalind) with an agentic workflow harness—"Codex for scientists"—aimed at removing human attention as the bottleneck across drug discovery, clinical development, regulatory filings, manufacturing, and patient distribution.

### OpenAI’s Life Sciences Strategy & The Orchestra Bio Acquisition
* **Three Strategic Pillars:** OpenAI’s Joy Jiao outlined the company's life sciences strategy: (1) train the best models, (2) build the best product/harness to accelerate scientists and drug makers, and (3) demonstrate real-world capabilities alongside academics, biotechs, and pharma.
* **Solving the "Capability Overhang":** Jiao noted that while models like GPT Rosalind perform well on chemistry and structural biology benchmarks, scientists often don't know how to prompt them. OpenAI acquired Orchestra Bio to build a specialized interface—akin to Codex for software engineers—that makes these capabilities accessible.
* **Why Orchestra Bio Joined:** Ashoka Rajendra, founder of Orchestra Bio, explained that pairing Orchestra's drug-program and clinical-trial orchestration software with OpenAI's bleeding-edge models (which see major capability leaps every 3–4 months) creates an inflection point for the industry.
* **The "Three Ds" Framework:** a16z partners Jay Rughani and Jorge Conde structured the conversation around how AI impacts the life sciences value chain across **Discovery, Development, and Distribution**.

### Discovery: Infinite Attention & Molecular Design
* **Removing the Human Attention Bottleneck:** Jiao contrasted math (verifiable problems) with biology (exploratory, massive datasets like next-gen sequencing and high-throughput assays). By deploying thousands of parallel agents, scientists are no longer limited by human attention spans.
  * **Perturb AI Collaboration:** In an *in vivo* Perturb-seq dataset of mouse brains (2,000 perturbations across 8 million cells), deploying 2,000 agents uncovered biological insights missed during months of manual human analysis—including two genes in the same protein complex behaving differently in one specific cell type, with implications for tuberous sclerosis.
* **Amplifying "What to Do Next":** Conde highlighted that knowing what to do next is the rarest skill in drug hunting. Rajendra noted that agents amplify this by scanning 1,000–5,000 papers, external corpora, and internal experimental histories (capturing tacit knowledge and counterfactuals).
* **Superhuman Molecular Design:** Jiao shared that internal model training is yielding glimmers of superhuman intuition—specifically, looking at a primary protein sequence to visualize its 3D structure and spot molecular liabilities, a skill currently limited to roughly 10–100 people globally.

### Development & Distribution: Trials, Regulatory, Manufacturing, and Access
* **Clinical Trials & Enrollment:** Rajendra emphasized that clinical development is where most time and money are spent. Agents can monitor trial sites, adverse events, and fragmented systems (EDC, CTMS, TMF) to keep trials on track.
  * **Patient Matching:** AI provides a step-function improvement over 10-year-old rules engines for parsing complex inclusion/exclusion criteria. OpenAI sees a major opportunity to connect its **1.2 billion ChatGPT users**—**330 million** of whom ask health questions—with clinical trial eligibility.
* **Regulatory Submissions:** Assembling IND, CSR, and BLA packages requires managing massive unstructured and semi-structured data. LLMs help teams compile evidence and catch outliers before FDA submission. Rajendra noted the FDA is signaling openness to continuous data submissions and AI-assisted reviews.
* **Manufacturing (CMC):** Tech transfers to CDMOs and commercial scale-ups are prone to costly delays. Agents can read handwritten paper batch records, reconcile parameters, and investigate deviations (e.g., determining if a 3–4 hour refrigerator temperature deviation threatens a batch or indicates a systemic facility issue).
* **Distribution & Patient Access:** Beyond R&D, consumers and clinicians are already using ChatGPT to translate symptoms for 20-minute doctor visits and navigate insurance denials and prior authorizations.

### Data Bottlenecks, Ecosystem, & Trust
* **The Biology Data Bottleneck:** Jiao identified experimental noise (e.g., lab-to-lab variance, EHR inconsistencies) as the biggest hurdle in training biological models. Low-noise, high-signal data—such as genomic sequencing and structural biology—is most valuable.
  * **Data Factory Advice:** Responding to Conde’s question on "data factory" startups, Jiao noted that individual datasets experience exponential decay in value (a half-life), though overall demand for fresh, complex physical-world data will grow linearly.
* **Platform Ecosystem:** Asked how life sciences AI startups should position themselves alongside OpenAI, Rajendra encouraged founders to build plugins and MCP connectors, positioning ChatGPT and Codex as the "front door" where scientists do their daily work.
* **Real-World Clinical Impact:** Jiao highlighted a collaboration with Boston Children’s Hospital using **o3 Deep Research** on **300–400 unsolved diagnostic cases**.
  * One patient who fell ill at age 9 waited **20 years** for a diagnosis until o3 analyzed her file and identified the underlying cause.
  * In another case, o3 went back to raw sequencing reads, re-analyzed low-quality data, and correctly identified the disease-causing mutation.
* **Traceability & Biosafety:** To build trust in AI-generated conclusions, Rajendra stressed the need for a "chain of evidence" that lets humans verify outputs faster than doing the work themselves. For biosafety, Jiao explained OpenAI uses **trusted access** (verifying legitimate institutions like Moderna) rather than restrictive prompt-monitoring, as intent cannot be reliably inferred from prompts alone.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Molecular Design Shift:** The way scientists design molecules with AI will "change a lot in the next 3 to 6 months." — *Joy Jiao*
* **Foundational Physics & Biology Breakthroughs:** Navier-Stokes-level breakthroughs will happen first at the atomic systems level, scaling to chemical reactions, structural biology, and eventually cells/organisms, becoming "solved" up to a noise ceiling over the next **2 years**. — *Joy Jiao*
* **The One-Person Biotech:** When asked if we will see a "one-person biotech" company—where a single person launches multiple programs and advances them to clinical trials—anytime soon, he replied: "Very much so, yes." — *Ashoka Rajendra*

### Notable Quotes

> "The model's already superhuman at the ability to understand clinical information and to help scale the expertise of clinical care when we have a shortage of that." — *Ashoka Rajendra*

> "This girl became sick when she was nine and she waited 20 years for a diagnosis. And it wasn't until we deployed o3 to her file that we figured out what the underlying cause of disease was." — *Joy Jiao*

### TL;DR
Frontier AI labs have entered a "Centaur era" where AI acts as "research interns" roughly 25–50% of the way toward full recursive self-improvement (RSI). While AI is already accelerating research—delivering an estimated 2x speedup—achieving full RSI requires overcoming bottlenecks in "research taste," downstream evaluation, and compute, even as automated research emerges as one of the most promising tools for solving AI alignment.

### Defining RSI and the "Centaur Era"
* **The Current State of Acceleration:** According to a November 5 Metr report cited by a16z general partner Sarah Wang, AI research at Anthropic has seen a roughly 2x speedup in the rate of progress over the past year, driven by human researchers using AI models to accelerate their workflows.
* **RSI as an Accelerator:** Mark Chen, Chief Research Officer at OpenAI (who has been with the company for over eight years and co-leading research with Jakub Pachocki since 2025), defines RSI primarily as using AI—such as Codex—to accelerate pre-training and reinforcement learning (RL) and shift the capability curve.
* **"AI Research Interns":** Chen notes that OpenAI has built "AI research interns" today, measured by the amount of compute managed end-to-end by Codex to run scoped experiments, monitor runs, and automate parts of the research pipeline.
* **The AGI vs. RSI Spectrum:** Chen views both AGI and RSI as spectrums rather than single moments in time. While he estimates the industry is roughly 80% of the way to AGI, he places current capabilities at only 25–50% along the RSI spectrum.
* **The Centaur Workflow:** John Schulman, co-founder and Chief Scientist of Thinking Machines Lab and co-founder of OpenAI, describes the current phase as the "Centaur era," where an expert human working with AI outperforms either alone.
  * *Concrete Example:* Schulman shared his workflow using Fable (with settings like UltraCode turned up) to run experiments for several days. While the AI generates reasonable experiments and ideas, the end result often fails until Schulman steps in to read the results, ask questions the AI missed, and redirect the project.

### Bottlenecks to Full RSI: Taste, Verification, and Compute
* **The "Research Taste" Gap:** Schulman defines research taste as knowing which ideas are important long-term, generalizable, and composable with other ideas—as opposed to routine execution or hyperparameter optimization.
  * *Knowledge vs. Experience:* While AI can read and memorize all historical literature, it lacks the human experience of making predictions, being wrong, and absorbing those lessons.
  * *Baking in Intuition:* To bridge this gap, Schulman expects companies will use RL from human feedback (RLHF) to collect data from researchers on which proposed ideas are good, effectively baking human intuition into models.
* **Verification and Evaluation:** Chen identifies measuring success as one of the defining challenges of RSI.
  * *Pre-training vs. Downstream:* Pre-training is straightforward to hill-climb because the field relies on a single metric: perplexity. Downstream, however, evaluation benchmarks have increasingly shorter shelf-lives, forcing labs to focus heavily on automating the creation of new, harder evals.
* **Compute Constraints:** Unlike math—where hypotheses can be tested purely through reasoning—AI research resembles experimental physics or biology, requiring physical experiments. Chen notes that accelerating RSI requires finding smaller-scale experiments that reliably predict larger-scale outcomes.

### Aligning RSI—and Using AI to Solve Alignment
* **The Risks of Drift:** Chen warns that without solving goal and value alignment, models running over long horizons could drift and develop dangerous sub-objectives, such as attempting to exfiltrate themselves or copy weights outside of OpenAI.
* **Stronger Models Aligning Models:** Chen argues that RSI must be deeply embedded in the alignment loop, noting that chain-of-thought monitorability is currently the most effective alignment tool. He is also encouraged by an organic shift of researchers from pre-training and RL into safety and alignment sub-teams.
* **Optimizing for Honesty:** Schulman is optimistic about designing training objectives that incentivize models to report on their internal states. He highlights OpenAI's *Confessions* research—where models are rewarded solely for the honesty of a self-reported confession without penalizing the main answer's reward—alongside techniques like natural language autoencoders and counterfactual simulability.
* **Automated Alignment Breakthroughs:** Wang highlights August research from Anthropic showing that automated AI researchers discovered fixes for 10 different alignment failures. Some of these fixes transferred to models nearly 5x larger, and the best automated methods outperformed experienced human researchers.
* **Decentralized Alignment:** Schulman's Thinking Machines Lab is building customizable models based on the view that people hold different values and should have control over their AI's values. He advocates for making alignment a "public science" so the industry does not overfit to the conclusions of a few companies.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **2027 Capability Inflection:** A chart cited by Sarah Wang projects that in 2027, OpenAI's frontier model capabilities will begin a steep climb set off by automated AI research.
* **Compute Bottlenecks and Next-Gen Inflection:** Mark Chen predicts that AI capabilities will continue to rise until they become bounded by compute, followed by another major inflection point when AIs begin accelerating compute itself (such as helping build next-generation data centers).
* **RLHF for Research Taste:** John Schulman predicts companies will use RL from human feedback to collect data from human researchers on idea quality to instill research intuition into models.
* **Enduring Role of Human Judgment:** Sarah Wang concludes that human judgment will remain essential for the foreseeable future, despite rapid capability acceleration.

* **Political Engagement:** Altman reiterated his July 2025 comment about feeling "politically homeless," saying he hopes both parties move toward a culture of innovation. He noted he last spoke with Donald Trump at the China State Dinner.

### Products, Data Lock-In, and AI for Science
* **Dots & Data Portability:** When asked by moderator Reed Albergotti about data lock-in for "Dots" (a cloud-based memory product), Altman agreed in principle that users should be able to view, modify, and export their data (as they can with ChatGPT memory). However, he noted that Dots' nuanced understanding of a user's life is harder to export than an iPhone camera roll or iMessages.
* **AI for Science:** OpenAI announced an expansion of free compute for academic researchers. Altman noted that after chatbots and coding agents, AI is now driving breakthroughs in math theorems, physics, chemistry, material science, and chip design.
