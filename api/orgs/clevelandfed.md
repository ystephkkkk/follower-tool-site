## Fed’s Hammack Prefers Small Hikes, Sees Risk of Bigger Moves

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qY-6Zv5GgKM
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-qY-6Zv5GgKM
- Speakers: Beth Hammack (President, Cleveland Fed); Michael McKee (Host, Bloomberg Radio)

---

**TL;DR:** Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack favors small, steady interest rate hikes to bring inflation back to the 2% target, warning that an entrenched "inflationary mindset" among businesses could force the FOMC to move in larger increments if it falls behind the curve.

### Monetary Policy & Rate Path
* **Open-Minded Approach:** Hammack enters every FOMC meeting—including the upcoming December 9th meeting—with an open mind, noting she does not yet know how she will vote.
* **Preference for Small Increments:** Her base case is to move in slow, steady, successive increments to restore policy restriction, which provides predictability for businesses and households.
  * She does not currently favor a 50 basis point move, though she noted the Fed can move in larger increments and to a higher destination if it falls behind the curve.
* **Neutral Rate & Dot Plot:** Hammack holds one of the higher long-run dots on the FOMC dot plot and believes that if her estimate is wrong, it is too low—arguing the economy can withstand more restriction than is currently in place.
* **Commitment to the 2% Target:** She firmly believes 2% remains a realistic and necessary goal for price stability, interpreting it as an average over time rather than 2.0% at every single data point.
  * Inflation has run well above 2% for the past six years, following a period from 2008 to 2020 where it ran consistently below 2%.
* **Fed Communication:** Hammack views transparent communication as critical so businesses and households can factor Fed views into their decisions, and she will continue sharing her personal perspective before passing her FOMC vote to her Chicago Fed colleague next year.

### Inflation Drivers & Business Pricing
* **Mandate Tension:** With unemployment in the low 4% range (near maximum employment), Hammack sees little tension in the Fed's dual mandate—the primary miss is on inflation, which is moving above 2% rather than toward it.
* **Broadening Sector Pressures:** After bringing inflation from above 7% to below 3% in 2024, recent data shows broadening inflationary pressure across health insurance, airfares, energy, tariff-related items, and the AI data center buildout.
* **Supply Chain & Pricing Shifts:** Businesses in aviation, transportation, and energy production are competing with data centers for parts.
  * A Northeast Ohio business told Hammack that while quality remains #1, priorities have flipped from price-then-availability to availability-then-price—a sign businesses are becoming more accepting of rising prices.
* **Margin Protection & "Inflationary Mindset":** Businesses that previously absorbed tariff and energy cost increases have exhausted their efficiency gains and are now passing costs on.
  * B2B price increases are being well-absorbed, whereas consumer-facing businesses face more difficulty passing on costs.
  * One retailer in her district is already building extra cushions into pricing to account for unforeseen future costs.

### Labor Market Conditions
* **Broadly in Balance, Not "Healthy":** Hammack describes the labor market as broadly in balance rather than "healthy," noting a lack of normal U.S. labor market dynamism.
* **Emerging Tightening:** While businesses have long struggled to find skilled labor, she is now hearing about shortages in unskilled labor as well.
  * A grocery chain in Western Pennsylvania reported running at a 5% vacancy rate compared to its typical 3% target.
  * If both the labor market and inflation point toward a need for tighter policy, it could alter the Fed's ultimate rate destination.

### AI, Manufacturing & Financial Conditions
* **AI Adoption & Labor:** In her district's Beige Book surveys, both very large firms and very small businesses (1 to 5 people) are adopting AI at large scale, with mid-sized firms experimenting.
  * Businesses report AI is acting as a complement to their labor force rather than a substitute.
* **Manufacturing Boom:** The Fourth District (headquartered in Cleveland), the second most manufacturing-intensive district in the country, is seeing growth driven by AI data centers, defense spending, onshoring, and nearshoring—which is simultaneously adding to inflationary pressure.
* **Bond Markets & Financial Conditions:** Hammack pushes back on the narrative that corporate AI debt issuance is driving interest rates up 100 basis points year-over-year, noting AI issuance is small relative to government debt.
  * Instead, she attributes higher rates to strong economic growth and expectations of Fed rate hikes.
  * Currently, financial conditions are not restraining investment for either AI or non-AI companies, reinforcing her view that policy needs to become more restrictive around the margins to temper demand.

### Notable Quotes
> "My preference has been to move in slow, steady increments, consistently, successively, at meetings to make sure we're getting that policy restriction back in the economy to help bring inflation back down to our 2% objective." — Beth Hammack

> "If this inflationary mindset sets in, it could mean that we're behind the curve and it could mean that we have to move more quickly, and move in bigger increments and to a higher destination than we might otherwise need to." — Beth Hammack

> "Clearly, where we are today, when I'm out in the district, I'm hearing people talk about inflation constantly, and so I think we are not meeting that price stability." — Beth Hammack
