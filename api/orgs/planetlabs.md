## Planet Labs CEO: Why AI Data Centers Will Move to Space

- Channel: Sourcery with Molly O'Shea

### TL;DR
Planet Labs co-founder and CEO Will Marshall explains how the company operates the world's only daily Earth-imaging satellite constellation, combining high-frequency hardware with AI-driven analytics to serve commercial, civil, and defense customers globally.

### Satellite Constellation & Technology Specs
* **Dove Satellites:** The company's mainstay scan fleet providing daily global coverage of Earth's landmass (over 200 million square kilometers) plus ocean territory in 8 spectral bands at 3-meter resolution.
* **Pelican Satellites:** High-resolution taskable satellites built for rapid revisit rates, allowing users anywhere on the planet to request an image, receive it, and have it analyzed within 30 minutes.
* **Tanager Satellites:** Hyperspectral satellites capturing 420 color bands per imaging point, enabling precise identification of tree and crop species, military camouflage detection, and methane emission tracking by identifying invisible gas leaks.
* **Manufacturing Capacity:** Planet Labs recently doubled its San Francisco manufacturing footprint to support rising global demand for both data and sovereign satellite sales.

### Geopolitical Defense & Sovereign Space Demand
* **Ukraine Support:** Planet Labs provided early warning data of Russian troop buildups before the full-scale invasion, giving Ukrainian forces a critical information advantage despite conventional disadvantages in troops, tanks, and missiles.
* **US Indo-Pacific Command:** Admiral Paparo and the US Navy utilize Planet data to monitor 13 million square kilometers of the South China Sea for illegal fishing, North Korean oil embargo violations, and early indicators of industrial supply chain buildup or threats toward Taiwan.
* **Sovereign Satellite Sales:** Driven by geopolitical fragmentation and US requests for allies to shoulder more defense burden, foreign governments are buying custom-built Planet satellites for national control; recent deals include:
  * **Sweden:** First satellite delivered and launched 4 months after contract signing.
  * **Germany:** Sponsored and funded fleet launched in just 2 months.
  * **Japan:** Purchasing Planet-built satellites.
* **Safety & Compliance:** Planet maintains a strict no-go list governed by US and EU embargoes, UN lists, and an internal employee review team ensuring tech alignment with company values.

### Commercial & Civil Government Applications
* **Agriculture & Finance:** Working with major agriculture companies like John Deere, Bayer, and BASF to improve crop yields, alongside hedge funds tracking global commodities and stock alpha.
* **Hyperscaler Intelligence:** Cloud providers purchase Planet data to monitor not only their own data center construction globally, but also competitor infrastructure builds in the US, China, and elsewhere.
* **Civil Government & NGOs:** State and local agencies use the data to respond to wildfires, Brazil monitors deforestation, and NGOs have utilized imagery to track environmental conditions associated with river blindness and protect river dolphin habitats.

### AI Integration & Future Outlook on Space Data
* **Democratizing Earth Data:** Planet's new AI app in beta allows users to query 4 million daily 47-megapixel images using natural language, shifting satellite data accessibility from elite geospatial experts to everyday farmers, journalists, and local governments.
* **Compute Infrastructure:** AI compute workloads run primarily on Google Cloud alongside AWS integrations.
* **Space-Based Data Centers:** Energy constraints on Earth are driving interest in space-based data centers; solar panels in sun-synchronous orbit generate five times more power per panel than ground-based installations.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Extraterrestrial Life:** Will Marshall predicts humanity will find life off Earth within 6 to 7 years (originally projected within 10 years, stated a few years ago) by detecting atmospheric chemical signatures (like planetary oxygen levels) using modern telescopes and the James Webb Space Telescope.

## The IPO Comeback: Why Tech Giants Are Finally Going Public | All-In Liquidity IPO Panel

**TL;DR**
The tech landscape is undergoing a critical shift as newly public pioneers Cerebras Systems and Planet Labs demonstrate how real-time silicon architecture and space-based data infrastructure are converging to build the next generation of "Large Earth Models."

---

### The Reality of Going Public and the IPO Pendulum

*   **The Overhead of IPOs:** Andrew Feldman (CEO, Cerebras Systems) noted that the process of going public involves massive administrative friction, including "garbage meetings" of up to 130 attendees and endless document revisions. However, the event serves as a massive cultural milestone and point of pride for long-term employees and their families.
*   **Corporate Legitimization:** Will Marshall (CEO, Planet Labs) emphasized that being a public company provides critical validation, reassuring enterprise and government clients that the business is financially stable and structurally permanent.
    *   **Revenue Mix:** Security and defense contracts now account for roughly 60% of Planet Labs’ business, driven by geopolitical demands for early threat detection.
*   **Post-IPO Value Accrual:** Panelists agreed that the vast majority of historical tech investment returns occur *after* a company goes public, rather than during its private stages. 
    *   **Market Shifting:** While mega-caps like SpaceX and OpenAI have kept their massive valuation jumps in the private markets, Brad Gerstner (CEO, Altimeter Capital) noted that the pendulum is swinging back. More companies are aiming to go public at earlier valuations ($1 billion to $5 billion) to let public markets participate in their hyper-growth phase.
*   **Lock-up Innovations:** Cerebras and their banking partners pioneered a "dribble lock-up" structure, allowing investors to systematically liquidate shares over a six-month window based on specific performance hurdles. SpaceX is reportedly preparing a similar mechanism.

### The Horizon for Space-Based Data Centers

*   **Launch Cost Milestones:** Satellite launch costs have dropped nearly 10x over the last decade to roughly $1,000 per kilogram. Once launch costs reach $200–$300 per kilogram—a milestone expected in 2 to 3 years via SpaceX's Starship—it will be cheaper to run data centers in space than on Earth.
*   **The Space Power Advantage:** Terrestrial data centers face massive energy and battery storage constraints. In space, servers placed in sun-synchronous "dawn-dusk" orbits can view the sun 24/7, capturing five times more solar energy per panel than on Earth without requiring complex battery backups.
*   **Hardware Deployments:** 
    *   Planet Labs has already launched Nvidia GPUs into space and is currently partnering with Google to test Google TPUs in orbit.
    *   Marshall projects that within 10 years, a majority of the world's heavy compute will be deployed in space.
*   **Technical Caveats:** Feldman cautioned that space-based compute faces immense hurdles, particularly regarding the high-speed clustering and inter-chip networking protocols that remain incredibly difficult to optimize even on Earth.

### Redefining AI Silicon Architecture

*   **The AI Data Bottleneck:** The primary limitation in modern computer architecture is not processing speed, but the latency and energy required to move data from memory to the compute core.
*   **Wafer-Scale Innovation:** To solve this bottleneck, Cerebras rejected standard GPU design architecture. Instead of building standard postage-stamp-sized chips, they engineered a dinner-plate-sized "wafer-scale" chip that places massive amounts of memory directly adjacent to the processing cores.
*   **Real-World Speed Advantage:** This specialized design allows Cerebras systems to run OpenAI workloads 15 to 18 times faster than standard Nvidia GPUs. 
*   **The Real-Time Mandate:** Feldman highlighted that just as the market for "slow web search" or "dial-up internet" went to zero, the market for slow AI will also collapse. Real-time inference speeds are non-negotiable for future consumer and enterprise adoption.

### The Convergence of AI and Earth Data

*   **Beyond Text-Based LLMs:** Marshall observed that current Large Language Models are "blind" to physical reality because they are trained almost exclusively on internet text. They lack direct observation of physical events, agricultural trends, and ecological disasters.
*   **Large Earth Models (LEMs):** By combining space-based imaging (Planet Labs captures a complete visual scan of the Earth daily) with space-based AI compute, developers can construct "Large Earth Models" to automate decision-making for agriculture, energy grid management, infrastructure permitting, and global security.
