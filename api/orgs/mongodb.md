## CJ Desai, MongoDB & Laura Modiano, OpenAI in Conversation | RAISE Summit 2026

- Speakers: CJ Desai (CEO, MongoDB); Laura Modiano (Head of Founder Experience, OpenAI)

---

**TL;DR** 
As AI-native applications scale, the data layer must evolve from a secondary consideration to a high-performance, flexible partner that scales in tandem with AI models to move beyond the "prototype treadmill" into production.

### The Data Layer as the "Unsung Hero" of AI
*   **The Scaling Paradox:** Founders often focus exclusively on application logic and model selection (e.g., OpenAI), treating the data layer as an implicit, secondary decision. 
    *   As applications scale, many "easy" initial database choices "choke" under performance demands, necessitating a data layer that can grow "humongously" alongside the model.
*   **Architectural Synchronization:** AI models and data layers must scale in tandem. CJ Desai emphasizes that MongoDB’s architecture is designed to support this "real-time applications data layer" for both AI natives like ElevenLabs and legacy giants like JPMorgan Chase.
*   **The 70/30 Rule:** To maintain human-centric relationships in a transactional industry, Desai follows a 70% listening and 30% talking rule to identify where founders are struggling with talent, infrastructure gaps, or sudden spikes in volume.

### The Evolution of the "Builder" and "Vibe-Coding"
*   **The Rise of the Polymath:** Tools like OpenAI’s Codex have fundamentally changed who can build, shifting the role from traditional developers to "polymaths" who use multifaceted skills to create applications via natural language.
*   **Vibe-Coding:** Desai cites *Immersive*, a platform that grew from $0 to $100M ARR in eight months by allowing builders to create CRM or logistics apps using natural language and "minimal moving parts."
*   **The Necessity of Schemaless Design:** Rigid infrastructure prevents AI agents from moving fast. 
    *   Because models (like the anticipated OpenAI 5.6) and application demands evolve rapidly, the underlying data layer must be schemaless to avoid becoming a bottleneck to innovation.

### A Strategic Framework for AI ROI
For enterprises to move from experimentation to production, Desai proposes a four-pillar framework to measure AI success:
*   **Innovate Faster:** Using tools like Codex to "shift-left" the product roadmap and accelerate new product creation.
*   **Sell More:** Utilizing AI to increase sales velocity and provide better intelligence to go-to-market teams.
*   **Serve Better:** Empowering customer success teams with proactive diagnostics and AI-driven support tools.
*   **Operational Efficiency:** Driving internal productivity to ensure the company remains "AI-forward" while managing the increased costs of AI infrastructure.

### Transitioning from Prototype to Production
*   **The Production High Ground:** In 2026, the focus has shifted from "prompting" to "production." Desai argues that Online Transaction Processing (OLTP) is the critical ground for agentic applications.
*   **Unified Data Layers:** Rather than managing fragmented systems, Desai advocates for integrating auto-embeddings, vector databases, and operational data into a single layer.
*   **Hybrid Multicloud & Sovereignty:** Especially in the European market, the ability to run AI applications anywhere—on-prem, in private environments, or across multiple public clouds—is essential for meeting data sovereignty and French/EU regulations.

## Data is Back: MongoDB, Databricks, Snowflake

- Channel: Sourcery with Molly O'Shea
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ts-f5ZBwDFw
- Published: 2026-07-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260720.html#video-Ts-f5ZBwDFw
- Speakers: CJ Desai (CEO, MongoDB); Molly O'Shea (Host, Sourcery)

---

**TL;DR**
Data has returned to the forefront of the AI cycle as the essential foundation for agentic applications, while hyperscaler capacity constraints and data sovereignty requirements are unexpectedly pushing large-scale AI workloads back to on-premise environments.

### The Resurgence of On-Premise and Sovereign AI
*   **Hyperscaler Capacity Limits:** Major cloud providers are reaching physical capacity limits. 
    *   CJ Desai cites a Fortune 100 customer in Texas that was denied additional cloud capacity despite being a "top 50" customer, forcing them to re-commission their own data centers for AI workloads.
*   **Data Sovereignty:** In regions like France, strict regulations and privacy concerns are driving a shift away from public clouds toward on-premise infrastructure.
*   **The Multi-Cloud Hedge:** Frontier labs and AI natives are increasingly adopting MongoDB because of its ability to run across multiple clouds (AWS, GCP, Azure), providing resiliency and avoiding vendor lock-in.

### MongoDB’s Role in the AI Stack
*   **Operational vs. Analytical Data:** Desai clarifies the market distinction between MongoDB and competitors like Snowflake or Databricks.
    *   **Real-time OLTP:** MongoDB handles "online transaction processing" (real-time interactions, credit card transactions, and agent memory).
    *   **Analytical OLAP:** Snowflake and Databricks focus on offline business analysis and "asking questions" of historical data.
*   **The Memory Layer for Agents:** MongoDB is being used as the "memory" for agentic workflows.
    *   **Scale Example:** ElevenLabs currently runs over 50 million agents on MongoDB.
    *   **Physical AI:** Robotics companies are using the database to store real-time sensory and action data generated by robots in the field.
*   **Frontier Lab Adoption:** Major AI labs use MongoDB for inference, voice products, and video/image creation due to its document-based architecture and ability to handle unstructured data.

### The Complexity of Enterprise Agentic Architecture
*   **Exploding Architectural Complexity:** A major bank's "agentic architecture" recently showed 55+ distinct components—including various LLMs, frameworks, and guardrails—compared to half that number just a year ago.
*   **Deterministic Outcomes:** In regulated industries like insurance, agents must provide deterministic results. Companies are building complex "human-in-the-loop" systems to audit agent decisions for bias and regulatory compliance.
*   **Shift in Labor Needs:** Modern AI companies refuse to hire traditional Database Administrators (DBAs). They demand "machine-based administration" and auto-scaling capabilities so engineers can focus on models rather than infrastructure.

### CEO Strategy and the Pace of Innovation
*   **Customer-Obsessed Cadence:** Desai maintains a pace of 10–12 individual customer meetings per week to ensure the product roadmap pivots in real-time based on scaling pain points.
*   **Software Writing Software:** The speed of innovation is accelerating because AI is now writing code, significantly lowering the incremental cost of experimentation and product development.
*   **Operational Agility:** Desai highlights that "speed is paramount," noting that even the most established companies are pivoting their AI strategies every 8–12 weeks as new models and frameworks emerge.
