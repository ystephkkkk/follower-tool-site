## How to close gender gaps in tech - and the one skill AI can't learn

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cMk8HczjOm0
- Published: 2026-02-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260224.html#video-cMk8HczjOm0
- Speakers: Ayumi Moore Aoki (Founder, Women in Tech Global)

**TL;DR**
While women’s representation in the tech workforce has grown slightly to 28%, a "leaky pipeline" at every career stage remains a barrier; achieving true parity requires shifting focus from mere mentorship to direct funding and leveraging "uniquely human" skills like empathy in the AI era.

### The "Leaky Pipeline": Identifying Gaps by Stage and Region
Ayumi Moore Aoki highlights that progress is slow, with the global gender gap estimated to take 123 years to close at the current pace. The "leaks" in the talent pipeline vary significantly by life stage and geography.

*   **Education Bottlenecks:**
    *   **Critical Age:** Research identifies ages 14–15 as the primary period when girls drop out of STEM interests.
    *   **Solution:** Effective interventions must include "inspirational" role models rather than just highlighting underrepresentation, alongside training for parents and teachers to combat unconscious bias.
*   **The Conversion Paradox:**
    *   **Middle East & North Africa:** These regions produce high numbers of female STEM graduates (often reaching gender parity) but struggle to convert them into the workforce. 
    *   **Europe & North America:** These regions face the opposite problem, with lower graduate numbers (approx. 22% in France) but higher successful workforce entry.
*   **Late-Career Stagnation:**
    *   **Board Representation:** Women occupy only about 10% of leadership and board positions. Aoki argues that culture flows from the top down; without women in leadership, organizations struggle to "walk the talk" on diversity.

### Leadership and Soft Skills in the Age of AI
As generative AI automates technical tasks, LinkedIn data suggests that "soft skills" will become the primary differentiator for human leaders.

*   **The "Empathy Advantage":** 
    *   **Human-Centric Skills:** Empathy and discernment are the two skills AI cannot learn. Aoki notes that women often excel here due to socialized nurturing traits, making them well-suited to oversee ethical AI implementation.
    *   **AI as Prediction, Not Wisdom:** Aoki reminds leaders that AI is merely a "formula of prediction." Humans must remain the final decision-makers to ensure results align with culture and values.
*   **Managing AI Systems:**
    *   **Verification is Vital:** Leaders must move beyond "prompting" to "verifying." Aoki’s research found that LLMs frequently hallucinate academic references; leaders must "check and recheck" data to avoid making decisions based on misinformation.
    *   **LLM Bias:** Research into Large Language Models shows higher gender bias in gendered languages (Latin, Russian, Arabic) compared to neutral English.

### Institutional Change: The Osaka Protocol and Funding
Bridging the gap requires moving beyond "awareness" toward concrete commitments and financial equity.

*   **The Funding Gap:**
    *   **Critical Statistic:** In 2024, only 2.6% of global venture capital funding went to all-women teams.
    *   **The Funding Mandate:** Aoki emphasizes that while mentoring and accelerators are helpful, they are no substitute for capital. "They need funding," she states flatly.
*   **The Osaka Protocol:** 
    *   **Scale of Ambition:** A multilateral framework designed to impact 100 million women by 2030.
    *   **Concrete Pledges:** Unlike vague promises, the protocol requires members to commit to specific actions, such as training a defined number of women in digital skills.
*   **Digital Sovereignty:**
    *   **Defining the Future:** Aoki predicts "digital sovereignty"—the ownership and usage rights of a nation’s data—will be the defining theme of the next 12 months, shifting the power balance between the global North and South.

### Personal Leadership Lessons
Aoki’s background—from growing up in apartheid South Africa to founding a global NGO—shapes her approach to advocacy.

*   **Resilience via Entrepreneurship:** 
    *   **Founding Hardships:** When founding Women in Tech Global, Aoki received zero initial sponsorships and had to self-fund by selling t-shirts with her children. 
    *   **Advice to Self:** "Use my voice much earlier." Aoki regrets the times she stayed silent and encourages leaders to take the opportunity to speak when they have it.
*   **Managing Complexity:**
    *   **Who Not How:** Aoki recommends the book *Who Not How*, advising leaders to stop trying to learn "how" to do everything and instead find the "who"—the right people to facilitate progress.
    *   **Data Integrity:** Aoki emphasizes being "data-oriented" and never trusting a secondary source. She advises leaders to find the original "dirty" data and interpret it themselves.
