### Scaling Health Intelligence via General-Purpose Frontier Models
* **Specialized Training in General Models:** OpenAI avoids using smaller, specialized healthcare models because they fall behind frontier capabilities too quickly. Instead, specialized healthcare data is added to every stage of training for ChatGPT models, blending general reasoning with clinical expertise.

* **Administrative Cost Reductions:** Early enterprise adopters like Advent Health have reported roughly an 80% reduction in administrative workflow costs using AI across their workspaces.
* **Clinician Safety Nets:** Experiments with Panda Health deployed clinician copilots that proactively flagged medication interactions and helped clinicians avoid issues like antibiotic overuse.

* **Proactive Personal Assistants:** OpenAI is developing proactive health agents designed to act as a health "quarterback"—managing schedules, integrating health records securely, and prompting users during chronic care journeys (e.g., flagging a rising A1C in a diabetic patient for medication review).
* **Bridging Information and Care:** While ChatGPT provides abundant health information and helps patients navigate care journeys (such as interpreting lab reports or preparing for doctor visits), OpenAI views AI as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, traditional medical care.
* **Democratizing Second Opinions:** Access to fast, cheap AI-driven health intelligence acts as a safety net for patients to double-check treatment plans, medication interactions, and diagnoses.

* **Rigorous Mental Health Benchmarks:** Partnering with licensed mental health experts worldwide, OpenAI developed mental health benchmarks to measure both safety floors and positive model behaviors in complex, tricky scenarios.
* **Safety Priority:** When user preferences conflict with clinical safety guidelines, the model is trained to prioritize safety and direct users to seek professional care.

### TL;DR
Jean-Denis Greze shut down his profitable, near-$1M-revenue AI tax startup because it lacked venture scale and faced an insurmountable ticking clock from slow-frequency sales cycles and fast-moving incumbents. He pivoted to Town, an email-and-calendar AI assistant, achieving rapid product-market fit by prioritizing consumer-grade usability over heavy enterprise sales and positioning the product as a neutral token-allocation engine.

### The Tax Startup Experiment & Pivot
- **Initial Traction:** The SMB tax product made nearly $1M in its first year and was on track for $2.5M to $3.M in year two (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Go-to-Market Failure:** Despite technical success, the business lacked venture scale because tax prep is sold only once a year, making it impossible to build a high-frequency DAU (daily active user) tool or rely on word-of-mouth (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Incumbent Threat:** Incumbents with massive distribution advantages could automate tax prep for existing customers faster than a startup could capture market share (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **The Pivot Window:** The annual nature of tax created a strict, one-month window between October 15th and November to make the pivot decision before the door closed for the cycle (Jean-Denis Greze).

### Building Town: Product Strategy & Market Fit
- **Core Insight:** Recognized that no great AI agent existed to handle common knowledge worker workflows like meetings, meeting prep, daily scheduling, and email management out of the inbox and calendar (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Explosive Growth:** Developed from December to April with product usage compounding daily by 1% to 1.5%, driven by organic user pull (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **PLG Over Top-Down Enterprise:** Following lessons from Dropbox, Town chose a product-led growth (PLG) and consumer-grade UX approach, targeting SMBs and mid-market firms first, betting that CEOs and CISOs will demand the same consumer ease internally (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Surprising Use Cases:** Finding unexpected global adoption (such as strong traction in Japan) and quirky vertical workflows (such as an island surfboard rental business using Town to inspect damage and decide on deposit refunds) driven by local tinkerers (Jean-Denis Greze).

### Infrastructure vs. Application: Competing With Suppliers
- **The Supplier Dilemma:** Town competes in the application layer while buying tokens from foundational labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, creating a risky dynamic of competing with suppliers (Alex Rampell, Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Neutrality and Model Routing:** Town differentiates itself by acting as a neutral app that uses whatever supplier model is best for the user at the moment, protecting enterprises from vendor lock-in or future price hikes (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Data and Context Ownership:** Customers truly own the context and memory assets created on Town; switching costs are low by design, allowing users to move to another solution within a day if needed (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Privacy and Training Stance:** Town does not train on customer data, assuring businesses that their operational secrets will not end up in model weights (Jean-Denis Greze).

### The Future of AI Workflows & The Death of Supply Aggregation
- **Jevons Paradox of Assistants:** Just as more efficient steam engines increased coal demand, cheaper silicon intelligence enables massive expansion of assistant tasks—such as generating meeting prep notes for every single calendar event—that were previously too expensive with human EAs or expensive tokens (Alex Rampell, Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Token Allocation Machines:** Town functions as a token allocation machine, offering options to humans to apply cheap tokens to long-tail tasks (such as a restaurant owner analyzing thousands of wholesale item prices monthly to save hundreds of dollars) (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Humans as Slow Routers:** The future features far fewer humans in the loop; Town relies on AI to mediate internal company queries and security/privacy boundaries instantly rather than relying on humans to read and route messages in Slack (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Obsolete Supply Aggregation:** Supply aggregation (such as Amazon's product/ad model) is becoming far less valuable because human attention and shopping searches can now be automated away by agents (Jean-Denis Greze).

## Why AI Can Write Code But Can’t Keep It Running (Yet)

---

### TL;DR
AI can generate production code and boost engineering productivity by a factor of 10 to 20, but running and operating real-world distributed systems remains fundamentally harder due to massive state spaces, unpredictable workloads, and the gap between digital models and physical reality.

### Engineering Leverage and AI Workflows
- **Productivity Multiplier:** 
  - Jad Naous shares that his 10-person team builds complex distributed systems with massive efficiency gains.
  - A data lake auto-promotion/shredding project that once took a single engineer six months now takes roughly 10 days.
- **The Modern AI Coding Stack:**
  - Jad runs Windows with Windows Subsystem for Linux (WSL) running the Linux kernel inside a hypervisor, utilizing command-line tools without an X/UI interface.
  - Relies heavily on Claude Code powered by Opus 5.5.
  - Utilizes persistent command-line sub-agents to maintain session context across 3 or 4 parallel window streams rather than burning tokens on isolated stateless queries.
- **Developer Capability Gap:**
  - AI does not turn weak engineers into strong ones; instead, it amplifies the output of truly great engineers by roughly 10x because superior architectural thinking and the ability to break down complex tasks are still required.

### The Economics of Observability and Infrastructure
- **The Observability Squeeze:**
  - Companies historically spent 5 to 20 percent of their infrastructure budget on observability as a proxy for purchasing reliability.
  - As architectures shifted to tightly integrated microservices, telemetry data volume exploded, causing observability costs to grow much faster than actual output functions while marginal reliability gains flattened out.
- **Grepr's Product Approach:**
  - Sits directly in the data stream to extract signal upstream, allowing companies to reduce observability costs by 90% while simultaneously channeling clean data into downstream AI reliability layers.
- **Commercial Realities:**
  - When pitching enterprises, customers immediately fixate on cutting massive existing line-item costs (like Datadog bills) rather than buying futuristic AI operations agents.

### Reasoning About Production Systems and Operations
- **The State Space Problem:**
  - Martin Casado notes that every time a bit is added to a program, its operational state space increases by a factor of 2, quickly exceeding the number of atoms in the universe. Historically, systems engineers solved this via heavy over-provisioning rather than exact prediction.
- **Anomaly Detection vs. Generative Prediction:**
  - Grepr does not rely on lagging indicators or complex reverse-engineering of root causes after a metric spikes.
  - Instead, a deterministic predictive model scans petabytes of daily logs to detect anomalies, new patterns, or early warning messages (such as connection or memory exhaustion).
- **AI-Driven Remediation:**
  - When an anomaly or configuration change is flagged, the AI forms hypotheses, traces the code where the error originated, and verifies causal chains to help engineers troubleshoot faster.
- **Query Optimization Benchmark:**
  - When generating SQL-equivalent pipeline code, AI models initially produce substandard code, but when grounded in strict performance benchmarks and provided with user query datasets, they successfully reason through optimizations.

### Digital Worlds vs. The Physical Universe
- **Constrained vs. Unconstrained Domains:**
  - Martin Casado argues that AI takes off like a wildfire in constrained domains where the solution space distribution matches the training set distribution (e.g., code syntax, image generation).
  - Real-world domains where systems interact with the natural universe face severe economic and operational friction—analogous to self-driving cars requiring 20 years and $170 billion to achieve worse unit economics than an Uber.
- **Syntax vs. Semantics:**
  - Code generation succeeds because it operates within a purely digital abstract simulation; true operations struggle because they must account for heavy-tailed exceptions, human organizational factors, and external physical randomness.

#### TL;DR

MakerMaker.AI co-founders are empirically observing early glimmers of systems improving performance while simultaneously reducing energy and resource costs.

### Defining True Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)
* **Distinction from Autocatalysis:** **Owen He** notes that many pitches labeled as RSI are actually autocatalysis—humans using AI tools to develop better AI. True RSI swaps the optimizer with the optimized, creating a closed loop where the model's cognitive improvement is reinvested directly back into itself.
* **Control Theory & Closed Loops:** **Dhaval Adjodah** explains that autocatalytic systems lack a closed loop and require continuous human intervention. In contrast, a closed-loop RSI system scales marginal gains or maintains constant improvement over time because capability correlates directly with the ability to improve.
* **The Multi-Agent Foundation:** **Owen He** clarifies that their RSI approach relies on a multi-agent system rather than a single digital agent. Human researchers and engineers act as a special subset of agents cohabitating within this broader recursively improving hybrid system.

### The Mathematical Framework and Economics
* **The Rate of Improvement Equation:** **Owen He** defines capability progress as a differential equation driven by a numerator (performance) and a denominator (cost/capital/difficulty). Traditional industries plateau because their numerator is fixed, whereas RSI can tie the numerator directly to capability to achieve constant improvement or exponential takeoff.
* **Company Culture & The "Bleederboard":** **MakerMaker.AI** tracks the financial cost of experiments using an internal tool called the "bleederboard" to ensure capability gains are not artificially driven solely by pouring in massive amounts of capital.
* **Nano GPT Benchmark Example:** **Owen He** highlights that nano GPT benchmarks demonstrate how algorithmic, data, and kernel improvements have allowed developers to retrain GPT-2 on a single GPU within hours for a cost of just a few hundred dollars, down from weeks of training on multiple GPUs previously.

### Empirical Challenges and Optimization Scope
* **The Danger of Theoretical Complexity:** **Dhaval Adjodah** notes that many academic RSI papers rely on complicated Rube Goldberg-style setups inspired by human behavior that fail when tested at scale. Simpler systems that close the loop are what actually scale.
* **Variance and Reward Hacking:** **MakerMaker.AI** employs an engineer nicknamed the "viceroy of variance" to rigorously test, stress-test, and dismantle internal results. **Dhaval Adjodah** stresses that no replacement exists for "quality time"—running hundreds of experiments to detect stochastic noise, hardware-level differences (such as minor physics variations between B200 GPUs), and multi-level reward hacking.
* **Stack-Wide Optimization:** **Owen He** states that MakerMaker.AI optimizes across the entire AI stack, including prompts, harnesses, and weight updates. Optimizing isolated components (like harnesses or weights alone) fails because these components heavily impact one another.

### Application Domain: The Inference Problem
* **Focus on Inference:** **MakerMaker.AI** targets the inference problem because it sits as close to AI itself as possible, creating massive economic value by helping the entire industry serve open-weight models (like Kimi K3) as efficiently as possible.
* **Self-Directed Optimization:** **Owen He** explains that their multi-agent system autonomously chooses which LLMs to deploy and which parts of the stack to optimize, with even the master prompt undergoing continuous recursive improvement by the agents themselves.
* **Amdahl’s Law and Physical Bottlenecks:** **Dhaval Adjodah** points out that intelligence is a multi-dimensional vector rather than a single scalar. Fields requiring physical manipulation of atoms (such as wet-lab drug discovery and clinical trials) will face severe bottlenecks compared to fast, cheap evaluation domains like coding and mathematics.
