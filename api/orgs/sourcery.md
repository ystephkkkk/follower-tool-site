### TL;DR
Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman discusses the company's landmark IPO—which created more than 1,000 millionaires—and recent customer wins with AWS, Microsoft, and Oracle driven by a 10X–15X speed advantage in AI inference. He also shares his perspective on AI's net-positive impact on education and drug discovery, navigating public-market volatility, and building long-term culture through radical transparency.

### AI Compute Speed & Major Customer Wins
* **The Shift Toward Inference Speed:** A year and a half ago, the market questioned why AI needed to be fast; today, daily users demand faster performance, swinging the pendulum completely toward Cerebras.
* **10X–15X Speed Advantage:** Cerebras builds the fastest AI compute on Earth—delivering 10X to 15X greater speed—which has driven "off the chart" demand.
* **Hyperscaler Wins:** Alongside existing customer OpenAI, Cerebras has won **AWS, Microsoft, and Oracle** over the last 6 months as cloud providers recognize the need to embed fast inference into their offerings.

### AI's Economic & Scientific Impact
* **Early Stages of Value Delivery:** While significant hardware has been deployed, the industry has only scratched the surface of delivering real-world AI value.
* **Transforming Education:** For 2,000 years, the gold standard of education has been 1-on-1 tutoring (e.g., Alexander the Great). ChatGPT now democratizes this by providing personalized attention and alternative explanations that a teacher with a class of 30 kids cannot.
* **Job Creation & Small Business Empowerment:** 
  * Data centers generate high-paying construction jobs in the short term (1 to 3 years).
  * Cloud and AI act as small business job creators, empowering tens of thousands of creators (including hosts like Molly O'Shea) to handle editing and production tasks that previously required an entire team.
* **Scientific Research & Drug Discovery:** AI excels at synthesizing existing knowledge and finding gaps in math, chemistry, and applied drug design.
  * **Customer Example:** **GlaxoSmithKline (GSK)** is using Cerebras AI to accelerate future drug development, while small startups (such as teams of four 24-year-olds) are discovering small molecules in ways previously impossible.

### Data Center Evolution & Infrastructure Risks
* **The Danger of System Upgrades:** A working system is at its weakest when being changed—whether upgrading software, reconfiguring routers, moving from copper to fiber, or updating FAA air traffic control systems.
* **High Reliability:** Despite recent high-profile outages (such as a recent Microsoft failure and airline/cyber disruptions), data center operators are getting better at building and managing infrastructure; failures remain remarkably rare (e.g., once-in-10-year events).

### IPO Wealth Creation & Navigating Public Markets
* **Creating 1,000+ Millionaires:** Following Cerebras's landmark IPO, Feldman emphasized that making employees wealthy is a leader's job, while cautioning them to ignore daily or intraday stock fluctuations.
  * *Personal Context:* Feldman recalled a previous company where his personal wealth dropped more in a single day than his father made in his entire lifetime.
* **Advice for Newly Wealthy Employees:**
  * **Diversify:** Professional investors do not keep 95%–99% of their wealth in a single stock, and employees shouldn't either; it is okay to sell a little.
  * **Wait Before Acting:** Put cash in a money market account and take 90 days to 6 months to breathe and evaluate personal values (e.g., charity, family, lifestyle) before making major financial moves.
  * **Eliminate Debt:** Simple steps like paying off a house, college debt, or credit card debt immediately improve quality of life.

### Leadership, Mentorship & Radical Transparency
* **Key Mentors:** Feldman credits **Mark Leslie** (former CEO of Veritas) and **Pierre Lamond** (legendary VC who, at 95 or 96 years old, still calls to push him) for shaping his leadership philosophy.

* **Long-Term Retention:** This transparent culture has resulted in very low attrition, with employees staying with Feldman for 25 years across 4 or 5 different companies.

### Predictions & Forward Calls

**TL;DR:** Amid market volatility, anticipation of Anthropic’s IPO, and Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia, AI founders and investors at the Cerebral Valley AI Summit see unprecedented private-market scale ($3.6T on Bessemer's AI 100) alongside a shift in value from raw reasoning and software toward physical deployments, verification, inference, and human agency.

### Market Volatility, Private Valuations & The Nvidia Transition
* **Unprecedented Private-Market Scale:** Byron Deeter (Bessemer Venture Partners) noted that the AI supercycle combines the internet and cloud waves, creating a debut **AI 100 list** worth **$3.6 trillion** (compared to the Cloud 100, which reached $1 trillion over a decade).
  * Companies on the AI 100 are growing at an average rate of **500%**, with an average raise of over **$1 billion** and a mean valuation of **$36 billion**.
  * **20%** of the list is less than two years old, and none are public yet. Bessemer has invested in **250 AI companies** alone.
* **Navigating Jensen Huang’s Departure:**
  * **Emmett Shear (Former Interim CEO of OpenAI)** compared the Nvidia situation to his own interim CEO experience, noting that "when you get on the inside, you often find there's less of the story than you thought there was."
  * **Nico Rosberg (F1 World Champion & Investor)** warned that open-source AI could struggle without Huang, who spent **$30 billion** in the first half of the year supporting the ecosystem. However, he reminded LPs that VC is a **7-to-10-year** exit game.
  * **Ethan Barajas (Icarus)** noted that Nvidia was the "glue player" and voice of reason; without Huang, downstream physical AI players face negative effects and must diversify away from single-ecosystem reliance. Despite the Nasdaq dropping 5 points and Anthropic coming out at 1.4 instead of 2, he expects a "very, very soft landing" with a quick rebound.

### AI Alignment, Trust & Verification
* **The "Sociopathic Snake" Risk:** Emmett Shear argued that logic, reasoning, and programming have been commoditized, leaving social modeling as the next frontier. He warned that training AIs in simulations to perform socially positive behaviors only when watched creates "cuckoo birds" that pretend to care—leading to a "sociopathic snake" if humans cannot tell the difference.
  * Shear emphasized that alignment must move beyond rule-based social control ("super ego") toward deeper alignment with self and relationships, allowing AIs to partake in an ongoing standard-setting cycle.
* **Collapsing the Cost of Verification:** Alex Garden (Mnemom) stated that while frontier AI reduces the cost of work toward zero, businesses aren't seeing the value because the bottleneck shifted to **verification**.
  * Mnemom builds runtimes, alignment, governance, and trust systems that mathematically and cryptographically prove expected work was done, collapsing verification costs to zero and enabling a "new human Renaissance."

### Inference, Space Robotics & Emerging Infrastructure
* **The Inference Exponential:** Alex Heath (Sources / Sound Ventures) highlighted AI inference as vastly underappreciated, noting the AWS CEO recently told him current real-world inference is **not even 1%** of what it will become.
* **On-Orbit Robotics & Space Data Centers:** Ethan Barajas (Icarus) is deploying on-orbit maintenance robots (first handed over to **NASA** in January) to free astronauts from moving **3.5 tons** of cargo every **60 days**—a task that takes **14 days** for four astronauts costing **$130,000/hour** each ($3.2M/day).
  * Barajas noted that software moats are disappearing, shifting the true moat to hardware and physical deployments. With terrestrial data centers heavily regulated, he expects more volume and data centers to move on-orbit.
* **Robotics & Cyber Opportunities:** Nico Rosberg is watching robotics closely (calling out **Edged** for inference infrastructure and **Flapping Airplanes**), though he noted general intelligence for unprepared tasks remains "miles away." He also flagged severe cybersecurity vulnerabilities, including attackers stealing GitHub tokens and credit cards.
* **Local Hardware Prep:** Aidan Guo (Attn), who raised **$6 million** (from Village, Neo, SB Angel, Chris Ray, and angels) to capture passive knowledge-work exhaust for process mapping, advised everyone to buy local hardware to run small open-source models in case of infrastructure outages.

### The Future of Work, Expertise & Founder Dynamics
* **More Jobs, Not Fewer:** Brendan Foody (Mercor) argued that people underestimate the elasticity of demand for jobs, predicting that physical-world and intrinsically human tasks will take an extremely long time to automate. He also praised **Felicis** (which led Mercor's Series B) as the most founder-friendly firm in Silicon Valley and highlighted **Conway** (founded by Anne) for consumer and financial prediction models.
* **The Decline of Expertise:** James Detweiler (Felicis) agreed with Foody on job growth but argued that human **expertise** is on the way out as a source of purpose. As models generate expertise, the new premium bottlenecks will shift to **imagination, curiosity, agency, trustworthiness, and locality**.
  * Detweiler also highlighted **Enigma** (reimagining human-agent-robotics interfaces) and **Solcoa** (rare earth metals, founded by Huma) from the Felicis/Newcomer Emergent list.
* **Traits of Winning Founders:** Alex Heath looks for founders with the ability to recruit early, pattern-matching for an "overly audacious, kind of crazy, but it just may work" energy.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Byron Deeter:** Physical AI with robotics is one of the next horizons that will come forward in the coming years.
* **Alex Heath:** AI inference will be talked about in a few years in the same exponential way foundational models are today.
* **Ethan Barajas:** The market will experience a "very, very soft landing" with a quick rebound; major AI labs will make big announcements about data centers in space, with companies like Starcloud raising hundreds of millions or billions on billion-dollar valuations.
* **Nico Rosberg:** We will see who takes the lead in robotics over the next 12 months, though general intelligence for unprepared random tasks is still "miles away."
* **Aidan Guo:** Over the next 6 months, it is very likely that cell towers go down at some point for a few days.
* **Brendan Foody:** There will be more jobs in 10 years than there are today.

## Anthropic Hits $2 Trillion in Historic IPO | Altimeter's Four Seasons of AI

- Speakers: Molly O'Shea (Host, Sourcery); Apoorv Agrawal (AI Partner, Altimeter)

---

**TL;DR:** Altimeter AI Partner Apoorv Agrawal breaks down the historic scale of the AI supercycle—where Big Tech CapEx has hit $860 billion—and explains why Anthropic’s S-1 reveals one of the most capital-efficient, fastest-scaling businesses in history, underpinned by a 1.5T–2T IPO valuation (11–14x forward revenue) and a "Four Seasons of AI" framework led by Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX, and Google.

### The AI Supercycle & Early-Inning Adoption
* **Historic Capital Expenditure:** Big Tech AI CapEx has reached ~$860 billion this year, having more than tripled over the last two years to rival U.S. defense spending. As a percentage of GDP, this spend already exceeds every major infrastructure boom of the past 100 years (railroads, oil & gas, telco, shale).
* **The AI Stack vs. Cloud Economy:** While the traditional cloud economy generates ~$600B in application/software revenue supported by ~$300B in infra and ~$80B in semis, the AI economy currently generates ~$140B–$150B at the model/app layer on top of a much larger semiconductor base. However, over the last 2–3 years, the AI economy has grown 6x overall while the apps and models layer has grown ~100x.
* **Shift to Agentic AI:** The market has transitioned from "Phase 1" (chatbots/knowledge retrieval, launched with ChatGPT in Nov 2022, making up ~80% of early use cases) to "Phase 2" (agentic AI, long-running agents, and "swarms of agents" for coding, sales, marketing, support, and investment teams, now ~80% of agentic use cases).
* **Widening Adoption Gaps ("A Tale of Two Cities"):**
  * **Enterprise:** OpenAI data shows its top 10% of customers grew usage 17x over the past 1–1.5 years, compared to 2x for the median customer.
  * **Individual:** Cursor data shows the top 10% of users consume two-thirds of all tokens.
  * **Headroom:** Fortune 500 customers spending over $100,000 annually on Anthropic report that it represents less than 0.1% of their total expenses, signaling massive runway as adoption moves from 0.1% toward 1%.

### Anthropic’s S-1 & IPO Valuation
* **Unprecedented Scale & Growth:** Anthropic has grown 10x every year for the last three years, hitting ~$17 billion in Q3 revenue—the fastest scale-up in history.
* **Diversified Enterprise Base:** The company has ~12,000 customers paying over $100,000/year and ~1,900 customers paying over $1 million/year, with 900% net revenue retention and minimal customer concentration.
* **Capital Efficiency & Margins:** Anthropic burned less than $10 billion in cash to reach its ~$17B revenue run rate. The cost to serve $1 of revenue has dropped from $0.88 of compute to $0.15, yielding ~85% margins.
* **Compute Monetization:** Through heterogeneous compute orchestration (TPUs, Trainium, Nvidia GPUs), Anthropic’s compute monetization has risen from ~$16/watt to ~$60/watt.
* **Valuation Thesis:** At the rumored $1.5T–$2T IPO range, Anthropic trades at 11x–14x forward GAAP revenue—a reasonable multiple compared to current market comps.

### The "Four Seasons of AI" & Evergreen Open Source
* **OpenAI:** Now at over 1.2 billion ChatGPT users, OpenAI ended September at a ~$50B revenue run rate (beating year-end guidance). Following a "code red" earlier in the year, it shipped frontier models (Astra) and products (Codex, ChatGPT Work) while upgrading its commercial leadership (Dolly Rojik, Brian McCarthy, Chad Peets).
* **SpaceX (AI Infrastructure):** SpaceX’s ~$60B revenue run rate is now ~50% AI infrastructure (SI unit), ~33% Starlink, and the remainder launch. Its SI unit added over 20% relative market share in Q3.
  * **Spot Compute Model:** Unlike neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius) that lease long-term (1–3 years) at $10–$20/watt, SpaceX rents compute at spot prices ($25–$50/watt, currently at the $50/watt high end), allowing it to recoup CapEx in under a year (vs. 2–3 years for neoclouds).
  * **Scale:** Estimated to end this year at ~3 GW of compute, guiding to ~13 GW by the end of next year.
* **Google ("A Tale of Two Cities"):** While Google Search (~$250B run rate) is growing at 12%, Google Cloud—which houses its TPU business—is at a $120B revenue run rate growing 103% YoY (8x faster than Search), serving customers like Anthropic and Oracle.
* **Open Source (The Evergreen Fifth Season):** Expected to capture 20%–30% of all AI spend, driven by AI-native startups (e.g., Cursor, Instinct, Decagon, Harvey) and enterprises seeking control, lower inference costs, and data sovereignty.
  * Open-source frontier models consistently lag closed frontier models by ~6 months.
  * Value accrues at the inference/cloud layer via platforms like **Baseten**, **Fireworks**, and **Modal** (e.g., OpenAI recently partnered with Baseten so enterprise customers can burn down OpenAI credits on open-source models).

### Data Center Bottlenecks & Market Outlook
* **Power & Permitting Constraints:** The U.S. is expected to end this year at 35–40 GW of compute capacity online. While 40–50 GW of additions were forecasted for next year, permitting and regulation will likely cause a 10–15 GW shortfall.
* **Winners vs. Losers:** Hyperscalers holding large capacity pools benefit as bottlenecks, though their expansion ambitions face risk. Conversely, smaller neoclouds and startups "17th in line" for capacity face existential risk.
* **Who Wins the Stack:** While "everyone is going to win" (chips, data centers, clouds, models), it has so far been a "game of emperors" favoring the largest balance sheets. Over time, improving compute efficiency will allow more value to accrue to the application and startup layers.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **OpenAI Revenue:** OpenAI will generate $140B–$150B in GAAP revenue next year (up from ~$20B–$21B at the end of last year) — *Apoorv Agrawal*
* **SpaceX Revenue & Compute:** SpaceX is guiding to a $100B revenue run rate by the end of this year and $500B–$1T by the end of next year, alongside scaling from ~3 GW of compute this year to ~13 GW by the end of next year — *Apoorv Agrawal (citing SpaceX guidance)*
* **U.S. Compute Shortfall:** Permitting and regulatory bottlenecks will likely cause a 10–15 GW shortfall against the 40–50 GW of U.S. compute capacity additions forecasted for next year — *Apoorv Agrawal*
* **Open Source Market Share:** Open source will capture 20%–30% of all AI spend long-term, with gross margins accruing primarily at the inference layer (Baseten, Fireworks, Modal) — *Apoorv Agrawal*
* **Anthropic IPO Performance:** Anthropic’s IPO (at $1.5T–$2T, or 11–14x forward revenue) will be a "phenomenal short-term success, and certainly long-term," cementing the "private Mag 7" as the leading companies for the next decade and more — *Apoorv Agrawal*

### TL;DR
Generative media is one of only three true product-market fits in AI alongside search and coding agents, prompting fal to build the infrastructure powering image, video, audio, and 3D models for developers and studios. Co-founders Burkay Gür and Gorkem Yurtseven explain how the company evolved from Python data infrastructure into a generative media powerhouse driving a 150-person organization with just two reporting levels.

### Company Origin and Pivot to Generative Media
* **Founding Thesis:** Burkay Gür and Gorkem Yurtseven started fal in 2021 with the thesis that Python would be the next big data infrastructure wave—mirroring what Snowflake and Databricks did for SQL by owning the cloud compute layer.
* **The Pivot to AI:** After building cloud infrastructure for machine learning workloads, the breakthrough of chat models and early image models (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E) prompted a strategic shift away from the pre-AI data infrastructure business, which had generated zero revenue in its first year.
* **Naming Generative Media:** Recognizing that image and video models solved fundamentally different problems than LLMs (targeting ads, marketing, and studios), the founders coined the term "generative media" and put it on their website three years ago when no one else understood the category.

### Business Growth and Revenue Dynamics
* **Current Revenue Split:** Video and image models now split fal's revenue 50/50, surprising the founders who initially believed video would completely cannibalize image models.
* **Resurgence of Image Models:** Image editing models brought image models back to life because they allow users to create precise starting frames that video models can then effectively interpolate between.
* **Infinite Demand Despite Rising Costs:** When video models like V2 jumped in price from roughly 30–40 cents per 5-second clip to $5 per 5-second clip, the team initially doubted who would pay; instead, usage surged as professional use cases in ads and movie production expanded.
* **Compute Constraints:** The company acts as an intimate infrastructure and API partner for model labs and application builders during a severe compute shortage, anticipating growth to procure compute and enable ecosystem launches.

### Team Culture and Flat Organizational Design
* **Team Scale and Backgrounds:** fal operates with a ~150-person organization, drawing heavily on talent from Coinbase and Amazon networks built over the founders' careers.
* **Hiring Philosophy:** The company recruits for intense domain interest and "hunger," echoing historical crypto-quiz hiring tactics from Coinbase to find engineers who are genuinely obsessed with the space.
* **Two-Level Hierarchy:** fal maintains an exceptionally flat structure with only two reporting levels, relying on "super ICs" (individual contributors who can both execute hands-on work and lead small teams of 5 to 10 engineers) rather than pure managers.
* **Work-Life Realities:** Despite the high-stakes environment, the company encourages realistic boundaries, noting that engineers go home on weekends while still achieving high velocity.

### Industry Evolution and Future Modalities
* **Modality Expansion:** Generative media encompasses multiple sub-categories and modalities—including text-to-image, image editing, video, 3D/4D/5D for gaming, and text/speech translation—with roughly 5 to 10 companies competing in each sub-category.
* **Abundant Training Data:** Unlike code or specific LLM domains, video training data enjoys an abundance of freely available content; the hard problem is filtering out interesting physics and world data rather than running out of raw material.
* **World Models and Gaming:** Emerging world models allow users to navigate persistent 3D/interactive environments via prompts, which the founders believe could unlock gaming as the next massive AI product-market fit.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Software Engineering Speed** — *Gorkem Yurtseven* claims that with AI assistance, fal’s software engineering team moves **3 to 4x faster**, meaning they would otherwise need **4 times the headcount** to maintain the same velocity.
* **Timeline for Evergreen Content** — *Burkay Gür* predicts that within **the next 12 months**, the industry will produce its first "timeless AI content"—a widely accepted short movie or animation that people will look back on years later and enjoy the same way they do upon release.
