## 2026 Brookfield Affiliates Investor Day: Fireside Chat with Bruce Flatt and Howard Marks

- Channel: Brookfield

---

### TL;DR
Brookfield CEO Bruce Flatt and Oaktree Co-Chair Howard Marks discussed the evolution and resilience of private alternative asset management, emphasizing an ownership mentality, rigorous downside protection, and the strategic advantages of deploying capital counter-cyclically during market dislocations.

### Journey and Evolution of Alternative Assets
* **Pioneering High Yield and Alternatives:** Howard Marks recounted starting at Citibank in 1969 when investing was restricted to stocks and bonds, later being assigned in 1978 to figure out high-yield bonds ("the first alternative investing"), which subsequently paved the way for private equity in the mid-1980s, venture capital in the 1990s, hedge funds, and private credit.
* **Oaktree's Founding:** In 1995, Marks and four co-founders left TCW managing $7 billion to launch Oaktree, driven simply by the belief that good performance would sustain them, eventually growing to approximately $230 billion today.
* **Brookfield's Operating Roots:** Bruce Flatt explained that Brookfield emerged from an operating perspective rather than purely financial engineering, managing real estate, infrastructure, and renewables for over 125 years and utilizing outside capital to scale without diluting common shareholders.
* **Early Innings and Consolidation:** Flatt noted that private asset management is still in its early innings and will continue to grow for another 10, 20, or 30 years as capital piles up globally from sovereign funds, pensions, and private wealth. Simultaneously, industry consolidation is driving large institutional allocators to concentrate relationships with massive, multi-product platforms like Brookfield and Oaktree.

### Ownership Mentality vs. Hired Hands
* **Brookfield's Skin in the Game:** Marks highlighted that unlike typical "hired hands" running pieces of paper, Brookfield commits its own capital directly alongside clients and listed entities, guaranteeing alignment of interest.
* **Buying the Unpopular:** Marks praised Brookfield’s willingness to buy assets for its own account that no one else will touch ("nirvana in the investment world"), noting that the long-term backbone of Brookfield is acquiring cash-flowing businesses with strong downside protection.
* **Scale and Exclusivity:** Flatt emphasized that in asset classes like power, infrastructure, and real estate, greater scale yields more exclusivity, less competition, and more certain or superior returns.

### Case Studies in Counter-Cyclical Investing
* **Oaktree's $11B 2008 Deployment:** In October 2008—amid the collapse of Lehman, Bear Stearns, and AIG—Oaktree sat on the largest distressed debt fund in history ($11 billion, 90% uninvested). Reasoned that if the world ended, investing wouldn't matter; if it didn't, not investing meant failing their job. Bruce Karsh invested an average of $450 million a week for 15 weeks ($7 billion in one quarter).
* **Brookfield's Enron Hydro Purchase:** In 2002 following Enron's bankruptcy and total chaos in the power markets, Brookfield invested $900 million in hydro plants in Maine and upstate New York, which required patience and doubling down before eventually being refinanced and yielding multiple times their money.
* **The Westinghouse Acquisition:** Brookfield leveraged its 50+ years of power market expertise and proprietary intelligence—recognizing that the plants' operating grids could not shut down without crashing regional power grids—to underwrite a worst-case, downside-protected 15% runoff return, with massive upside from electrification and AI.
* **The Rules of Conviction:** Flatt stressed that successful contrarian investing requires proprietary information, a fortress balance sheet to survive early losses or falling knives, and institutional backing (board, partners, and investment committee) to avoid panicking during drawdowns.

### Due Diligence and Risk Control
* **Exclusionary Filtering:** Flatt stated that most potential deals are rejected within an hour or two (throwing out 8 out of 10 incoming deals immediately) because Brookfield's existing portfolio ($1.3 trillion of assets) provides unmatched proprietary pricing data.
* **Credit as a Negative Art:** Marks cited Graham and Dodd's view that fixed income is a "negative art" where lenders have no upside, only degrees of failure; thus, Oaktree’s foundational motto is "if we avoid the losers, the winners take care of themselves."
* **Underwriting the Borrower:** Both leaders emphasized putting themselves in the shoes of the borrower to rigorously evaluate survival capability and worst-case recovery outcomes before committing capital.

### Macroeconomics, Inflation, and Interest Rates
* **Historical Perspective on Rates:** Marks argued that current rates are not historically "high," but rather "in the zip code of normal," having only appeared elevated relative to the zero-rate policy maintained from 2009 onward to rescue the financial system.
* **Natural vs. Administered Rates:** Marks welcomed a potential return to natural interest rates set through direct negotiation between borrowers and lenders rather than decades of Fed-administered rates.
* **Spread Compression on Borrowing:** Flatt noted that equity strategies earning 12% to 25% are largely indifferent to a 5% vs. 6% base rate, and because lenders have compressed spreads from 400 basis points down to roughly 100 basis points since the zero-rate era, actual borrowing costs have not risen as drastically as headline rates suggest.
* **Geopolitical vs. Structural Inflation:** Flatt and Marks agreed that current inflation (around 3.4%, down from 9.5% in 2021-2022) is primarily driven by oil prices and two ongoing wars rather than classic "too much money chasing too few goods," meaning it is transitory rather than permanent. Consequently, markets are currently punishing sound assets due to flawed assumptions that high inflation and rates will last forever.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Bruce Flatt:** Predicts that private asset management will continue its inexorable shift into private hands for another 10, 20, or 30 years due to the relentless accumulation of capital in sovereign wealth funds, pension funds, and private wealth.

### Notable Quotes

* > *“The cop who caught [Son of Sam] passed away a few years ago and in his obituary it reported that they said to him, 'How did you catch the son of Sam?' And he said, 'I put myself in position to get lucky.'”* — **Howard Marks** (referencing Brookfield's approach to opportunistic investing)

### TL;DR
Despite global uncertainties and regional instability, Qatar continues to maintain strong GDP growth and attract record foreign investment, while partnerships like Brookfield and the Qatar Investment Authority are scaling up local AI infrastructure and energy-to-compute projects.

### Economic Resilience and Stability in Qatar
* **Long-Term Regional Growth:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal bin Thani bin Faisal Al-Thani noted that Qatar has achieved 6% year-over-year GDP growth across the past decade despite facing multiple crises including Covid-19, oil price crashes, and regional instability.
  * Bruce Flatt described the development in the Middle East and Qatar over the past 25 years as "almost a miracle," noting that capital availability and a push for system resilience will drive significant investments over the next 15 years.
* **Ground-Level Business Activity:** 
  * In the three months following the onset of the recent conflict, over 5,000 new foreign-owned companies were established in Qatar, representing a 60% increase compared to the same period last year.
  * Local Qatari manufacturers increased their exports by 12% over the same timeframe.
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal noted that active discussions are ongoing with international firms to relocate regional offices or expand operations, representing at least $15 billion of foreign direct investment (FDI) with plans remaining unchanged.

### $20B AI Infrastructure and Compute Partnership
* **Energy-to-Compute Transition:** 
  * Brookfield and the Qatar Investment Authority (QIA / Chi-I) formed a $20 billion joint venture in December to focus on artificial intelligence infrastructure.
  * Bruce Flatt highlighted that Qatar's abundant natural gas allows the country to convert local energy directly into compute services, creating high-margin economic value.
* **Global Demand for Localized Compute:** 
  * Data centers are being commissioned and construction will start shortly to sell compute both domestically—as every country requires local AI capacity—and internationally.
  * Bruce Flatt noted that global data storage and intelligence are shifting away from being exclusively centralized in the United States toward local country build-outs due to domestic capacity constraints.
* **Agnostic Supplier Strategy:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal stated that Qatar and Brookfield remain agnostic regarding technology suppliers, utilizing both Chinese and American technology based on customer demand (such as building identical neighboring data centers in the Maldives and Indonesia using different national technologies).
  * Bruce Flatt observed that the global market is developing into parallel systems—a Chinese-led system and a Western/US-led system—operating independently.

### Investment Strategy and Public-Private Collaboration
* **Core Investment Rules:** 
  * Bruce Flatt outlined Brookfield’s 40-year investment philosophy: pick the right countries (operating in roughly 30 nations including the US, UK, France, Australia, Canada, Brazil, and Colombia), partner with good people, and buy or build good backbone infrastructure.
  * Brookfield manages approximately $700 billion of investments in the United States and is a major infrastructure investor across the globe.
* **Domestic Policy and KPIs:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal explained that while QIA is a financial institution prioritizing financial returns, resilience metrics (such as food, medical, and manufacturing security) serve as positive considerations under Qatar's National Development Strategy (which features eight key pillars, including manufacturing).

### Value of Global Institutions and Dialogue
* **Continued Relevancy:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal emphasized that international gatherings like the UN General Assembly and G20 remain essential because they maintain channels for dialogue, even among opponents.
  * Bruce Flatt agreed on the value of global meetings, noting that despite event fatigue, human commonalities make regular dialogue a positive force for stability.

### TL;DR
NVIDIA has formed a strategic coalition with six major Wall Street financial institutions—Goldman Sachs, BlackRock, Blackstone, KKR, Apollo, and Brookfield—to secure over $500 billion in third-party, independent financing for the global AI infrastructure buildout.

### The $500B AI Infrastructure Financing Initiative
- **Consortium Formation:** 
  - **Jensen Huang (NVIDIA):** Brought together the six premier institutional financiers for infrastructure to establish partnerships that pull together long-term, independent capital without utilizing NVIDIA's balance sheet.
  - **David Solomon (Goldman Sachs):** Approached by NVIDIA with a deep belief in the 3-to-10-year opportunity set, bringing Goldman Sachs' unique capital distribution network to the initiative.
  - **Jim Zelter (Apollo):** Described the coalition as a "calling all precincts" effort combining all equity, debt, and banking markets to support the next decade of American exceptionalism and compute as an asset class.
- **Scale and Economic Impact:**
  - **Larry Fink (BlackRock):** Noted that each gigawatt requires $50B to $60B to build, with the U.S. alone needing over 70 gigawatts; emphasized that the buildout will create millions of blue-collar job work orders.
  - **John Gray (Blackstone):** Highlighted a sevenfold increase in demand for LLMs over the past six months, noting that compute capacity is failing to keep pace with demand.
  - **Vlad Slezak (KKR):** Pointed out that infrastructure capacity is now being added on a quarterly basis rather than over 15-year cycles, marking a generational investment opportunity.

### Compute as a New Investable Asset Class
- **Asset-Backed Financing:**
  - **Jensen Huang:** Explained that NVIDIA’s AI factory platform has shifted from a technology component to a revenue-generating, long-lived, and fungible infrastructure asset class.
  - **David Solomon:** Noted that capital markets are utilizing asset-based financing against real assets with tangible value, drawing from $9T in U.S. money market funds and over $100T in U.S. equities.
  - **Vlad Slezak:** Stated that the efficiency of producing tokens per watt of energy consumed is creating a step-change function where the compute cost declines rapidly while adoption surges.
- **Risk Mitigation and Counterparties:**
  - **John Gray:** Compared compute to aviation or residential housing financing, where underwriters look at both the credit of the operator and the intrinsic, flexible value of the physical asset (GPUs and CUDA software).
  - **Jensen Huang:** Confirmed that systems are specified so that if a primary player runs out of cash, alternate users can seamlessly take over and operate the computing platform due to universally adopted NVIDIA architecture.

### Energy and Power Renaissance
- **Power Constraints and Nuclear Buildout:**
  - **Bruce Flatt (Brookfield):** Stated that Brookfield cannot build power or compute fast enough to meet demand, noting they currently have 14 nuclear plants in various stages of construction with another 40 planned (and 100 more coming).
  - **Bruce Flatt:** Highlighted that Brookfield bought Westinghouse out of bankruptcy seven years ago, sparking a carbon-free, baseload energy renaissance in the U.S. led by Westinghouse without government funding.
  - **Jensen Huang:** Confirmed that the entire supply chain—from chips, memory, and packaging to photonics, land, and power—will remain constrained for some time due to overwhelming, profitable global demand.

### Private Markets to Public Capital Evolution
- **Funding Lifecycle:**
  - **John Gray:** Explained that private capital must build out the infrastructure during the initial years when income is zero, after which assets migrate to yield-based public markets.
  - **Larry Fink:** Confirmed BlackRock will provide both private and public financing, working with global pension funds to transition short-term money market capital into long-dated returns.
  - **Jensen Huang:** Noted that upcoming AI lab IPOs will be the biggest in history once these companies go public and demonstrate profitability.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Larry Fink:** Stated that over time, investors will see increasing allocations into data center financing as the next evolution of financial engineering akin to the 1970s mortgage-backed securities market.
- **David Solomon:** Predicted that looking out 3, 5, 7, and 10 years, economic growth and productivity gains from AI deployment will filter broadly through the economy, though not in a straight line.
- **Jensen Huang:** Claimed that within months, it will become clear that AI labs and startups are the fastest-growing technology companies in history and are extremely profitable.
- **Jensen Huang:** Predicted that when AI startups and labs go public in the future, they will represent the biggest IPOs in history.
