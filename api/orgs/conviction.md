#### TL;DR
The AI landscape is fiercely competitive and moving at breakneck speed, driven by roughly 250 frontier researchers and high-agency founders. Sarah Guo emphasizes that navigating this era without historical backtesting requires rigorous first-principles thinking, intense execution, and deep intuition about technical bets rather than relying solely on pedigree.

### The AI Investment Landscape & Strategy
* **The Reality of Non-Backtestable Markets:** Investing in the current AI boom is a high-speed, high-stakes game where traditional venture playbooks cannot be backtested. 
    * **Sarah Guo's Approach:** Conviction was founded on the thesis that a generational technology transition was happening, and traditional venture firms were bogged down by generational transitions of their own. Guo leveraged focused first-principles thinking to gain access and make high-conviction bets.
    * **Execution Over Pomp:** Guo initially pitched LPs without a polished, rigid slide deck or a clean master narrative, stating simply that she backed extraordinary people and would "execute like hell."
* **Portfolio Construction and Time Allocation:** 
    * **Time Split:** Guo spends about two-thirds of her time on portfolio company support (recruiting, strategy, ecosystem influence), followed by fundraising and evaluating new deals.
    * **Deal Volume:** She sees 4 to 6 new companies a week—a much lower volume than her early days at Greylock (where she saw 500 in a couple of months)—allowing for deeper calibration and conviction.
    * **Individual Decision Making:** Guo rejects collective decision-making models, insisting that an individual partner must own and drive the investment decision.

### The Frontier Ecosystem & The 250 Researchers
* **The Core Group:** Guo's partner Mike tracks a group of roughly 250 individuals—entrepreneurs and researchers—who show up every day pushing the technical frontier forward. 
* **Shifting Researcher Psychology:** 
    * **The Exponential Intelligence Narrative:** A growing belief within the last 12 months is that recursive self-improvement of AI research models puts exponential intelligence only 1 to 2 years away.
    * **Disempowerment at Scale:** As major labs expand to thousands of employees and require hundreds of billions in compute, individual researchers increasingly feel that their personal contributions don't move the needle—that either the model will do it itself, or only compute scale matters.
* **Standout Talent (Sunday Robotics):** Guo highlights Tony Xho and Changi at Sunday Robotics (backed immediately in their first meeting as Stanford PhDs around age 25). They have tackled robotics generalization and robustness by solving cheap data collection and transfer learning, aiming to ship general semi-humanoid robot prototypes into homes.

### Compute, Energy, and Infrastructure Constraints
* **The 2030 Bottleneck:** A hyperscaler infrastructure leader noted that nothing will move the needle for power scale before 2030, raising severe concerns about natural gas availability and grid capacity for data centers.
* **Regulatory and Alignment Hurdles:** The primary limits to building abundant cheap energy (such as nuclear base load) and massive data centers are regulatory and societal alignment (convincing local populations to permit construction), rather than technical or entrepreneurial shortcomings.
* **Compute Independence:** Rebuilding the US industrial base via automation requires massive compute and energy inputs. Guo compares this to energy independence, warning that critical vulnerabilities in the global hardware supply chain (like TSMC's manufacturing concentration) necessitate domestic and allied capacity building across data centers, robotics labor, nuclear energy, and alternative chip architectures.

### Open Source and Global Competitiveness
* **The Cat is Out of the Bag:** Open-source models from international fronts (including China, Europe, and the US) are already powerful, highly competitive, and used everywhere.
* **Economic Necessity for Enterprises:** Enterprises and downstream users rely on open-source models because frontier provider APIs are often too expensive, slow, or sensitive for specific workflows. 
* **The Folly of US Restrictions:** Guo argues that restricting open-source models in the United States would only handcuff law-abiding American businesses while failing to stop bad actors who operate outside domestic jurisdictions. Instead, the US should focus on rigorous safety testing and understanding model capabilities.

### Sector Shifts: Legal, Biology, and Beyond
* **Legal Tech (Harvey):** The bet on Harvey was rooted in pure technical logic: law is structured language requiring text generation and retrieval. Starting with trivial tasks like reading landlord-tenant agreements, the ambition is scaling to handle 85% of complex M&A work (like Activision Blizzard).
* **Computational Biology (Chai Discovery):** Conviction made the first institutional check in Chai Discovery, which partners with top-10 pharma companies. Guo noted a major shift in conventional wisdom: AI models can now create and capture enormous value within pharma R&D, moving beyond the old dogma that software investors cannot build platform businesses in biotech without discovering a drug themselves.

## How Chess.com Became the World’s Biggest Chess Community with CEO Erik Allebest

- Channel: No Priors: AI, Machine Learning, Tech, & Startups
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hplb9IHWuw0
- Published: 2026-08-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260813.html#video-hplb9IHWuw0
- Speakers: Erik Allebest (CEO, Chess.com); Sarah Guo (Founder, Conviction)

---

### TL;DR
Erik Allebest and Sarah Guo discuss how Chess.com scaled to 250 million registered members, 10 million daily active users, and over $200 million in annual revenue by prioritizing pure user experience, community, and the cultural revival of chess.

### Company Backstory & Scale
* **Domain Acquisition:** In 2005, Erik Allebest and his co-founder/CTO bought the chess.com domain name for **$56,000** out of a bankruptcy auction with the vision of building a MySpace-like community for chess.
* **Bootstrapped Growth:** Facing rejection from venture capitalists who called the idea an "uninvestable niche passion project," the founders grew the business slowly funded by cash flow, revenue from online learning memberships launched in **2007** (within 18 months of launch), and personal savings.
* **Current Metrics:** Today, Chess.com employs **650 people**, boasts **250 million registered members**, **10 to 50 million monthly active users**, **10 million daily active users**, and projects slightly over **$200 million** in revenue this year.
* **Recent Milestones:** After explosive viral waves during the COVID-19 pandemic, *The Queen's Gambit*, short-form content, the Mittens bot, and online cheating scandals in **2023**, the platform successfully stabilized at a much higher baseline by **2024**.

### Private Equity & Investment Evolution
* **General Atlantic:** Entered around **2020 or 2021** as the first private equity firm to believe in the mission, buying secondary shares from existing investors without injecting new capital.
* **CVC Capital Partners:** Recently partnered with Chess.com to buy out earlier shares, bringing deep expertise in gaming, sports, and community businesses; General Atlantic also reinvested.
* **Operational Impact:** Allebest notes that working with General Atlantic and CVC has positively transformed the company through rigorous forecasting, operational excellence, and active, in-the-weeds product collaboration rather than top-down pressure.

### AI, Human Expertise, & Anti-Cheating
* **Evolution of Chess Engines:** Early chess engines like Stockfish made games overly mechanical and boring, but subsequent neural network engines like Leela Chess Zero introduced aggressive, unconventional play that reinvigorated human creativity and gameplay.
* **Accelerated Learning:** Modern AI tools, personalized puzzles, game reviews, and LLM-powered coaching help young players (including children and 7-to-10 prodigies) reach elite levels much faster.
* **Human-Centric Retention:** Despite computers outclassing humans for **30 years**, chess popularity has surged because humans fundamentally want to compete against other humans and value shared cultural touchstones.
* **Anti-Cheating Operations:** Chess.com dedicates substantial resources, statistical models, and machine learning models to detect cheating, protect game integrity for both casual and professional players with prize money, and ban bad actors.

### Product Expansion & New Ventures
* **Pure Software Focus:** Unlike capex-heavy businesses or hardware companies, Chess.com succeeded as a software and content platform driven by relentless focus on user experience and community.
* **Gambit.com:** Chess.com recently launched *gambit.com*, a poker platform designed to apply the chess playbook—introducing skill-based ratings rather than just chip volume or financial risk.
* **Future Games:** The company plans to roll out additional classic games built around wholesome, educational, and pure gaming principles.
* **Internal AI Stack:** The company utilizes LLMs for customer support automations, internal data analysis, and an internal authentication/knowledge layer called **GNS** built by CTO Josh to accelerate product development cycles.
