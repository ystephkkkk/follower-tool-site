#### TL;DR
Current generative AI models and Large Language Models (LLMs) are fundamentally incapable of reaching human-level intelligence because they rely on forward propagation and cannot understand the complex, high-dimensional physical world. True progress will require moving toward objective-driven architectures utilizing hierarchical world models and inference by optimization.

---

#### Limitations of Current AI
* **The Failure of Generative AI and LLMs:** 
  * **Limited Training Data Scope:** LLMs are pre-trained on roughly $10^{14}$ bytes of publicly available text (approx. 20-30 trillion tokens), which is equivalent to only about half a million years of reading. In contrast, a 4-year-old child processes roughly the same volume of data ($10^{14}$ bytes) through 16,000 hours of optic nerve visual input (2 million nerve fibers at ~1 byte per second).
  * **Inability to Generalize to the Physical World:** Despite passing bar exams and math olympiads, current AI fails to deliver domestic robots or level-5 self-driving cars. Imitation learning for driving has been a "complete failure."
  * **The Moravec Paradox:** As formulated by Hans Moravec in 1988, things that are easy for humans/animals are difficult for AI, and vice versa.
* **Flaws in Forward Propagation:**
  * **Lack of Variable Thinking Time:** LLMs use pure forward propagation through transformer layers, meaning the computational effort spent on a "yes/no" answer does not scale with question complexity.
  * **Chain-of-Thought Limitations:** Techniques like chain-of-thought force LLMs to generate intermediate tokens, but true reasoning in biological systems is an optimization/search process rather than feedforward propagation.

#### The Science of Human and Animal Intelligence
* **Intelligence Defined:** Citing a developmental psychology concept attributed to Jean Piaget, *"Intelligence is not what you know, it's what you do when you don't know."* Intelligence should be measured by how fast a system can learn a new task zero-shot.
* **Developmental Milestones:** Cognitive scientist Emmanuel Dupoux's research shows babies learn basic concepts sequentially:
  * **2 Months:** Animate/inanimate distinction and object permanence.
  * **9 Months:** Intuitive physics (gravity, inertia, conservation of momentum).
* **Non-Linguistic Reasoning:** Most animal and human reasoning happens in abstract mental representation space (e.g., mental rotation of a 3D cube), not through language. Language is largely restricted to narrow domains like coding and mathematics.

#### Objective-Driven Architecture & World Models
* **Core Components:** Instead of generative prediction, Yann LeCun proposes an objective-driven architecture containing:
  * **Perception Encoder:** Converts raw observations ($X_T$) into a state representation.
  * **World Model:** A trainable neural network predicting the next state ($S_{T+1}$) given the current state and a proposed action ($A_T$).
  * **Objective/Cost Function:** Evaluates whether the task is accomplished ($0$ for success, positive numbers for distance from goal).
  * **Guardrail Objectives:** Evaluated on predicted states to ensure safety and prevent harm.
* **Inference by Optimization:** Unlike forward-propagation, the system searches for an optimal sequence of actions that minimizes the objective function using model predictive control.
* **Hierarchical Planning:** 
  * Real-world problem solving requires multi-level abstraction (e.g., planning a trip to Paris breaks down into: going to the airport $\rightarrow$ hailing a taxi $\rightarrow$ taking the elevator $\rightarrow$ standing up from a chair).
  * Multi-level world models predict short-term details at lower layers (e.g., ant leg positions over 200 milliseconds) and long-term abstract goals at higher layers (e.g., overall trajectory over several seconds), drastically increasing planning success rates.

#### Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)
* **The Problem with Generative Prediction:** Forcing a model to predict every pixel in a video (e.g., predicting what every person in a meeting room looks like) fails because most information in the real world is unpredictable. Generative models result in blurry outputs or hallucinations.
* **JEPA Mechanism:** Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures run both observations ($X$) and targets ($Y$) through encoders to predict *abstract representations* rather than raw pixel details, ignoring unpredictable elements.
* **Preventing Representation Collapse:** 
  * Without intervention, encoder networks tend to cheat by outputting constant representations to achieve zero prediction error.
  * **SIGREG (Sketch Isotropic Gaussian Regularization):** A technique that projects representation vectors against multiple random dimensions and uses Fourier transform/CDF distance to drive the empirical marginal distribution toward an isotropic Gaussian with variance 1.
  * **Alternative Anti-Collapse Methods:** Self-supervised distillation frameworks (like DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2/2.1) use teacher-student encoder pairs trained with exponential moving averages.
* **Emergent Properties of V-JEPA:** When trained purely on video to predict missing parts, V-JEPA models spontaneously discover the 3D structure of the world (enabling depth prediction and object segmentation without explicit supervision).
