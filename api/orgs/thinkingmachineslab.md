## Thinking Machines’ Murati on AI’s Next Chapter

**TL;DR** 
Mira Murati, Co-Founder and CEO of Thinking Machines Lab, outlines her company's core bet on "interaction models" designed for real-time human-AI collaboration, while reflecting on the governance failures of her former employer, OpenAI, and emphasizing the need for robust institutional checks and balances in the AI sector.

### Redefining Human-AI Collaboration: Time-Based Interaction
* **The Turn-Based Limitation:** Current AI systems operate on a turn-based system where the model is functionally "deaf and blind" while processing a prompt, failing to perceive real-time human reactions.
* **Time-Based Interaction Models:** Thinking Machines is developing a new class of models that continuously ingest and output audio, text, and video.
    * **20-Millisecond Processing:** The system processes data in 20-millisecond chunks, enabling the AI to detect natural human nuances such as interruptions, silence, and simultaneous speech.
    * **The "Tandem Bike" Philosophy:** Murati envisions human-AI interaction as a tandem bike where both parties pedal, rather than an autonomous system that removes human agency entirely.
* **AI as Tools for Thought:** The objective is to bring machine intelligence closer to the messiness of real-world human experience, comparing the shift to historical cognitive leaps like the invention of modern numeral systems.

### Strategic Differentiation and the Talent Landscape
* **Diverging from Major Competitors:** While rivals like OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta race toward pure autonomy, Thinking Machines is positioning itself as a frontier lab focused primarily on human-in-the-loop systems.
* **The "War" for Talent:** Murati downplays the term "war for talent" despite rumors of nine-figure compensation packages in the industry. 
    * **Compressed Start-up Cycles:** She notes that building a frontier AI lab from scratch compresses 5 to 10 years of typical startup volatility—both talent acquisition and departures—into a single year.
* **Infrastructure Capital:** While Thinking Machines has raised substantial funding, Murati emphasizes that high capital reserves are a baseline requirement to build the physical infrastructure needed for frontier science.

### Reflections on the OpenAI Board Crisis and Governance
* **Preventing Implosion:** Reflecting on the late 2023 OpenAI board crisis, Murati stood by the feedback she provided to the board regarding Sam Altman's leadership. She stepped in as interim CEO to prevent what she believed was a catastrophic threat of company collapse.
* **Hindsight and Regrets:** With the benefit of hindsight, Murati regrets not pausing longer to critically evaluate the board's transition plan, which lacked transparency, continuity, and clear communication for the team.
* **Systemic Checks Over Individual Morality:** Murati argues that the industry focuses too heavily on the character of individual leaders rather than institutional design. 
    * **Distribution of Power:** AI governance must rely on structural checks and balances rather than a single authority deciding what is safe or beneficial.

### Future Outlook and the Path Forward
* **Avoiding Dystopian/Utopian Binaries:** Murati rejects simplified predictions of massive white-collar job displacement or instant utopia, arguing that humanity still retains the agency to shape how these tools are integrated.
* **Upcoming Product Preview:** Thinking Machines is on track to release a product preview later this year to showcase its interaction models.
* **Five-Year Success Metric:** For Murati, ultimate success in five years means creating a future where AI progress enhances human agency, dignity, and a sense of possibility, rather than leaving civilization behind.

> "Morality is not everything, you have to think about actual decision-making structures and transparency and governance... to get as many people involved in these things as possible, you actually have to share the knowledge, you have to share tools."
