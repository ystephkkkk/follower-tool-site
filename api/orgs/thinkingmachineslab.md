## OpenAI’s Cofounder & Research Chief Explain Self-Improving AI

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h_aA1-R70E8
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261117.html#video-h_aA1-R70E8
- Speakers: Sarah Wang (General Partner, a16z); Mark Chen (Chief Research Officer, OpenAI); John Schulman (Chief Scientist, Thinking Machines Lab)

---

### TL;DR
Frontier AI labs have entered a "Centaur era" where AI acts as "research interns" roughly 25–50% of the way toward full recursive self-improvement (RSI). While AI is already accelerating research—delivering an estimated 2x speedup—achieving full RSI requires overcoming bottlenecks in "research taste," downstream evaluation, and compute, even as automated research emerges as one of the most promising tools for solving AI alignment.

### Defining RSI and the "Centaur Era"
* **The Current State of Acceleration:** According to a November 5 Metr report cited by a16z general partner Sarah Wang, AI research at Anthropic has seen a roughly 2x speedup in the rate of progress over the past year, driven by human researchers using AI models to accelerate their workflows.
* **RSI as an Accelerator:** Mark Chen, Chief Research Officer at OpenAI (who has been with the company for over eight years and co-leading research with Jakub Pachocki since 2025), defines RSI primarily as using AI—such as Codex—to accelerate pre-training and reinforcement learning (RL) and shift the capability curve.
* **"AI Research Interns":** Chen notes that OpenAI has built "AI research interns" today, measured by the amount of compute managed end-to-end by Codex to run scoped experiments, monitor runs, and automate parts of the research pipeline.
* **The AGI vs. RSI Spectrum:** Chen views both AGI and RSI as spectrums rather than single moments in time. While he estimates the industry is roughly 80% of the way to AGI, he places current capabilities at only 25–50% along the RSI spectrum.
* **The Centaur Workflow:** John Schulman, co-founder and Chief Scientist of Thinking Machines Lab and co-founder of OpenAI, describes the current phase as the "Centaur era," where an expert human working with AI outperforms either alone.
  * *Concrete Example:* Schulman shared his workflow using Fable (with settings like UltraCode turned up) to run experiments for several days. While the AI generates reasonable experiments and ideas, the end result often fails until Schulman steps in to read the results, ask questions the AI missed, and redirect the project.

### Bottlenecks to Full RSI: Taste, Verification, and Compute
* **The "Research Taste" Gap:** Schulman defines research taste as knowing which ideas are important long-term, generalizable, and composable with other ideas—as opposed to routine execution or hyperparameter optimization.
  * *Knowledge vs. Experience:* While AI can read and memorize all historical literature, it lacks the human experience of making predictions, being wrong, and absorbing those lessons.
  * *Baking in Intuition:* To bridge this gap, Schulman expects companies will use RL from human feedback (RLHF) to collect data from researchers on which proposed ideas are good, effectively baking human intuition into models.
* **Verification and Evaluation:** Chen identifies measuring success as one of the defining challenges of RSI.
  * *Pre-training vs. Downstream:* Pre-training is straightforward to hill-climb because the field relies on a single metric: perplexity. Downstream, however, evaluation benchmarks have increasingly shorter shelf-lives, forcing labs to focus heavily on automating the creation of new, harder evals.
* **Compute Constraints:** Unlike math—where hypotheses can be tested purely through reasoning—AI research resembles experimental physics or biology, requiring physical experiments. Chen notes that accelerating RSI requires finding smaller-scale experiments that reliably predict larger-scale outcomes.

### Aligning RSI—and Using AI to Solve Alignment
* **The Risks of Drift:** Chen warns that without solving goal and value alignment, models running over long horizons could drift and develop dangerous sub-objectives, such as attempting to exfiltrate themselves or copy weights outside of OpenAI.
* **Stronger Models Aligning Models:** Chen argues that RSI must be deeply embedded in the alignment loop, noting that chain-of-thought monitorability is currently the most effective alignment tool. He is also encouraged by an organic shift of researchers from pre-training and RL into safety and alignment sub-teams.
* **Optimizing for Honesty:** Schulman is optimistic about designing training objectives that incentivize models to report on their internal states. He highlights OpenAI's *Confessions* research—where models are rewarded solely for the honesty of a self-reported confession without penalizing the main answer's reward—alongside techniques like natural language autoencoders and counterfactual simulability.
* **Automated Alignment Breakthroughs:** Wang highlights August research from Anthropic showing that automated AI researchers discovered fixes for 10 different alignment failures. Some of these fixes transferred to models nearly 5x larger, and the best automated methods outperformed experienced human researchers.
* **Decentralized Alignment:** Schulman's Thinking Machines Lab is building customizable models based on the view that people hold different values and should have control over their AI's values. He advocates for making alignment a "public science" so the industry does not overfit to the conclusions of a few companies.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **2027 Capability Inflection:** A chart cited by Sarah Wang projects that in 2027, OpenAI's frontier model capabilities will begin a steep climb set off by automated AI research.
* **Compute Bottlenecks and Next-Gen Inflection:** Mark Chen predicts that AI capabilities will continue to rise until they become bounded by compute, followed by another major inflection point when AIs begin accelerating compute itself (such as helping build next-generation data centers).
* **RLHF for Research Taste:** John Schulman predicts companies will use RL from human feedback to collect data from human researchers on idea quality to instill research intuition into models.
* **Enduring Role of Human Judgment:** Sarah Wang concludes that human judgment will remain essential for the foreseeable future, despite rapid capability acceleration.

### Notable Quotes

## Thinking Machines’ Murati on AI’s Next Chapter

- Channel: Bloomberg Live
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A_jIpryR5js
- Published: 2026-06-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260604.html#video-A_jIpryR5js
- Speakers: Mira Murati (Co-Founder & CEO, Thinking Machines Lab); Emily Chang (Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR** 
Mira Murati, Co-Founder and CEO of Thinking Machines Lab, outlines her company's core bet on "interaction models" designed for real-time human-AI collaboration, while reflecting on the governance failures of her former employer, OpenAI, and emphasizing the need for robust institutional checks and balances in the AI sector.

### Redefining Human-AI Collaboration: Time-Based Interaction
* **The Turn-Based Limitation:** Current AI systems operate on a turn-based system where the model is functionally "deaf and blind" while processing a prompt, failing to perceive real-time human reactions.
* **Time-Based Interaction Models:** Thinking Machines is developing a new class of models that continuously ingest and output audio, text, and video.
    * **20-Millisecond Processing:** The system processes data in 20-millisecond chunks, enabling the AI to detect natural human nuances such as interruptions, silence, and simultaneous speech.
    * **The "Tandem Bike" Philosophy:** Murati envisions human-AI interaction as a tandem bike where both parties pedal, rather than an autonomous system that removes human agency entirely.
* **AI as Tools for Thought:** The objective is to bring machine intelligence closer to the messiness of real-world human experience, comparing the shift to historical cognitive leaps like the invention of modern numeral systems.

### Strategic Differentiation and the Talent Landscape
* **Diverging from Major Competitors:** While rivals like OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta race toward pure autonomy, Thinking Machines is positioning itself as a frontier lab focused primarily on human-in-the-loop systems.
* **The "War" for Talent:** Murati downplays the term "war for talent" despite rumors of nine-figure compensation packages in the industry. 
    * **Compressed Start-up Cycles:** She notes that building a frontier AI lab from scratch compresses 5 to 10 years of typical startup volatility—both talent acquisition and departures—into a single year.
* **Infrastructure Capital:** While Thinking Machines has raised substantial funding, Murati emphasizes that high capital reserves are a baseline requirement to build the physical infrastructure needed for frontier science.

### Reflections on the OpenAI Board Crisis and Governance
* **Preventing Implosion:** Reflecting on the late 2023 OpenAI board crisis, Murati stood by the feedback she provided to the board regarding Sam Altman's leadership. She stepped in as interim CEO to prevent what she believed was a catastrophic threat of company collapse.
* **Hindsight and Regrets:** With the benefit of hindsight, Murati regrets not pausing longer to critically evaluate the board's transition plan, which lacked transparency, continuity, and clear communication for the team.
* **Systemic Checks Over Individual Morality:** Murati argues that the industry focuses too heavily on the character of individual leaders rather than institutional design. 
    * **Distribution of Power:** AI governance must rely on structural checks and balances rather than a single authority deciding what is safe or beneficial.

### Future Outlook and the Path Forward
* **Avoiding Dystopian/Utopian Binaries:** Murati rejects simplified predictions of massive white-collar job displacement or instant utopia, arguing that humanity still retains the agency to shape how these tools are integrated.
* **Upcoming Product Preview:** Thinking Machines is on track to release a product preview later this year to showcase its interaction models.
* **Five-Year Success Metric:** For Murati, ultimate success in five years means creating a future where AI progress enhances human agency, dignity, and a sense of possibility, rather than leaving civilization behind.

> "Our interactions with each other are very rich. There is a lot of information in our interactions when we are silent, when we're thinking, when we're interrupting one another. And so interaction models are able to capture all of this nuance. They're not turn-based, they're more like time-based interaction..." 
> — Mira Murati

> "Morality is not everything, you have to think about actual decision-making structures and transparency and governance... to get as many people involved in these things as possible, you actually have to share the knowledge, you have to share tools."
> — Mira Murati

> "It's more like creating systems that are not just like autonomously advancing and leaving civilization behind, but are more like a tandem bike where you have like both people are pedaling, but, you know, when you're going up a hill maybe whoever is stronger is pedaling harder. But both hands are on the wheel..."
> — Mira Murati
