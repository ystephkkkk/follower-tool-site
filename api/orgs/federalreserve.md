## San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly on the Fed’s New Communications Regime

- Channel: Hoover Institution
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vTXPnV8-7CU

- Speakers: Mary Daly (President, Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco); Steven Davis (Senior Fellow, Hoover Institution)

---

#### TL;DR
The Federal Reserve is reevaluating its communication regime under Chairman Kevin Warsh to improve the signal-to-noise ratio and avoid over-committing via rigid forward guidance. Simultaneously, the Fed is navigating severe crosscurrents—including sticky inflation, supply-side overlapping shocks, and the multi-year economic impacts of AI adoption—requiring a flexible, forward-looking policy framework rather than rigid reliance on conventional playbooks.

---

### Federal Reserve Communications Regime and Strategy

* **Shift from Historical Over-Communication:** 
  * Over the past three decades, central bank communications evolved from near-silence to heavy engagement, amplified significantly after the Global Financial Crisis (GFC) to manage expectations.
  * **Mary Daly on Tool Overuse:** Daly notes that "if I have a hammer, I'm always looking for a nail," arguing that central banks must step back to ensure communication tools build trust and clarity rather than creating low signal-to-noise ratios.
* **The Role of Chairman Kevin Warsh:** 
  * Daly views the transition under Chairman Kevin Warsh as an opportune catalyst to rigorously review Fed communications and balance-sheet tools alongside external task forces.
  * **Avoiding Short-Term Misinterpretation:** Frequent remarks about immediate meeting outcomes (e.g., the upcoming October meeting) risk mischaracterizing the Fed as short-term thinkers rather than forward-looking policymakers acting with 6-to-18-month lags.
* **Revisiting Forward Guidance and Balance Sheet Tools:** 
  * **Forward Guidance Discipline:** Daly suggests forward guidance should be reserved for times when the Fed has high clarity on its direction, rather than during periods of balancing active risks.
  * **Balance Sheet Coupling:** Daly questions whether the size and composition of the Fed's balance sheet should remain tightly coupled with the federal funds rate, or evaluated as a distinct operational tool.

### Inflation Dynamics and Supply-Demand Shocks

* **Conventional Wisdom vs. Current Reality:** 
  * The traditional economic rule of thumb dictates that central banks should "look through" exogenous, one-time supply shocks (such as tariffs or oil price spikes) because policy lags prevent effective offsets.
  * **Daly's Shift:** While Daly favored looking through these shocks six months ago, the persistent duration and growing willingness of businesses to pass price increases downstream have pushed her to be more cautious about overlapping inflationary pressures.
* **Inflation Expectations and the Risk Dashboard:** 
  * **Compensating Pressures:** Daly monitors a specific "inflation risks dashboard," noting that market-based inflation compensation rose ahead of the September FOMC meeting, signaling upward drift.
  * **Upside Risks:** Daly stresses that current risks skew entirely to the upside, requiring the Fed to remain ready and willing to tighten further if necessary.
* **Labor Markets and Real Wages:** 
  * Nominal wage stickiness downwards means that negative supply shocks inherently lower real wages and reduce purchasing power. 
  * While restrictive monetary policy can prevent wage-price spirals during demand overheating, applying it against supply-side shocks risks over-constraining the labor market and worsening real wage compression.

### Artificial Intelligence and Economic Implications

* **The 1-to-3-Year Horizon vs. Decadal Productivity:** 
  * While AI represents a generational general-purpose technology comparable to electrification, monetary policy operates on a 1-to-3-year window. 
  * Long-term productivity gains 10 years out do not directly solve current full-employment and price-stability mandates.
* **Near-Term Demand Pressures vs. Supply Adaptation:** 
  * **Hyper-scaler Bottlenecks:** The current phase is dominated by heavy infrastructure buildouts—data centers, GPUs, and memory chips—creating immediate demand shocks and input constraints (e.g., water, power, and chip availability).
  * **Private Sector Mitigation:** Firms are actively bypassing chip shortages by engineering products to use existing hardware longer, adopting small language models (SLMs), utilizing open-weight models, and deploying internal "gatekeepers" to route queries efficiently.
* **AI Boom-Bust Cycles:** 
  * Similar to historical semiconductor cycles, the rapid AI investment boom will eventually transition into a steady-state maintenance phase. 
  * Daly highlights that a potential "bust" or slowdown in AI infrastructure spending could emerge within the 1-to-3-year horizon, naturally dissipating upward price pressures.

### Financial System Stability and Cyber Risk

* **Commercial Bank Resilience:** 
  * When questioned about systemic threats like catastrophic cyber attacks disabling large financial institutions, Daly emphasized that private bank leaders (such as JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Diamond) and banking regulators are actively addressing cyber threats.
  * **Fed Oversight:** Vice Board Governor Michelle Bowman and the broader Federal Reserve system treat cyber risk as a top-tier supervisory priority, continuously ring-fencing internal IT data while pushing commercial institutions to fortify operational defenses against rapidly evolving technology.

### TL;DR
Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller noted that a surge in long-term US Treasury yields is helping cool interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing and autos, functioning as a de facto rate hike. Speaking at Bloomberg's New Economic Forum, Waller also addressed how massive AI infrastructure spending is creating short-term inflation pressures before driving long-term productivity and growth.

### AI Economy, Global Disparities, and Productivity
- **Global AI Divide:** 
  - **U.S. Leadership:** The U.S. is the dominant builder of AI models, alongside China.
  - **Inputs & Infrastructure:** Inputs are largely produced in Taiwan and South Korea; data centers are deployed globally.
  - **GDP Implications:** There is a high correlation between GDP per capita and workplace AI usage, risking widened global gaps if adoption lags in certain regions. Europe is a major user though it produces little AI hardware or models.
- **Productivity Lag:** 
  - **Trial and Error:** Major technological changes take years to show up in measured productivity as firms experiment with use cases.
  - **Coding Impact:** Coding tools are shockingly good and fundamentally changing the job, shifting workers from basic coding to expanded output.
  - **Welfare vs. GDP:** Efficiency gains may manifest as increased leisure time rather than higher annual output per worker, challenging traditional GDP metrics alongside life-improving non-GDP inventions like Google Maps.

### Inflation Pressures from the AI Buildout
- **Short-Term Price Pressures:** 
  - **Inelastic Demand:** Hyperscalers are spending trillions with perfectly inelastic demand, bidding up prices for semiconductors of all types and spilling over into broader goods.
  - **Timeline:** Price pressures driven by the AI buildout could last **2 to 3 years**, based on current commitments from hyperscalers.
- **Long-Term Deflationary Supply Effects:** Once the massive buildout concludes, demand pressure will taper off and supply effects will allow more output produced faster and at lower prices.

### Global Bond Yields, Growth, and Fiscal Pressures
- **Drivers of Higher Real Yields:** 
  - **Real Growth, Not Inflation:** High yields are driven by real growth, productivity expectations, and AI investments rather than rising inflation expectations (which remain well-anchored).
  - **Corporate Bond Example:** Google issued a 40-year bond paying a **100 basis point** premium over US Treasuries.
- **Fiscal Concerns & Safety Premium:** 
  - **International Strain:** Advanced countries face fiscal challenges (e.g., France), and recent academic research indicates that the safety and liquidity premium on U.S. Treasuries—historically worth **50 to 70 basis points**—has disappeared over the last couple of years.
- **Monetary Policy Spillovers:** Rising 10-year yields, driven by global supply and demand rather than overnight Fed rates, are tightening demand in housing, autos, and other interest-rate-sensitive sectors, doing some of the work of tighter monetary policy.

### Central Bank Independence and Fiscal Dominance
- **Historical Parallels:** Following World War II, when the U.S. debt-to-GDP ratio exceeded **100%**, high inflation over the subsequent two years eroded **40%** of that debt in real terms, leading to the Fed-Treasury Accord.
- **Threats to Independence:** 
  - **Institutional Protections:** While the administration has pressured the FOMC to lower rates, U.S. institutions (Congress, the courts) and the bond market serve as the ultimate protectors of central bank independence.
  - **Bond Market Vigilance:** If an administration creates a high-inflation environment, bond markets will push yields up rather than letting interest rates fall.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **AI Infrastructure Timeline:** Hyperscalers plan on spending trillions of dollars on the AI buildout over the **next 3 years** — Christopher Waller.
- **Inflation Pressure Duration:** Price pressures stemming from the AI infrastructure buildout could last anywhere from **2 to 3 years** — Christopher Waller.

### State of Inflation and Monetary Policy Goals
* **Elevated Price Pressures:** Inflation remains above the 2% target, driven by persistent demand pressures and recurring negative supply shocks such as generalized commodity and diesel price increases.
* **Objective:** The Federal Reserve's primary mandate is to achieve price stability to keep financing costs manageable for households, businesses, and local governments, and to protect real incomes.
* **Timely Horizon:** Musalem defines a "timely manner" for returning inflation to target as approximately 18 months (one and a half to two years).
* **Policy Direction:** Additional monetary policy firming will be required over the next six to nine months to prevent the broadening of inflation pressures and second-round effects.

### Economic and Labor Market Conditions
* **Strong Overall Economy:** The U.S. economy remains very strong, supported by robust investment and consumption driven by wealth effects and artificial intelligence expectations.
* **Balanced Labor Market:** 
  * Layoffs remain stable, employment grows near the break-even rate, and wages grow at roughly **3.5%**, consistent with a 2% inflation rate given current high labor productivity.
  * Certain sectors are overheated—such as construction, where electrician wages have doubled over the last two years due to data center demand—while others are not.

* **Consumer Financial Stress:** 
  * Consumers exhibit low historical savings rates and rely increasingly on credit cards, HELOCs, and "buy now, pay later" services—often for necessities like food rather than luxury spending.
  * Lower- and middle-income families face growing difficulties making ends meet, as reported by community and regional banks.

### Bond Market Dynamics and Capital Demand
* **Nominal and Real Yields:** Bond yields have risen primarily because real yields have increased, driven by rising expected real policy rates rather than a lack of Fed credibility (as inflation expectations remain anchored).
* **Drivers of Real Rates:** 
  * Strong economic growth expectations tied to an "abundant AI future" are propelling business investment.
  * Hyperscalers have shifted from positive to negative free cash flow, accessing capital markets in large size alongside large federal government financing needs.
* **Crowding Out:** Two major non-interest-rate-sensitive borrowers (hyperscalers and the federal government) are competing for capital, putting upward pressure on real interest rates and squeezing interest-rate-sensitive borrowers like small businesses and households.
* **Capital Demand Scale:** Total U.S. capital demand runs at **3% to 4% of GDP per year**, roughly two-thirds of the fiscal deficit and larger than the current account deficit.

### Fiscal Sustainability and Institutional Independence
* **Unsustainable Fiscal Path:** The U.S. federal government has maintained an unsustainable fiscal path for the better part of two decades across multiple administrations, posing potential long-term financial stability risks.
* **Monetary Independence:** 
  * Monetary policy operates independently of debt management policy, adhering to the dual mandate given by Congress.
  * Musalem strongly supports maintaining the separation established by the **1951 Treasury-Fed Accord**, rejecting any integration of Fed purchases with Treasury issuance that could compromise inflation control.

### Financial Conditions and Credit Quality
* **Overall Conditions:** Financial conditions have tightened modestly and in an orderly fashion, remaining accommodative and supportive of economic growth. Total bank credit and capital markets financing remain at very healthy levels.
* **Credit Watchpoints:** 
  * Corporate credit spreads in high-yield and Triple-C segments (both private and public) have widened.
  * At the margin, non-accruals and default rates show a slight deterioration, though absolute levels remain very low.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Alberto Musalem** predicts that interest rates will need to increase further over the next **6 to 9 months** to bring inflation back to target in a timely manner.
* **Alberto Musalem** estimates the appropriate monetary policy timeframe to return inflation to the 2% target is approximately **18 months** (between a year and a half and two years).

### TL;DR
IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva praised Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech for its clarity on price stability, while warning that high global debt levels and rapid technological innovation complicate central bank efforts to manage inflation.

### Jackson Hole and Central Bank Credibility
* **Praise for Fed Leadership:** **Kristalina Georgieva** commended Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole speech for clearly articulating monetary policy evolution and a firm commitment to the 2% inflation target.
* **Central Bank Accountability:** Georgieva stated that central banks should only be blamed if they fail to learn from past experiences, noting that policymakers now appear to recognize the need for more restrictive policies.
* **Developed Market Credibility:** Georgieva emphasized that central banks must remain laser-focused on maintaining trust in their ability to contain inflation, and urged them not to hesitate if action is required.

* **Impact of Consecutive Crises:** **Kristalina Georgieva** explained that shock after shock—including COVID-19, the Ukraine war energy shock, and food price spikes—pushed governments to borrow and spend more than they should.
* **Surpassing Historical Highs:** Global debt levels are now above where they were immediately following the Second World War.
* **Complication for Monetary Policy:** High debt levels make it harder for central banks to act decisively by raising interest rates because higher rates increase debt-servicing costs and risk undermining strong economic performance, particularly under dual mandates.
* **Divergence in Fiscal Discipline:** Emerging markets have performed "fabulously well" with stronger monetary and fiscal frameworks compared to advanced economies, because they learned painful lessons from past crises.

### Economic Resilience, Shocks, and Technological Risks
* **Dual Competing Shocks:** The global economy is currently navigating two opposing forces: energy supply shocks that drag growth down, and artificial intelligence (AI) which pushes growth up.
* **Sources of Resilience:** Despite fears of oil prices reaching $100 to $150, the global economy remained resilient due to deployed reserves, increased US energy production, shifts to alternative energy, and greater-than-expected price sensitivity that reduced demand.
* **Technological Acceleration:** Financial innovation and technology increase risks by accelerating the speed and impact of crises, leading to spillovers within economies and across borders.
* **Inflation Expectations:** Underlying inflation trends do not show fears of inflation getting completely out of whack, and inflation expectations remain well anchored.

### The Status of the US Dollar
* **Lack of Alternatives:** When evaluating potential alternatives to the US dollar as a reserve currency, **Kristalina Georgieva** noted that 98% of stablecoins originate in the US and are backed by Treasuries, leaving no clear march toward a rival currency.
* **Underpinning Strength:** The true strength of the dollar is rooted in the strength of the US economy, which features deep and liquid capital markets, heavy venture capital and innovation investments, and deep overall attractiveness to foreign capital.

## AI Could Push Inflation Either Way, Says BOE’s Bailey

- Channel: Bloomberg Tech
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m1oGaHK38Cg
- Published: 2026-08-28
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260828.html#video-m1oGaHK38Cg
- Speakers: Andrew Bailey (Governor, Bank of England); Lisa Abramowicz (Host, Bloomberg Tech); Kevin Warsh (Former Member, Federal Reserve Board of Governors)

---

### TL;DR
Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey stated that AI could be either inflationary or disinflationary depending on whether demand shocks arrive before supply-side productivity gains. Speaking with Bloomberg's Lisa Abramowicz at Jackson Hole, Bailey also discussed UK inflation, defended a pluralistic committee approach, and emphasized that the BOE will make independent rate decisions regardless of Federal Reserve actions.

### AI and Economic Shocks
* **Dual Economic Impacts:** AI and robotics will generate both demand and supply shocks that affect price stability.
* **Sequencing Dictates Inflation Direction:** 
  * **Upward Pressure:** If demand shocks and related asset market effects outpace supply-side productivity gains, the net impact will be inflationary.
  * **Downward Pressure:** If productivity and supply-side efficiencies materialize first, the technology will prove disinflationary.
* **UK Productivity Need:** Bailey noted that the UK specifically requires faster growth and improved productivity, making AI and robotics a critical source for long-term economic gains.

### Monetary Policy Frameworks and Forward Guidance
* **Flaws of Forward Guidance:** Bailey agreed with Fed Chair Kevin Warsh that forward guidance can become "antiquated," overly complicated, and tend toward inflexible, unconditional statements that complicate policy—noting the 2021 rate-hiking delay as an example of challenges during massive shocks like COVID-19.
* **Conditional Decision-Making:** Monetary policy must look to the future due to the transmission lag of interest rates, but decisions remain inherently conditional amid ongoing uncertainty.
* **Decoupled Central Banks:** The Bank of England will base its rate decisions exclusively on UK domestic economic conditions and inflation outlooks, regardless of whether the Federal Reserve hikes rates.

### Committee Governance and Transparency
* **Pluralism and Deliberation:** Bailey defended the Bank of England's nine-member Monetary Policy Committee as a deliberate body designed for diverse viewpoints and rigorous debate.
* **Enhanced Transparency Innovations:** The BOE adopted recommendations from Ben Bernanke, introducing transparency measures such as paragraphs detailing individual members' views alongside a clear majority consensus statement.
* **Rejecting Binary Labels:** Bailey declined to label himself a hawk or a dove, pointing out that second-round inflation effects in the UK remain relatively subdued due to a softening labor market.

### Structural and Fiscal Challenges
* **Balance Sheet Management:** Central bank balance sheet reduction is a complex global challenge; Bailey noted the importance of retaining future policy scope while reducing interest rate risk.
* **Long-Term Macro Pressures:** Developed economies face interconnected pressures outside direct monetary control, including aging populations and the reversal of post-Cold War defense expenditure dividends, which are impacting fiscal policy and debt levels.

### Notable Quotes
> "Monetary policy by its nature is in the business of making policy forwards. We can't influence what inflation is gonna be tomorrow. We can influence what inflation is gonna be at points further into the future." — **Andrew Bailey**

> "The whole reason we have nine members on the committee is to have a plurality, to have different views, to have deliberation, which is absolutely key to this process." — **Andrew Bailey**

> "If you get the demand shocks ahead of the supply shocks, then you will get pressure on inflation upwards... If you get the supply shocks first, you'll get the opposite." — **Andrew Bailey**

### TL;DR
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh used his Jackson Hole address to outline a framework for monetary policy centered on humility, reduced forward guidance, and vigilance against persistent inflation. While praising the economy's resilience and AI-driven capital expenditure, Warsh stressed that current inflation readings remain too high and that the Fed's primary focus must remain firmly on price stability.

### Artificial Intelligence and Economic Growth
* **Rapid Technological Progress:** AI adoption has outpaced evangelist predictions, driven by scaling laws and significant capital deployment into related infrastructure.
* **Token Sales and Market Activity:** Annualized token sales for the two leading labs exceed **$100 billion**, representing a **500% increase** from 12 months prior.
* **Productivity and Job Task Force:** The Fed has established a task force on productivity and jobs to study AI's long-term impacts on the economy, labor, and capital intensity.

### Monetary Policy Principles and Forward Guidance
* **Abandoning Routine Forward Guidance:** Warsh criticized routine forward guidance as having outlived its usefulness since the global financial crisis, arguing that overcommitting to future decisions inhibits the Fed's freedom to act.
* **Avoiding the "Hall of Mirrors":** Warning against circular dependencies between the Fed and financial markets, Warsh emphasized that policymakers must rely on unfiltered market signals rather than letting markets trade primarily on Fed guidance.
* **Core Operating Principles:** Warsh outlined foundational rules for the central bank, asserting that the **2% PCE inflation target** is a firm, fixed mandate, that "money matters" in monetary policy, and that unconventional crisis tools should be used sparingly.

### Current Economic Assessment
* **Robust Private Investment:** Business CAPEX is rising rapidly, with the four-quarter change in investment for equipment and intangibles hitting **9%** (its highest growth rate since 2021), more than half of which is attributable to AI infrastructure.
* **Corporate Profits and Equities:** S&P company profits have grown by more than **20%** over the past year with elevated profit margins, while equity volatility remains low.
* **Strong Private Domestic Purchases:** Real consumer spending has increased by more than **2%** over the past four quarters, and private domestic financial purchases have risen at a pace of **3%** so far in the year.
* **Labor Market Stability:** The unemployment rate stands at **4.1%**, and unemployment claims on a four-week moving average are near multi-decade lows, indicating full employment alongside low turnover.

### Inflation and Price Stability Concerns
* **Elevated Headline and Core Metrics:** The 12-month change in the PCE price index stands at **3.7%**, with the six-month change slightly above **4%**, both remaining well above the Fed's 2% target.
* **Component-Level Disaggregation:** Over the past 12 months, **54%** of goods and services in the PCE basket showed price increases above **3%**, down from post-pandemic highs of **77%** but still elevated compared to historical averages.
* **Anchored Expectations:** Medium-term inflation expectations and market-based compensation measures appear stable and well-anchored, though Warsh cautioned that these metrics can shift rapidly.
