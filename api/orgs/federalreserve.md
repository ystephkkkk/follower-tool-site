**TL;DR**  
While AI is currently driving a technology-led U.S. growth boom (accounting for 1.5 percentage points of the 2.1% real GDP growth), its transformative productivity "magic" has not yet materialized in aggregate data, and overlapping supply shocks—including AI-related price surges—have kept inflation above target for nearly six years, requiring further monetary policy tightening.

### Current Economic Impact of AI: Growth, Productivity, and Inflation
* **Technology-Led Growth Boom:** Real GDP is growing at 2.1% over a four-quarter/12-month change, but without technology goods, growth would be just 0.6%.
  * **Sustained Pipeline:** Data center construction shows an upward trajectory in 2026 with a large pipeline of planned projects, ensuring that related spending on materials, goods, and software will continue to support growth even if enthusiasm cools.
* **Mixed Productivity Evidence:** Labor productivity growth in 2023–2026 has exceeded the previous decade's 1.5% historical average, but Total Factor Productivity (TFP)—the sustainable "magic" behind transformative tech—has cooled to average or slightly below average after being strong in 2023–2024.
  * **Firm-Level Reality:** A survey by Nick Bloom and his team shows that over the past three years, firms have not yet seen a broad AI impact on productivity growth, though green shoots exist in specific industries.
* **AI-Driven Price Pressures:** After two to three decades of deflation/disinflation in technology goods, prices have reversed sharply to 15.7% (nearly 16%) inflation due to demand outstripping supply.
  * **Modest Headline Spillover:** AI/tech goods currently contribute a modest 0.2 percentage points directly to overall headline inflation, meaning other variables are still doing most of the driving.
  * **Energy & Infrastructure:** Power-hungry data centers are creating a demand shock on top of existing community energy needs, raising electricity and input costs for businesses that must either absorb margin hits or pass prices on to consumers.

### Monetary Policy & Financial Conditions
* **Rethinking Supply Shocks:** Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco President and CEO Mary C. Daly noted that while textbook monetary policy looks through supply shocks (since policy takes 12 to 18 months to take effect), the U.S. is facing three overlapping shocks—tariffs, war/energy prices, and AI—on top of inflation that has been above target for almost six years.
* **Inflation Expectations & Sticky Services:** Medium- and longer-run inflation expectations remain anchored around 2%, but Daly emphasized that price pressures are bleeding into sticky services inflation. She supported the September rate increase and maintains a bias toward further tightening.
* **Financial Conditions Alignment:** SIEPR Director Neale Mahoney noted the 10-year Treasury yield crossed 5% this morning. Daly welcomed tighter financial conditions as complementary to the Fed's goals, noting markets are reacting appropriately to incoming data, though the Fed retains the ultimate responsibility for price stability and full employment.

### Labor Market Dynamics & The Early-Career Slowdown
* **Low Hiring, Low Firing:** Aggregate labor market data shows a stable unemployment rate consistent with the natural rate, characterized by low hiring and low firing rather than widespread AI-driven layoffs.
* **Disproportionate Stress on Young Workers:** Unemployment rates for recent college graduates are rising relative to all workers—an unusual trend—and an Anthropic survey confirms younger and early-career workers are significantly more worried about AI than senior staff.
  * **Why Firms Are "Going Skinny":** Daly's employer networks report holding off on hiring ("going skinny") because they are revamping workflows (e.g., accounting) and do not yet know what skill mixes they will need (such as a CPA with cybersecurity skills). Employers also need senior staff who can evaluate whether AI outputs are correct, a role newly minted graduates cannot easily fill.
* **Medium-Run Labor Scenarios:** Mahoney outlined two competing scenarios: a benign transition where job changes get absorbed in normal labor market churn, versus a worst-case "hollowing out" where a recession triggers structural job losses—noting that historically, nearly 90% of routine jobs were lost during recessions or the six months afterward.
  * **Demographic Accelerants:** Daly highlighted a third scenario: firms automated in response to the post-pandemic labor shortage, and with U.S. labor force growth projected to hit zero by 2030 without immigration, employers are using AI to maintain output with fewer workers.

### Market Valuations, Private Sector Discipline & Research Needs
* **Orderly Valuation Repricing:** Addressing recent volatility where the "Magnificent 7" dropped relative to the S&P 493, Daly described it as an "orderly" repricing of risk rather than an unwinding of AI's underlying value or investment, noting she sees no "burgeoning signs of fragility."
* **Private Sector Cost Discipline:** Market forces are beginning to curb AI-related inflation. The rising share of tokens processed by less-expensive open-weight models on OpenRouter has correlated with token price reductions from OpenAI and Anthropic. Non-tech firms are also extending chip depreciation cycles beyond two years or redesigning products to use fewer/older chips.
* **What the Fed Needs from Researchers:** Daly urged economists to study how overlapping relative price shocks become sticky, to conduct firm-level case studies on how AI changes business processes (similar to how the unit motor transformed manufacturing during electrification), and to focus on proactive training/policy solutions—comparing U.S. worker pessimism to the less pessimistic views in the EU cited by the ECB's Philip Lane.

**TL;DR:** Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller warned that inflation is broadening beyond one-off shocks and becoming entrenched near 3%, arguing the Fed must be more aggressive—potentially delivering a 50-basis-point rate hike in December—and use clearer forward guidance to break firms' pricing psychology.

### Inflation Outlook & Broadening Price Pressures
* **Persistent, Broadening Inflation:** Waller expressed strong concern that inflation has remained above the Fed's 2% target for over five years and is now spreading across the economy rather than staying confined to a few categories.
  * **Core Services:** Roughly 70% of core services items—representing about 50% to 55% of the overall CPI basket—are rising at rates above 3%, unaffected by tariffs or oil prices.
  * **Recent CPI Readings:** Monthly CPI inflation hit 0.3% for the third consecutive month, which annualizes to roughly 3.5% rather than moving closer to 2%.
* **Pricing Psychology & Calendar Effects:** Firms are increasingly passing through 3% price increases because consumers remain resilient, creating a risk that 3% becomes the accepted norm.
  * Waller noted that Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) seasonal adjustments have been unsatisfactory for several years, raising concern that inflation will not fall off at the start of the year as it historically has.
* **Supply Shocks:** While Waller defended initially looking through tariffs as a one-off shock (akin to a 1% sales tax), he noted that tariff pass-throughs have been drawn out amid uncertainty from a Supreme Court decision, and two separate oil shocks (last spring and this fall, including gas price spikes from the Iran war) have kept energy prices elevated.

### Rate Hike Strategy & Forward Guidance
* **Case for a 50-Basis-Point Hike:** Following his speech last week, Waller reiterated that the Fed needs to consider a 50-basis-point hike in December if it cannot credibly signal a follow-up hike in January.
  * **25 vs. 50 bps:** Doing 25 bps in December and 25 bps in January only achieves the same market pricing effect if the Fed explicitly signals the January move; without that signal, Waller favors going 50 bps in December to lock in the tightening.
* **Rate Path:** To get inflation under control, Waller stated the Fed must first take back the three "insurance cuts" made in 2025, and will likely need to go beyond three hikes because the summer 2025 policy rate was not bringing inflation down.
* **Communication & Forward Guidance:** Steve Liesman pressed Waller on whether the Fed is hobbling itself by moving away from forward guidance and shrinking the FOMC statement to around or under 130 words.
  * Waller emphasized that 30 years of central banking literature supports using communication as a policy tool—whether through the statement or the Chair's press conference—to get markets to help the Fed do its job.
  * **December SEP Limitation:** Unlike the September Summary of Economic Projections (SEP), which clearly signaled two or three hikes over the next few months, the December SEP will only show rate expectations for the end of 2027, offering no signal for January, March, or the first half of the year.

### AI Boom, Anthropic IPO & Financial Stability
* **No AI Bubble:** Ahead of Anthropic's IPO pricing and market debut—one of the largest IPOs in history—Liesman asked whether the AI build-out signals a market bubble.
  * Waller described himself as an "AI optimist," arguing AI is a fundamental, transformative technology rather than a "2000s-era sock puppet selling dog food."
  * He cited AI's ability to solve the Navier-Stokes fluid dynamics problem, noting that PhD mathematicians are facing existential questions about their roles.
* **Systemic Risk & Bank Exposure:** Addressing concerns from Friday's Financial Stability Report regarding circular financing and debt in the AI build-out, Waller said equity or debt losses by individual firms are normal market functioning.
  * However, he warned that the Fed is closely monitoring bank exposure to leveraged lending to ensure banks do not become overextended to AI firms and face solvency risks if some companies fail.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **December Rate Action:** The Fed should execute a 50-basis-point rate hike in December if it does not clearly signal to markets that it will follow a 25-basis-point December hike with another 25-basis-point hike in January — *Christopher Waller*.
* **Total Tightening Needed:** The Fed will have to reverse the three insurance cuts from 2025 and likely raise rates even further ("three hikes plus maybe some more") to bring inflation back down — *Christopher Waller*.
* **Anthropic IPO:** Anthropic will price its IPO today and begin trading tomorrow in one of the largest IPOs in history — *Steve Liesman*.

### TL;DR
Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh and tech CEOs Max Levchin (Affirm), Patrick Collison (Stripe), and Nikesh Arora (Palo Alto Networks) argue that superintelligence (SI) is poised to break decades of secular stagnation by automating idea generation, with the U.S. holding a decisive lead in both model creation and enterprise diffusion. While frontier companies are already seeing 5x engineering productivity gains and a 2x surge in new business formation, the next two to three years will require a massive cybersecurity and infrastructure rebuild as AI exposes latent software vulnerabilities and reshapes labor markets.

### The Fed's Economic Framework & Contingency Planning
* **Ideas as the Ultimate Resource:** Drawing on endogenous growth theory (Paul Romer, Chad Jones), Fed Chair Kevin Warsh noted that while U.S. research efforts have increased more than 20x since the 1930s, research productivity has fallen by an even larger amount, fueling fears of secular stagnation.
  * Unlike past general-purpose technologies (the internal combustion engine, electricity, transistors), SI could automate and supercharge the creation of new ideas itself, driving sustained above-trend growth.
  * Warsh emphasized that machines cannot replace human judgment, morality, or responsibility: "Humans are the designers and the builders, and humans must be the deciders."
* **Three Illustrative Fed Scenarios:** Emphasizing that the Fed is focused on contingency planning rather than forecasting—having put this task force at the front of the line 5.5 months ago as one of five convened under his chairmanship—Warsh outlined three stylized economic outcomes:
  * **Broadly Shared Gains:** SI boosts total factor productivity across labor and capital, keeping real GDP persistently above its long-term average with full employment.
  * **Labor Market Disruption:** GDP growth rises above average, but widespread automation and labor frictions push unemployment above full-employment levels.
  * **AI Disappointment:** After heavy capital investment and stock market gains, expected returns turn down, equity valuations fall, and the economy slows with rising unemployment and no lasting productivity gains.
* **Deflationary Compute Costs:** Warsh noted that token prices are falling roughly 50% per quarter at an accelerating rate, suggesting that market forces may push the vast majority of the economic surplus toward technology users rather than just model builders.

### Enterprise Adoption & Productivity at the Frontier
* **Engineering & Underwriting Gains (Max Levchin, Affirm):** Affirm has achieved roughly 5x engineering productivity (cutting cost per feature to 1/5th, with expectations to reach 1/10th) with zero AI-related layoffs.
  * Affirm used LLMs to design a new transformer-based underwriting model that outperformed its non-LLM baseline by 80%, translating to roughly $2 billion more in annual loan volume.
  * Levchin argued that AI enables "mass customization," allowing engineers to rescue long-tail ideas from the cutting room floor, though human prioritization remains critical to avoid shipping bugs ahead of high-stakes periods like Black Friday.
* **Startups vs. Incumbents (Patrick Collison, Stripe):** Echoing Paul David’s 1989/1990 "productivity paradox" paper on electricity, Collison noted that new firms are capturing SI gains faster than incumbents encumbered by governance and compliance.
  * The 2026 cohort of new businesses launching on Stripe every week is up approximately 2x year-over-year—an unprecedented jump in Stripe's 15-year history, surpassing even pandemic-era surges.
  * Internally at Stripe, G&A hiring has been substantially reduced as a share of revenue, while R&D/engineering and sales hiring are accelerating because cheaper feature production and higher seller ROI make it mathematically advantageous to buy more.
* **Democratizing Intelligence (Nikesh Arora, Palo Alto Networks):** Arora highlighted that while enterprise ROI dominates current headlines due to the $50 billion cost of training frontier models, AI's broader societal impact is putting "genius in our pocket" at near-zero marginal cost for the 6 billion people globally who lack access to specialized expertise.

### Cybersecurity, Infrastructure & Edge-Case Risks
* **The "Technology Rebuild" Moment:** Both Arora and Collison warned that AI has shifted the asymmetric battle in cybersecurity. Open-source models like Mythos (released six months ago) allow even unsophisticated hackers to rapidly uncover latent bugs in decades-old code.
  * Collison noted that even heavily scrutinized software like the Linux TCP stack has been found to be "riven with bugs" and exploitable issues.
  * Arora expects more frequent cyber events—especially targeting interconnected systems like banking—but noted that AI can also be used to refactor and rewrite legacy code over the next two to three years.
* **The 20% Edge-Case Problem:** Arora cautioned against overestimating near-term autonomous job replacement, citing Waymo: since he rode in Google's first driverless Lexus in 2009, it has taken 17 years (to 2026) and over $30 billion to replace a single human job, largely because handling the final 20% of edge cases is extraordinarily difficult.
* **Market Concentration & "Soft Despotism":**
  * Levchin warned that unlike past tech revolutions, capital-rich incumbents are adopting AI from day one, risking an oligopolistic economy dominated by the Fortune 100 while hollowing out the middle tier (Fortune 10,000), even as solo-preneurs thrive in the long tail.
  * Collison cited Alexis de Tocqueville’s warning of "soft despotism," questioning what human ambition and the nature of work will look like if AI eliminates labor and creates a "suffocating tranquility."

### Global Competition: U.S. vs. The Rest of the World
* **Widening U.S. Lead:** All panelists agreed that the U.S. is in a preeminent position, not just in building models (centered in the Bay Area), but in diffusing them.
  * **Collison:** Stripe data shows AI usage intensity is higher in the U.S. than elsewhere, widening the economic divergence with Europe.
  * **Levchin:** The European Union is still "a couple of meta levels away" from accepting AI's reality, focusing on regulation while the U.S. and China race ahead on energy and compute investments.
  * **Arora:** Few countries can afford $50 billion model runs, meaning intelligence will be built in a few global hubs, while the U.S. captures the fastest enterprise margin and competitiveness gains.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Drop-in remote workers:** "Many people in the AI sector expect drop-in remote workers within the next 12 months"—autonomous agents on Slack and Zoom capable of doing anything a human with a monitor and keyboard can do (Patrick Collison).
* **Global energy bet:** Two influential San Francisco AI figures have a bet on whether global energy use will double in a single month within the next 10 years (cited by Patrick Collison).
* **Technology rebuild:** The next two to three years will be a "technology rebuild moment" as companies use AI to find and fix decades of legacy software vulnerabilities (Nikesh Arora).
* **Engineering cost floor:** Affirm expects its cost per engineering feature to fall from 1/5th of its pre-AI baseline to 1/10th (Max Levchin).
* **Gradual reskilling:** Job displacement will not arrive as a sudden shock, but as a gradual reskilling where entire job categories quietly disappear over a 10-year horizon (Nikesh Arora).

### Monetary Policy Stance & Economic Conditions
* **Interest Rate Decision:** 
  * **Current Rate:** Maintained at 3.75% to 4% to support the Fed's dual mandate of maximum employment and stable prices.
  * **Timing on Rates:** Chairman Worsch emphasized that quashing inflation is not about a fixed calendar date or an isolated data point, but rather about ensuring clear objectives and evaluating incoming data.
* **Economic Activity & Growth:** 
  * **GDP Revisions:** First-half GDP growth was revised up from 1.8% to 2.4%, with third-quarter growth projected to run above 3% led by consumer spending.
  * **Business Investment:** Capital expenditures are at a historically high share of GDP, running at a double-digit pace with high-tech investment leading the acceleration.
* **Labor Market Resilience:** 
  * **Employment Metrics:** The jobless rate is lower than a year ago, payroll growth is noticeably above last year's pace, and the 4-week moving average of unemployment insurance claims is at the lowest level in generations.

### Inflation & Price Stability
* **Inflation Status:** 
  * **Persistent Pressures:** Inflation has been running above the 2% objective for nearly 6 years, with essentially no recent progress toward the target.
  * **Sector Pressures:** Energy and related prices have moved higher recently; the Fed's job is to ensure relative price changes do not broaden across the economy and that inflation expectations remain anchored.
* **Financial & Credit Conditions:** 
  * **Tighter Conditions:** Following the September rate action, financial and credit conditions have tightened for some businesses and households.
  * **Market Adjustments:** Market participants are becoming more discerning in capital allocations, and inflation compensation measures are moving in the right direction.

### Fed Governance & Meeting Schedule Changes
* **Meeting Schedule Overhaul (Starting 2027):** 
  * **Meeting Frequency:** Transitioning to 8 meetings per year.
  * **2027 Format:** One meeting will focus exclusively on broad monetary policy strategy with no rate vote or policy statement anticipated.
  * **2028 Format:** Two meetings dedicated to strategic discussions and six meetings for setting policy.
  * **Strategic Rationale:** Designed to reserve time and space for strategic deliberations, thinking anew about big questions, and looking over longer time horizons.
* **Internal Governance & Independence:** 
  * **Individual Policymakers:** Chairman Worsch noted he does not submit an economic projection dot, but 18 independent colleagues do.
  * **No Political Influence:** Worsch stated that politics, elections, and the upcoming midterm elections played zero role in the FOMC's decision.

### Task Forces, Research, & Technology
* **Working Groups Status:** 
  * **Task Force Deliverables:** Five task forces are working on AI, data, balance sheets, productivity, and jobs, with a year-end deadline expected to be met by some groups.
  * **Balance Sheet:** Recommendations from the Balance Sheet Task Force are expected to feed into the FOMC likely early in the first quarter of 2027.
* **Productivity & Jobs Task Force:** 
  * **Field Hearing:** A public field hearing is scheduled for November 16, lasting 3 to 4 hours, to examine near-, medium-, and long-term implications of new technology on jobs and the economy.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Meeting Schedule Execution:** Chairman Worsch expects to hold one meeting in 2027 dedicated exclusively to broad monetary policy strategy with no rate vote or policy statement, and two strategy-dedicated meetings in 2028 out of six total meetings — Chairman Worsch.
* **Balance Sheet Recommendations Timeline:** Recommendations from the Balance Sheet Task Force will likely be fed into the FOMC early in the first quarter [of 2027] — Chairman Worsch.
