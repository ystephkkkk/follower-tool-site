## How To Build A Jet Engine From Scratch

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ieJwYq4j-o
- Published: 2026-11-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261124.html#video--ieJwYq4j-o
- Speakers: Jared Friedman (Host, YC Hard Tech); Blake Scholl (Founder and CEO, Boom Supersonic)

---

**TL;DR:** After Rolls-Royce abruptly walked away from building the engine for Boom Supersonic’s 60-to-80-seat Overture airliner, Boom built its own engine core from scratch—and discovered it can first be deployed as a 42-megawatt natural-gas turbine for AI data centers, generating the cash flow and 1 million hours of operating data needed to fund and certify supersonic flight.

### From Crisis to Dual-Use Engine
* **The Rolls-Royce Breakup:** In September 2022, Rolls-Royce abruptly walked away from Boom’s engine partnership without warning, just as Boom had rolled out its XB-1 demonstrator and signed up United and American Airlines.
  * Founder and CEO Blake Scholl described the breakup as "the startup equivalent of a stage four cancer diagnosis," noting Boom could not raise a dollar or hold customer/supplier conversations overnight.
  * Boom launched a crash program in December 2022 to build its own engine, firing up its first full-scale engine core on November 21, 2026.
* **The AI Power Pivot (Superpower):** In 2025, after XB-1 became the first independently developed jet to break the sound barrier, Scholl noticed 5-to-10-year grid interconnect queues and 5-year wait times for GE power turbines.
  * Scholl texted day-one Boom investor Sam Altman asking if a 40-megawatt-class natural gas turbine was relevant to AI; Altman replied: "yes, please. As many as you can make."
  * By removing the fan and adding a generator, Boom’s shared core converts from 40,000 lbs of thrust at Mach 1.7 (for Overture's **Symphony** engine) into 42 megawatts of clean electricity (for the **Superpower** turbine).
* **Funding & Certifying Overture:** Selling turbines first allows Boom to self-fund Overture without raising billions from investors.
  * Making just 2 turbines a month creates a "very profitable multi-billion dollar business," and the day Boom ships its first unit, the company flips cash flow positive.
  * Jet engines are typically tested for ~5,000 hours before certification; running 24/7 in data centers will give Boom 1 million hours of real-world operating data to present to the FAA.

### The Turbine Supply Bottleneck
* **Massive Unmet Demand:** Host Jared Friedman (Y Combinator) notes the US needs ~100 GW of new generating capacity over the next 10 years—roughly $300 billion worth of Superpower turbines.
  * Boom’s original plan was to produce 1 GW of turbines per year, but a single prospective customer wants to deploy 1.6 GW every 30 days.
* **Incumbent Complacency:** Scholl attributes the 5-year backlog (with GE gas turbines sold out through 2030) to $1–$2 trillion industrial incumbents founded over a century ago that refuse to invest in capacity because their prices and margins have tripled and Wall Street might punish capex.
* **Industrial Stagnation Since 1973:** Scholl traces US industrial decay to 1973, the same year the US banned supersonic flight and halted the 6% annual compounded growth in electricity per capita that had held since 1882; since 2000, power production per capita has declined.

### Vertical Integration at the Superpower Superfactory
* **Buying the Printer Over the Parts:** When suppliers quoted Boom an 18-month lead time and $1.3 million for one ship set of 3D-printed turbine blades, Scholl discovered the printer itself cost $1.1 million with a 2-week lead time—so Boom bought the printer.
* **The South Denver Superfactory:** Boom leased a nearly 200,000-square-foot facility in South Denver in January, building a vertically integrated factory from raw materials (stainless steel castings, nickel super alloy forgings) to completed turbines under one roof.
  * Co-locating machinists next to designers eliminates supply chain delays; during Sprint Core assembly, Boom’s machine shop fabricated a custom wrench overnight.
* **All-Digital Single-Crystal Foundry:** Turbine blades must spin at 10,000 RPM at 3,000°F continuously for 3 years, requiring single-crystal blades (one perfect grid of atoms, like a diamond) currently made by only three companies worldwide.
  * Boom is building its own all-digital single-crystal foundry next door to design a blade on Monday, cast it in the afternoon, and test it on Tuesday.

### Software-Driven Engineering & Small Teams
* **Every Hardware Team Has a Coder:** Boom embeds a software engineer on every hardware team to automate workflows, and "every person at Boom down to the receptionist is a coder in the AI-enabled sense."
* **BladeRunner Software:** Boom built a custom tool called BladeRunner with its own CAD kernel and GPU cluster.
  * One engineer can drag a slider and view real-time aerodynamics and structural behavior—compressing work that typically takes a team of 100 engineers 3 to 6 months into real time.
* **Small, Elite Headcount:** Boom currently employs 165 people (~40 in manufacturing, ~125 in engineering and everything else), following the model of the 50-person team that built XB-1.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **First Turbine Shipments:** Boom will begin shipping Superpower turbines in mid-2027 (*Jared Friedman*).
* **Cash Flow Positive:** The day Boom ships its first Superpower turbine, the company will flip cash flow positive (*Blake Scholl*).
* **In-House Manufacturing:** Boom expects to manufacture 85% of every Superpower turbine in-house by weight (*Blake Scholl*).
* **Headcount Cap:** Outside of manufacturing, Boom will never exceed 250 employees (*Blake Scholl*).
