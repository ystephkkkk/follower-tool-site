**TL;DR**
At SC26, NVIDIA VP and GM of Hyperscale and HPC Ian Buck unveiled a full-stack push into "agentic AI for science," anchored by the open **Nemotron for Science** model, the new **Rubin HPC** GPU roadmap (coming 2028), and desktop-to-exascale tools like the **DGX Spark HPC Toolkit** and **Vera CPU**. Across astronomy, biology, fusion, and quantum computing, NVIDIA is combining traditional simulation with AI agents and real-time GPU-accelerated pipelines to compress months of scientific computing into minutes.

---

### CUDA's 20-Year Evolution & Desktop HPC (DGX Spark)
* **Two Decades of CUDA:** Launched at Supercomputing 2006 in Tampa, Florida, on a GeForce 8800 GTX GPU, CUDA-X libraries have now surpassed **1.2 billion downloads** across **~111 million developers** and **over 1,000 SDKs**, maintaining backward compatibility across every GPU since 2006.
* **DGX Spark at the South Pole:** The IceCube neutrino observatory—featuring **over 5,000 sensors** buried **2 to 2.5 km** deep in South Pole ice—generates **~1 TB of data per day**. Researchers (led by 2026 Physics Nobel laureate Prof. Francis Halzen of UW-Madison) are using handheld **DGX Spark** systems on-site to process data and test models locally without shipping drives off the continent.
* **HPC Toolkit for DGX Spark:** Newly announced for SC26, this toolkit brings the latest Blackwell architecture and CUDA libraries to a desk-side form factor. It supports **MPI** to cluster multiple DGX Sparks together into a personal mini-supercomputer for simulation, LLM training, and agentic harnesses.

### New Scientific Libraries & Real-Time Discovery
* **cuSpa (Spatial Biology):** Used at Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center to reconstruct **~1.2 billion RNA strands** across **~700,000 cells** in a 1 sq. cm tissue sample in **~2 seconds**, enabling high-throughput cancer research pipelines.
* **Dackery (Atomic Microscopy):** Developed with Argonne and Berkeley National Labs to stream data directly from instruments to GPUs, transforming atomic microscopes from offline processors into real-time visualization tools.
* **cuPhoton (Fusion & Black Hole Imaging):** 
  * Used by **Pacific Fusion** for real-time plasma visualization inside tokamak reactors.
  * Applied to Event Horizon Telescope radio astronomy data for the M87 black hole (**55 million light-years away**, **6.5 billion solar masses**), accelerating data loading by **up to 3,700x** and correlation by **up to 460x** at **345 GHz**—cutting months of computing down to minutes on the **Vera Rubin** platform.
* **Gordon Bell Highlights:**
  * **Jülich & KIT:** Built a **4-exaflop**, **2.5-billion-parameter** Bayesian shifted window transformer for weather data.
  * **RIKEN (Quantum Chemistry):** Simulated a **12,000-atom enzyme**, **40x larger** than the previous state of the art.
  * **RIKEN & Astro:** Ran a **4.4-trillion-particle** Milky Way formation simulation (at 4.5 billion years old) using **16,000 GH200s** at nearly **1 exaflop**.

### Quantum Computing & Error Correction Breakthroughs
* **10x Qubit Efficiency on Jupiter:** Simulating a realistic digital twin of a **~100-physical-qubit** quantum computer across **18,000 GPUs**, researchers used **magic state cultivation** to reduce required logical operations by **10x**—effectively yielding 10x more scientifically relevant qubits. This capability is being integrated into **CUDA-Q**.
* **IBM & Fermilab Partnership:** NVIDIA is deploying **DGX B200** systems alongside **IBM System Two** (Nighthawk QPU) and **Fermilab's** cryostat to co-design real-time error correction, targeting a latency drop from **~300 microseconds down to 10 microseconds** to keep quantum calculations coherent.
* **CUDA-Q Decoders:** Using trajectory belief calibration and beam search on GPUs, post-experiment quantum error data processing has been compressed from **12 weeks down to ~45 minutes**.

### Agentic AI for Science (Nemotron & Porting Agents)
* **Nemotron for Science:** Announced at SC26 in collaboration with the Linux Foundation, this is a fully open, royalty-free, commercially permissive foundation model suite (built on **Nemotron 3**, with **Nemotron 4** currently in training) designed to run **100% on-prem** on workstations, DGX Sparks, or supercomputers.
* **Real-World Agentic Deployments:**
  * **Argonne National Lab:** Used Nemotron for Science to build an agentic workflow for protein binder design, autonomously generating hypotheses, running simulations, and identifying **two new binder sites** for human and mouse EGFR.
  * **Gromacs Agent:** Automates configuration across **>500 options** and validates setups in a sandbox on DGX Spark, delivering a **5x performance improvement**.
  * **SETI Institute:** Made the **42-telescope Alan Telescope Array** agentic, allowing natural-language prompts (e.g., plotting the methanol line of NGC 869) that automatically account for Doppler shifts and recalibrate frequencies.
* **Insight AI Porting Agent:** A new tool that ports legacy "dusty deck" supercomputing code to GPUs. Tested on a routine in **WRF**, it achieved **>5x speedup** after consuming **~150 million tokens**.

### Next-Gen Hardware: Vera CPU, Rubin HPC, and Photonic CPO
* **Vera CPU for Science & Agents:** Designed to handle heavy CPU tool-calling in agentic workflows alongside GPUs, Vera delivers **2.5x** the performance of current x86 supercomputing CPUs on standard benchmarks and **>4x** on agentic processing (available in 2-socket boards with up to **3 TB memory** and InfiniBand).
  * **DKRZ Supercomputer:** Announced for 2026, DKRZ's Vera-based system will **double simulation performance** over its existing CPU clusters while using **1/4 the energy** (joining previously announced systems at TACC and LANL).
* **Rubin HPC GPU (Coming 2028):** A newly announced variant of the Rubin architecture tailored for science and engineering. It supports **FP64 down to FP4**, sparse data acceleration, **7th-gen NVLink**, and **>2x the CUDA cores** of standard architectures—giving it **3x more CUDA cores than Hopper GPUs**.
* **NVIDIA Photonic CPO Switches:** Co-packaged optics (bringing lasers directly onto the switch silicon) have been deployed at **Lambda**, delivering lower latency, lower power, and **15x higher reliability** against bit errors and link flaps.

### The Activist Playbook for Reinstating Jensen Huang

* **Workforce & IP Security:** With young AI companies growing rapidly, the Director urged CEOs to take security seriously and protect frontier model weights—calling them some of the most valuable assets in the world.

* **Unprecedented Market Shifts:** Gurley describes the past few weeks—encompassing Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia, Anthropic's historic IPO, and Washington D.C.'s deepening involvement—as unprecedented.
* **Regulatory Capture:** Anthropic's efforts to seek regulatory capture are in a stronger position than they were two weeks ago, driven by China hawks stepping forward in Washington.

### The Battle for Open-Source AI
* **Open Source as Defense:** Gurley compares Nvidia's open-source strategy to Google's past defensive plays—launching **Android** to prevent an Apple mobile monopoly and **Kubernetes** to counter Amazon AWS—noting that not all CEOs understand how to execute this.
* **Can Google Carry the Torch?** Despite having open-weight models, Android, and **TPUs**, Google has not shown the same execution around open-source AI as Nvidia did with Numetron and Hugging Face. Gurley says it would require Google switching into a "new gear" to counter **OpenAI** and **Anthropic** potentially achieving escape velocity.
* **Startups & Coalition Building:** Independent open-source startups backed by Nvidia—such as **Reflection** and **Thinking Machines**—struggle to fight on an island.
  * If **Google**, **Meta** (which previously pivoted away from **Llama**), Reflection, and Thinking Machines collaborated on a shared platform with the **Linux Foundation**—sharing weights and code development—they would have a **10X higher chance of success**.
* **Monetization vs. Regulation:** Because the marginal cost of software is zero, open-source models naturally drive prices toward zero, making them harder to monetize than proprietary products. However, Gurley argues this is a competitive dynamic (used by challengers like China) and should never be used as a regulatory argument to restrict open source.

### The "Bank of Nvidia" & Circular Deal Concerns
* **Aggressive Deal Terms:** *Wall Street Journal* reporting over the past three days points to internal concern at Nvidia over how aggressive marginal deal terms became in building out the **Neocloud** space.
* **Q2 Financial Red Flags:** Nvidia's Q2 numbers showed accounts receivable (AR) increasing dramatically while free cash flow fell, consistent with aggressive financing. Investors will be watching Nvidia's earnings call tomorrow to see if this trend continued.
* **The Circularity Conundrum:** Gurley notes a contradiction in the bull case: if Nvidia's deals were not circular and simply reflected real market demand, there would be no concern about them going away; fearing their disappearance implies they were "juicing revenue."
* **Industry-Wide Accounting:** While companies should not use their balance sheets to drive their income statements, Gurley notes that Google, Amazon, and Microsoft engaged in similar practices, and he believes the entire dynamic went too far.

### Hyperscaler CapEx & Market Risk
* **Exhausting Cash Flows:** Hyperscalers have poured hundreds of billions of dollars into AI CapEx, exhausting their free cash flow. Gurley highlights a chart showing Google's free cash flow rising steadily for 25–30 years before turning negative over the past two years—a trend seen across all major players except Apple.
* **Turning to Debt and Equity:** Beyond burning cash flow, tech giants have tapped debt markets, used off-balance-sheet commitments (such as a deal by Meta), and, in Google's case, tapped equity markets.
* **Pushed to the Edge:** While bulls point to Anthropic's miraculous growth rate as proof of real demand, Deirdre and Gurley highlight the risk of a timing mismatch between supply and demand (referencing Dario Amodei's "cone of uncertainty") and point to the **$900 billion in commitments** in Anthropic's S-1 as the single biggest concern.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Accountant Pushback:** "I suspect when this is all over, the accountants will come to the table and suggest they should have been more restrictive of what was happening." — Bill Gurley
* **Nvidia Earnings Watch:** Analysts and investors will be closely watching Nvidia's earnings call tomorrow to see whether the Q2 spike in accounts receivable and drop in free cash flow continued. — Bill Gurley

  * **Market Appetite:** Altman dismissed concerns about OpenAI being the "Lyft to [Anthropic's] Uber," noting that over the past year there has been "way too much investor demand and not enough supply."

### Supply Chain, Memory Shortages, and Data Center Opposition
* **Chip "Double Counting":** Altman clarified his closed-door comments to Samsung executives, explaining that when there was only one main chip provider, wafer demand was easy to gauge. Now, multiple chipmakers are talking to the silicon supply chain assuming OpenAI, Anthropic, and others will buy *all* of their chips.
* **Shifting Bottlenecks:** OpenAI's supply chain constraints constantly shift between memory, rack assembly, data center capacity, and random components like PCB laminate.
* **The Memory (DRAM) Squeeze:** Addressing backlash over his comments on home computer RAM, Altman said he wants people to have RAM for home tinkering, but noted that years ago he warned the world needed $7 trillion in capex for the chip and memory supply chain.
  * Because the industry didn't build enough—due to decades of boom-and-bust cycles where memory makers "invest $20 billion and make $19 billion"—DRAM prices have jumped roughly 5x to 7x.
* **Data Center Backlash & Debt:** Following a "blue wave" where all but one data center referendum bill passed, Altman attributed voter opposition to a terrible industry sales pitch ("you are going to live like a Labrador") and deep-seated fears over jobs and human agency.
  * He defended debt-funded data center buildouts by pointing to unprecedented actual revenue growth and great margins, noting that businesses are actively coming to OpenAI after seeing what AI can do for their products.

### AI Safety, Recursive Self-Improvement, and Politics
* **"Pragmatic Centrist" Security:** Addressing corporate espionage—including a hidden microphone placed in his office and foreign agents targeting engineers—Altman described OpenAI's approach to security and safety as "pragmatic centrists." They neither build in total secret nor treat AI like a normal tech rollout.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** OpenAI is using its current models to develop new models faster, but is *not* pushing a button for unmonitored RSI. Altman stated OpenAI is holding back on pushing further into RSI as a tactic to ensure humans stay in control of the future.
* **External Evaluators:** OpenAI is working with embedded external evaluators, including METR (referenced as "Meter" in the transcript), to get outside opinions on system safety and robustness.
* **Political Engagement:** Altman reiterated his July 2025 comment about feeling "politically homeless," saying he hopes both parties move toward a culture of innovation. He noted he last spoke with Donald Trump at the China State Dinner.

### Products, Data Lock-In, and AI for Science
* **Dots & Data Portability:** When asked by moderator Reed Albergotti about data lock-in for "Dots" (a cloud-based memory product), Altman agreed in principle that users should be able to view, modify, and export their data (as they can with ChatGPT memory). However, he noted that Dots' nuanced understanding of a user's life is harder to export than an iPhone camera roll or iMessages.
