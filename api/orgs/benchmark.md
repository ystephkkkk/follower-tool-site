## Bill Gurley: Searching for Feynman

- Channel: All-In Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A4Q7zAayW20

### TL;DR
Bill Gurley examines historical catastrophes (like the 737 MAX, Fukushima, and Challenger) to demonstrate how society successfully investigates failures through independent searchers and root-cause analysis—and contrasts this with the systemic failure to uncover the origins of COVID-19.

### Catastrophic Investigations & The CAPA Framework
- **The Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) Framework:** A five-step scientific process used in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and aerospace (Detect, Investigate, Confirm root cause, Correct, Prevent) where skipping root-cause analysis makes prevention impossible.
- **Surfside, Florida Condo Collapse (June 2021):** 
  - **Casualties & Cost:** 98 deaths.
  - **Investigation:** Four parallel investigations, including NIST, Alan Kilsheimer, a Florida grand jury, 17 consolidated victim lawsuits, and the *Miami Herald* (staffing 37 people, winning a Pulitzer Prize).
  - **Outcome:** Led to major rule changes in Florida and coastal states to prevent future collapses.
- **Boeing 737 MAX Crashes (Oct 2018 / Mar 2019):**
  - **Root Cause:** A fuel-efficient engine was attached forward on an older airframe, causing an imbalance; secret software called MCAS pushed the plane down during sensor malfunctions.
  - **Investigation:** Six search groups included US Senate/House commissions, the DOJ, Indonesia's KNKT, *Seattle Times* reporter Dominic Gates (Pulitzer winner), 57 whistleblowers, and the pilots' union (who recorded meetings and filed FOIA requests).
  - **Blockers:** Boeing and the FAA acted as blockers, initially blaming pilots and refusing document requests; 45 days after the first crash, the plane was still not grounded, leading to a preventable second crash.
- **Hurricane Katrina (2005):**
  - **Casualties & Cost:** 1,800 deaths, 400,000 displaced, $125 billion in damage.
  - **Root Cause:** Levees washed out from underneath during a Category 3 hurricane due to man-made engineering errors by the US Army Corps of Engineers.
  - **Investigation:** House/Senate commissions, Pulitzer-winning journalists (Mark Schleifstein and John McQuaid), NSF-backed UC professors, and an LSU team.
  - **Blockers:** The Army Corps of Engineers attempted to block investigations and encapsulated the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE) after paying them $1 million.
  - **Outcome:** Led to 90 recommendations and $15 billion in sea walls that have held for 20 years.
- **Fukushima Nuclear Disaster (2011):**
  - **Casualties & Cost:** 2,200 deaths, 154,000 displaced, $200 billion in cleanup.
  - **Root Cause:** A seawall of 5.7 meters was overtopped by tsunamis (internal reports showed expected tsunamis of 10-15 meters were buried).
  - **Investigation:** Led by Dr. Kyoshi Kurokawa heading Japan's first-ever private parliamentary commission with zero institutional ties to regulators or the power company.
- **Space Shuttle Challenger Disaster (January 1986):**
  - **Root Cause:** O-rings on solid rocket boosters failed in cold weather.
  - **Searcher:** Caltech physicist Richard Feynman served on the Rogers Commission, cleared his schedule for six months, conducted independent investigations, and famously demonstrated the O-ring failure with ice water on live television.

### COVID-19 Origins & The Absence of Investigation
- **Scale of Impact:** 15 million deaths, $45 trillion in global cost, and severe learning loss for children—making COVID-19 3,000 times larger in cost and death toll than the combined five historical catastrophes ($349B, 5,000 deaths).
- **The Missing Search Infrastructure:** Unlike smaller historical disasters, COVID-19 lacked committed newsrooms with 30+ investigators, bipartisan congressional committees, NIH/NSF-funded third-party scientific investigations, or massive victim lawsuits.
- **The "Blocker" Insiders:** Francis Collins, Anthony Fauci, Peter Daszak (EcoHealth Alliance), Jeremy Farrar (Wellcome Trust/WHO), and Ralph Baric (UNC) acted as blockers due to:
  - Being virology insiders protecting their institution.
  - Leading a misinformation campaign to discourage searching.
  - Being outspoken prior advocates of **Gain-of-Function** research (intentionally making viruses more dangerous).
  - Failing to ever launch a search.
- **The Heroes and Searchers:**
  - **Alina Chan:** Molecular biologist who co-authored a book; wrote two op-eds published by *The New York Times*.
  - **Katherine Eban:** Wrote a foundational *Vanity Fair* article ("The Fight to Discover COVID Origins") detailing the discovery blockade.
  - **Emily Kopp:** Uncovered the majority of FOIA documents working for U.S. Right to Know.
  - **Zeynep Tufekci:** Princeton professor and writer who exposed the coordinated cover-up.
  - **Alison Young:** Author of *Pandora's Gamble*, examining prior lab leak failures.
  - **Drastic:** A loosely affiliated group of independent scientist-researchers.
  - **U.S. Right to Know:** A nonprofit filing FOIA requests to uncover key documents.

### The Misinformation Campaign & The Defuse Proposal
- **Manufactured Consensus:** Insiders leveraged scientific journals to quickly publish papers (such as the *Proximal Origins* paper and *The Lancet* statement) to steer public narrative toward natural spillover and label lab-leak inquiries as conspiracy theories.
- **The Defuse Proposal:** Uncovered by Drastic three years post-outbreak, this proposal by Peter Daszak’s EcoHealth Alliance detailed plans to insert a furin cleavage site at the S1/S2 junction of a bat coronavirus to increase viral infectivity. Major scientific journals and 78% of surveyed scientists were unaware of it.
- **Current Institutional Obstructions:** University of North Carolina (UNC) and the State of North Carolina have refused to deliver documents requested by searchers regarding gain-of-function research.

## Andrew Feldman on Building Cerebras and the Future of Chips | Ep. 57

- Channel: Uncapped with Jack Altman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HFOhG1jH2tk
- Published: 2026-09-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260915.html#video-HFOhG1jH2tk
- Speakers: Andrew Feldman (CEO, Cerebras Systems); Jack Altman (Host, Uncapped); Eric Vishria (General Partner, Benchmark)

---

#### TL;DR
Cerebras Co-Founder and CEO Andrew Feldman and Benchmark Partner Eric Vishria discuss the grueling 10-year journey of building the world's largest AI chip company, the Wafer Scale Engine, overcoming nearly fatal technical hurdles, and rethinking the semiconductor supply chain to tackle incumbent giants like Nvidia.

### The Genesis of Cerebras & The 100x Strategy
* **The 2016 Insight:** As a computer architect, Feldman recognized that AI workloads would be exceptionally compute-intensive, requiring a radically different architecture rather than remodeled existing chips.
* **The Goliath Strategy:** Feldman and Vishria argue that attacking an incumbent like Nvidia with a 10% or 2x better product is a losing game because incumbents possess massive margin flexibility to cut prices and bundle products.
* **The 100x Rule:**
  * **Scale timeline:** A new compute company takes at least 5 years to reach scale.
  * **Incumbent compounding:** If an incumbent doubles every year ($2^5 = 32\text{x}$ over 5 years), a new entrant must target a **100x to 500x performance multiple** to successfully intersect the market ahead of them.
* **Control Your Own Stack:** To achieve radical innovation, Cerebras brought chip, board, system, software, and API development entirely in-house.
  * **No Off-the-Shelf Parts:** Building a chip the size of a dinner plate meant no commercial catalog items (like heat sinks or packaging tools) existed; they had to pioneer world-class packaging through years of failure.

### The Valley of Death: Surviving the 18-Month Nightmare
* **The Boardroom Reality:** For 18 months, Cerebras faced a continuous existential hurdle: burning **$8 million a month** while walking into board meetings every six weeks with the exact same update: *"Still can't make it."*
* **The Mantra:** The team operated on a strict engineering discipline: **"Only new mistakes."** Every failure underwent rigorous analysis so they never repeated the same error twice.
* **The Breakthrough:** In **July 2019**, after months of shattering wafers in seconds, then minutes, a test run maintained a flat temperature in a makeshift lab with a hole drilled in the wall for ventilation—solving a problem that had baffled computer architecture for 75 years.

### Demystifying the Semiconductor Supply Chain
* **The Modern Pyramid (TSMC & ASML):** A cutting-edge fab costs **$40 to $50 billion**, takes 4 to 5 years to build, and utilizes football-field-sized facilities powered by continuous concrete pouring.
* **The Lithography Monopoly:** ASML builds the $500M+ photolithography machines (50–60 feet long, 20 feet high) that etch transistors into silicon wafers—representing a true technological monopoly.
* **The Geopolitical Bottleneck:** Three decades of domestic policy pushed fabs and packaging vendors (like Amcor and ASE) overseas. Feldman advocates for waiving local ordinances to build robust US domestic fab capacity with partners like TSMC, Samsung, and GlobalFoundries.

### Data Center Constraints & Energy Realities
* **The Real Estate Mismatch:** AI moves at the speed of software while data centers move at the speed of real estate, creating a permanent lag.
* **Power and Grid Hurdles:** 
  * The US grid relies on 1940s-1950s technology.
  * Data center expansion is driving intense innovation in backup power, including jet-engine power adaptations (from Boom), fuel cells (Bloom Energy), and renewed interest in nuclear power.
* **The Water Fallacy:** Data centers utilizing closed-loop water systems consume between **4x and 7x less water** than California almond growers.

### Scaling Throughput & Disaggregation
* **Speed Creates Markets:** Just as fast internet transformed Netflix from mailing DVDs into a movie studio, instant frontier intelligence unlocks entirely new applications.
* **Disaggregation Partnerships:** Cerebras leverages its architecture to partner with other chipmakers (such as AMD and AWS), achieving up to **5x additional throughput** while maintaining constant speeds by splitting inference workflows.

> *"We're a professional David in the battle with Goliath and I I wake up every day with that mentality... We're only interested in solving problems that other people can't solve."* — **Andrew Feldman**

- Channel: Invest Like The Best
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0e7aG_MIHlQ

### TL;DR
Eric Vishria, General Partner at Benchmark, explains that the competitive frontier for AI and software has entirely shifted, making adherence to outdated plans value-destructive, while the market's explosive growth is heavily undersized by observers. He analyzes key investments like Cerebras, Fireworks, and Sierra, detailing how hardware complexity, enterprise adoption, and shifting leverage points define the emerging AI era.

### The Shift in Software Value and SaaS Strategy
- **Obsolete Execution:** Every day a legacy SaaS company hits its traditional financial plan, it is actively destroying equity value because the competitive frontier has fundamentally shifted.
- **The Multiple Compression Trap:** While many private and public SaaS companies have grown revenue 4x since 2021, valuation multiples have compressed sixfold (from 30x to roughly 3x to 6x), leaving employees and investors stuck.
- **Inverting the Management Model:** CEOs of large traditional software companies must completely invert their operational focus, spending the vast majority of their time on AI integration rather than treating it as a 5-to-8 PM side project.
- **First-Principles Hiring:** Experienced SaaS sales leaders with quota-capacity mentalities (e.g., $1.5M–$2.5M territory assignments) fail in AI companies because early AI products sell like "magic," with individual reps generating $10M, $30M, or even $50M.

### AI Infrastructure, Fireworks, and the Cloud Analogy
- **Surprising Inference Complexity:** Running multi-trillion parameter models efficiently is exceptionally difficult; open-source models running on Fireworks achieve a **5x performance speedup** and massive throughput advantages over standard cloud providers using identical Nvidia hardware.
- **The AWS Parallels:** In 2007, smart investors gave AWS a 0% probability of being a durable, high-margin business rather than a commodity, completely missing the multi-hundred-billion-dollar application and infrastructure layers (Snowflake, Confluent, Datadog, Databricks) that eventually emerged.
- **Oligopoly Over Monopoly:** Just as the cloud market split into an oligopoly (AWS, Azure, GCP, Cloudflare) rather than a single winner, the AI market is vastly too large for any single vendor to consume entirely.
- **The Energy Bottleneck:** Models translate compute into intelligence with seemingly unlimited demand; however, energy constraints—such as China bringing on 10x more energy capacity than the U.S.—pose the single greatest threat to token supply and pricing.

### Hardware Investing and the Cerebras Story
- **Naive Optimism in Semis:** Benchmark invested in Cerebras in 2016 based on a five-founder deck arguing that GPUs suck for deep learning, leveraging the core hardware maxims: increase cores, maximize core communication, and bring memory closer to compute.
- **Wafer-Scale Architecture:** The Cerebras chip integrated **450,000 cores** and **20 gigabytes of SRAM** on a single wafer-scale chip, eliminating the need to go off-chip for memory access.
- **The Hardware Roofline Reality:** While software allows teams to own 80% of their destiny, hardware brings theoretical roofline performance that software, compilers, and supply chain constraints (TSMC, HBM, DRAM, geopolitics) gradually chip away at.
- **A New CPU Generation:** Vishria notes an unannounced Benchmark investment addressing a coming sixth computing generation: specialized CPUs designed specifically to execute the code generated by LLMs, bypassing legacy CPU baggage.

### Robotics and Real-World Deployment
- **The Data Bootstrap Challenge:** Unlike LLMs that leverage internet-scale data, robots lack equivalent web-scale training data, forcing companies to rely on teleoperation and high-value data targeting.
- **Sunday Robotics Case Study:** Sunday Robotics uses vertically integrated hardware (such as custom gloves matching robot hands) to collect pristine pre-training data, followed by post-training and reinforcement learning to yield emergent behaviors like arbitrary laundry folding.
- **Autonomous Vehicle Precedents:** Following the Waymo and Tesla playbooks, vertically integrated data collection and fleet learning serve as the foundational blueprint for generalizable robotics.

### Benchmark's Venture Model and Going Public
- **Partner Selection and Chemistry:** Vishria defines his role through the "green button test"—whether he is genuinely willing to pick up the phone at 9:00 PM on a Saturday night for the founder—focusing on high conviction and deep personal chemistry over investment-grade math alone.
- **Growth Fund Expansion:** Benchmark recently raised a growth fund to capture high cash-on-cash multiples outside of early-stage investing, recognizing that modern multi-hundred-billion-dollar outcomes extend the high-multiple investment circle.
- **The Going Public Window:** Companies should go public to access public trust, transparency, and a valuable currency, noting that many private companies with $100M–$500M in ARR leave employees and investors trapped without liquidity.

## Benchmark's AI Bets: Cerebras, Sierra, Legora, Fireworks, Starcloud, Gumloop..

**TL;DR** 
The rise of AI has dismantled the traditional "spreadsheet investing" paradigm of software venture capital, decoupling business scale from risk reduction and replacing predictable SaaS metrics with highly capital-intensive, margin-challenged business models. As massive late-stage AI players secure unprecedented capital and prepare for historic liquidity events, the entire venture ecosystem is shifting toward multi-product alternative asset management to support the immense costs of frontier computing.

---

### The Death of "Spreadsheet Investing" and the New Risk-Scale Paradigm

* **The Broken Scale-Risk Relationship:** Historically in software, scaling a startup sequentially de-risked the business (validating product-market fit, unit economics, TAM, and market leadership). In the AI era, companies can scale to over $1 billion in revenue without proving durable product differentiation or sustainable unit economics, making large companies potentially as risky as early-stage ones.
* **Inversion of Traditional SaaS "Golden Rules":**
    * **Gross Margins:** Historically, 70% to 90% gross margins were mandatory. In AI, high margins can indicate that customers are not actually utilizing resource-intensive AI features, distorting traditional margin health.
    * **Distribution and Services:** High-touch implementation consulting (the "Palantirification of everything") is replacing pure software plays. "FDE (Forward Deployed Engineer) is the new PLG (Product-Led Growth)," which significantly depresses gross margins.
    * **CAPEX and R&D Moats:** Rather than remaining capital-light, competitive AI companies must build in-house research labs to post-train models, resulting in massive, non-traditional CAPEX outlays.
* **The New Economic Taxonomy ($P \times Q \times M$):** While gross margins ($M$) are structurally lower in AI, the pricing power ($P$) is exponentially higher. AI infrastructure and application companies are routinely securing nine-figure contracts, a phenomenon virtually unseen in traditional SaaS.

### The Monetization of Inference and the Rise of AI Agents

* **The Inference "Waterfall":** Inference is driving unprecedented revenue growth. Ev Randle advises startups to position themselves under the "waterfall" of inference demand, enabling companies to scale from $1M to $20M or $100M+ at rates far outpacing historical software benchmarks.
* **Selling Work, Not Software:** AI agents represent the most significant product and pricing model shift since the birth of cloud software. Buyers are moving away from purchasing seat licenses to purchasing "intelligence on tap" or completed white-collar tasks via outcome-based pricing (e.g., Sierra charging for completed customer support deflections).
* **Astronomical Developer Spend:** Individual developers are spending up to $3,000 per month ($36,000 annually) on tools like Claude Code. This level of spend turns individual developer seats into enterprise-level contract values.
* **The Value of Independent Routing Platforms:** Benchmark invested in Gumloop, an independent collaborative agent and automation canvas for enterprises. 
    * **Multi-Model Orchestration:** Because AI models are "jagged" (with Gemini excelling at multimodal tasks and Claude at coding), enterprises require independent third-party platforms to route tasks efficiently and prevent employees from wasting expensive frontier tokens on low-complexity tasks.

### Frontier vs. Open-Source Models and the "AI Mom Test"

* **The "AI Mom Test":** Randle evaluates model utility by analyzing what an average, non-technical user requires. The vast majority of daily consumer and enterprise queries do not require expensive frontier intelligence and can be easily managed by cost-effective open-source models.
* **Cost Optimization via Task Distillation:** Companies like Cognition are training open-source models specifically on low-complexity tasks identified within their platforms, saving up to 95% on inference costs by avoiding proprietary frontier APIs.
* **A Non-Zero-Sum Ecosystem:** The market is not currently a winner-take-all battle between proprietary frontier labs and open-source models. Demand is growing parabolically across on-device, open-source, and proprietary frontier categories simultaneously.

### Late-Stage "Rebirths" and the Evolution of Venture Capital

* **The Phenomenon of Late-Stage Upside:** Because companies are staying private longer and AI markets are massive, late-stage companies can offer higher investment upside than mid-stage rounds. 
    * **SpaceX as a Blueprint:** Kleiner Perkins’ investment in SpaceX at a $100B+ valuation succeeded because Starlink acted as a "rebirth" of the company, shifting the primary revenue driver from launch services to consumer and B2B broadband.
* **Venture Firms as Alternative Asset Managers:** Multi-stage firms like Andreessen Horowitz and General Catalyst are no longer pure venture capital firms; they have evolved into alternative asset managers offering wealth management, private credit, and debt products to support the capital-intensive lifecycles of modern tech giants.
* **An Unprecedented Liquidity Shock:** Randle notes the scale of Anthropic's $30 billion raise at a $380 billion valuation. If Anthropic goes public at a $1.5 trillion valuation, the gross return on that single late-stage round would be 35 times larger than the return of Snowflake’s historic pre-IPO round, representing a liquidity event of a magnitude the tech ecosystem has never experienced.

## What Most People Miss When Using AI | Bill Gurley

- Channel: The Knowledge Project Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBBhd0-Os74

---

**TL;DR** 
Long-term success in investing and entrepreneurship requires combining a deep study of historical industry "bedrock" with obsessive learning on the bleeding edge of technological disruptions. Navigating these highly complex, non-linear environments demands a strong foundation in systems thinking, robust storytelling, and an awareness of regulatory capture.

---

### ## Systems Thinking and the Financial Bedrock of Investing

* **Systems Thinking as a Shield:** Bill Gurley credits systems thinking (specifically citing the book *Thinking in Systems* and his work with the Santa Fe Institute) with helping him navigate "multivariable, non-linear systems" like the stock market. 
    * **Second-Derivative Effects:** Decision-makers must look past single metrics to avoid delayed negative consequences. For example, a major dating platform increased profile lengths to boost short-term engagement, only to discover months later that it severely damaged overall conversion rates.
* **The Importance of Financial Bedrock:** While many Silicon Valley venture capitalists lack a deep background in finance, Gurley argues that understanding traditional finance is essential because Wall Street is the ultimate "buyer" of the products VCs create via IPOs and M&A.
    * Gurley's foundation was built on studying classics: Peter Lynch's *One Up on Wall Street*, Burton Malkiel's *A Random Walk Down Wall Street*, Benjamin Graham, Warren Buffett's letters, and Howard Marks.
* **Redefining Value for Tech Growth:** Through his friendship with financial writer Mike Mauboussin and legendary investor Bill Miller (who beat the S&P 500 for 15 consecutive years), Gurley learned to view value investing through a growth lens.
    * Miller, a major early Amazon shareholder, argued that "value" simply means an asset is underpriced relative to its future worth—a thesis justified in tech by the long-term compounding power of network effects.

### ## The Power of Historical Depth vs. Bleeding-Edge Curiosity

* **Differentiating via Historical Mastery:** Deeply studying the history of one's field is a powerful, rare differentiator that signals true passion to employers and partners.
    * **The John Lasseter Dinner:** Benchmark partner Alex Balkanski won a charity dinner with Pixar's John Lasseter, who served a 10-course meal where each course was paired with a classic, historically significant cartoon that Lasseter deemed essential to understanding the evolution of animation.
    * **The Magnus Carlsen Trivia Win:** World Chess Champion Magnus Carlsen easily won a chess history trivia contest during a tournament break, illustrating that the greatest masters are also historians of their craft.
    * **The Picasso Example:** Pablo Picasso was a master realist painter by age 14 before transitioning to Cubism; his revolutionary style was built on top of absolute technical mastery.
* **Obsessive Learning on the Edge:** While history provides the frame, survival requires rapid, obsessive learning on the dynamic edge of technology waves (e.g., mobile app development in 2008, AI today).
    * **Gurley's AI Strategy:** Gurley maintains five premium AI accounts to stay competitive. He notes that most users underestimate LLMs because they do not utilize advanced prompting—such as asking models to categorize, rank, and outline pros and cons in a single step, rather than executing sequential manual tasks.
    * **Model Choice by Use Case:** Gurley notes a highly fragmented model landscape: ChatGPT is favored for its memory/project features, Google Gemini is used for deep restaurant and menu analysis due to integrated Google Review data, Claude is preferred by coders, and Perplexity is highly regarded for financial and international company research.

### ## AI Dynamics, Western Regulation, and China's Open-Source Engine

* **China's Open-Source Speed Advantage:** China has roughly 10 major open-source AI models that share weights, methodologies, and training techniques. Gurley believes this collaborative system allows China to innovate far faster than the highly siloed, proprietary competitive landscape of the West.
* **Regulation as an Oligopoly Moat:** Gurley warns that leading US AI firms are actively lobbying for complex, expensive regulations under the guise of safety to establish protective moats, intentionally raising the bar to shut out open-source competitors.
* **The Training Data Wall:** The AI industry is running out of organic internet data to train models, a phenomenon Gurley calls "painting in the corners." 
    * Companies are spending thousands of dollars an hour to hire human experts for fine-tuning, but Gurley aligns with Meta’s Chief AI Scientist Yann LeCun, who believes LLMs will hit a hard asymptote because language-based models cannot capture non-linguistic, real-world complexity (unlike specialized, constrained models such as Google’s AlphaGo).

### ## Venture Capital Dynamics, Circular Deals, and Benchmark’s Equal Partnership

* **The Rise of "Circular Deals":** The venture capital ecosystem is seeing unprecedented capital inflation. Large cloud service providers are giving billions of dollars in capital to AI startups (such as Anthropic), which is immediately spent back on the providers' own cloud infrastructure. This loop artificially inflates growth rates and obscures true unit economics.
* **Unprecedented Burn Rates:** While burning $1 million a month was considered highly risky a decade ago, top AI startups are now burning upwards of $100 million a month ($5 billion a year), making risk management and financial tracking incredibly difficult.
* **Benchmark’s Equal Partnership Structure:** Gurley attributes much of Benchmark's success to its non-hierarchical, five-equal-partner structure, which directly addresses the structural flaws of traditional "patriarchal" VC firms:
    * **Recruiting Advantage:** It makes recruiting elite talent from hierarchical firms easy, as incoming partners are immediately given equal economic standing.
    * **Absolute Alignment:** Because the pie is split equally, there is no internal competition or annual compensation reviews. Partners actively share deal flow and resources because one partner's win is mathematically equal to everyone else's.
    * **The Operational Tradeoff:** Without a designated CEO, Benchmark struggles with operational tasks and scaling. For example, rather than managing a highly complex corporate website, former partner Matt Cohler replaced their site with a single-sentence splash page 15 years ago, which remains unchanged today.

### ## Market Inefficiencies and the Threat of Regulatory Capture

* **Credit Card Duopolies and Stablecoins:** Visa and Mastercard enjoy massive 60% operating margins largely due to regulatory capture. While nations like the UK (*Faster Payments*), Argentina (*Pix*), and China (*WeChat Pay*/*Alipay*) long ago adopted instant, free bank-to-bank transfers, US banks have lobbied to delay government alternatives like *FedNow*.
    * **The Stablecoin Workaround:** Stablecoins like USDC (backed dollar-for-dollar by US Treasuries) bypass the three-day ACH settlement delays and 2.5% credit card transaction fees, offering instant, borderless, and low-cost payments over crypto rails.
* **The Wall Street IPO "Heist":** Gurley remains an outspoken critic of the traditional IPO underwriting process, calling the investment banks' ability to hand-pick share prices and select favored institutional buyers a "greedy power grab." He advocates for direct listings, tokenization, and transparent auction mechanisms to match supply and demand.
* **The Rise of Passive Indexing and ISS Overreach:** The massive shift toward passive indexing has left institutional giants (like BlackRock) without the time to evaluate individual corporate proxy votes. Consequently, they outsource decisions to proxy advisory firms like ISS.
    * Gurley criticizes ISS for operating as a "black box" where companies must hire them to learn how they are scored. He argues that ISS focuses on risk mitigation rather than shareholder wealth creation, pointing to their opposition to Elon Musk’s Tesla performance-based compensation package as a key example of misaligned incentives.
