## Howard Marks Flags the Uncertainty in AI Investing

### TL;DR
Markets have been dominated by optimists driven by strong economic aggregates, the TINA trade, and AI enthusiasm, though this requires heightened investor caution. While AI's transformative potential is undeniable, extreme uncertainty surrounding future profitability and societal impact makes it impossible to definitively label current market exuberance as irrational.

### The Macro Environment & Market Psychology
- **Federal Reserve Communication:** Howard Marks argues that less central bank communication is preferable, as it forces businesses and portfolios to become antifragile rather than relying entirely on Fed projections.
  - Expresses a personal preference for a less activist central bank that lets the free market determine natural interest rates through borrower-lender negotiations unless the economy is heading off the rails.
- **Optimism-Driven Markets:** Markets have been ruled by optimists since roughly October 1, 2022, with the S&P 500 more than doubling in that period.
  - The S&P surge is supported by positive underlying truths, including a US economy that performs as the best-developed world economy, the "TINA" (there is no alternative) trade, and AI excitement.
  - Marks attributes persistent optimism partly to the absence of a truly severe downturn since the global financial crisis market bottom in March 2009, leading people to extrapolate good times.
  - When optimists are in charge, they slough off bad news and react positively to settlement rumors without getting depressed when deals fall through, creating a positive market bias that calls for built-in defense and caution.

### AI Investment & Valuation Challenges
- **The Definition of Value Investing:** Marks defines his approach as a "small v" value investor whose goal is to calculate an asset's intrinsic value and buy it at a fair price relative to that value.
- **The "Too Hard" Pile:** Evaluating pure-play AI companies is extremely challenging because they may not have produced profits yet, making it difficult to quantify earnings potential 5 years out.
  - Hyperscalers like Microsoft and Amazon have supporting legacy businesses, but companies all-in on AI present an unusual degree of uncertainty for intrinsic valuation, fitting Warren Buffett's description of the "too hard pile."
- **Exuberance vs. Irrationality:** Alan Greenspan's 1997 phrase "irrational exuberance" defines a bubble, and while AI exuberance is undeniable, Marks hesitates to label it definitively as irrational.
  - Because nobody can definitively scope what AI will accomplish, its profitability, or its broader effects, nobody can prove the exuberance is irrational.
  - The presence of uncertainty does not mean investors should do nothing, but it must be acknowledged to properly inform investment sizing and strategy.

### Societal Impact & Government Policy
- **Existential Economic Concerns:** Marks previously warned about the acute societal ramifications of AI displacing workers in Middle America and across the country.
  - Reiterates that sentient human beings must acknowledge these risks, though he notes his wife defines worrying as "bleeding before you're shot" since individuals cannot personally stop the trend.
- **Policy Vacuum:** Marks criticizes the lack of government foresight, stating there should be a dedicated task force examining the coming effects of AI.
  - Poses a critical unanswered fiscal question for policymakers: If AI replaces human workers, will it replace them as taxpayers, and what will happen to government revenues when people lose their jobs?

### Corporate Integration & Critical Thinking
- **Enterprise Usage at Oaktree:** Oaktree Capital Management uses AI internally to marshal data and perform early analysis on tasks that used to take a long time, but has not turned business or investment decisions over to algorithms.
  - Exceptional investing does not come from computers, but from a superior ability to assess the import of data—a superiority AI does not yet possess.
- **Preserving Cognitive Muscles:** Oaktree does not encourage younger hires to outsource critical thinking to AI.
  - Stopping critical thinking causes individuals to "lose the muscle," and the ultimate edge in investing remains fundamentally human.
  - Marks compares AI's disruptive potential to indexation in equity investing—putting many active investors out of business, but not the best.

### Notable Quotes

**TL;DR:** Legendary investor Howard Marks emphasizes that superior results come from "second-level thinking" and the courage to act despite fear, noting that true success is defined by the autonomy to live life on your own terms.

### AI and the Future of Investment Judgment
Howard Marks recently updated his stance on AI, moving from skepticism to a recognition of its unprecedented potential, specifically regarding its **autonomy**.

*   **Distinctive Capabilities:** Unlike previous technological shifts (railroad, internet) which were merely tools for speed, AI possesses autonomy—the ability to figure out *how* to do a job without being told.
*   **The "Defrocking" of Mediocrity:** AI will likely "unfrock" investors who lack true talent, much like indexation exposed active managers who couldn't outperform averages.
*   **The Human Edge:** Marks believes experienced investors remain essential for two reasons:
    *   **The "Hair on the Neck" Test:** The ability to sense "bad people" or "wrong" deals through undefinable intuition and judgment.
    *   **Lack of Historical Data:** AI relies on pattern recognition; humans are still required to navigate unprecedented events where no training data exists.

### Investing Through Crisis and Fear
Marks recounts raising $11 billion for a distressed debt fund in 2008, highlighting that the best opportunities often feel the most terrifying.

*   **Building the Ark:** The best time to raise money is *before* a flood. Oaktree raised their massive fund in 2007–2008 because they saw market discipline disappearing, not because they knew exactly when the crash would happen.
*   **Acting Without Conviction:** Marks admits that during the Lehman Brothers collapse, he was not 100% confident. He argues that acting without trepidation in a crisis suggests something is wrong with the investor.
*   **The Rationality of Trepidation:** If you wait until there is nothing to fear, the opportunity has already passed. Success requires moving forward despite uncertainty.
*   **Quantitative Safety:** In 2008, Oaktree invested $500M/week for 15 weeks based on the logic that even if the companies they bought ended up being worth only 25% of their purchase price, the debt would still break even.

### The Architecture of Successful Partnerships
Marks and his co-founder, Bruce Karsh, have worked together for 39 years without a single fight. Marks attributes this to two core pillars:

*   **Shared Values:** A partnership fails if one person is a "cowboy" and the other is a "chicken." Disparate ethics or risk tolerances create friction during market shifts.
*   **Complementary Skills:** Marks and Karsh avoid overlap; Marks handles the external communication and "memos," while Karsh manages the money and stays out of the spotlight.
*   **Mutual Respect:** Acknowledging that the partner is better at certain tasks removes the "financial maximizer" ego that often destroys professional relationships.

### Success and Intentionality
Despite his legendary status, Marks admits he spent his first 25 years in the industry "unintentionally," making haphazard decisions based on luck and convenience.

*   **The Only Real Success:** Quoting Christopher Morley, Marks defines success as "living your life your own way." 
*   **The Late Start to Intentionality:** Marks did not start Oaktree until he was 49. He encourages younger people to find work that plays to their strengths and avoids their weaknesses, rather than following societal or parental pressure.
*   **Parenting via Autonomy:** Marks advocates for letting children make choices early—even incorrect ones—to build their own decision-making muscles, rather than asserting parental superiority.

### Insights on Warren Buffett and Reading
Marks shares observations from his long-standing relationship with Warren Buffett, particularly regarding the late Charlie Munger.

*   **The Buffett-Munger Dynamic:** Munger’s greatest contribution was moving Buffett away from "cigar butt" investing (buying cheap, low-quality companies for a final "puff" of value) toward buying "great companies at fair prices."
*   **Intellectual Humility:** Marks stresses that the most dangerous phrase in investing is "I am 100% convinced."
*   **Recommended Reading:**
    *   ***A Short History of Financial Euphoria*** **by John Kenneth Galbraith:** Essential for understanding the psychology behind market cycles.
    *   ***Fooled by Randomness*** **by Nassim Nicholas Taleb:** A guide to understanding how much of "success" is actually attributable to luck.

**TL;DR** 
The current AI-driven market surge and high-profile IPO pipeline exhibit classic signs of historical technological bubbles. To navigate this highly uncertain environment, investors must deliberately position themselves on a spectrum between analytical value investing and speculation, recognizing that while AI will fundamentally change the world, long-term success will ultimately require a return to business profitability.

### Navigating the AI Wave: Speculation vs. Analytical Investing

*   **The Dilemma of "Irrational Exuberance":** Howard Marks (Co-founder and Co-chairman of Oaktree Capital Management) notes that while current markets are undeniably exuberant, it is impossible to analytically prove whether this exuberance is "irrational." 
    *   Unlike past technological breakthroughs, the ultimate capabilities, profit timelines, and dominant winners of AI remain completely unspecifiable.
*   **The Investment Spectrum:** Marks defines a spectrum of market activity that dictates how investors should allocate capital:
    *   *Analytical Investing:* Practiced by traditional value investors who evaluate a company's current output, project 5-to-10-year earnings, determine a fair valuation, and compare it to the current price. 
    *   *Speculative Investing:* Driven by futuristic concepts and narrative-based "thumbsucking" where concrete financial modeling is virtually impossible. Marks places IPOs like Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceX closer to speculation than analytical investing.
*   **Historical Bubble Parallels:** AI is the latest in a long line of paradigm-shifting innovations, including railroads (1860s), radio (1920s), the automobile, computers (1950s–60s), and the internet (2000). 
    *   Historically, every single technological wave has attracted excessive capital, built redundant infrastructure, and resulted in a wealth-destroying bubble. 
    *   Marks warns that the phrase *"this time it's different"* is a recurring, dangerous justification used in every cycle to dismiss overvaluation.

### Valuation Realities and the AI Asset Spectrum

*   **Lofty But Not "Nutty" Valuations:** While the Shiller PE ratio sits near 42 (close to its historical bubble peak of 44) and the S&P 500 PE is high at 23 (compared to the 80-year average of 16), Marks does not view current pricing as entirely irrational.
    *   During the "Nifty Fifty" era of 1969, premier stocks like Xerox, Kodak, and IBM traded at PE ratios of 60 to 90. 
    *   Today’s leading tech giants (excluding Tesla) trade in the more reasonable 30s, backed by high incremental profitability due to the low marginal cost of digital products.
*   **The Erosion of Competitive Moats:** Traditional "moats" are deteriorating at an unprecedented pace. Just as digital media disrupted newspapers, AI-driven automated coding is now threatening SaaS (Software as a Service) business models.
*   **Positioning Across the AI Spectrum:** Marks outlines three distinct ways to invest in the AI boom:
    *   **The Hyperscalers (Low-Risk):** Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, and Meta possess massive established cash flows and secure business moats. While they are safe plays, their non-AI legacy businesses may dilute overall returns if AI experiences exponential growth.
    *   **Established Pure-Plays (Moderate-Risk):** Nvidia, OpenAI, and Anthropic are highly likely to remain industry cornerstones in 5 to 10 years, though investors face the risk of overpaying at current IPO valuations.
    *   **Early-Stage Startups (High-Risk/Lottery Tickets):** These companies often lack revenue or clear product definitions. While most will fail and lose 100% of investor capital, a select few will generate astronomical, incalculable returns.

### The Future of Profitability and Investment Management

*   **The Profitability Mandate:** Despite narratives that hyper-growth in revenue justifies hundreds of billions of dollars in losses, Marks stresses that profitability will ultimately matter.
    *   Marks recalls Warren Buffett’s dot-com warning: technological efficiency is not synonymous with corporate profitability. 
    *   If AI is primarily a labor-saving productivity tool, intense competition among AI providers may spark price wars, shifting the financial benefits away from the AI creators to the end-users (such as shipping, warehouse, and retail companies).
*   **The Asset Management Evolution:** Passive indexation now commands the majority of mutual fund equity capital. This shift occurred because traditional active managers historically underperformed the S&P 500 while charging high fees.
*   **The 17-Year Bull Run:** Since the Global Financial Crisis low in March 2009, a sustained period of declining interest rates created highly favorable "salad days" for alternative investments. Marks warns that this era was highly anomalous and that a future market correction will expose unprepared managers.

### Private Credit and Direct Lending Realities

*   **Explosive Growth of Direct Lending:** The private credit sector has scaled from virtually nothing in 2010 to a $1.7 trillion asset class today, featuring roughly 700 managers.
    *   Crucially, only 3% of these 700 managers were in business prior to the 2008 Global Financial Crisis, meaning the vast majority have never managed assets through a severe economic downturn.
*   **Overblown Systemic Fears:** Marks believes widespread panic regarding private credit defaults is exaggerated. However, the asset class suffers from a clear liquidity mismatch.
    *   Retail investors frequently purchase non-traded Business Development Companies (BDCs) without realizing they are locked into strict withdrawal limits, typically capped at 5% of assets per quarter.

**TL;DR** 
True investment outperformance requires "second-level thinking" that departs from consensus, as market cycles are driven by emotional excesses rather than predictable mechanical laws. While artificial intelligence can optimize pattern recognition, exceptional human insight remains essential for navigating unpredictable market shifts, pricing distressed assets, and managing downside risk.

---

### The Philosophy of Preparation and the Mechanics of Cycles

*   **The Concept of Mujo:** Howard Marks attributes much of his foundational perspective to his study of Japanese literature, specifically the concept of *Mujo*—the inevitability of change.
    *   **Inherent Unpredictability:** Because change is inevitable, unpredictable, and uncontrollable, investors must learn to accommodate to it rather than trying to forecast specific outcomes.
*   **Preparation vs. Prediction:** Marks emphasizes that investors should focus on preparation rather than prediction.
    *   **Portfolio Sub-optimization:** Rather than building a portfolio optimized for a single predicted future, investors should build portfolios that can survive multiple potential futures, avoiding extreme vulnerability to negative tail events.
*   **The Psychology of Market Cycles:** Economic cycles do not behave like regular physical waves; they are dictated by human behavior.
    *   **Excesses and Corrections:** Market cycles oscillate around a long-term trend line due to psychological extremes—excessive optimism leads to over-expansion, which inevitably requires a correction down to the mean.
    *   **The Illusion of the Average:** While the S&P 500 has returned an average of 10% annually over the last 100 years, its actual annual return is almost never between 8% and 12%. The market is almost always reacting to excesses of greed or fear.

---

### Debt Investing and Oaktree’s Risk-Controlled Strategy

*   **The Appeal of Contractual Returns:** Despite having capped upside compared to equity, debt investing appealed to Marks's naturally conservative disposition.
    *   **Underpriced Assets:** Oaktree historically targeted fields that the broader market viewed with extreme skepticism, such as high-yield "junk" bonds or distressed debt (rebranded as "opportunistic credit"). 
*   **Calculating the Probability of Default:** The core skill set at Oaktree is predicting default probabilities more accurately than the consensus.
    *   **Excess Returns:** If the market demands a high interest rate based on an assumed 5% default rate, but Oaktree's bottom-up analysis determines the true risk is only 2%, they capture an "excess return."
*   **Institutional Discipline:** Oaktree has maintained a default rate roughly one-third of the market's 3.6% to 3.7% long-term high-yield average through specific structural choices:
    *   **Lifetime Analysts:** Unlike firms where analysts quickly transition to portfolio managers, Oaktree encourages analysts to remain in their roles long-term, building deep institutional knowledge.
    *   **Rigorous Coverage:** Analysts must evaluate eight core areas of inquiry for every company to ensure they do not miss systemic blind spots.

---

### The Limits of AI and the Power of "Second-Level Thinking"

*   **AI's Edge and Boundaries:** AI models excel at analyzing historical data, identifying repeating patterns, and executing strategies with disciplined consistency, free from fear and greed.
    *   **The Novelty Problem:** AI is structurally limited when dealing with situations that have no historical precedent or pattern, such as evaluating unique business plans or identifying exceptional leaders like Steve Jobs.
*   **Second-Level Thinking:** To achieve above-average investment performance, an investor must think differently and *better* than the consensus.
    *   **The Herd Limitation:** If an investor relies on the same consensus data and thinking as everyone else, they will achieve average results. 
    *   **Uncoachable Trait:** Just as you "cannot coach height" in basketball, Marks believes you cannot easily teach the intuitive, non-consensus mindset required for second-level thinking.
*   **The Valuation of Information:** Marks notes that readily available quantitative data about the present cannot yield superior returns because it is already priced into the market. Superior insight relies on interpreting this data differently.

---

### Mindset, Intentionality, and Lifelong Learning

*   **Drifting vs. Intentionality:** Marks describes the first 35 years of his career as being "adrift" on a river of happenstance and luck, which included his transition from equities to bonds in 1978.
    *   **The Entrepreneurial Trigger:** It was not until the founding of Oaktree in 1995 that Marks transitioned to acting with deliberate, proactive intentionality. He suggests that entrepreneurship naturally forces individuals to stop drifting.
*   **Staying Sharp at Eighty:** Marks maintains his cognitive agility through genetic luck, puzzle-solving, reading outside his field, and collaborating with younger professionals—specifically his son, Andrew.
    *   **Intellectual Flexibility:** Despite working in credit for nearly five decades, Marks remains willing to challenge his own assumptions, as demonstrated by his recent deep dives into generative AI models.

## What's Going on in Private Credit?

- Channel: Oaktree Capital
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=coXK2_bm-1I
- Published: 2026-04-09
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260409.html#video-coXK2_bm-1I
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management)

---

**TL;DR**
The private credit market, specifically direct lending, is entering a period of disillusionment as the long-term tailwinds of low interest rates and undisciplined underwriting collide with AI-driven disruption in the software sector and retail redemption pressures.

### The Evolution of the Credit Sector
* **Historical Chronology:** Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital) outlines a 50-year progression from the acceptance of high-yield debt in the 1970s to the current marketing of direct lending to retail investors.
    * **1970s-80s:** Michael Milken popularized non-investment grade debt; the birth of Leveraged Buyouts (LBOs).
    * **1990s-2000s:** Growth of broadly syndicated loans, securitizations (CLOs), and "alternative investments" after the tech bubble.
    * **2010s-2020s:** Post-GFC regulation forced banks to retreat, creating a vacuum filled by non-bank "direct lending" to mid-market PE-sponsored companies.
* **Market Scale:** The private credit sector has ballooned from roughly $150 billion 20 years ago to approximately $2 trillion today.
* **Risk vs. Volatility:** Marks warns that the lack of price volatility in private loans—due to the absence of a public market—has been misinterpreted as a lack of credit risk. 

### The Anatomy of a Credit Bubble
* **The "New Thing" Trap:** Novel investment forms often escape skepticism because they are untested. Early success fuels envy, leading latecomers (the "imitators" and "idiots") to lower standards to deploy capital.
* **The Quality Erosion:** Hundreds of firms entered direct lending post-GFC without experiencing a full credit cycle. This influx of capital forced lenders to compete by accepting:
    * Lower yields and narrower spreads.
    * Reduced safety and "covenant-lite" loan documents.
    * Higher debt levels and "ARR loans" (based on revenue rather than profit).
* **The Role of Liquidity Mismatches:** Drawing parallels to 1929, Marks cites three primary bubble drivers: selling to unsuitable investors, providing heavy leverage, and the mismatch between illiquid assets and short-term redemption expectations.

### The Software Debt Crisis and AI Disruption
* **Concentration Risk:** Software debt now represents a massive portion of credit markets, specifically 20-30% of direct lending (compared to only 4-5% of the high-yield bond market).
* **The AI Tipping Point:** In late 2025 and early 2026, the release of powerful AI coding models (such as those from Anthropic) disrupted the "moat" thesis of the software industry, which previously relied on high-cost human programming and recurring subscription revenue.
* **Sentiment Shift:** As equity cushions for software companies evaporated due to AI disruption, the credit market faced a "cognitive tipping point" in February 2026.
    * **Redemption Pressures:** Investors in semi-liquid Business Development Companies (BDCs) began requesting withdrawals, leading to redemption limits and skepticism regarding Net Asset Values (NAVs).

### The End of the "Sea Change" Era
* **The Interest Rate Tailwind:** Private equity and direct lending matured during a 40-year period (1980-2021) of secularly declining interest rates, which boosted asset values and lowered borrowing costs.
* **The New Reality:** The 400-500 basis point rate hike starting in 2022 has "thrown sand in the gears."
    * **Refinancing Challenges:** Portfolio companies are struggling with lower coverage ratios (earnings vs. interest expense).
    * **Lower Returns:** MSCI estimates that U.S. PE funds saw annualized returns of only 5.8% between 2022 and Q3 2025, significantly trailing the S&P 500's 11.6%.
* **Oaktree’s Positioning:** Marks emphasizes that Oaktree maintained discipline by limiting direct lending to 20% of its performing credit assets and keeping software exposure "extremely small" relative to peers.

> "What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end." 
> — **Howard Marks**, quoting a classic investment adage

> "In the last several years, a lot of private equity companies have been saddled with capital structures that didn't anticipate a 400-basis-point increase in interest rates." 
> — **Bruce Karsh**, Co-Chairman and CIO, Oaktree Capital

> "The enduring lesson is not that booms can be prevented or that busts can be fully averted. It is that we need to remember how easily we forget. The antidote to irrational exuberance is not regulation by itself... but humility." 
> — **Howard Marks**

> "Today, investors are not discriminating between the potential winners and losers in the software space, and the entire sector is under pressure. The disruptions in headlines are largely flow- and sentiment-driven rather than the result of credit deterioration." 
> — **Armen Panossian, Bob O’Leary, and Craig Wiele**, Oaktree Internal Memo extract


## Reflections on Oaktree Conference 2026 with Howard Marks

- Channel: Oaktree Capital
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9wwuvo_0fUw
- Published: 2026-03-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260324.html#video-9wwuvo_0fUw
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management)

---

**TL;DR** 
At the Oaktree Conference 2026, Howard Marks and senior Oaktree leaders analyzed the evolution of credit markets, highlighting how market crowding and human emotion create mispricings, and emphasized the necessity of disciplined risk management, multi-asset solutions, and strong corporate leadership to navigate today's increasingly volatile credit environment.

---

### The Evolution and Indigestion of Credit Markets

* **The Credit Evolution:** Armen Panossian (Co-CEO) outlined the vast expansion of corporate debt since the 1970s and 1980s, when sub-investment grade debt was considered uninvestable "junk." 
    * Today's market boasts a highly diverse array of liquid and private credit instruments, including high-yield bonds, senior loans, CLOs, CMBS, and distressed credit.
* **The Rise of Private Credit:** Non-bank direct lending grew rapidly post-GFC to fill the void left by heavily regulated traditional banks.
    * Since 2011, this sector has expanded into a **$1.5 trillion market**.
    * Direct lending transitioned over the last 15 years from junior mezzanine debt to senior first-lien positions.
* **Current Direct Lending "Indigestion":** Howard Marks noted that massive capital inflows since 2011 have eroded the premium safety terms and yield spreads that early direct lenders enjoyed.
    * **Vanishing Alpha:** Marks stated that "excess returns" have largely disappeared from standard direct lending, which now offers a fair, but not "lush," liquidity premium of roughly **100 to 125 basis points** over public credit.

### Portfolio Construction: Balancing Liquid and Private Credit

* **Multi-Asset Credit Solutions:** Danielle Polly (Co-Portfolio Manager, Global Credit) emphasized that managing multi-strategy credit portfolios requires dynamic asset allocation based on relative value, supported by rigorous bottom-up fundamental research.
    * **The Shift to Solutions:** Marks noted that the investment management industry is shifting away from selling individual off-the-shelf products toward delivering comprehensive, tailored solutions to meet client return objectives.
* **Complementary Dynamics:** Rather than viewing public and private credit as a binary choice, Oaktree advocates for a balanced approach.
    * **Private Credit** offers higher prospective yields if credit selection is sound.
    * **Public Credit** provides essential liquidity, allowing managers to restructure portfolios and quickly exit deteriorating positions.

### Capitalizing on Market Mispricings and Emotion

* **Exploiting Seller Mistakes:** Steve Tesslerari (PM, Value Opportunities) highlighted that market mispricings are driven by human emotion and misinformation. Oaktree targets several key sources of bargains:
    * **Complexity and Obscurity:** Assets with messy disclosures or off-balance sheet components that are hard to value.
    * **Institutional Barriers:** Assets facing institutional biases or mandate-limited buyers.
    * **Technical Forced Selling:** Structural rules that force institutions to sell (e.g., when a bond is downgraded below investment grade), regardless of price.
* **Avoiding Overloved Sectors:** Madeleine Jones (Head of European Liquid Performing Credit) cautioned against top-down sector hype, pointing to European healthcare as a recent primary driver of defaults.
    * Because the sector was highly favored, lenders overloaded these companies with excessive leverage, leaving them highly vulnerable to cost inflation.
    * Marks reiterated that "it is not what you buy, it’s what you pay that matters," and that popular assets are rarely priced to deliver excess returns.
* **Leverage as "Dynamite":** High leverage magnifies financial results in good times but destroys companies during downturns. Marks noted that the market became complacent during the long run of good times from March 2009 to early 2026, leading to structural vulnerabilities that are now coming to light.

### Corporate Governance: Leadership, Culture, and AI

* **The Impact of Management:** Charles Blackburn (Co-Head of Europe, Global Opportunities) emphasized that operational success relies on leadership capable of defining a vision, executing strategy, and cultivating a strong corporate culture.
    * Marks observed that superior management is the key variable that maximizes the productive capacity of a company's tangible assets. 
* **The Role of Culture:** A constructive culture directly impacts a business's bottom line and longevity. Marks highlighted Oaktree's foundational business principles—prioritizing client care, handling conflicts with integrity, and maintaining a non-hierarchical workspace—as primary drivers of its 30-year talent retention and success.
* **The Limits of AI:** While Oaktree actively invests in themes like data centers to safely participate in AI, Marks remains skeptical of AI’s capability at the leadership level.
    * AI lacks the subjective intuition and emotional intelligence required to evaluate human character and select outstanding corporate managers.

## Oaktree's Howard Marks on Unpredictablility, Importance and Investing in AI

- Channel: Bloomberg Television
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nWaFDNs3Zio
- Published: 2026-03-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260318.html#video-nWaFDNs3Zio
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management)

---

**TL;DR**
Artificial intelligence has rendered the global investment landscape more unpredictable than at any time in history, while a 17-year period of easy credit has created a "frothy" market where high standards have been replaced by FOMO (fear of missing out) and lower safety margins.

### The AI Impact: Unpredictability and Investment Strategy
*   **A "Less Predictable" World:** Marks argues that AI makes the future harder to forecast than ever before. He emphasizes that investing requires two things: a view of what will happen and a view of the *probability* that the view is correct. AI significantly lowers the probability of being right.
*   **Technology vs. Investment:** While AI as a technology may not be a bubble (and its impact may even be underestimated), the *investments* in AI companies are subject to different rules.
    *   **Equity vs. Debt:** Marks advises against lending to AI companies with fixed returns. If an investor is taking "fundamental business model risk" in a volatile sector, they should seek the upside of equity ownership rather than the limited return of a loan.
*   **Labor Displacement:** Marks highlights the efficiency and ruthlessness of AI, noting that the company Block recently cut 40% of its workforce because AI could perform tasks "cheaper and faster."

### The Current State of Private Credit
*   **Loss of "Specialness":** In 2011, private credit offered high rates and safety as banks retreated. Today, the influx of capital has driven interest rates and safety covenants down. 
    *   **Equilibrium:** Marks notes that with public credit yielding ~7% and direct lending at ~8.25%, the 125-basis-point liquidity premium is "fair but not lush." The "specialness" of the asset class has largely vanished as it reached equilibrium with public markets.
*   **Retail Risks:** Marks expresses concern regarding the expansion of private credit vehicles (like BDCs) to retail investors who may not fully understand the ramifications of illiquidity or the absence of mark-to-market pricing.
*   **Cycle Maturity:** He compares the current enthusiasm for private credit to the movie *Casablanca*, suggesting that investors are only "shocked" by risks—like illiquidity—when the market turns, even though those risks were always present.

### Market Cycles and the "Default Vacuum"
*   **The 17-Year Exception:** Since 2009, the market has seen an unusually long period of low defaults, aided by aggressive Federal Reserve intervention. This has conditioned investors to disregard the possibility of failure.
*   **The "Naked Swimmer" Phase:** Marks cites Warren Buffett, noting that "it's only when the tide goes out that we find out who's swimming naked." He believes the market has not yet seen the "washout" required to reveal who lent money unwisely during the good times.
*   **Early Warning Signs:** Marks points to recent bankruptcies and potential frauds (referencing "Cockroaches in the Coal Mine") as indicators that the "music" may be stopping. He notes that the worst loans are invariably made during the best of times.

### Human Intuition vs. Machine Logic
*   **AI as a Predictive Tool:** AI does not "answer questions" so much as it "makes predictions" based on historical patterns and data. While it can process information without emotional or arithmetic error, it lacks essential human qualities.
*   **What AI Lacks:**
    *   **Intuition:** AI cannot experience the "hairs standing up on the back of your neck" when reading a suspicious prospectus.
    *   **Character Judgment:** Marks believes humans remain better judges of "bad people" and management integrity than algorithms.
*   **Oaktree’s Stance:** Oaktree uses AI (specifically tools like Claude) to marshal data and identify patterns, but the firm refuses to invest based on AI hypotheses alone. Marks maintains a "cautious" stance, waiting for a significant market decline before becoming aggressive.

> "Life under AI is one of the most unpredictable things I've ever contemplated... the same power that gives it its importance gives it its unpredictability." — Howard Marks

> "What the wise man does in the beginning, the fool does in the end... the early success becomes visible, other people emulate, the entrants bid for the thing in question, and the specialness goes away." — Howard Marks

> "I think AI understands history, recognizes patterns, makes extrapolations... but I don't think it has intuition. I don't think AI has hairs on its neck." — Howard Marks

> "The worst of loans are made in the best of times." — Howard Marks


## Offense or Defense? Positioning Portfolios in Today's Market with Howard Marks and Alper Daglioglu

- Channel: Brookfield
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVe2enQ0SOw
- Published: 2026-02-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260223.html#video-YVe2enQ0SOw
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management); Alper Daglioglu (Head of Global Investment Strategies, Morgan Stanley)

---

**TL;DR** 
The current market is characterized by extreme optimism following a historic three-year bull run, signaling a critical need for investors to shift from aggressive growth toward defensive positioning and predictable outcomes.

### Assessing the Current Market Cycle
* **Historic Performance:** The three-year period of 2023–2025 ranks as the 7th best three-year period for the S&P 500 in the last 100 years.
* **Sentiment Shift:** Optimism is currently "in the ascendancy." Howard Marks notes that when optimism rules, prices are high relative to intrinsic value, prospective returns are modest, and risk is elevated.
* **Strategic Adjustment:** Because the world remains highly unpredictable and certain 20th-century growth drivers (globalization, post-WWII recovery) are slowing, investors should prioritize defensiveness over sheer aggression.
* **Predictability vs. Maximization:** Investors face a fundamental tradeoff between maximizing returns and enhancing the reliability of those returns. Current conditions favor emphasizing "reliable outcomes."

### The Psychology of Risk and Contrarianism
* **Second-Level Thinking:** Successful investing requires the courage to be "uncomfortably idiosyncratic." Marks highlights that attractive valuations only appear during periods of "abject terror" (e.g., September 2008 or March 2020).
* **The Paradox of Risk:** The riskiest market environment is one where everyone believes there is no risk. Conversely, the safest time to buy is when the consensus believes risk is everywhere, as this sentiment drives prices to their floor.
* **Emotional Discipline:** Most human instincts—wanting to buy when things feel good and sell when things feel bad—are detrimental to wealth accumulation. To outperform, one must act as a contrarian, buying more when assets go "on sale."

### Overcoming Institutional and Behavioral Constraints
* **Volatility vs. Risk:** Marks argues that the investment industry wrongly equates volatility with risk. For long-term, well-funded investors, interim price fluctuations are a "part of life" and should not be avoided if they lead to higher terminal returns.
* **Agency Friction:** Institutional "agents" often prioritize career preservation over performance. This creates a bias toward avoiding losses that might lead to being fired, even at the cost of superior long-term gains.
* **The "Do Something" Bias:** Investors often focus on irrelevant short-term data, such as the exact month the Fed will stop raising rates. Marks asserts that "what really matters" is the long-term growth of company value and the ability of borrowers to repay debt.

### Evolving Asset Management Trends
* **Private Assets:** The shift toward private markets offers a "liquidity trade-off." While these assets lack daily mark-to-market pricing (reducing perceived volatility), they require specialized manager skill to navigate the lack of reference prices.
* **Concentrated Partnerships:** Global allocators are trending toward fewer, deeper relationships with General Partners (GPs). These strategic partnerships aim to treat the manager as an extension of the investor’s team to solve complex liability and liquidity needs over 5–10 year horizons.
* **Artificial Intelligence:** While AI will undoubtedly change the world, Marks warns that the "how, when, and who benefits" remains uncertain. Superior investing in this space will rely on personal judgment rather than "machinable" formulas.

> "The riskiest thing in the world is the belief that there's no risk. So one of the safest things in the world is the belief that risk is everywhere, because it puts prices so low that risk is minimal." — **Howard Marks**

> "It’s nothing short of an oxymoron to say, 'this is a bargain-priced security that everybody loves.' When everybody loves something, it’s unlikely that it’ll be on the bargain counter." — **Howard Marks**

> "Buffett says, 'I’d rather have a lumpy 15% return than a smooth 12.' And I would say to people, if you’d rather have a smooth 12 than a lumpy 15, you have to ask yourself what’s going on." — **Howard Marks**
