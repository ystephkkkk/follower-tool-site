## How One Piece Of Advice Helped Remitly’s Founder Build A $4.6 Billion FinTech Giant

- Channel: Forbes
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w2YkXHUoiXE
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261117.html#video-w2YkXHUoiXE
- Speakers: Matt Oppenheimer (Co-founder & Chairman, Remitly); Nittin Noria (Professor, Harvard Business School)

---

**TL;DR:** A single piece of advice from Harvard Business School professor Nittin Noria—to take "the road less traveled" by leaving Barkley's London headquarters for Nairobi, Kenya—led Matt Oppenheimer to discover the broken cross-border payments system and build Remitly into a $4.6 billion FinTech giant processing $75 billion annually across 175+ countries.

### The Advice That Sparked Remitly
* **The Mentor:** Nittin Noria, Oppenheimer's professor in Harvard Business School's *Authentic Leadership Development* class (originally started by Bill George) and later Dean of HBS, gave Oppenheimer career-altering advice after business school.
* **The Road Less Traveled:** When Oppenheimer accepted a global leadership role at Barkley's Bank in London (One Churchill Place), Noria warned him that everyone would advise against leaving headquarters for his second rotation—and urged him to go to Nairobi, Kenya instead to gain a truly global perspective.
* **Discovering the Problem:** In Nairobi, Oppenheimer was the only North American expat on Barkley's consumer leadership team. Living in Kenyan shillings while paid in British pounds—and watching local friends like Fiona rely on a sister's Bank of America debit card for basic living expenses—exposed how expensive, slow, and inconvenient cross-border financial services were.

### Scaling Through Intense Short-Term Focus
* **Current Scale:** Remitly now has a ~$4.6 billion market cap, processes $75 billion+ in annual transfers across 175+ countries, reaches 5 billion+ bank accounts and hundreds of thousands of cash pickup locations, and delivers 90%+ of transactions in under an hour.
* **Disciplined Corridor Rollout:** Founded in 2011, Remitly tackled state-by-state regulation and working capital complexity by focusing exclusively on US (specifically Washington state) to the Philippines—a ~$10 billion annual volume corridor at the time—for its first two years, before adding India in year three and Mexico in year four.
  * *Trade-off:* This narrow focus initially made fundraising harder, as VCs questioned whether Remitly could replicate its model in new countries, but Oppenheimer credits it with enabling intentional, customer-centric growth without sacrificing trust.
* **Customer-Centric Testing Programs:**
  * **Remitly Travel:** Reimburses employees who travel to Remitly's operating countries and use the platform to send money, surfacing real-world friction points like cash-pickup delays or Know Your Customer (KYC) issues.
  * **Remitly Scholars:** Provides tuition reimbursement using Remitly's platform.

### Navigating the 2021 IPO and Investor Relations
* **Don't Make the IPO the Goal:** Remitly went public in 2021 at a $7.8 billion valuation—just two weeks before the public markets "slammed shut" for several years. Oppenheimer kept IPO preparations tight, inviting ~75 team members and two or three of Remitly's earliest customers to the event to keep the focus on serving customers rather than market milestones.
* **Private-to-Public Cadence:** Even as a private company, Remitly treated angel and VC updates (initially monthly) as formal investor relations. That discipline, combined with listening to VCs who passed on earlier rounds—asking *why* and implementing their feedback—helped Remitly raise subsequent rounds and transition smoothly to quarterly public reporting.

### Transitioning From CEO to Chairman
* **Timing the Handoff:** After 15 years as CEO (including four years as a public company CEO), Oppenheimer transitioned to Chairman earlier this year (2026) while the business was performing strongly.
* **Chair, Not Executive Chair:** Oppenheimer intentionally avoided the "Executive Chair" title, calling it poorly defined and messy, and instead committed to being a true Chair who is "invited to the party" by the CEO rather than backseat driving.
* **Bringing in Sebastian:** To lead Remitly's expansion into broader financial services and AI-first reinvention (informed by Oppenheimer's involvement with the Seattle AI startup group *Foundations*), the board hired Sebastian, a mathematician who grew up in Argentina and brings deep operating experience:
  * Ran Oracle's Latin American business for 15 years, reporting to Larry Ellison.
  * Worked at Apple, reporting to Tim Cook and working closely with Steve Jobs.
  * Built Amazon's marketplace business over 10+ years, reporting directly to Jeff Bezos.
  * Served as Vice Chair and Co-CEO at WeWork.

### Filtering Advice and Mentoring Others
* **Handling Bad Advice:** Oppenheimer approaches feedback with a growth mindset, assuming everyone has a nugget of good advice even if they lack full context. When he hears bad advice, he pauses and asks "why" three to five times to uncover the underlying insight.
* **Paying It Forward:** To be a "Nittin" for someone else, Oppenheimer advises mentors to share their own lived experiences *before* giving advice so the recipient can connect the dots themselves.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **1–5 Year Product & Customer Expansion (Matt Oppenheimer):** Remitly will expand beyond core senders of a few hundred dollars into "growth accelerators," including:
  * Moving up-market (referencing HBS professor Clay Christensen's *The Innovator's Dilemma*) to serve high-value senders ($5,000 transactions), small businesses, and the 30 million receivers already on Remitly's network.
  * Expanding the product suite with the **Remitly card** (enabling stored value and multi-currency accounts over time) and **"send now, pay later"** products to help customers smooth cash flow.
