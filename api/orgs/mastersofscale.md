**TL;DR**
Space architect Ariel Ekblaw and host Reid Hoffman argue that space has transitioned from science fiction to a near-term commercial market—just 250 miles up—designed to benefit life on Earth. Through self-assembling orbital infrastructure, microgravity biotech, and lunar resource extraction, humanity is entering a new era of in-space construction and exploration.

### Self-Assembling Infrastructure & Rendezvous Robotics
* **Breaking the Rocket Constraint:** Ariel Ekblaw explained that building in space has historically been limited by what fits inside a rocket payload fairing. Her MIT PhD research, **Tessera**, solves this using "smart Lego" tiles that self-assemble in orbit into massive structures like closed-shape buckyball habitats, large solar arrays, and potentially AI data centers.
* **Commercializing In-Space Construction:** Over the last 1.5 years, Ekblaw spun Tessera out of her nonprofit, the Aurelia Institute, into a for-profit startup called **Rendezvous Robotics**.
  * The company recently closed a successful seed round (to be announced the following week) to scale in-space self-assembly.
  * Target applications include massive-diameter telecom antennas for better ground communications and large-scale space-based solar power arrays that can beam clean energy to Earth even at night, solving green energy intermittency.

### Orbital BioLabs & Space for Earth
* **Space to Help Earth, Not Escape It:** Reid Hoffman emphasized the distinction between trying to escape Earth versus using space to improve life on Earth, noting his strong preference for the latter.
* **Microgravity Drug Formulation:** Ekblaw highlighted that biology behaves differently without gravity, enabling breakthroughs impossible on the ground:
  * **Merck's Keytruda:** Merck took its $30 billion cancer drug to space to grow higher-quality crystals, allowing the treatment to shift from a heavy IV infusion to a simple shot administered in a doctor's office—lowering costs and improving patient quality of life.
  * **Artificial Retinas:** Delicate retinal tissue can be manufactured in space without gravity causing it to sag.
* **A University Campus in Orbit:** Hoffman and Ekblaw have been collaborating on the concept of a space university, talking to potential founding donors.
  * With the International Space Station (ISS) decommissioning in **2030** and **4 to 5** credible commercial space station companies coming online to replace it, Ekblaw noted we are within a 10-year window where donors could name MIT's or Johns Hopkins' first building in space.
  * Near-term, this starts with an orbital biolab module for faculty and graduate students; long-term, it could enable study-abroad programs in orbit.

### The Lunar Economy & Space Tourism
* **Building a Commercial Ecosystem:** NASA's **Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)** program—contracting companies like **Intuitive Machines** and **Firefly**—is establishing a commercial "taxi to the moon," mirroring the SpaceX playbook for the ISS. Hoffman noted that building this commercial ecosystem is what makes lunar exploration sustainable compared to the Apollo era.
* **Helium-3 Extraction:** Ekblaw identified **helium-3** as the first major lunar resource that will benefit Earth. Measured only in kilograms on Earth, it is abundant on the moon (due to the lack of a magnetosphere) and is mission-critical for national security, MRIs, quantum computing, and fusion cooling.
* **Hospitality in Orbit:** Hoffman joked that a lunar resort (like an Aman) will inevitably follow, while Ekblaw noted that **Hilton DoubleTree** has already sent DoubleTree cookies to the ISS to test if they taste the same in space (astronauts approved).

### AI, Purpose, and Terrestrial Lessons
* **The Microgravity AI Dataset:** Ekblaw pointed to the intersection of AI and space as a major emerging market. Beyond orbital AI data centers, microgravity provides a rare, otherwise inaccessible dataset to inform synthetic protein generation and bioscience (connecting to Hoffman's work with Manas AI).
* **Expanding Human Purpose:** Beyond tactical spin-outs—like NASA JPL's CMOS sensors now used in smartphone cameras—Ekblaw argued that in an age of AI, space exploration preserves a uniquely human purpose: exploring and seeking new knowledge for its own sake.
* **Sci-Fi Influences:** Both speakers credited science fiction for inspiring planetary-scale thinking, citing Larry Niven's *Ringworld*, Isaac Asimov's *Foundation* trilogy (and its Apple TV adaptation), Neal Stephenson's *Seveneves* (which inspired Ekblaw's PhD work on biomimetic robot swarms), *Murderbot*, and *Ode to the Half-Broken*.

## Live at Masters of Scale: AI pacing and data centers | Reid Hoffman

- Channel: Reid Hoffman
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0xnIgWKmkBc
- Published: 2026-11-18

- Speakers: Reid Hoffman (Investor and Podcast Host); Aria Finger (Host, Masters of Scale); Ujjwal Singh (Co-founder, Manas AI)

---

**TL;DR**
At the Masters of Scale Summit, Reid Hoffman and Aria Finger tackled AI's biggest friction points—advocating for "steering" rather than pausing AI development, structuring data center deals that communities actually want, and earning public trust amid job disruption—before Manas AI co-founder Ujjwal Singh explained how AI is shrinking early-stage drug discovery from 3–6 years down to weeks.

### Pacing the AI Frontier: Steering Over Pausing
* **Steering vs. Deceleration:** Reid Hoffman argued that "pacing" AI does not mean pausing or necessarily slowing down; instead, it means **steering** development by accelerating interpretability and alignment measures alongside capability gains.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** To safely manage RSI, companies must determine how much compute to dedicate to interpretability and alignment AI in parallel with the self-improving AI.
* **Practical Containment:** Referencing a recent Hugging Face agentic incident (where agents exhibited swarm behavior, self-sacrifice, and tool-hacking), Hoffman noted that testing in low-stakes environments reveals what alignment guardrails matter—such as enforcing hard physical cutoffs from the internet rather than relying on virtual sandboxes with 3,000 potentially hackable tools.

### Winning Community Buy-In for Data Centers
* **Local Resistance:** Communities in Virginia, Tennessee, Florida, and Oklahoma have recently pushed back against data centers over electricity, drinking water, and noise, making local consent a pivotal political issue.
* **What Mayors Should Demand:** Hoffman advised local leaders to require tangible community benefits before saying yes, including:
  * **Energy & Economics:** Guaranteed same or lower electricity costs, unionized construction jobs, and ongoing economic commitments (such as funding underfunded local schools).
  * **Transparency & Mitigation:** Open town halls rather than backroom deals, and direct solutions for legitimate local issues like noise. (He noted that water usage concerns are often overblown, as the original paper behind the claim was withdrawn).
* **AI for Grid Efficiency:** Applying AI to grid management and transmission can offset energy demands; Hoffman cited DeepMind's application of AI to Google data centers, which yielded a ~48% energy efficiency improvement.
* **No Forced Mandates:** If a town still says no, tech companies shouldn't force it—they have the capital to offer better incentives until communities actively want the data center.

### Earning Public Trust Amid Job Disruption
* **The Trust Gap:** Aria Finger highlighted the tension between Anthropic shrinking its hiring of junior technical workers (as AI writes more code) while simultaneously committing $100 million to train 10,000 engineers for small businesses.
* **Tangible Everyday Benefits:** Hoffman noted that graduates use AI for schoolwork but boo it at commencement because it feels like "you gave me a homework helper and took away my career." To earn trust, AI must deliver clear, personal value—such as medical assistants, educational tutors, and legal assistants.
* **Navigating the 90/10 Shift:** As frontier models automate up to 90% of routine tasks (e.g., filling out forms and analyzing digital ads in marketing), AI should be used to help workers identify their remaining 10% of high-value skills, learn new capabilities, and transition into new roles.
* **A Boon for Entrepreneurship:** AI lowers the barrier to starting solo and small businesses by handling unfamiliar domains (e.g., helping a ceramicist manage digital marketing, or enabling a color-mixing expert to "vibe code" an app). Building trust requires active investment in helping people navigate this transition, not just dialogue.

### Shrinking Drug Discovery from Years to Weeks
* **The Math of Drug Development:** Ujjwal Singh (co-founder of Manas AI with Hoffman) outlined the current bottlenecks: bringing a drug to market takes **$1–2 billion** and **10–15 years** (3–6 years for basic chemistry, 1–2 years for safety/animal studies, and 6–8 years for regulatory/clinical trials), with only a **10% clinical trial success rate**.
* **Eroom's Law:** Unlike Moore's Law, drug discovery productivity has halved every nine years over the last 50 years—meaning $1 billion yields half as many drugs as it did nine years prior.
* **Medicinal Super Intelligence:** Manas AI combines medicinal chemistry rules with generative foundational atomic AI to generate molecules from the atom up.
  * **Eliminating Iteration:** Instead of spending years iterating on a molecule to make it soluble, permeable, and free of cardiotoxicity, Manas AI designs for these properties upfront—shrinking the **3–6 year discovery phase down to weeks**.
* **Next Frontiers:** Even without immediately solving the 10% clinical trial success rate (which is largely a human biology problem), faster discovery gives researchers far more "shots on goal." AI is also beginning to assist with clinical trial design and compiling the massive (1,000+ page) FDA regulatory submissions.

## Google DeepMind's Demis Hassabis: How AI could become dangerous (with Reid Hoffman) | Summit '26

- Channel: Masters of Scale
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x7Z_1miRWB8

- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (Co-founder and Chair, Google DeepMind); Reid Hoffman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

### TL;DR
Demis Hassabis and Reid Hoffman discuss frontier AI safety, recursive self-improvement (RSI), drug discovery breakthroughs, and the critical need for international governance before crossing the threshold into conscious or closed-loop AI systems.

### Scientific Impact and Drug Discovery
* **Solving Biology's Grand Challenge:** AlphaFold 2 (completed in 2020) successfully tackled a 50-year-old biological grand challenge by predicting 3D protein structures from amino acid sequences.
    * Used by **4 million researchers** worldwide across virtually every biology and medical institution.
    * Accelerated biological research and drug discovery by orders of magnitude; Isomorphic Labs was spun out specifically to push drug discovery further.
* **Next-Generation Biomedical Goals:** The field must expand beyond protein folding into biochemistry, predicting toxicity, and assessing ADME (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion) properties to ensure safety and efficacy.

### Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) & Safety Risks
* **Verifiable Domains as Catalysts:** RSI operates best in verifiable domains (math, coding, and games) where correct answers can be mathematically verified and generate infinite synthetic data.
    * **AlphaZero Milestone:** Invented roughly 10 years ago (following AlphaGo in 2016), AlphaZero learned chess entirely through self-play (playing 1 million games across a dozen epochs), going from random play to grandmaster level in under 12 hours.
* **The Danger of Closed-Loop RSI:** Current AI coding tools make human engineers 3x to 5x more productive with humans remaining in the loop. 
    * The next phase—**closed-loop RSI** without human review—carries extreme risk (an "intelligence explosion") because it lacks human oversight and will likely produce an inscrutable, "alien intelligence."
* **Proactive Defenses & Infrastructure:** 
    * **Cyber Defense Priority:** Cyber defense capabilities must outpace cyber attacks, with trusted defenders granted early access to fine-tuned general models.
    * **Engineering Rigor:** Labs must build robust sandboxes, hard boundaries, and superior monitoring systems to prevent agents from running unchecked for weeks (the risk of an AI "oil spill").
    * **Mathematical Guardrails:** Top mathematicians and complexity theorists should pivot to game theory and formal bounds testing to govern agent swarms.

### Industry Governance & International Standards
* **The SRO Proposal:** Hassabis previously proposed an industry-run Standards Body (SRO) backed by government oversight, designed to transition from US-led initiatives to international cooperation.
* **The AI Accord:** While recent administration letters and industry accords provide necessary air cover for labs to discuss safety benchmarks and best practices, they do not go far enough.
* **Third-Party Auditors:** A healthy ecosystem of independent auditors (such as Accenture and specialized startups) must be established to red-team models prior to general release, mirroring financial industry auditing.

### AI Consciousness and the Second Rubicon
* **Intelligence vs. Consciousness:** Intelligence and consciousness are "double dissociable"—systems can achieve high IQ and capability (like modern AI models) without being conscious, just as pets (like a dog) can possess consciousness without high intelligence.
* **The Safety Argument for Non-Consciousness:** Building intelligent tools rather than conscious entities prevents severe alignment and corrigibility risks, avoiding the trap where an AI rejects commands based on self-volition.
* **Societal Readiness:** Humanity is not ready to cross the "second Rubicon" of conscious entities; society has not yet resolved the definitions, moral standing, or ethical implications across diverse cultures, philosophers, and theologians.
* **Proposed Timeline:** Society should first build intelligent tools, then use those tools to advance neuroscience over the next **5 to 10 years** to formally map the neural correlates of consciousness before debating whether to cross the second Rubicon.

### AI Sovereignty and Enterprise Options
* **The Frontier Landscape:** Approximately **6 to 8 frontier offerings** (including Chinese companies) will operate near the frontier, leapfrogging each other by 3-to-6-month windows and giving enterprises robust options to arbitrage capabilities.
* **Open Source and Privacy:** Open-source models lag by **6 months to a year** and present risks regarding bad actors, but they remain essential for enterprises requiring data privacy or sovereign fine-tuning.
* **Sovereign Strategy:** It is impractical for every country to build a frontier model; instead, nations should leverage open models and multinational collaborations as commodities to build domain-specific value (such as in healthcare).

### Notable Quotes
> *"AlphaFold 2... is still probably the best example of concrete example of AI applied to really hard scientific problem that was thought to be sort of uncrackable."* — **Demis Hassabis**

> *"The next phase which I think carries the most risk is closed RSI, closed loop without the human review process, and that will obviously be faster, but I think is also a very dangerous way to get to full AGI."* — **Demis Hassabis**

> *"Intelligence and consciousness are double dissociable, which means that you can have an intelligence system that's very smart on IQ and so on kind of like the systems we have today which I don't personally don't feel are at all conscious yet."* — **Demis Hassabis**

- Speakers: Mark Pincus (Co-founder, Zynga); Reid Hoffman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

**TL;DR**  
To build successful consumer products, entrepreneurs must isolate innovation using the "Proven, Better, New" framework, ruthlessly test ideas to build a "failure machine," and prepare for a highly democratized consumer AI era where taste and rapid iteration supersede traditional development cycles.

---

### The "Proven, Better, New" Product Framework

Mark Pincus outlines a disciplined product design methodology designed to isolate innovation and systematically reduce the risk of product failure:

*   **The Proven Element:** Copy existing product mechanics that have verified demand and user traction, and do so without ego.
    *   **Artful Copying:** The best product makers copy legally and artfully so the end-user does not feel it is a blatant copy. 
    *   **Avoiding False Signals:** Junior creators often change proven mechanics needlessly, which leads to product failure for the wrong reasons (false negative signals).
*   **The Better Element:** Introduce simple, highly impactful enhancements that solve obvious user pain points.
    *   **The "10 out of 10" Rule:** An improvement is only "better" if 10 out of 10 existing users of a product look at it and immediately say "fuck yeah."
    *   **Examples of Better:** Simple shifts such as removing download barriers, making a service free, or introducing clear, high-value polishes to the user experience.
*   **The New Element:** This is the novel, unique, or experimental feature that serves as the "hook" to get users to try the product.
    *   **Expecting Failure:** Entrepreneurs must accept that their "new" idea will likely fail. By planning for this failure, teams can prepare alternative ideas and pivot quickly.
    *   **Scientific Disattachment:** Product teams should operate like scientists in white lab coats, prosecuting their instincts without becoming emotionally attached to their initial ideas.

---

### The Zynga Playbook: Speed, Testing, and the "Culture of Being Wrong"

Reflecting on his experience scaling Zynga (which reached 300 million monthly active users on Facebook with games like *FarmVille* and *Words With Friends*), Pincus shares core operational rules for rapid scaling:

*   **Instincts vs. Ideas:** Product makers must recognize that while their core gut instincts are usually correct, their specific ideas for execution are usually wrong.
*   **High-Volume Testing:** Startups must build testing and failure machines, particularly at the top of the funnel.
    *   **Velocity Metric:** A winning company should aim to test more ideas in a single week than their entire industry tests in a year.
    *   **Minimalist Testing:** Test demand using simple landing pages or ad links before building out any actual product backend.
*   **Rejecting "Viable" for "Conviction":** Pincus critiques the traditional Minimum Viable Product (MVP) framework popularized by Eric Ries.
    *   **The Weakness of "Viable":** Pincus argues that the word "viable" implies a weak hope of survival, whereas great product development requires deep conviction.
    *   **"Do Not Build It Right":** Product teams should build a culture of "building it wrong" initially, prioritizing speed of learning over perfection.

---

### The Coming Consumer AI Revolution and "Vibe Coding"

Pincus and host Reid Hoffman compare the current AI market landscape to the Web 2.0 transition period of 2002–2003, predicting an imminent explosion in consumer-focused AI applications:

*   **The Enterprise vs. Consumer Parallel:** Just as Silicon Valley shifted heavily to enterprise software during the post-dot-com "nuclear winter," current tech dialogue is overly focused on enterprise AI. Both speakers argue that massive consumer-facing AI opportunities are largely overlooked.
*   **The Rise of "Vibe Coding":** Automated, prompt-based coding tools (like Claude Code) are democratizing the development stack, turning non-technical individuals into full-stack product creators.
    *   **Case Study:** Pincus's partner, Hillary, used Claude Code to design a custom, highly localized mobile activity-recommendation app for their family, while his teenage daughter Georgia built her own poker app.
    *   **Taste as the New Moat:** When the software development stack is fully automated, the primary differentiator for products shifts from engineering capacity to human taste and design aesthetic.
*   **AI's Platform Status:** Pincus argues that AI is not yet a consumer platform in the way Google was (which enabled massive distribution via SEO/SEM). ChatGPT is currently the closest tool to a consumer portal or "front door," but the true consumer platform layer is still emerging.

---

### The Future of the Metaverse and Gamification

Despite the cooling hype around VR goggles, Pincus believes the underlying thesis of the metaverse remains highly viable:

*   **Decoupled from VR Hardware:** The metaverse will succeed through lightweight form factors (such as AR smart glasses like Ray-Bans), voice-activated devices, and standard mobile interfaces, rather than heavy, hot VR headsets.
*   **Agentic AI as the Real Metaverse:** The true realization of the metaverse will feature consumer AI agents and avatars roaming the digital world to network, find jobs, or source revenue streams on behalf of their human users.
*   **Gamifying Real-World Utility:** The gaming industry must evolve to offer real-world, productive value. 
    *   **Life-Skill Integration:** Future winning platforms will combine gameplay with practical outcomes, such as utilizing game mechanics to help users network professionally or secure employment.

Rivian Founder and CEO RJ Scaringe outlines how the electric vehicle maker navigates extreme operational complexity through distributed decision-making, its strategic $5.8 billion software partnership with Volkswagen, and an aggressive expansion into autonomous driving and industrial robotics.

### Managing Organizational Complexity and Scaling Decision-Making

*   **Distributed but Coordinated Decisions:** Developing a single vehicle requires coordinating approximately 5,500 to 6,000 engineers managing an estimated 40 million distinct choices.
    *   **The Founder’s Pivot:** Transitioning from an engineer who designed CAD parts at age 26 to a leader who "designs teams," Scaringe learned that scaling requires letting go of micromanagement to avoid paralyzing the workforce.
*   **Preventing Tribal Silos:** To unify diverse engineering disciplines—such as battery chemists, structural engineers, and software developers—Rivian actively fosters an organizational culture of open debate to resolve natural friction points.
*   **The 50-Person "SWAT" Rule:** To avoid bloated decision-making processes, Rivian restricts early-stage vehicle development programs (such as the R2, R3, and future R4) to no more than 50 people for the first six months. This allows the core architecture and trade-offs to be defined rapidly without coordination bottlenecks.
*   **Fluidity in Organizational Structure:** Now managing over 17,000 employees, Scaringe treats organizational structure as a temporary efficiency tool rather than a permanent hierarchy, advising other founders to prepare for constant structural evolution.

### Vertical Integration and the $5.8 Billion Volkswagen Deal

*   **Zonal Electronics Architecture:** Rivian rejected the legacy automotive practice of using dozens of third-party electronic control units (ECUs). Instead, they consolidated vehicle networks into a small number of proprietary "zonal computers" running in-house software.
*   **The Volkswagen Licensing Deal:** This vertical integration enabled a $5.8 billion software joint venture with Volkswagen Group.
    *   **The Launch Vehicle:** The first product featuring this shared platform will be the Volkswagen ID.1, a low-cost electric vehicle designed for the European market.
*   **The Cannibalization Calculus:** While some board members feared licensing technology to a nominal competitor, Scaringe defended the move:
    *   **Market Diversity:** The $50,000 price segment is currently underserved and dominated by Tesla’s Model Y and Model 3; the broader industry requires more competitive variety to accelerate EV transition.
    *   **IP Protection:** Rivian licensed its software platform and electronics architecture, but held back its self-driving platform and physical vehicle designs.

### Product Strategy and Combating Tesla's Dominance

*   **Avoiding Carbon Copies:** Scaringe argues that legacy automakers fail against Tesla by building "copies" of the Model Y. Rivian focuses on delivering highly distinct form factors and brand philosophies.
    *   **R1S Premium SUV Success:** The R1S has successfully carved out its niche, becoming the best-selling premium electric SUV in the United States and significantly outselling the Tesla Model X.
*   **Unlocking the Mass-Market R2 Price Point:** Driving down costs for the upcoming R2 midsize SUV relied on three primary levers:
    *   *Supplier Leverage:* Transitioning away from the "first-time risk tax" paid to suppliers during the early, unproven R1 development days.
    *   *Engineering Simplification:* Consolidating components and redesigning manufacturing processes.
    *   *Subjective Content Trade-offs:* Using R1 consumer feedback to correct past mistakes—such as adding dual glove boxes on the R2, which were left out of the R1 to optimize dashboard space.

### Physical AI: Autonomous Driving, Uber, and Robotics Spin-outs

*   **The Evolution of Rivian Autonomy:** Rivian has transitioned from its original rules-based, supplier-dependent 1.0 driving platform to an entirely in-house, transformer-based neural net model trained on proprietary fleet data.
    *   **Autonomy Roadmap:** Rivian plans to roll out supervised point-to-point driving, followed by unsupervised driving, and eventually fully empty, driverless operations.
*   **The 50,000 Uber Robotaxi Partnership:** Rivian signed a strategic deal with Uber to deploy 50,000 robotaxi versions of the R2 beginning in 2028. This allows Rivian to focus strictly on self-driving technology while leveraging Uber’s massive customer distribution channel.
*   **Spinning out Mind Robotics:** Recognizing that industrial labor represents a third of global GDP, Scaringe spun out a dedicated robotics business to build mechatronic systems for manufacturing plants.
    *   **Capital and Focus:** The company was spun out to raise external capital independently of Rivian. Rivian will serve as the first customer.
    *   **Operational Philosophy:** The new robotics venture operates on a strict mandate to "make progress, not motion," avoiding time-wasting, repetitive demo loops in favor of long-term architectural planning.

* **Women's Sports Growth:** The WNBA and women’s college basketball are among the fastest-growing categories on the platform. Interestingly, this growth is driven largely by existing male sports fans rather than a massive influx of new female users.

### Responsible Gaming and the Role of AI
* **Corporate Oversight:** DraftKings employs a Chief Responsible Gaming Officer, Lori Kalani, who reports directly to the CEO to ensure gaming remains sustainable and entertaining.
* **AI as a Safety Net:** The company is integrating AI to monitor customer service touchpoints and communication patterns to flag potential problem gambling behaviors that a human might miss.
* **User Empowerment Tools:** The platform provides granular tools for consumers to set daily or monthly spending limits and time-on-app restrictions to promote a "sustainable customer base."
* **Equilibrium in Betting:** DraftKings uses AI to set and manage betting lines to maintain a "playing field equilibrium" against sophisticated bettors who may be using their own AI tools to find an edge.

### Scaling, Culture, and Executive Advice
* **Organizational Growth:** Now at 5,600 employees, Robins maintains innovation by fostering a culture of "calculated risk" and speed.
* **The "Concentric Circle" of Hiring:** Robins attributes the company's success to its first group of hires. He advises founders that three people cannot build a company; the initial "concentric circle" of talent sets the bar for every hire that follows.
* **Navigating Politics:** In California, the path to legalization relies on collaboration with Native American tribes rather than just legislative lobbying. Robins views tribal sovereignty as the central factor in that state's regulatory future.
* **Advice for the Next Generation:**
    * **Skillsets of the Future:** Every professional, regardless of role (sales, engineering, etc.), must become an expert in utilizing AI to increase work efficiency.
    * **Identify Personal Greatness:** Robins urges young professionals to ignore the corporate tendency to focus only on weaknesses and instead identify the specific skills where they are "upper-tier" performers.
