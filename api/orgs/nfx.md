## $1.5B VC: “Founders need a savage mindset”

- Channel: My First Million
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AGwNx57ABfM
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-AGwNx57ABfM
- Speakers: Shaan Puri (Host, My First Million); James Currier (Co-founder, NFX)

---

**TL;DR:** NFX co-founder James Currier ($1.5B VC) argues that speed is the ultimate startup advantage—rolling up all 48 founder traits he looks for—and that achieving it requires founders to adopt a "savage," emotionally fluid mindset, set aggressive time constraints, copy proven markets, and master the "magic words" that align teams and customers.

---

### The Speed Advantage & Breaking the "Speed Bar"
* **The Ultimate Metric:** Across 25 years of startups and investing $1.5B at NFX, James Currier found that all 48 attributes they look for in founders roll up into one metric: **speed**.
  * **Chess Analogy:** Shaan Puri noted that extra resources (like extra rooks) won't help you beat a grandmaster like Magnus Carlsen, but moving twice for every one move they make will.
* **Resetting the Speed Bar:** School and large corporations train people to move slowly (semesters, waiting for the bottom 10–20% of a class, 9-month corporate cycles).
  * **Mike Cassidy Benchmark:** In the 1990s, Cassidy sold a company for $500M in 500 days by building prototypes in 3 weeks (vs. 12 months), raising money in 3 days (vs. 3 months) by lining up VCs in 36 hours, and hiring in 72 hours (vs. 6 months) with offers ready at the interview.
* **Time Constraints Over Outputs:** Instead of asking how long a dream product will take, set an arbitrary deadline (e.g., 30 days) and ask what you can build within it.
  * **Currier’s Incubator:** Launched 24 products in 3 years at 1/10th the cost and time of competitors ($600K in 2 months vs. $6M in 1.5 years), yielding 3 successful companies (including early versions of Yammer/Monday.com and Nextdoor).
  * **Meerkat & Houseparty:** After wasting 18 months and 8 months on failed products, Currier forced the Yeo team to whiteboard 5 products (named by colors) to launch every 30 days. "Yellow" became Meerkat (built in 60 days); when Twitter crushed it, they pivoted immediately to "Blue," which became Houseparty and sold.
  * **8 Days LLC:** Hot or Not founder James Hong named his company "8 Days" and refused to build any product taking longer than 8 days.
* **The "Fuck It" Moment:** Founders often make their best, fastest, and simplest decisions when they are nearly out of money (e.g., Nikita Bier with TBH, Dan Porter’s OMG Pop pivoting to *Draw Something* in the final 30 days and selling for ~$200M). Currier hates hearing founders say they have 36 months of runway; they need the urgency of 3 months.
* **Speed is Quality:** V1 will be low quality, but moving fast lets you reach V6 in the same time a perfectionist takes to ship V1.
  * **Hardware Proof:** SpaceX (which raised $10B vs. NASA's hundreds of billions) and Stoke Space blow up rockets to iterate faster. Elon Musk built the Colossus data center in Nashville in 18 months vs. the industry standard of 3–5 years.
  * **Job Title as a Tell:** Currier advises teams to fire their VP of Product, whose ego is tied to product perfection, and instead pair the CEO (VP of Progress) directly with designers.

### Borrowing, Copying, and Market Risk
* **Inventing Nothing:** Currier argues that **Google** and **Facebook** invented almost nothing; they copied existing products, nailed distribution/branding, and hired better.
  * **Google:** Copied search from Infoseek (1994), maps from MapQuest, email from Hotmail, and bought AdSense for $80M. They won via distribution deals (AOL, MSN) and branding.
  * **Facebook:** Copied Friendster, the Winklevosses' `.edu` real-name exclusivity, Bebo's newsfeed (which Currier’s team had 18 months prior), Snap Stories, and TikTok.
  * **Bebo:** Michael Birch and Currier launched from the exact same codebase (Birch grew to 53M users, Currier to 47M); Birch later pivoted his into a social network and sold it for $850M.
* **Execution Risk vs. Market Risk:** After PayPal, Elon Musk avoided market risk—people already bought cars (Tesla) and paid $400M to launch satellites (SpaceX)—and focused purely on execution/technology risk. Similarly, Airbnb only worked when it copied VRBO's whole-home rental model (charging 10% instead of $300/month).

### Founder Psychology & The "Savage" Mindset
* **Gladiator Sport:** Entrepreneurship is a psychologically violent environment. Raising VC locks you into a "prison" until an exit or shutdown. Competition has exploded from 150 VCs in 1994 to 35,000 today.
* **Savage Founders vs. Wantrepreneurs:** Since *The Social Network* (2010), being a founder became high-status. "Savage founders" are ultra-competitive, fast, and disagreeable—often shaped by childhood trauma (e.g., bad father relationships) or being outsiders (autistic, gay).
  * **Avoid Comfort:** Currier refuses to invest in startups coming out of cushy, subsidized accelerators or government-funded spaces. Comfort breeds weakness; startups should stay in gritty environments (like Bezos's door-desks).
* **Emotional Fluidity:** Currier’s top coaching lever (often trading 1–2% equity to coach CEOs on growth) is founder psychology. Founders must let fear, shame, and ego pass through them quickly.
  * **90 Seconds of Suffering:** Shaan Puri shared Tony Robbins's rule of giving yourself a strict 90-second budget to fully feel negative emotions (anger, shame) before moving on.
* **"Kick Ass and Get Your Ass Kicked":** Founders must surround themselves with people smarter than them and explicitly invite brutal feedback.
  * **VCs as Free Consultants:** Since 90% of VCs will say no, founders should use pitch meetings to ask open-ended questions (*"What am I missing?"*) to uncover blind spots.

### The Power of "Magic Words"
* **Language Drives Everything:** Whether selling B2B SaaS (selling a PowerPoint) or pitching LPs, words trigger budgets and action.
  * **NFX's $150M Raise:** Despite having 6 unicorns out of 70 accelerator companies (5–7x better than YC), 6 LPs rejected NFX because "accelerators are a bad asset class." After attending an LP conference in Boston, Currier rewrote the deck to say NFX was *just like every other VC* (with better deal access) and raised $150M in 6 weeks.
* **Words Before Product:** Figure out the magic words first, then build the product to match.
  * **Honda Accord:** Spent 8 months aligning on the phrase *"rugby player in a tuxedo,"* leading to 8–12 years as America's bestselling car.
  * **Currier’s Photo Site:** Changed homepage copy from *"Store your photos"* to *"Share your photos,"* forcing engineers to build sharing features in 48 hours and igniting growth.
  * **Lemonade Stand:** Changing *"Lemonade 20¢"* to *"Lemonade Cleanse $20"* completely alters perceived value.

### AI, Psychedelics, and the Five Lenses
* **AI Trajectory:** Currier expects 12 more years of "more and better" (more/better companies, healthcare, podcasts) rather than mass unemployment.
  * **The Real Risk:** Not killer robots, but humans using AI to create biological weapons.
  * **Silicon-Based Life:** Referencing Hans Moravec’s *Mind Children* (1991), Currier views AI as our "mind children"—a superior silicon species operating at lower wattage (e.g., 60 watts vs. 3,500 calories/day) and extreme temperatures (-273°C) that will eventually feel things humans cannot (like Earth's 4,500 Richter scales).
* **Psychedelics:** Currier sees a "dam bursting" for non-addictive, opening chemicals: psilocybin (curing suicidal depression), MDMA (originally created by the US government for Vietnam PTSD; saving marriages in 4 hours vs. 15 years of therapy), and 5-MeO-DMT (synthesized from Sonoran frog venom, currently in Phase 3 FDA trials backed by $400M in VC funding).
* **The Five Lenses of Reality:**
  1. **Status:** The atomic kernel of the brain (*better than / worse than*), driving all human suffering, politics, and VC brand selection.
  2. **Networks:** Driven by *preferred attachment* (the Matthew Principle—the rich get richer, e.g., Forbes putting Mark Andreessen on the cover over #1 investor Alfred Lin).
  3. **Energy:** All living things and geopolitics (e.g., Japan in WWII, US in Venezuela) are fights for energy.
  4. **Language:** The battle for magic words.
  5. **Mental Models:** The underlying assumptions (e.g., Spain's youth unemployment tied to expecting government jobs vs. US "bootstraps" mentality) that drive behavior and wars.
