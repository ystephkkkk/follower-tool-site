### TL;DR
Brendan Foody, CEO of Mercor, explains how Reinforcement Learning (RL) environments—comprising worlds, apps, and tasks—are replacing low-skilled crowdsourced data to train agentic AI models. He highlights how this technology is shifting from frontier labs to application-layer companies, which are building proprietary datasets as their primary competitive moat.

### Evolution of AI Training Data
- **Shift to Agentic Data:** The industry transitioned away from 2020-era crowdsourced behavior cloning (supervised fine-tuning and simple RLHF) toward hiring world-class experts (software engineers, lawyers, doctors, bankers) to build frontier evals and RL environments.
- **The Three Pillars of RL Environments:** Brendan Foody defines an RL environment as consisting of:
  - **Worlds:** Messages, slides, docs, sheets, and files that mirror real-world projects.
  - **Apps:** High-fidelity clones of popular tools like Salesforce, ServiceNow, and Microsoft 365, accessed via MCP, CLI, or Kua.
  - **Tasks:** Prompts paired with rubric-based verifiers or unit tests for evaluation and training.
- **Scaling Expert Networks:** Mercor’s expert talent network scaled to **2.5 million expert hours in Q2 alone**, driven by the massive distribution of jobs in the Bureau of Labor Statistics (GDP eval) that require specialized app scenarios and tasks.

### Why Humans Remain Essential
- **Measuring Beyond the Frontier:** Humans are necessary to create rubrics because models struggle to grade their own complex work (e.g., slide decks or legal memos) and cannot reliably identify their own mistakes without a professor-like rubric.
- **Solving Verifier Complexity:** Crafting comprehensive verifiers requires understanding vast problem spaces and dozens of potential errors. Mercor uses a process called **trajectory analysis** (rolling out 10 to 100 trajectories with agentic quality control and human review) to ensure auto-grader scores align with human judgment.
- **Nuanced Synthetic Data:** While models act as helpful co-pilots for experts populating environments and writing outlines, humans are indispensable for setting the ground truth and avoiding noisy reward hacking.

### Real-World Legal Environment & Post-Training Results
- **Apex Agents Legal Environment:** Built in collaboration with lawyers from top firms like Latham and Watkins, this environment simulates a real big-law project with a comprehensive data room rendered into Google Workspace clones.
- **Task Example:** Evaluating maximum total liability for *Star Tanker Tankers International Limited* versus *Cooper Jefferies Energy Corporation* under the Oil and Petroleum Act.
- **Dramatic Performance Jumps:** Post-training GLM 47 on just **1,800 tasks** with about **$500k in compute** yielded massive gains:
  - Corporate law performance jumped from **4.7% to 26.6%**.
  - Generalization was observed across GDP eval and Apex V1 even without data rooms.

### Data Pricing and Curation Models
- **Pricing Strategies:** Mercor prices data based on customer value (target model improvement on leaderboards) and cost structures (paying experts **$150/hour**, resulting in cost bases ranging from **$50 to $10,000 per task**).
- **Three Data Curation Options:**
  - **Custom Tasks:** Bespoke data creation where customers pay **$2,000 per task** (some frontier labs buy **50,000 tasks a month**; tasks can take humans anywhere from a few hours to a month to complete).
  - **Off-the-Shelf Data:** Pre-built datasets funded by Mercor's multi-hundred-million-dollar investments, favored by neo-labs.
  - **Hourly Experts:** Direct organization of talent networks (used historically by companies like Harvey).

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Adoption Timeline:** Brendan Foody predicts that over the next **12 months**, dozens of application-layer companies will build their own industry-leading models and own their intelligence, mirroring Cursor's trajectory.
- **Horizons and Co-workers:** Future RL environments will shift toward **ultra-long-horizon tasks** taking a human **100 to 1,000 hours** (up from current sub-10-hour limits) and introduce **virtual co-workers** to bridge the realism gap in social and multi-agent workplace interactions.
