### TL;DR
Neil Movva, co-founder of Sail Research, explains how his company acts as a "token factory" to drive down AI intelligence costs by focusing on long-running background agents, distributed non-traditional hardware, and cheap decentralized power.

### The Token Factory & The Shift to Background Agents
* **The Token Factory Concept:** 
  * Sail Research operates an API serving open-source large language models at unbeatable market prices, hosting long-running agent virtual machines ("sandboxes") designed for tasks running hours, days, or weeks.
  * The overarching corporate theme is abundance, striving to make intelligence a cheap, sustainable commodity for every industry.
* **Real-Time vs. Background Workloads:** 
  * Current market infrastructure is overly focused on low-latency inference (pulled by tools like Cursor) because chatbots require humans to wait.
  * Moving forward, the industry will shift from a 50/50 split to a 90/10 split in favor of background workloads where latency is irrelevant ("the best latency is no latency").
* **Autonomous Use Cases:** 
  * Long-horizon tasks like deep research (e.g., Parallel Web Systems indexing the entire internet) and cybersecurity proof-of-work (e.g., Fable/Mythos pen-testing software autonomously) thrive best under background agent architectures.

### Hardware, Throughput, and the "Scavenger" Strategy
* **Throughput Over Latency:** 
  * While Nvidia GPUs and NVLink interconnects dominate low-latency, real-time chatbot serving, background inference prioritizes massive throughput and hardware efficiency over speed.
* **The Heterogeneous Chip Play:** 
  * Sail applies a "scavenger" philosophy: buying any chip anywhere (including AMD, TPUs, Tranium, and emerging accelerators from Etched, Tenstorrent/Senova, or DMatrix) at the right price, building custom parallelism schemes rather than relying solely on high-end Nvidia infrastructure.
* **Memory Constraints & KV Cache:** 
  * While weight storage can be optimized via SRAM/DRAM hybrids (such as Cerebras wafer-scale engines), the dynamic KV cache remains a massive bottleneck at long context lengths (e.g., 1 million tokens), driving ongoing architectural innovation in compression.

### Data Centers, Power, and Distributed Infrastructure
* **Distributed 1-Megawatt Data Centers:** 
  * Unlike training clusters that demand massive 1-gigawatt centralized footprints with robust power redundancy, inference fleets can utilize distributed 1-megawatt configurations (fitting into roughly 8 refrigerator-sized racks).
* **Embracing Low Uptime for Economics:** 
  * Sail utilizes unglamorous, distributed data centers with lower uptime (e.g., 95%) and single-line fiber connectivity, letting a robust control plane dynamically reroute workloads when hardware drops.
* **Intermittent Renewable Energy:** 
  * Leveraging under-tapped solar and wind power, Sail models weather patterns to shift workloads away from regions experiencing prolonged generation lulls, accessing cheap power that traditional data center operators reject.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Neil Movva predicts** that by the end of the year, background and real-time workloads will sit at a 50/50 split, eventually shifting to **90/10 in favor of background workloads**.
