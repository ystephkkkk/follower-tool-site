**TL;DR:** The conflict with Iran has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, causing a massive 6.7 million barrel-per-day "shut-in" of Middle Eastern oil; if military operations extend beyond two weeks, the global economic ramifications could be catastrophic.

### The Energy Crisis and Strait of Hormuz Blockage
* **Strait of Hormuz "Parking Lot":** Helima Croft reports that the Strait is effectively closed. While the U.S. administration claims occasional ships pass through, normal flow is 60–100 ships per day; currently, flows are nowhere near normalized.
* **Oil "Shut-ins":** Middle Eastern producers (specifically Iraq and the UAE) are being forced to "shut in" production because they lack storage capacity for oil that cannot be exported.
    * **Iraq Impact:** Iraq has already shut in approximately 3 million barrels of its 4.3 million barrel-per-day capacity.
    * **Total Regional Impact:** Roughly 6.7 million barrels per day have been shut in across the Middle East, representing a significant portion of the ~103 million barrels consumed globally.
* **LNG Blockage:** Qatari LNG is completely blocked. This creates a severe supply crisis for Europe and Asia, as Europe had been using Qatari gas to backfill the loss of Russian energy supplies.
* **Restart Challenges:** Restarting oil fields and liquefaction facilities is not a "light switch" operation. A protracted shutdown could lead to long-term infrastructure damage and delayed recovery.

### U.S. Strategic Vulnerabilities
* **Depleted SPR:** The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) currently stands at approximately 400 million barrels. It was not refilled after the 180-million-barrel drawdown following the Russian invasion of Ukraine. 
    * **Limited Buffer:** With 20 million barrels of crude and products normally moving through the Strait of Hormuz daily, the current SPR provides a very thin "shock absorber" for a total disruption.
* **The "Trump Doctrine":** Croft notes a shift from the historic "Carter Doctrine" (which viewed any threat to Persian Gulf energy as a threat to U.S. security) to a "Trump Doctrine" focused on the "loss of American life."
    * **Iranian Strategy:** Iran is attempting to "internationalize the cost" of the war by targeting energy prices to see if President Trump’s "pain point" is $4.00-per-gallon gasoline ahead of midterms.

### Military Timelines and Escalation Risks
* **The Two-Week Window:** Pentagon sources suggest limited military aims (targeting missiles, launchers, and naval assets) could be wrapped up in two weeks. However, regional leaders fear even two weeks is too long for the global economy to sustain a total blockade.
* **"Winning" Ambiguity:** There is significant uncertainty regarding U.S. war aims. Potential "mission creep" includes:
    * Removing the Supreme Leader.
    * Securing 400kg of highly enriched uranium (which may require ground forces).
    * Total regime change (favored by Israel).
* **The Houthi "Reserve":** The Houthis in Yemen have not yet entered the conflict. If they do, they could threaten the Saudi East-West Pipeline and Red Sea shipping, closing off the only remaining alternative route for Saudi oil.

### Market Volatility and Corporate Earnings
* **Geopolitical Uncertainty:** Steve Eisman notes that traditional equity analytical tools (discounting future cash flows) are currently useless. The market is trading exclusively on headlines and oil price fluctuations ($100/barrel triggers sell-offs; sub-$90 triggers rallies).
* **Oracle (ORCL) Case Study:** Oracle serves as a bellwether for AI sentiment. 
    * **The Crash:** The stock fell over 50% from its October 2025 peak due to jitters over OpenAI's backlog and fears that Oracle must fund massive AI CapEx with long-term debt rather than cash flow.
    * **Recent Results:** Oracle beat 3Q25 expectations with $17.19B in revenue (up 22% YoY) and $1.79 EPS, though investors remain wary of the balance sheet.
