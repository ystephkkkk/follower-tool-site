## Why Secondary Markets Are Eating the IPO | All-In Liquidity Secondary Markets Panel

- Channel: All-In Podcast

- Speakers: Brad Gerstner (Founder & CEO, Altimeter Capital); Gavin Baker (Managing Partner & CIO, Atreides Management); Kelly Rodriques (CEO, Forge Global)

---

**TL;DR**  
The secondary market has reached record volumes, doubling its 2021 peak to become a primary liquidity mechanism that actively competes with IPOs. As companies choose to stay private longer, structured secondary markets are shifting from a shadow market into a highly regulated, democratized asset class, forcing venture capitalists to adopt active public-style selling strategies to return capital to LPs.

---

### ## The Exploding Scale of Secondary Markets
* **Record-Breaking Volume:** Secondary market transactions have reached record highs, doubling the previous peak observed at the end of 2021. 
* **Competing with Traditional Exits:** Employee and early investor secondaries now represent 31% of all primary venture activity in 2025, solidifying secondaries as a major alternative to traditional IPOs and M&A.
* **Shift to Premium Pricing:** In previous years, secondary shares traded at an average 20% discount (80 cents on the dollar) to incentivize buyers. By Q1 2025, secondary transactions surged to a premium of 106% of net asset value (NAV).
* **The Rise of "Quasi-Public" Giants:** Massive late-stage private companies—such as SpaceX, Anthropic, Anduril, and Databricks—now trade on secondary desks daily, effectively operating as public companies in terms of volume and interest without the associated regulatory disclosure burdens.

### ## The Debate on Staying Private vs. Going Public
* **The Danger of Private Market "Sycophancy":** Both Gavin Baker (Atreides) and Brad Gerstner (Altimeter) argue that staying private for too long harms executive decision-making. 
    * **Lack of Clean Feedback:** Private board members and investors often act as "sycophants," telling founders what they want to hear to protect their allocations for future rounds.
    * **Public Market Accountability:** Public markets provide immediate, rigorous, and unsentimental price discovery and pressure testing that forces management to execute cleanly.
* **The Mark Zuckerberg HTML5 Lesson:** Gavin Baker highlighted Facebook's historical 2010–2012 delay in building mobile native apps (relying too long on HTML5). Zuckerberg later reflected that had Facebook been public during that internal debate, rigorous questioning from public equity analysts would have forced him to correct the mistake years faster.
* **The Necessity of Liquidity for Talent:** With companies remaining private for decades (e.g., SpaceX for 24 years), employees frequently find themselves paper-rich but cash-poor. Structured liquidity programs are essential to allow early, hard-working employees to buy homes and support families without forcing the entire company into an premature IPO.

### ## Democratization and the Forge-Schwab Alliance
* **The Schwab Partnership:** Kelly Rodriques (CEO of Forge) detailed Forge’s landmark deal with Charles Schwab, which integrates Forge's private market trading infrastructure with Schwab’s 46 million retail accounts and $12 trillion in assets under management. 
* **Regulated Retail Access:** Private market access is rapidly evolving from high-fee, unregulated Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs) toward regulated, diversified fund structures. 
    * **Interval and Closed-End Funds:** New fund products allow unaccredited retail investors to buy diversified private market baskets (featuring companies like SpaceX) with minimum investments as low as $500.
    * **Accreditation Changes:** Anticipated changes to SEC rules are expected to introduce a "sophisticated investor" test, replacing arbitrary wealth thresholds and further opening access to the private markets.
* **Systematizing Price Discovery:** Historically, secondary trading operated like a "ticket broker" grey market with fragmented bids. Forge is building an exchange-like infrastructure to provide clean, standardized price discovery for late-stage private equities.

### ## Active Portfolio Management and Venture Capital Exits
* **DPI Imperialism:** With LPs aggressively demanding Distributed to Paid-In Capital (DPI), venture capitalists can no longer afford to hold late-stage positions indefinitely. Brad Gerstner emphasized that Altimeter actively sells 30% to 40% slices of high-performing private positions at 4x or 5x valuations to return capital to LPs.
* **Crossover and Mutual Fund Constraints:** Large asset managers (like Fidelity, Wellington, and Baillie Gifford) are capped by SEC rules from holding more than 15% of their mutual funds in illiquid private assets (with most self-imposing stricter limits of 3% to 7%). 
    * **Unlocking Dry Powder:** When major private holdings finally go public and lockups expire, these assets move out of the restricted "private" bucket. This structural shift will unlock hundreds of billions of dollars in fresh, institutional late-stage demand.
* **Active Public-Style Discipline:** VCs must transition from a pure "buy and hold" mindset to active portfolio management, determining daily whether they should buy, hold, or sell, regardless of a company's private status.

### ## Top Late-Stage Private Targets of Interest
The panel highlighted several high-conviction private companies currently driving secondary interest:
* **Revolut:** Recommended by Chamath Palihapitiya as a premier European neo-bank built on a next-generation software stack. The company is actively unbundling incumbent banks, expanding rapidly in the US, and showing strong profitability across 14 lines of business.
* **Zipline:** A highly successful autonomous drone delivery network. The company achieved operational scale by delivering critical medical supplies in Africa—reducing maternal mortality by 90% to 95% in key regions—and is now successfully scaling commercial operations in the US.
* **Aria & DriveNets:** Highlighted by Gavin Baker as critical infrastructure plays. As AI data centers grow more specialized, these companies are rewriting the networking layer to synchronize disaggregated chips and coordinate prefill/decode processing workloads.
* **Sierra:** Co-founded by Bret Taylor and highlighted by Brad Gerstner. The company focuses on building sophisticated, agent-native customer service, sales, and marketing platforms.
* **Neural Robotics:** Highlighted by Kelly Rodriques as a German-based, AI-powered logistics robotics company that is quietly generating $100 million in revenue outside the Silicon Valley echo chamber.

## Gavin Baker on Orbital Compute, TSMC, and Frontier Models

---

1. **TL;DR**  
The artificial intelligence expansion represents the most extraordinary acceleration of revenue and compute demand in the history of capitalism, driven by unprecedented inflections like Anthropic’s massive ARR growth. However, sustaining this trajectory requires overcoming physical constraints in energy ("watts") and silicon manufacturing ("wafers"), forcing a shift toward custom silicon, usage-based enterprise pricing, and highly advanced orbital compute networks.

---

### The Unprecedented Scale of AI Growth & Valuation Dynamics

* **The Anthropic Milestone:** Anthropic added approximately $11 billion in ARR in an incredibly short period. To put this in perspective, it took three of the most successful SaaS companies of the last decade—Palantir, Snowflake, and Databricks—ten years of collective operations and tens of thousands of employees to build businesses of that combined scale.
* **Disparities in Capital Efficiency:** Anthropic has operated with roughly 80% less capital burn than OpenAI to reach a comparable revenue scale. Consequently, Anthropic is positioned to potentially generate positive cash flow this year, demonstrating a significantly higher structural return on invested capital (ROIC).
* **The Transition to Usage-Based Pricing:** AI is rapidly shifting from "all-you-can-eat" flat-rate subscriptions to pay-by-the-drink usage pricing. 
    * This shift mirrors the cellular telecom expansion of 2005–2007, where usage-based pricing unlocked massive, compounding revenue curves. 
    * Unconstrained by flat-rate boundaries, top-tier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are positioned to easily clear $200 billion in ARR.
* **Cross-Sectional Valuation Anomalies:** Public market valuations currently exhibit massive logical contradictions. Semiconductor capital equipment companies trade at 40x next-quarter annualized earnings, while DRAM (memory) manufacturers trade at mid-single-digit multiples, despite the vertical pricing growth of High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM).

---

### Solving the Compute Bottleneck: Watts and Wafers

* **The "Watts" Bottleneck & Zoning:** Near-term energy availability is constrained more by zoning, approvals, and turbine casting limits than by grid capacity. However, capitalism is rapidly adapting, with massive capital expenditure projects coming online to expand grid infrastructure and repurpose alternative energy sources by 2027–2028.
* **The Case for Orbital Compute:** Long-term energy limitations will inevitably be resolved by migrating inference compute into space.
    * **Racks in Space:** Orbital compute is not characterized by giant, floating space stations, but by standard server racks (such as a 3,000-pound Nvidia Blackwell rack) operating as individual satellites in sun-synchronous orbits.
    * **Thermal and Power Efficiency:** In sun-synchronous orbits, 500-foot-long solar panels remain constantly illuminated, while massive radiators extend into perpetual shade. 
    * **Inter-Rack Connectivity:** Racks are linked into virtual data centers using vacuum-traveling lasers, building upon the laser-communication architectures SpaceX already deploys at scale on Starlink V3 satellites.
* **TSMC as a Bubble Preventer:** Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) single-handedly prevents a speculative hyper-bubble by strictly limiting wafer supply. If TSMC expanded capacity to meet unconstrained demand, Nvidia could potentially sell $2 trillion to $3 trillion in GPUs by 2026/2027, leading to a catastrophic overbuild.
* **The Texas "TeraFab" Initiative:** A joint venture involving Elon Musk, Tesla, and SpaceX aims to build the world’s largest fabrication facility in America. Partnering with Intel to access 50 years of institutional manufacturing knowledge, the project leverages Musk's ability to construct hardware facilities in a fraction of standard timelines (such as building the Memphis supercluster in 122 days).

---

### The Chip Design Battlefield: Prefill, Decode, and Hardware Lifespans

* **The Iron Triangle of Chip Design:** Similar to military tank design (balancing attack, defense, and mobility), chip architects must negotiate rigid physical trade-offs between memory capacity, memory bandwidth, and compute power within TSMC's manufacturing rules.
* **Prefill vs. Decode Disaggregation:** Modern model architectures allow designers to optimize chips by separating these two distinct phases:
    * **Prefill (Loading the Cannon):** The process of ingest-loading the prompt and context, which is heavily memory-capacity bound.
    * **Decode (Firing the Cannon):** The step-by-step generation of output tokens, which is memory-bandwidth constrained.
* **Cerebras and Wafer-Scale Computing:** Chip startups cannot succeed by trying to build a "better GPU" because Nvidia maintains insurmountable scale and supply chain advantages. Startups must do something fundamentally different and physically difficult. Cerebras achieved this through wafer-scale engine architecture, keeping massive compute and memory on a single giant silicon wafer to bypass traditional chip-to-chip packaging bottlenecks.
* **Salvaging GPU Capital and Private Credit:** The disaggregation of prefill and inference tasks allows older GPUs (like Nvidia Hopper or Ampere architectures) to remain highly productive. By placing ultra-fast inference processors (like Cerebras or Grok LPUs) in front of older GPUs to handle prefill, operators can extend the useful life of legacy silicon to 10–15 years, securing the underlying assets of the massive GPU private credit market.

---

### The Application Layer Crisis and Geopolitical Realities

* **Trillions in Value Destroyed:** At the application layer, AI has actually net-destroyed trillions of dollars of enterprise software value. Shallow SaaS applications have struggled to adapt as foundational frontier models commoditize basic software workflows, though highly specialized coding tools (such as Cursor and Cognition) remain strong exceptions.
* **The New Prisoner's Dilemma of Frontier Labs:** Model builders face a critical strategic choice: do they release their state-of-the-art weights via open APIs, or keep them entirely closed? 
    * Releasing them open-source allows competitors—particularly resource-constrained Chinese firms—to rapidly "distill" and clone the models using cheap reasoning traces.
    * If the top Western labs collectively agree to stop releasing frontier APIs, they can effectively halt the rapid catch-up of open-source competitors.
* **Geopolitical and Battlefield AI Dominance:** The war in Ukraine has demonstrated that battlefield superiority is no longer governed solely by physical hardware or drone counts, but by the sophistication of real-time targeting and orchestration AI. This algorithmic edge is shifting the global balance of power, potentially ushering in a new era of Pax Americana driven by Western computational dominance.

### Gavin Baker’s Investing Framework and Philosophy
*   **The Fidelity DNA:** Baker maintains the core Peter Lynch philosophy that deeply engaging with products as a consumer is the best way to understand a stock ("if you like the store or product, you'll love the stock"). 
    *   **The Contrarian Challenge:** He struggles to balance this with a natural tendency to over-focus on low valuations and contrarian positions, noting that "pulling weeds and watering flowers" (selling losers and riding winners) is a lifelong discipline.
*   **Formative Mistakes and Leverage:** Baker highlights his early investment in Nextel International as a painful, defining lesson in risk management. 
    *   He publicly pressured the board to buy back stock 15 months before the company went bankrupt, teaching him to be highly wary of high leverage in competitive markets.
*   **Information Edge:** Baker prioritizes reading primary source materials, transcripts, and expert network transcripts over meeting with public company executives. He notes that public executives rarely deviate from official filings, and "reading is much faster than they can speak."
*   **The "Lightning Round" Framework:** 
    *   *Underrated:* Pattern recognition, sleep.
    *   *Overrated:* Scuttlebutt.
    *   *Appropriately Rated:* Position sizing (knowing whether you play for "slugging percentage" or "batting average" and staying consistent).

### The Semiconductor Bottleneck and Avoiding an AI Bubble
*   **A Unique Capacity Cycle:** While historical memory cycles suggest selling memory stocks now (with Micron's margins in the high 60s versus a 16% historical average), Baker believes this mirrors the mid-1990s capacity cycle where a structural shortage extended gains far longer than anticipated.
*   **TSMC as the Safeguard Against a Bust:** The typical tech cycle bubble-and-bust may be prevented by physical constraints imposed by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC).
    *   **The TSMC Mentality:** TSMC's leadership, whom Baker describes as "flinty old men and women in their 70s," acts as the conservative custodian of Morris Chang's legacy. 
    *   **Rate-Limiting Nvidia:** Despite Nvidia's Jensen Huang wanting to double or triple wafer capacity, TSMC expands capacity conservatively (around 5% at a time), enforcing a physical ceiling on the market that prevents oversupply.

### AI Monetization, Usage Pricing, and "Token Maxing"
*   **The Shift to Usage-Based Pricing:** Frontier AI companies are transitioning from $250/month flat subscriptions to enterprise usage-based pricing with overage costs. Baker compares this to the early growth days of cellular and long-distance telecom, where overage charges unlocked massive customer lifetime value.
*   **Coding as the Ultimate App:** Code generation is not just a killer app; it is the shortest path to Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) and Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). 
    *   Tools like Claude Code and Code X are highly recommended even for non-coders seeking high-quality investment and analytical answers.

### Hardware and Infrastructure Winners: Trainium and Neo-Clouds
*   **The Rise of Trainium:** Amazon’s Trainium 3 (ramping up in the second half of 2026) is the most underestimated chip in the market. 
    *   **Architectural Advantage:** Google made highly conservative design choices with its TPU V8 (and refuses to submit TPUs to its own MLPerf benchmarks). Conversely, Nvidia and Amazon made aggressive choices. 
    *   **Mixture of Experts (MoE) Scale:** Nvidia and Amazon's Trainium possess the only two functioning "switched scale-up networks" globally, which are required to run inference on modern MoE models.
*   **The Durability of Neo-Clouds:** Specialized cloud providers like CoreWeave, Crusoe, and Nebius have highly durable business models, rejecting the idea that they are mere arbitrage plays on hyperscaler capacity.
    *   **The Formula 1 Analogy:** Managing GPU clusters is exceptionally complex. High-quality neo-clouds achieve two to three times higher GPU utilization per hour than lower-tier providers, justifying premium pricing.

### The Future of Infrastructure: Orbital Compute
*   **Timeline to Orbit:** Space-based "orbital compute" will become commercially viable and proven within the next two years, gaining meaningful market share by the end of the decade.
*   **Disruption of Terrestrial Ecosystems:** While existing physical data centers will always hold value for training and reinforcement learning, the shift to space will heavily penalize land-based power, cooling, and industrial equipment companies that have over-expanded capacity.
*   **Mechanics of Space Computing:** Space-based setups bypass terrestrial power grids and cooling challenges.
    *   **The Setup:** Rather than building massive warehouses, orbital compute uses sun-synchronous satellites designed as 8-foot-tall racks stitched together via lasers. Power is drawn directly from solar wings, and cooling is achieved by positioning giant (300 to 400-foot) radiators in the shadow of the rack.
