## Replit CEO Amjad Masad on What Young People Should Learn in the AI Era

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q8yFrGwIftE
- Published: 2026-09-23

- Speakers: Amjad Masad (Founder and CEO, Replit); Erik Torenberg (Host); Gagan Biyani (Co-founder and CEO, Horowitz and Andreessen Academy)

---

### TL;DR
Educational structures must evolve past rigid credentialism and premature startup optimization to empower young people as "heretics" who question deeply held beliefs through project-based learning and curiosity-driven side quests.

### The Utility of Youth and the Failure of Modern Education
* **Challenging Monocultures:** Amjad Masad argues that the fundamental utility of young people in society is to act as "heretics" who question deeply held beliefs and blind spots within cultural monocultures like Silicon Valley.
* **Flawed Academic Pipelines:** Traditional colleges and universities often beat independent thought out of students, preparing them to be "perfect citizens" while offering no guarantee of employment despite massive debt.
* **The Steve Jobs Call-Back Principle:** 
  * Meandering and exploring seemingly random topics—such as Steve Jobs dropping in on a calligraphy class that later inspired the Mac's typography—proves more valuable than strictly prescriptive curricula.
  * Gagan Biyani notes that natural interests (like tying mathematics to basketball statistics or salary caps) should be used as bridges to learn necessary analytical skills.

### Project-Based Learning and Institutional Trust
* **Action Over Abstraction:** Gagan Biyani explains that the Academy's model eliminates grades, tests, and graduation requirements, focusing instead on project-based learning where students build things they care about using tools like Python and AI without needing customized syllabi.
* **The High-Trust Silicon Valley Advantage:** 
  * Gagan Biyani highlights local politics and nonprofit fundraising to illustrate how high-trust environments dramatically accelerate execution speed, allowing builders to make weeks of progress while waiting on verbal commitments.
  * Colleges should actively encourage side quests rather than punishing attendance or forcing students to hide entrepreneurial efforts (e.g., taking investor calls from school bathrooms); institutions should provide resources and demo days instead.

### Premature Optimization and the Trap of Early Startups
* **The Dangers of Pre-Commitment:** Amjad Masad compares the current venture capital practice of handing out million-dollar checks to pre-idea Stanford students to the predatory music industry locking young artists into early contracts.
* **Premature Optimization:** Starting a company out of envy or because peers are raising unicorns is a form of "premature optimization" (the root of all evil in programming) that locks 18-year-olds into 10-year commitments before they have found genuine internal gravity.
* **The Two-Year Exploration Buffer:** Gagan Biyani advocates for an underutilized middle ground—grant-funded or unstructured exploration over a couple of years—rather than forcing teenagers into an immediate choice between a rigid four-year university and an enterprise startup track.

### AI, Self-Driving Companies, and Machine Learning Research
* **The Evolution of the Firm:** Amjad Masad predicts that the nature of companies will fundamentally change as AI agents handle organizational glue and bureaucracy in the background, enabling true worker autonomy and self-driving companies.
* **Vibe Research and AI-Driven ML:** Following a late-in-life obsession with chess, Amjad Masad realized that large language models struggled with chess hallucinations, leading him to explore "vibe research" (or auto-research) where AI makes machine learning accessible for anyone with data to fine-tune models.
* **Challenging Industry Dogma:** Reflecting on being "cancelled" a year prior for claiming people no longer need to learn how to code, Amjad Masad notes that industry critics now use AI prompting in the exact same way.

### Cultivating Virtue, Interpersonal Skills, and Discernment
* **Virtue Ethics and Moral Compasses:** Amjad Masad rejects pure utilitarianism and consequentialism, aligning instead with Aristotle's virtue ethics—acting in a way one is proud of regardless of who is watching.
* **Interpersonal Skills as a Pillar:** Gagan Biyani emphasizes that high-trust ecosystems breed thoughtful, grounded individuals, and the Academy explicitly builds interpersonal skills and positive-sum thinking into its core curriculum to produce well-adjusted adults capable of interfacing with the broader world and Congress.
* **Traits of Inspiring Young Talent:** When evaluating 16-to-20-year-olds, Amjad Masad looks for individuals who have dove deeply into a project, demonstrated persistence and intrinsic motivation, possess the ability to teach something new, and balance contrarian intelligence with authentic likability and respect.
