- Speakers: Palmer Luckey (Founder, Anduril); Ed Ludlow (Host, Bloomberg Tech)

---

### TL;DR
Defense tech startup Anduril is partnering with Qualcomm on an initiative called Blue Chip to manufacture a 100% U.S.-made rugged laptop, aiming to eliminate dependence on Chinese hardware for critical infrastructure and the military.

### Overview of the Blue Chip Initiative
* **Supply Chain Goals:** The initiative aims to rebuild an American computer supply chain covering chips, glass, metals, batteries, and plastics.
* **Target Audience:** Initial deployment focuses on military applications, government agencies, and critical infrastructure operators (such as energy and grid companies handling classified data).
* **Current Status:** Prototypes are currently about 90% sourced from the U.S. and allies, featuring Qualcomm CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs manufactured in Arizona, along with American-made glass.

### Manufacturing & Sourcing Challenges
* **Packaging Bottlenecks:** Palmer Luckey noted that U.S. semiconductor subsidies have historically served as a subsidy for Chinese-made laptops because domestic packaging and final assembly infrastructure were lacking.
* **Reviving Domestic Ecosystem:** Anduril is collaborating with suppliers that have either never been in the computer industry or have been absent for roughly 20 years since the last U.S. plants shut down.
* **Component Localization:** While current display panels are made overseas, Anduril is working with multiple vendors on a technical roadmap to achieve 100% U.S. sourcing.

### Anduril's Software & Hardware Integration
* **AI Core:** Anduril operates primarily as an AI company, with more personnel dedicated to software than to all hardware products combined.
* **Lattice Integration:** The Lattice AI brain—which powers Anduril's fighter jets, submarines, and missiles—is being applied to the new laptops.
* **Legacy Compatibility:** The software development effort includes building drivers to interface with legacy hardware systems deployed by the government dating back to the 1980s, 1990s, and 2000s.

### Project Meridian & Defense Industry Perspectives
* **Steering Committee:** Project Meridian is a 120-day project run by the Miter Corporation, chaired by Palmer Luckey, Elon Musk, and Newt Gingrich, alongside approximately 20 participants including Axon CEO Rick Smith.
* **Strategic Focus:** The project examines the future of warfare in 2040 and 2050 rather than day-to-day Pentagon operations.
* **Lessons from Ukraine:** Luckey highlighted the vital capability demonstrated in Ukraine to rapidly procure effective systems and immediately stop purchasing equipment when it ceases to be relevant.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Product Announcements:** Palmer Luckey stated that Anduril plans to have more announcements regarding display vendors in 2027, with potential updates at CES.
* **Production Timeline:** Palmer Luckey confirmed that Blue Chip will enter production next year, with early prototype production already underway at the Arsenal-1 facility in Ohio.

---

### TL;DR
Federal Reserve policy, labor market shifts driven by AI, and massive capital expenditures on tech infrastructure took center stage as economic and investment experts debated inflation targets, equity valuations, and upcoming mega-cap earnings.

### Macro Economy & Central Bank Policy
* **Rate Hike Debate:** Constance Hunter noted two distinct camps among economists—those expecting one final rate hike followed by a disinflationary downward trajectory, and those advocating for ongoing hikes due to underlying inflation, energy price spikes, and potential demand-pull effects from AI.
* **Ex-Post Central Banking:** Hunter described global central banks (including the Fed, Bank of Japan, and Bank of England) as massively ex-post, taking a wait-and-see approach while facing both demand-pull and cost-push inflationary pressures.
* **Inflation Target Reconsideration:** Onshoring manufacturing and restricting immigration are pushing up wages and prices, leading to questions over whether the Fed's rigid 2% inflation target remains appropriate in a higher nominal GDP world.
* **Consumer Financial Stress:** Hunter addressed credit card debt, noting that while debt is growing in line with overall consumption, a significant cohort of specific households and consumers is experiencing stretched finances and ticking-up delinquencies.
* **Yield Curve & Debt Service:** Dominic Konstam stated that 10-year Treasury yields sitting near 5% are creating major debt service problems for governments globally, arguing that with inflation at 2% and real growth at 3%, government securities should realistically trade closer to 4%.
* **Inflation Expectations & Fed Credibility:** Konstam emphasized that front-end inflation expectations matter much more for actual inflation than direct Fed actions; the Fed's primary value is providing credibility to stabilize the long end of the rates market and prevent 10-year and 30-year yields from surging toward 6%.

### AI, Labor Markets, and Capital Deepening
* **Labor Displacement vs. Reabsorption:** Dominic Konstam addressed recent seismic job cuts—such as Microsoft asking 60,000 workers to leave—comparing the AI productivity wave to historical shifts, expressing confidence that displaced labor will eventually be reabsorbed, though potentially on less favorable real-wage terms that create a K-shaped economy.
* **Capital Deepening & Depreciation:** Konstam explained that AI represents a profound capital deepening phase (increasing the capital-to-labor ratio via intelligent software and chips), noting that slower-than-expected chip depreciation is driving even greater capital intensity without immediate replacement friction.
* **Enterprise AI Adoption:** Konstam observed that business adoption of large language models like Claude has transitioned from peripheral search replacement to core business usage, though overall spending is still in its early stages.
* **GDP vs. Well-Being:** Citing Simon Kuznets' 1971 Nobel Prize insights, Konstam argued that GDP is a terrible measure of welfare, noting that high corporate profits reflect companies capturing consumer surplus rather than translating directly into broad societal well-being.

### Commodities, Infrastructure, and Multi-Asset Investing
* **Thematic AI Infrastructure:** David Schassler argued that investing in the AI trade should focus broadly on infrastructure development companies (utilities, power bills, rails, energy MLPs) rather than trying to pick short-term software winners whose models get leapfrogged every 6 to 8 weeks.
* **Full-Spectrum Commodity Exposure:** VanEck's multi-asset strategy spans the entire opportunity set from below the earth (copper, minerals, metals, energy) through refining, distribution, and final data center delivery to capitalize on physical asset bottlenecks.
* **Gold Catalysts:** Schassler noted that despite gold correcting from above $5,500 down toward $4,000 due to being overbought alongside higher interest rates and a stronger dollar, the fundamental catalysts for gold (inflation protection, geopolitical shock, weaponization of the US dollar) are significantly more elevated today.
* **Scarcity and Bottleneck Investing:** Schassler emphasized that physical assets are the ultimate bottleneck and building blocks of growth; in a higher-growth regime driven by AI productivity, boring commodities and agricultural distributors (such as Nutrien) become critical assets.

### Big Tech Earnings & Preview
* **Stock Performance Divergence:** Ed Ludlow highlighted a sharp split among major technology names year-to-date: top-tier companies like Apple and Nvidia throw off massive cash and prioritize shareholder returns, while lower-tier companies like Meta and Alphabet have faced negative free cash flow concerns and taken active headcount-reduction steps.
* **Alphabet's Earnings Preview:** Ludlow reported that Google's cloud business is expected to grow **91% or more year-on-year**, representing a potential upside surprise, even as the market struggles to connect strong cloud growth to tangible profitability improvements in core search.
* **Spending Trajectory:** Ludlow confirmed there is no reason to believe Microsoft or Alphabet will signal any reduction in their massive capital expenditure growth rates during their earnings calls.
* **Local and Edge AI:** Ludlow noted a growing industry trend toward edge computing and on-premise data centers, where companies and consumers increasingly build local infrastructure to run AI applications privately rather than relying solely on the cloud.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AI Adoption Timeline:** David Schassler predicts it will take **5 to 7 years** to reach full proof of AI concept for skeptics, noting that while investors want immediate results, pivotal technologies require time to move through the economy.
* **Gold Price Outlook:** David Schassler projects that gold will move back up past its previous peak of **$5,500 and beyond**, driven by structural macroeconomic uncertainties and geopolitical catalysts.
* **Google Cloud Growth:** Ed Ludlow cites market consensus and expectations that Google's cloud business will grow by **91% or more year-on-year** in the reported quarter.

---

### TL;DR
The Federal Reserve faces a contradictory US economy defined by strong growth and corporate profits alongside frozen housing, high borrowing costs, and stubborn inflation. Meanwhile, alternative asset managers like Harrison Street navigate higher-for-longer interest rates through essential-service real estate, and quant trading firms fight an existential talent war against frontier AI labs for top machine learning PhDs.

### The Fed's Confusing Economy and Macro Pressures
* **Conflicting Macro Signals:** Enda Curran notes the economy is sending contradictory indicators simultaneously:
  * **Frozen vs. Strong Markets:** Housing markets are frozen while house prices remain high; bond yields are soaring while stock markets hit new highs.
  * **Spending vs. Sentiment:** Consumer spending is strong, but consumer sentiment is in the doldrums; unemployment is low, yet wage gains are hard to secure.
* **K-Shaped Reality:** 
  * **High-Income vs. Low-Income:** Headline retail spending looks robust, but it is largely driven by higher-income earners, whereas lower-income workers feel significant financial pain.
  * **Large vs. Small Businesses:** Big corporations are thriving, but smaller businesses struggle with expensive local bank loans, rising insurance costs, and higher material prices.
* **Broad-Based Inflation Pressures:** September inflation figures showed broad-based price accelerations beyond energy, including record increases in toy prices potentially tied to tariffs.
* **External Shocks Beyond Control:** Key economic developments for 2026—such as Middle Eastern conflicts driving up energy (diesel/food) costs and the AI chip-demand boom—are largely beyond the Federal Reserve's control and cannot be solved by adjusting interest rates.

### Real Estate and Infrastructure Resilience
* **Divergent Real Estate Recovery:** Chris Merrill highlights that real estate outcomes are diverging significantly across sectors, markets, and capital structures as prior expectations for declining interest rates and normalized capital markets gave way to resilient growth and persistent inflation.
* **Essential-Service Focus:** Harrison Street ($110B AUM) focuses on need-based, demographic-driven asset classes (student housing, senior housing, medical offices, life sciences, self-storage, data centers) that perform well in inflationary and higher-for-longer interest rate environments.
* **Higher Education Vulnerability:** 
  * **University Attrition:** Chris Merrill predicts **15% to 20% of universities will go out of business over time** as smaller schools struggle to survive.
  * **Power Four Resilience:** Harrison Street focuses on larger enrollment-taker public universities ("Power Four" schools) that embrace AI and online learning while offering in-person social environments and seeing actual enrollment increases.
* **Senior Housing Demographics & Care:** 
  * **Aging Population Surge:** Nearly **2 million people turn 80 every year**, and **1 in 3 people develop dementia or memory care issues past age 85**, creating severe undersupply amid tariff and interest rate constraints.
  * **Care Continuum:** Focus remains on private-pay, rental-based continuum of care (independent, assisted, memory care) while avoiding volatile public-pay skilled nursing.
* **Data Centers and Power Constraints:** 
  * **Scale:** Harrison Street has heavily leaned into data centers with **5.8 gigawatts** under management.
  * **Bottlenecks:** Access to power is severely slowing down the sector, forcing investors to take risks on land purchases or rely on net-leasing core-and-shell facilities to hyperscalers with established power access.
* **Capital Return Strategy:** Harrison Street projects selling **$6 billion to $7 billion** in assets, emphasizing DPI (Distributed to Paid-In capital) as the new IRR to return capital to global private wealth and institutional investors.

### The Quant vs. AI Lab Talent War
* **Lucrative Compensation Packages:** First-year compensation packages for machine learning and quantitative finance PhDs range from **$450,000 to $650,000, reaching up to $800,000 per year** in some cases.
* **Existential Recruitment Threat:** Justina Lee reports that quant trading firms face an existential recruitment concern as machine learning, stats, and physics PhDs increasingly choose Frontier AI labs (like OpenAI) over quant funds.
* **Shifting Value Proposition:** 
  * **Prestige & Equity:** AI labs offer prestige, visibility, and potential windfalls from equity stakes in fast-growing tech companies, whereas quant firms predict markets.
  * **Cultural Shift:** Top researchers often prefer discussing artificial general intelligence (AGI) at social gatherings over financial market prediction.
* **Quant Countermeasures:** 
  * **Academic Partnerships:** Quant firms are building early university pipelines by funding PhDs and postdocs at institutions like Princeton and Stanford.
  * **Internal AI Labs:** Secretive firms like Hudson River Trading and Jump Trading are building internal AI labs both to utilize machine learning for trading operations and to market themselves directly to academic talent.

### Notable Quotes

> *"You can't pump a keg virtually, Carol."* — **Chris Merrill**

* **Market Doing the Work:** Panelists (including Tom Keene, Jonathan Ferro, and Lisa Abramowicz) suggested Warsh came close to admitting that recent market volatility and tighter financial conditions are "doing the work" for the Fed, reducing the immediate urgency to hike rates further.
* **The "Fed Put" vs. Market Sensitivity:** Jeff Rosenberg (BlackRock) noted that while it may not be a traditional "Fed put," Warsh demonstrated acute sensitivity to market stability and credit spread dynamics, acknowledging that market fluctuations act as a macro disinflationary event.

### Committee Dynamics & Institutional Alignment
* **Internal Division:** The Federal Reserve entered Warsh's regime already divided on whether ongoing price pressures stem from a supply shock or a demand shock.
* **Core Alignment vs. Chair:** Panelists observed that recent speeches by core committee members John Williams and Jefferson—emphasizing the luxury of taking a month to skip and gather data—pre-dated the data and served as more reliable policy guidance than Warsh's press conference.
* **International Parallels:** Jonathan Ferro compared the Fed's current communication challenges under Warsh to the European Central Bank (ECB) under recent leadership, noting a shared frustration from markets over vague institutional messaging.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Meeting Schedule Reductions:** The FOMC will officially phase in an adjusted meeting schedule next year, voting at seven of its eight meetings (as revealed by Chair Kevin Warsh).
