## AMD CEO Lisa Su: Are We in an AI Bubble? | TomorrowX Summit

- Channel: iConnections
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MtFNcfB4JEU
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261118.html#video-MtFNcfB4JEU
- Speakers: Lisa Su (Chair and CEO, AMD); Dom Rizzo (Investor, T. Rowe Price)

---

**TL;DR:** AMD Chair and CEO Lisa Su argues that the current AI infrastructure build-out is not a cyclical bubble, but a generational transformation—comparable to the most impactful technologies of the past 50 years—that has propelled AMD into a data-center-first powerhouse growing over 80% year-on-year.

### AMD’s Evolution & Data Center Dominance
* **From Small Cap to Trillion-Dollar Contender:** Dom Rizzo (T. Rowe Price) noted that when he first met Lisa Su, AMD was a ~$5 billion market cap company trading at $5/share (with 1 billion shares outstanding); today, AMD is north of $500/share and approaching a $1 trillion market cap.
* **Early Architectural Bets:** Su attributed AMD’s trajectory to focusing on the bleeding edge of high-performance computing (HPC) and anticipating the slowdown of Moore’s Law over 10 years ago by pioneering modular "chiplet" architectures.
* **Data Center Business Mix:**
  * In 2025, data center represented less than 50% of AMD's business.
  * This year, data center will exceed 60% of the business and more than double in size.
  * Overall company revenue is projected to grow over 80% year-on-year, with data center eventually exceeding 80% of AMD's total business.
  * Su cited a $2 trillion total addressable market (TAM) over the next five years, heavily concentrated in data centers.

### AI Infrastructure ROI & The "Bubble" Debate
* **Generational, Not Cyclical:** Addressing investor concerns about hyperscalers spending north of 100% of operating cash flow on CapEx, Su stated that AI is not following the traditional semiconductor boom-bust cycle. Instead, it is a generational shift where AI-driven productivity creates a positive feedback loop justifying 3-to-5-year investment horizons.
* **Managing the Ecosystem:** Su emphasized that AMD is working closely with partners across the entire supply and value chain to ensure balanced investment—avoiding both underinvestment bottlenecks and overinvestment gluts.
* **Nvidia & Industry Noise:** Regarding recent headlines around main competitor Nvidia, Su praised Jensen Huang and Colette Kress, stating that AMD's focus is on separating itself from the noise, executing its roadmap, and promising/exceeding expectations on delivery.

### Geopolitics, Export Controls & Public Perception
* **Navigating China Restrictions:** Su acknowledged that semiconductors are now central to national security and economic prosperity. AMD actively works with the U.S. administration and Congress on export rules, noting that while the highest-performing chips will remain restricted, AMD anticipated many of the latest restrictions and still sees demand substantially exceeding supply.
* **Exporting the American AI Stack:** AMD recently received approvals in Commerce Department export packages, supporting the goal of ensuring the global ecosystem builds on the American AI stack.
* **Connecting with Everyday Americans:** Su stressed that the tech industry must do a better job showing ordinary Americans—amidst midterm election dialogues and data center concerns—how AI improves lives through scientific discovery and healthcare.

### Product Momentum: CPUs, GPUs & The Software Stack
* **ROCm & Anthropic’s Weekend Breakthrough:** Historically, switching hardware architectures was viewed as difficult. However, AMD has acquired roughly 10 software companies and leveraged Frontier models for kernel optimizations. Su highlighted that an Anthropic engineer used Claude over a single weekend (3 days, compared to the usual 9–12 months) to get their model running smoothly on AMD hardware.
* **The CPU Comeback via AI Agents:** Rizzo and Su agreed that 2026 is the "year of agents" (moving from simple chatbots to autonomous task completion). Because AI agents require CPUs to orchestrate workloads, the CPU market—previously growing in the single digits at $20B–$30B—is now projected to reach $220 billion over the next 4–5 years.
  * AMD recently launched **Venice** (following Genoa and Turin), delivering leading performance, performance per watt, and performance per dollar.
* **The Year of Instinct (GPUs):** AMD began shipping its newest **MI455** GPUs and **Helios** racks in Q3, which are currently being brought up in data centers by OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle.
* **Full-Stack Portfolio & Xilinx Integration:** Acquired in 2022, Xilinx brought over 6,000 customers across aerospace, defense, industrial, and test/measurement. Su noted that combining Xilinx's FPGAs, NICs, and controllers with AMD's CPUs, GPUs, and custom ASIC capabilities creates a "1+1 > 3" formula, delivering "the right compute for the right workload."

* **Data Center TAM:** The overall market represents a $2 trillion TAM over the next five years — *Lisa Su*.
* **AMD Growth & Mix:** AMD's overall company revenue will grow over 80% year-on-year, with the data center business finishing this year at greater than 60% of total revenue (more than doubling) and eventually exceeding 80% of AMD — *Lisa Su*.
* **CPU Market Expansion:** Driven by agentic AI workloads, the CPU market will grow from a $20B–$30B market to a $220 billion market over the next four to five years — *Lisa Su*.
* **Supply vs. Demand:** Even with new export restrictions, AMD expects demand to remain substantially higher than supply — *Lisa Su*.
* **Societal Impact:** Within a couple of years, every family should have a story of how AI improved their lives through healthcare or scientific discovery — *Lisa Su*.
