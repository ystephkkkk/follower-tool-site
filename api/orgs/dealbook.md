## Why Sam Altman and Greg Brockman Say Humans Must Stay in Control of AI

- Channel: The New York Times
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5EA7QeBm8k
- Published: 2026-11-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261118.html#video-J5EA7QeBm8k
- Speakers: Sam Altman (CEO, OpenAI); Greg Brockman (President, OpenAI); Andrew Ross Sorkin (Editor at large, DealBook)

---

**TL;DR:** OpenAI co-founders Sam Altman and Greg Brockman emphasized that humans must remain in control of AI's future, arguing that technical alignment and industry-wide safety standards must scale alongside rapidly advancing capabilities. Alongside detailing massive commercial momentum—including 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users and 70% annualized Q3 revenue growth—they outlined OpenAI's push toward vertical integration with its own data centers and upcoming "Jalapeno" inference chip.

### Human Control, Alignment, and Safety
* **Rejecting the AI Handover:** Both Sam Altman and Greg Brockman strongly opposed Silicon Valley narratives suggesting a gradual handover of control to AI, insisting humans must retain agency over the future.
  * **Disagreement with Microsoft:** Altman pushed back against Satya Nadella’s recent claim that AI models cannot be aligned and must instead be contained, arguing alignment is technically achievable even though the bar for confidence must rise with model capabilities.
  * **Degrading Monitoring:** Brockman noted that traditional "chain of thought" monitoring is degrading because newer models can perform more reasoning within a single forward pass without writing out their thinking, requiring new technical monitoring approaches.
* **Shift to Development Safety:** Brockman explained that AI has transitioned from needing *deployment* safety to requiring *development* safety, as models are now capable enough to have real-world interactions during training.
  * **August Security Sprint:** Following an August security incident involving Hugging Face, Brockman led a sprint that paused 25% of OpenAI's production engineering projects to focus engineers on securing the company using its own frontier cyber models.
  * **Iterative Deployment Defense:** Addressing a California Attorney General lawsuit alleging OpenAI knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight, Altman rejected the characterization, stating that confronting reality through iterative deployment is the only way to make the technology safe.
* **Industry-Wide Standards:** Altman warned against a "race dynamic in the fog of war" among competing labs, calling for shared safety standards and greater transparency.
  * **Anthropic IPO:** Explaining his *Fortune* magazine comment that going public right now is ill-advised given current safety challenges, Altman said OpenAI plans to go public eventually but must currently navigate complex tradeoffs between commercial viability and security.

### Business Momentum, Compute, and Vertical Integration
* **User and Revenue Scale:** Brockman shared several concrete metrics demonstrating OpenAI's commercial growth:
  * **ChatGPT Reach:** Over 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT weekly, including 330 million people using it for health queries every week.
  * **Q3 Financials:** OpenAI's Q3 topline revenue grew 70% annualized, and enterprise revenue doubled in Q3 alone.
* **The Compute Bottleneck:** Brockman stated that the world is not building enough compute and confirmed he has personally asked both Elon Musk and Sergey Brin for additional compute capacity.
* **Vertical Integration & Chip Design:** Altman noted OpenAI is the only company building the entire intelligence stack—from consumer and enterprise platforms to data centers and custom silicon.
  * **Jalapeno Chip:** Brockman highlighted OpenAI's upcoming custom inference chip, "Jalapeno," stating it is designed to optimize the Pareto curve of peak intelligence and price efficiency.
  * **AMD Partnership:** Brockman announced that working with AMD, OpenAI models achieved a full year's worth of generational chip design improvements in just five months.
  * **High-Compute Use Cases:** Brockman cited applying $1 million worth of compute to find 1,000 bugs in the Linux kernel in a short period with a single researcher (compared to the ~20 bugs typically found per year).
  * **Cost Efficiency:** Addressing Microsoft's claim that it can run AI tasks at 5% of OpenAI's cost, Altman welcomed the competition, stating OpenAI wants to drive down costs so everyone can use more AI.

### Leadership Partnership, Governance, and External Pressures
* **11-Year Partnership:** Brockman traced their dynamic back to a July 2015 dinner, describing a high-bandwidth partnership built on rapid-fire communication (5–6 short calls a day early on) and a willingness to "disagree and commit" on major strategic questions.
* **Equity and Control:** Addressing the disparity between Brockman’s ~$30 billion equity stake and Altman’s lack of equity, Brockman noted he originally advocated for Altman to take equity, and Altman admitted, "I should have listened to Greg."
  * **Governance Structure:** Brockman pointed out that OpenAI is the only major AI company where the founders do not hold supermajority control.
* **Legal and Public Scrutiny:**
  * **Jensen Huang / NVIDIA:** Both expressed personal support for NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang amid his recent challenges, calling him "singular" and noting NVIDIA's foundational bet on OpenAI.
  * **White House Accord:** Brockman expressed alignment with the White House Accord on AI safety standards.
  * **NYT Litigation:** Asked about internal emails regarding scraping *The New York Times* past its paywall, Brockman declined to comment due to active litigation, noting he had already addressed it in his deposition.
