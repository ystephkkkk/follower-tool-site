## Meta’s $200 AI Agent ‘Hatch’, Sam Altman-back Helion Valuation Hits $15.5B, Snowflake’s New AI Tools

**TL;DR** 
Meta is internally planning a consumer-focused AI agent named "Hatch" targeting a $200-a-month premium tier, while Sam Altman-backed fusion energy startup Helion has raised $465 million at a $15.5 billion valuation to accelerate its power plant manufacturing.

***

### Meta’s Premium AI Agent: "Hatch"
* **Consumer-Friendly OpenDevin:** Meta is internally discussing a new AI agent tool called Hatch, designed to be a user-friendly, consumer-and-prosumer alternative to the complex OpenDevin platform. 
    * **Features:** Hatch can write code and execute basic agentic tasks such as email management and calendar scheduling. Because Meta lacks dedicated email or calendar programs, the tool is being engineered to operate off-platform.
* **Pricing and Tiered Model:** Internal documents indicate Meta is considering a premium tier priced at $199.99 per month to provide higher usage limits, alongside a baseline free version.
* **Release Timeline:** Originally targeted for April and then delayed to July, the official release date remains fluid but is tentatively expected by mid-to-late summer.
* **Underlying Infrastructure:** During current testing, Hatch relies on Anthropic’s Claude models. Meta aims to eventually migrate the tool to its own foundation models to avoid the high costs of operating a third-party wrapper.

### Helion’s $15.5B Valuation & Fusion Scaling
* **Massive Series G Funding:** Helion Energy raised $465 million in a round led by Thrive Capital, boosting the company’s valuation to $15.5 billion—nearly triple its valuation from the previous year.
* **The "TinyMerge" Strategy:** Helion is running a sub-scale testbed program called "TinyMerge" in parallel with its main Orion power plant facility. This allows engineers to rapidly iterate on electronics and materials while training the world's first fusion power plant operators.
* **Vertical Integration Focus:** To bypass supply chain bottlenecks and control costs, Helion is vertically integrating key component manufacturing. 
    * **Capacitors & Electronics:** The company manufactures its own energy-storage capacitors and power electronics, which represent approximately 50% of a fusion power plant's total cost.
* **Commercial and Regulatory Progress:** Helion continues to de-risk its path to market by securing environmental and building permits for its Malaga, Washington site and maintaining its landmark Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) with Microsoft.

### Netskope’s AI Security & Financial Outlook
* **Financial Performance:** Cloud security firm Netskope reported a slight top-line deceleration with 28% revenue growth this quarter. However, the company expanded ARR by 29%, achieved record gross retention, and grew new customer logos by nearly 60% year-over-year.
* **Sales Expansion Friction:** The top-line deceleration is attributed to a massive, ongoing sales team expansion where roughly half of the sales reps are still in their training/ramping phase.
* **Frontier Model Testing:** Netskope has early access to Anthropic's Mythos model and OpenAI's Daybreak program (GPT-5.5) to run vulnerability-detection harnesses on its own codebases prior to product releases.
* **Democratic Access vs. Safety:** CEO Sanjay Beri emphasized the tension in releasing powerful frontier models like Mythos, advocating for eventual democratization to protect infrastructure, while acknowledging the risk of advanced models falling into the hands of malicious actors.

### Snowflake’s Co-pilot and Co-work Rollouts
* **AI Product Launches:** At its annual summit, Snowflake announced new capabilities for its personal assistant, Snowflake Co-work (including Deep Search, Artifacts, and Skill Catalog), and its coding agent, Snowflake Co-pilot, which now supports cross-platform integrations on mobile, desktop, Excel, and Cloud Code.
* **The Choice Architecture:** Rather than restricting users to a single model, Snowflake partners with multiple providers—including Anthropic, OpenAI, and open-source options—allowing customers to choose the most cost-effective model for their specific task.
* **Customer Adoption:** Over 13,600 customers actively use Snowflake’s AI capabilities weekly, with more than 7,200 utilizing Cortex Co-pilot.
* **Overcoming Adoption Barriers:** Chief Data and AI Officer Anahita Tafvizi identified *trust* (ensuring deterministic accuracy and role-based access governance) and *cost predictability* as the two main barriers preventing wider corporate AI adoption.

### The Push to Keep Top AI Minds in Academia
* **The Academic Brain Drain:** Databricks and Perplexity co-founder Andy Konwinski is actively urging PhD candidates and researchers to reject multi-million dollar corporate compensation packages and remain in university research for a few years to secure fundamental breakthroughs.
* **The Problem with Closed Research:** Frontier labs (such as Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic) are increasingly keeping their training data, computing scales, and software details secret, making scientific replication impossible for academic institutions.
* **The Lotte Institute:** To combat this, Konwinski co-founded the Lotte Institute (a 501(c)(3) organization) in June 2025, committing $100 million of his own wealth. The institute funds 70 projects with grants of up to $10 million across top-tier universities including Stanford, Berkeley, MIT, and Carnegie Mellon.

Tesla faces scrutiny over narrow profitability and CEO Elon Musk's overextension, while the broader AI sector grapples with an "implementation gap" as corporations struggle to integrate agents and insurers move to exclude AI-related liabilities.

### Tesla’s Q1 Financials and the "Musk Factor"
* **Financial Performance:** Despite a 16% jump in revenue, Tesla reported GAAP earnings of only 13 cents per share. 
    * **"Funny Business" in Numbers:** Ross Gerber, CEO of Gerber Kawasaki, noted discrepancies between cash flow ($1.4 billion) and actual cash on the books ($700 million), suggesting that adjustments for FX, tariffs, and Musk’s massive compensation package are masking a lack of true profitability.
    * **SEC Disclosures:** A "hidden" $2 billion deal with an unnamed AI hardware company was discovered in SEC filings, highlighting Musk's massive capital investment in future compute.
* **CEO Tone and Focus:** Musk struck a "downbeat" tone on the earnings call, contrasting sharply with the optimism of 2021. 
    * **Overextension Concerns:** Gerber argues Musk has too many "irons in the fire"—including Tesla, SpaceX, xAI, and the Optimus robot—leading to a lack of focus that creates significant "Titanic-level" risk for investors.
    * **Strategic Vision:** Musk is currently building a "Terafab" factory for chips, solving for a projected compute shortage five to ten years away.

### Google Cloud Next: The Rise of "Agent Sprawl"
* **Major Announcements:** Google focused heavily on AI agents and unveiled new Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) specifically bifurcated for training versus inference.
* **The Implementation Gap:** Reporting from Erin Woo suggests that AI lab innovation is currently outpacing corporate ability to implement it.
    * **Bottlenecks:** Companies like Home Depot report that while individual steps (like coding or ad copy) are 50% faster, the overall business workflow remains stalled by human-centric processes like market research and internal discussions.
    * **Agent Sprawl:** Customers are struggling with "agent sprawl," where various employees set up uncoordinated AI agents, leading to management and data security difficulties.
* **Competitive Landscape:** Microsoft’s Copilot remains a dominant force due to its integration with the Office suite, though customers are increasingly looking to diversify with Google’s Gemini and Anthropic’s models.

### ServiceNow and the Software Sell-off
* **Market Overreaction:** ServiceNow reported 22% revenue growth but saw its stock drop 15%. 
    * **Profitability Guidance:** The dip was attributed to lowered profitability guidance following the acquisition of cybersecurity firm Armis and slight subscription delays in the Middle East.
* **Strategic Pivot:** ServiceNow is attempting to transition from a traditional software provider to a central "dashboard" for managing and safeguarding AI agents.

### Exclusive: The Emergence of AI Insurance Exclusions
* **Liability Shifts:** Major insurers are taking initial steps to file for "AI exclusions" in general liability policies, according to reporting by Laura Bratton.
    * **Covered Risks:** Exclusions target "advertising injuries" (such as copyright infringement from AI-generated ads) and potential bodily injury or property damage caused by AI-enabled hardware (e.g., malfunctioning robots).
    * **Policy Segmentation:** Insurers are signaling that AI-related losses should not be covered under traditional policies but rather through specialized, capped standalone cyber or "errors and omissions" policies.

### The Case for a Tesla-SpaceX Merger
* **Conflict of Interest:** Ross Gerber argues that a merger between Tesla and SpaceX is "inevitable" to resolve the legal and financial conflicts inherent in Musk running two highly intertwined companies.
* **The "Berkshire of AI":** A combined entity would allow investors a "pure play" bet on Musk’s entire technology and space empire, though it would carry high "key man risk" regarding Musk’s health and longevity.

**TL;DR**
SpaceX is pursuing a confidential IPO with a projected $1.75 trillion valuation driven by Starlink’s massive margins, while shifting speculative capital from crypto to prediction markets and a stabilization in private credit suggest a maturing but high-risk investment landscape.

### SpaceX Valuation and the Road to IPO
* **Trillion-Dollar Valuation Breakdown:** Franco Granda (Analyst, PitchBook) estimates SpaceX's total valuation at roughly $1.75 trillion based on three core pillars:
    * **Starlink (Satellite Internet):** Valued at $1.1 trillion. It is the company’s "cash cow," representing 70% of revenue with 50%+ EBITDA margins.
    * **Launch Business:** Valued at $400 billion. It dominates the market, having completed 165 launches last year (75% of which were internal Starlink payloads).
    * **xAI:** An attached component rumored to be worth $250 billion.
* **The "Starship" Bottleneck:** Theo Wayt (Reporter, The Information) notes that Starship is the single most critical dependency for SpaceX’s future. Delays in testing impact:
    * Next-generation Starlink deployments.
    * NASA moon missions.
    * Planned data centers in space.
* **Long-term Growth Strategy:** To reach a projected 1.1 billion customers by 2040, SpaceX is pivoting toward "direct-to-device" services (cell phones, cars, IoT). While this will lower the Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) to an estimated $3–$5 per month, it provides massive global scale.
* **TerraFab Ambitions:** Musk’s plan for a $70 billion chip fabrication project is viewed by Granda as a "narrative play" to ensure vertical integration for future AI-driven satellites, though it remains a high-risk endeavor with no clear timeline.

### Blackstone’s Opportunity in Private Credit
* **Contradictory Market Signals:** Despite Blackstone’s stock falling nearly 30% this year due to "AI anxiety" and software sector exposure, Anita Ramaswamy (Columnist, The Information) argues the company remains a strong investment.
* **Limited Software Exposure:** While the market is panicking over SaaS defaults, Blackstone’s actual exposure is relatively contained:
    * **Total Assets:** Only 7% exposure to software.
    * **Credit Portfolio:** Roughly 10% exposure.
* **The Confidence Signal:** To combat "redemption requests" (investors wanting to pull capital), Blackstone executives and employees personally committed $400 million to ensure liquidity for other investors. This move is unique compared to peers like Apollo or KKR, who simply capped withdrawals.
* **Fundraising Momentum:** Q4 2025 was Blackstone’s best fundraising quarter since 2022, suggesting that while the stock is volatile, assets under management (AUM) continue to grow among institutional and retail investors.

### Future VC Trends: Efficiency over Scaling
* **The "Rational" Public Market:** Yazan El-Baba (Partner, Emergence Capital) predicts that as "blue-chip" companies like OpenAI and Anthropic head toward IPOs, they will face a harsh "reality check" regarding their burn rates and efficiency.
* **Shift in AI Focus:** Venture capital is moving away from "scaling at all costs" toward:
    * **Bottom-of-the-stack efficiency:** Improving algorithms and hardware to handle the compute shortage.
    * **Alternative Modalities:** Investing in bio-foundation models and material science discovery rather than just Large Language Models (LLMs).
* **OpenAI’s Strategic Pivot:** El-Baba points to OpenAI’s decision to cut the "Sora" video project to focus on its core business as an early sign that even the biggest players are prioritizing efficiency to satisfy future public investors.

### Prediction Markets vs. Crypto
* **The Speculative Migration:** Ken Brown (Finance Editor, The Information) suggests prediction markets (like Polymarket and Kalshi) may be cannibalizing the crypto market. Speculators are moving "meme coin" capital into sports and election betting.
* **Stablecoin Synergy:** Prediction markets are currently a primary use case for stablecoins, which remain the most stable and "boring" part of the crypto ecosystem.
* **Existential Regulatory Risks:** Unlike crypto, which struggles with federal classification, prediction markets face an existential threat from state governments. States regulate and tax sports betting; if prediction markets bypass these rules, they face aggressive criminal and civil litigation that could shut down half their business volume.
