## One-on-One With President Donald Trump

**TL;DR**
In a post-2026 midterm interview with Stephen A. Smith at the White House, President Donald Trump attributed the GOP's electoral losses to his absence from the ballot and rising gas prices driven by the conflict with Iran. Trump defended his administration's sweeping tariffs, border enforcement under Chief of Staff Stephen Miller, and record with Black Americans, while outlining plans to veto initial Democratic bills before negotiating deals on healthcare and immigration.

### 2026 Midterm Losses & GOP Dynamics
* **Electoral Fallout:** Stephen A. Smith noted that Republicans suffered a net loss of 8 Senate seats (with Alaska uncalled), lost 5 governorships (Georgia, Iowa, Nevada, Ohio, and Texas), are projected to lose more than 40 House seats, and lost over 500 state legislative seats.
* **Trump's Defense:** Trump argued he was not on the ballot, noting he won all three times he ran and drew 15,000 to 25,000 people at rallies (including record votes in Florida, Texas, Alabama, and Alaska, and a 14-to-15-point win in Ohio).
  * He blamed the Alaska race on an unfair system allowing "two Dan Sullivans" to run and pointed to a $3 trillion stock market drop the day after the election as proof voters do not love Democrats.
  * Trump cited a 99% Republican approval rating on CNN and a 56% rating compared to Abraham Lincoln (8%) and Ronald Reagan (14%).
* **Primary Endorsements:** Pressed by Smith on endorsing Ken Paxton over Senator John Cornyn in Texas despite Paxton's "stained past" and 12-year refusal to debate, Trump said Cornyn was "disloyal" to him early on, though he agreed Paxton and other endorsed candidates made a mistake by skipping debates.

### Iran Conflict, Energy & Foreign Policy
* **Justification for War:** Trump maintained that military action against Iran—the "number one state sponsor of terror"—was necessary because Iran was two weeks away from a nuclear weapon over a year ago when B-2 bombers struck their sites (noting Pickaxe Mountain as another potential target).
  * He cited Iran's killing of 400 Americans on the USS Cole and more than 75,000 protesters in recent months.
* **Why the Conflict Continues:** Trump claimed Iran's navy, air force, anti-aircraft defenses, and leadership are largely gone, and that the country is disintegrating with over 300% inflation and unpaid soldiers and police.
  * He stated he could end the war in three days (one day each to destroy their energy, bridges, and manufacturing) but is holding back at the request of Middle Eastern allies—including Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE, Bahrain, and Israel—who prefer a slower approach.
* **Gas Prices & Oil:** Smith noted diesel fuel in Kansas jumped from $4 to over $6 per gallon, contributing to affordability concerns. Trump argued gas prices were higher under Presidents Biden and Obama, and claimed the U.S. is currently moving more oil through the Strait of Hormuz than before the war began.

### Tariffs, Manufacturing & Economic Claims
* **Reshoring Industry:** Trump credited tariffs with bringing back U.S. manufacturing, stating that "thousands of plants" are under construction and auto companies are leaving Mexico, Canada, South Korea, Germany, and Japan (which he said took 60% of U.S. car companies) to expand in places like Detroit.
  * He noted he saved U.S. Steel via a merger and that pharmaceutical giants Pfizer, Merck, and Eli Lilly are building hundreds of billions of dollars in U.S. plants under a tariff ultimatum: 0% in Year 1, 10% in Year 2, and 300% in Year 3.
* **Economic Metrics:** Trump highlighted a monthly jobs report of 165,000 jobs (compared to 20,000 expected), $22.2 trillion in U.S. investments, and $25 trillion in created value over less than two years.
* **Drug Prices:** Trump claimed he used tariff threats on countries like France and Germany to force them to double or triple their drug prices, bringing the U.S. from the highest to the lowest drug prices in the world.

### Record with Black Voters & HBCUs
* **HBCU Funding:** Smith thanked Trump on behalf of Tuskegee University for eliminating $1.6 billion in HBCU debt. Trump highlighted a $438 million one-time investment and an additional $174 million announced in September 2026 (totaling over $612 million in extra funding over two years), plus an April 2025 executive order restoring the HBCU Board of Advisors.
  * Smith countered that Barack Obama provided $2.4 billion to $2.9 billion in 2009, Trump provided $1.9 billion in 2019, and Joe Biden provided over $17 billion; Trump argued his 10-year financing saved HBCUs from bankruptcy and annual "begging."
* **Targeted Policies:** Trump read a list of initiatives benefiting Black Americans, including:
  * **Trump Accounts:** $1,000 from the government for 100% of Black newborns for stock market exposure.
  * **Tax & Military Benefits:** No tax on overtime, permanent Opportunity Zones (with Sen. Tim Scott), a $1,776 tax-free "warrior dividend" for military members, and a 70% reduction in the VA benefits backlog.
  * **Healthcare & Justice:** Medicaid expansion for sickle cell disease gene therapies covering 84% of Medicaid, Criminal Justice Reform, and naming Alice Marie Johnson (who served 22 years of a 50-year marijuana sentence before Trump pardoned her) as "pardon czar."
* **Federal Job Losses & Obama Comparison:** Pressed on over 300,000 Black women losing federal jobs, Trump said most moved on to better-paying private-sector jobs. In a hypothetical matchup against Barack Obama, Trump claimed he would win "easily," calling Obama "highly overrated" and a "divider."

### Border Security, Stephen Miller & Nvidia
* **Border & Stephen Miller:** Trump claimed zero illegal border crossings over the past 17 to 18 months. Smith read Chief of Staff Stephen Miller's record—including the 2018 zero-tolerance policy, refugee cuts, a ~$170 billion immigration enforcement expansion, travel restrictions, and a push for 3,000 ICE arrests per day. Trump defended Miller, noting Obama built the "jails for kids" and praising the removal of Tren de Aragua gang members and violent criminals.
* **Nvidia CEO Resignation:** Reacting to Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO of Nvidia, Trump called Huang a "tremendous talent" who is "going through hell" over the impact on his wife and family, adding that whether Huang should have stepped down depends on "if the woman was a spy." Trump suggested recently semi-retired Apple CEO Tim Cook as a strong candidate to replace Huang.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Iran War Timeline:** Trump stated the conflict with Iran "will end soon" and that if he chose to, he could destroy Iran's energy, bridges, and manufacturing in "three days, one day each." — *Donald Trump*
* **Pharmaceutical Tariffs:** Trump stated that pharmaceutical companies face a 0% tariff in Year 1, 10% in Year 2, and a 300% tariff in Year 3 if they do not build their manufacturing plants in the U.S. — *Donald Trump*
* **Dealing with Democrats:** Trump predicted he will veto "a lot of things" Democrats send him initially, after which they will call to make deals on healthcare, drug prices, and potentially immigration reform. — *Donald Trump*
* **Presidential Legacy:** Trump predicted his legacy will be remembered as "somebody that made America great again" with unprecedented domestic industry and $22 trillion in investments. — *Donald Trump*

## NEC's Kevin Hassett Talks US Economic Goals | Bloomberg Talks

- Speakers: Kevin Hassett (National Economic Director, White House); David Westin (Host, Bloomberg Television)

---

**TL;DR:** White House National Economic Director Kevin Hassett expressed strong optimism for U.S. economic growth, projecting GDP expansion around 4% driven by capital spending, labor supply increases, and an AI-fueled productivity boom, while acknowledging that fiscal deficit targets will depend on outside forces and policy trade-offs.

### Economic Growth & Supply-Side Drivers
* **Current Growth Metrics:**
  * **Real GDP:** Currently tracking in the 5% range (specifically cited at 5.014 real), supported by strong incoming economic data.
  * **Growth Projections:** Base case growth is expected to cruise around 4% (rather than 3%), assuming no disruptions from the outside world.
* **Growth Accounting Breakdown:**
  * **Productivity Growth:** Estimated at approximately 2.5%.
  * **Capital Deepening:** Estimated at 0.7% to 0.8%.
  * **Labor Supply:** Estimated to contribute roughly similarly to capital deepening.
* **Labor Market Indicators:**
  * **Weekly Wages:** The administration emphasizes average weekly wages over hourly wages because tax stimuli are driving longer work weeks and increased work hours.
  * **Unemployment Claims:** Initial claims for unemployment insurance are at their lowest levels since World War II, showing minimal labor market stress.

### Artificial Intelligence & Productivity
* **AI Productivity Boom:** 
  * The current productivity boom is being observed in real time *before* benchmark revisions, signaling exceptionally strong momentum.
  * AI subscription prices have remained stable despite massive quality improvements, reflecting unmeasured deflationary productivity gains similar to the 1990s internet boom.
* **Community Pushback:** 
  * Local communities and states (such as Texas and New York) are enacting data center moratoriums.
  * **Voluntary Framework:** The administration’s AI policy remains strictly voluntary—no town is forced to host a data center, but firms building them must generate their own power.
* **Labor Impact of AI:** 
  * Academic studies show that firms adopting AI experience significant increases in sales, employment, and wages.
  * Conversely, the primary labor market stress is concentrated in firms that fail to adopt AI and consequently struggle or fail.

### Fiscal Policy, Deficits, & National Net Worth
* **Federal Workforce Reductions:** 
  * Federal employment has been reduced by approximately 300,000 workers, yielding roughly $500 billion in budget savings over 10 years.
  * Displaced workers transitioning to the private sector earn on average about $30,000 more than in government work, generating an additional $100 billion in tax benefits.
  * DC-area job numbers show no negative disruption from these cuts, as workers either retired or quickly secured private sector employment.
* **Deficit and Debt Perspectives:**
  * Reaching a 3% of GDP deficit target by the end of the term depends on avoiding unforeseen "force majeure" measures, though deficit reduction remains a priority for the president and key members of Congress.
  * **Household Net Worth:** While government debt is a liability for future generations, external debt sits at around $15 trillion, whereas household net worth net of debt has surged by roughly $90 trillion over the past decade.
  * **Capital vs. Consumption:** Federal policy has focused on building future capital (via tax and capital formation reforms) rather than just mailing consumption checks, resulting in historic capital formation numbers.

### Interest Rates & Yields
* **Real Return on Capital:** 
  * Rising long-term bond yields (including a 3% real yield on 30-year TIPS with 2.1% inflation) reflect a higher real return on capital driven by deregulation and pro-capital tax policies.
  * General equilibrium dynamics mean that high returns on capital (e.g., borrowing at 6% to fund a data center yielding 20%) naturally push borrowing rates upward until markets clear.

### Trumpanomics vs. Prior Economic Frameworks
* **Pillars of Trumpanomics:**
  * **Worker-First Supply Side:** Features worker-centric tax policies (such as no tax on tips and average tax refunds near $3,500 after major tax legislation) alongside traditional Reagan-style business supply-side incentives and faster deregulation.
  * **Trade Policy:** Aggressive use of tariffs is driving a massive wave of domestic onshoring.
* **Deficit Trade-Offs:**
  * Similar to Reaganomics, upfront tax reforms and pro-growth policies can initially expand deficits, but the long-term benefit of a high-capital, high-growth economy justifies the policy bundle.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Kevin Hassett** predicts the U.S. economy will "cruise around four [percent] instead of three" for growth, driven by capital spending, labor supply increases, and an AI productivity boom.
* **Kevin Hassett** claims that the deficit will track toward a 3% of GDP target over the next two years, though achievement will depend on whether "force majeure measures" arise.

### Notable Quotes

And so, I'd say that we're really, really bullish on the momentum that we have going forward."* — **Kevin Hassett**

> *"If you look at the liability for future generations, it's actually smaller than you might think when we're looking at, US net numbers because, you know, a lot of, uh, debt is debt we owe ourselves."* — **Kevin Hassett**
