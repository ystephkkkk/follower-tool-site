## The airline CEO putting Starlink on every plane

### TL;DR
Scott Kirby, CEO of United Airlines, details the airline’s contrarian pandemic strategy of massive fleet and technology expansion, the rollout of SpaceX Starlink across every plane, and why customer surveys and traditional metrics fail in the airline business.

### Fleet Expansion & Contrarian Bets
* **Pandemic Wide-Body Order:** During COVID when travel experts predicted demand would drop 50% forever and global competitors permanently retired wide-body fleets, Scott Kirby and United did the opposite, executing the largest aircraft order in history: 200 wide-bodies and several hundred narrow-bodies.
* **Capital Discipline vs. Short-Termism:** United invested heavily during the downturn to secure 30-year assets at advantageous pricing, mirroring cyclical manufacturing strategies like Samsung's memory investments.
* **Market Dominance in Hubs:** Scott Kirby highlights that single-hub operations (like Atlanta and Dallas) drive more international service and economic activity than fragmented hubs like Chicago, where United and American Airlines duplicate 16 daily flights to Milwaukee.

### Technology Stack & In-House Software
* **Core Tech Modernization:** In 2020, United invested hundreds of millions to move off mainframes onto the cloud and build modern databases, enabling fast execution that competitors cannot replicate.
* **In-House Reservation Legacy:** United is one of only two airlines globally (alongside Copa Airlines) that owns its own reservation system, refusing to outsource to Sabre or Apollo.
* **Seat-Back Map & Entertainment Redesign:** United is taking over map software from Panasonic to eliminate annoying globe views and build personalized preferences and proactive milestone memories (such as a live countdown to a passenger's 2 millionth mile).
* **AI Vibe Coding:** After taking three 1-hour tutoring sessions, Scott Kirby independently wrote a sports betting aggregation web app in 2.5 hours at his home gym, sparking an internal AI push.

### Starlink Rollout & Competitive Differentiation
* **Speed Over "Free":** United chose SpaceX's Starlink not because it is free, but because fast bandwidth is the true value proposition; legacy free Wi-Fi is compared to slow dial-up internet.
* **Fleet Rollout Pace:** United is currently at 48% of flights equipped with Starlink, upgrading about three planes per day and planning to reach 50% within a week and 100% in hubs like Los Angeles in the first half of next year.
* **Targeting Competitors:** Scott Kirby views Starlink as a temporary multi-year competitive advantage that will drive passengers from competing airlines (including Delta and joint-venture partner ANA) to try United and stay for its superior overall technology.

### Operational Hurdles & Air Traffic Control
* **ATC as the Primary Bottleneck:** Air traffic control accounts for 90% of airline operational issues, constrained by inefficient annual appropriations processes and political distribution of 450 regional facilities.
* **Weather Simulation:** United is using AI simulations for Denver summer pop-up thunderstorms to potentially reduce weather-impacted delays by 50%–75%.
* **Low-Cost Carrier Failures:** Low-cost carriers like Spirit Airlines failed because cost inflation and exorbitant airport authority fees (such as $52 per passenger in New York airports) made their business model mathematically unsustainable.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Hub-Specific Starlink Target:** Scott Kirby stated that Los Angeles will be United's first 100% Starlink-equipped hub in the first half of next year.
* **Weather Delay Reductions:** Scott Kirby claimed that AI simulations can reduce weather-impacted delays in Denver by 50% to 75% in the summer.
* **Industry Financial Outlook:** Scott Kirby predicted that 6 out of 10 US airlines will lose money this year.

---

### TL;DR
Toto Wolff breaks down how Mercedes spots generational F1 drivers, enforces a strict "No Dickheads" culture across 2,500 employees, and navigates F1's strict cost cap to build the most valuable team in the sport.

### Talent Spotting & Driver Evaluation
* **Early Karting Indicators:** **Toto Wolff** explains that racing talent is identifiable very early—sometimes seeing an 8-year-old beat 10-year-olds in karting, or watching a child comfortably slide a kart at 100 kilometers per hour in the rain.
* **The Unquantifiable Element:** Unlike cycling metrics (such as Tadej Pogačar's watt-per-kilogram threshold or VO2 max), driving success relies heavily on unmeasurable personality traits like resilience and teamwork.
* **The "Speed" and Connectedness:** Speed is defined by billions of neurosensors allowing a driver to "strap the car onto themselves." **Toto Wolff** notes that Lewis Hamilton famously described a car understeering "from the front axle to where I sit."

### Organizational Culture & Talent Management
* **The "No Dickheads" Policy:** Borrowed from the All Blacks over 10 years ago, **Toto Wolff** enforces a strict cultural filter across Mercedes' 2,500 employees (split into 150 trackside staff and 2,350 engineering/operational personnel across two UK hubs for engines and chassis), rejecting brilliant engineers who lack humility, integrity, and honesty.
* **Team Leadership & Scale:** **Toto Wolff** contrasts his founder-led approach with other F1 teams like Ferrari (where the team principal is one of 10 direct reports to the CEO) and Red Bull, noting his long leash and lack of fear of being fired.

### Economics, Cap Ex, and Valuations
* **The Cost Cap Era:** **Toto Wolff** notes that the $200M+ annual cost cap introduced by F1 eliminated long-term speculative R&D and lab work, forcing teams to weigh compute power and upgrades against current and next-season lap-time gains.
* **Financial Performance:** **Toto Wolff** reveals that Mercedes F1 generates approximately $1 billion in revenue with a 30% profit margin ($300 million EBIT).
* **Valuation Surge:** Mercedes F1 was valued at **£4.6 billion** in recent transactions (reflecting a roughly 15x EBIT multiple), representing a 7x increase from previous years, driven by scarcity and a growing demographic.
* **Demographic Shifts:** Driven heavily by *Drive to Survive*, F1's audience now boasts **42% female fans**, with the 15-25 age bracket experiencing the fastest growth.

### Regulations, Loophole Engineering, and Technical Risk
* **The Mock Trial Process:** When Mercedes develops a grey-area innovation, leading engineers and **Toto Wolff** hold a mock trial where inventors act as prosecutors, and a general counsel defends the part before deciding whether to deploy it or consult the FIA.
* **Historic Innovations:** **Toto Wolff** details the "DAS" (Dual-Axis Steering) system—where drivers could push and pull the steering wheel to adjust front-wheel toe effects and heat tires—which was permitted for one year before being banned.

### Early Venture Investing & Dot-Com Exits
* **Dot-Com Portfolio:** Before racing, **Toto Wolff** ran a private holding company investing in tech startups, including a free SMS web portal (`sms.at`) started by a teenager in Graz that scaled to 2 million users in a 9-million-person Austria.
* **The 2000 Exit:** After selling `sms.at` to T-Mobile for **€250 million**, a conversation with his CFO on a flight from Bonn to Vienna prompted **Toto Wolff** to liquidate 8 of his 9 tech investments just as the dot-com bubble burst in spring 2000.

## The company building underwater drones for $50,000

### TL;DR
Will O'Brien, co-founder and president of Ulysses, joins John and Chris Sacca to discuss how his company is scaling autonomous underwater drones to $50,000 price points to revolutionize ocean conservation, maritime defense, and offshore energy infrastructure.

### Guinness History and Technical Innovation
- **R&D Culture:** Guinness established an in-house R&D team as early as the 1890s, operating similarly to Xerox PARC by hiring brilliant minds for undirected problem-solving.
- **The Student's T-Test:** 
  - Developed by William Sealy Gosset in the early 1920s to calculate standard deviation and means from small sample sizes (under 30).
  - Created because Guinness needed to test hundreds of hops for lipid/fat content without losing a few percent per batch, which impacted margins in the low-margin food-and-beverage industry.
  - Published under the pseudonym "The Student" in the *Journal of Mathematica* to hide innovation and margin improvements from competitors like Anheuser-Busch and Heineken.
- **Project ACORN:** Developed in the 1980s (initially called Project Dynamite) to solve at-home Guinness consumption as pint prices rose from £1 to £1.50, resulting in the iconic widget.
- **Global Marketing Domination:** Guinness won more Cannes Lions advertising awards than nearly any other company, championed the "Guinness is Good for You" campaign, the toucan mascot, and made Nigeria its top-performing market by producing localized films.
- **The 7Cs Quality Standards:** A rigorous system covering crafted presentation, consistent freshness, counter visibility, correct gas, correct temperature, clean lines, and clean glassware.

### Ulysses' Ocean Robot Stack & Architecture
- **Company Genesis:** Founded three years ago by Will O'Brien and co-founders (including Colm, who leads manufacturing), initially focusing on seagrass restoration before expanding into defense and energy infrastructure. Lower Carbon Capital was the pre-seed lead investor.
- **The Vehicle Architecture:**
  - **Leviathan:** An autonomous surface craft with a 2,000-nautical-mile range, Starlink integration, and wireless underwater charging capabilities.
  - **Mako:** An underwater robotic vehicle built from modular "Lego building blocks" (allowing swappable battery packs, rear thrusters, stereo 3D cameras, and sonar front modules).
- **Core Engineering Specs:**
  - **Operating Depth:** Typically operates in the 1,500-meter range (where most economic activity occurs), crush-tested to 2,200 meters (full ocean technical depth is 6,000 meters).
  - **Onboard Compute:** Runs a full NVIDIA 5090 onboard with an Intel CPU, providing roughly 100 times more onboard compute than any other AUV.
  - **Communications:** Underwater comms are acoustical and low-bandwidth (~10 kilobytes per second over five kilometers), functioning like a dial-up modem ping.
- **Cost Disruption:** 
  - Base underwater vehicles (**Mako**) can be sold to navies for as cheap as $50,000, compared to standard US Navy AUVs costing roughly $5 million.
  - Surface vessels cost between $500,000 and $1 million.

### Seagrass & Ecological Pragmatism
- **Seagrass Value Proposition:** 
  - Covers 2% of the seafloor but holds 20% of the ocean's carbon sink.
  - Approximately 20% of commercially important fish stocks rely on seagrass habitats.
  - Native species grow along Ireland, the UK, California, the East Coast, Australia, Virginia, Southern Florida, Sausalito, and the Great Barrier Reef.
- **Eco-Pragmatism:** O'Brien describes himself as a pro-growth environmentalist who believes a flourishing future requires a consistently growing economy supported by a healthy biosphere.
- **Green Tape Friction:** Regulatory "green tape" is cited as the single biggest barrier to environmental restoration, delaying massive projects like a planned seagrass initiative in Australia.

### Defense, Offshore Energy, and Vertical Integration
- **Target Markets & Customers:** 
  - **US Navy:** Contracted for mine countermeasures (mine hunting) in regions like the Strait of Hormuz.
  - **Offshore Wind:** Mapping floating wind turbine power cables daily to prevent abandoned fishing lines from snagging on cables and trapping dolphins and whales; building robotic implements to cut away marine debris.
- **Vertical Integration Strategy:** 
  - Moving aggressively from off-the-shelf components (which comprised ~80% of generation-one vehicles) toward in-house manufacturing for scale.
  - Building proprietary software, sensor suites (acoustics teams building sonars in-house), and inertial navigation systems to bypass suppliers capped at 100–200 units per year.
- **The Economics of Autonomy:** Traditional ocean operations require expensive surface ships ($100k+/day) and massive human crews constrained by union laws and seasickness; autonomous swarms eliminate human labor costs and operate 24/7 in harsh sea conditions.
