## Offense or Defense? Positioning Portfolios in Today's Market with Howard Marks and Alper Daglioglu

- Channel: Brookfield
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVe2enQ0SOw
- Published: 2026-02-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260223.html#video-YVe2enQ0SOw
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management); Alper Daglioglu (Head of Global Investment Strategies, Morgan Stanley)

---

**TL;DR** 
The current market is characterized by extreme optimism following a historic three-year bull run, signaling a critical need for investors to shift from aggressive growth toward defensive positioning and predictable outcomes.

### Assessing the Current Market Cycle
* **Historic Performance:** The three-year period of 2023–2025 ranks as the 7th best three-year period for the S&P 500 in the last 100 years.
* **Sentiment Shift:** Optimism is currently "in the ascendancy." Howard Marks notes that when optimism rules, prices are high relative to intrinsic value, prospective returns are modest, and risk is elevated.
* **Strategic Adjustment:** Because the world remains highly unpredictable and certain 20th-century growth drivers (globalization, post-WWII recovery) are slowing, investors should prioritize defensiveness over sheer aggression.
* **Predictability vs. Maximization:** Investors face a fundamental tradeoff between maximizing returns and enhancing the reliability of those returns. Current conditions favor emphasizing "reliable outcomes."

### The Psychology of Risk and Contrarianism
* **Second-Level Thinking:** Successful investing requires the courage to be "uncomfortably idiosyncratic." Marks highlights that attractive valuations only appear during periods of "abject terror" (e.g., September 2008 or March 2020).
* **The Paradox of Risk:** The riskiest market environment is one where everyone believes there is no risk. Conversely, the safest time to buy is when the consensus believes risk is everywhere, as this sentiment drives prices to their floor.
* **Emotional Discipline:** Most human instincts—wanting to buy when things feel good and sell when things feel bad—are detrimental to wealth accumulation. To outperform, one must act as a contrarian, buying more when assets go "on sale."

### Overcoming Institutional and Behavioral Constraints
* **Volatility vs. Risk:** Marks argues that the investment industry wrongly equates volatility with risk. For long-term, well-funded investors, interim price fluctuations are a "part of life" and should not be avoided if they lead to higher terminal returns.
* **Agency Friction:** Institutional "agents" often prioritize career preservation over performance. This creates a bias toward avoiding losses that might lead to being fired, even at the cost of superior long-term gains.
* **The "Do Something" Bias:** Investors often focus on irrelevant short-term data, such as the exact month the Fed will stop raising rates. Marks asserts that "what really matters" is the long-term growth of company value and the ability of borrowers to repay debt.

### Evolving Asset Management Trends
* **Private Assets:** The shift toward private markets offers a "liquidity trade-off." While these assets lack daily mark-to-market pricing (reducing perceived volatility), they require specialized manager skill to navigate the lack of reference prices.
* **Concentrated Partnerships:** Global allocators are trending toward fewer, deeper relationships with General Partners (GPs). These strategic partnerships aim to treat the manager as an extension of the investor’s team to solve complex liability and liquidity needs over 5–10 year horizons.
* **Artificial Intelligence:** While AI will undoubtedly change the world, Marks warns that the "how, when, and who benefits" remains uncertain. Superior investing in this space will rely on personal judgment rather than "machinable" formulas.

> "The riskiest thing in the world is the belief that there's no risk. So one of the safest things in the world is the belief that risk is everywhere, because it puts prices so low that risk is minimal." — **Howard Marks**
