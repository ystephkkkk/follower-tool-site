## Why OpenAI Is Racing to Make Computer Use Superhuman

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9v42ZOdiBJY
- Published: 2026-10-26
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261026.html#video-9v42ZOdiBJY
- Speakers: Ari Weinstein (Employee, OpenAI); James Sun (Employee, OpenAI); Seema Amble (Host, a16z)

---

### TL;DR
OpenAI's computer-using agents, supercharged by the Astra model, are now faster than average humans at operating software and entering a massive scaling phase across consumer and enterprise markets. Led by OpenAI's Ari Weinstein and James Sun, the company is tackling enterprise adoption through deterministic sandboxes and human-in-the-loop controls, while driving consumer adoption through proactive "dots" and universal web navigation.

### Astra Model Capabilities & Performance
* **Speed and Efficiency:**
  * **Faster Execution:** Ari Weinstein noted that Astra is faster on average than previous models because it uses fewer thinking tokens to accomplish the same tasks.
  * **Approaching Superhuman:** The models are now faster than the average human at using a computer and are getting extremely close to being faster than expert human users.
  * **Video Game Benchmarks:** Internally, Astra plays video games against older models like `5.6` and wins every time, successfully beating games like *Fallout* in roughly 30 hours.
* **Reliability and Creative Work:**
  * **Edge Reliability:** Reliability has increased in hard-to-perceive ways that matter significantly in practice, moving from roughly 97% to 99% reliability.
  * **Creative Tasks:** Excels at creative work, such as drawing things in Microsoft Paint during live voice interactions.
* **Alignment and Security:**
  * **Enhanced Safety:** James Sun highlighted that Astra is OpenAI's most aligned model, featuring system cards showing much better resistance to prompt injections and unintended actions.
  * **Enterprise Trust:** This alignment makes it significantly safer for sensitive enterprise use cases like procurement and vendor/customer communications.

### Enterprise Automation & Legacy Software
* **Boring Opportunities in Systems of Record:**
  * **Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP):** Automates complex legacy software that lacks APIs and has been used for 15 to 20 years.
  * **Procurement Workflows:** Replaces giant spreadsheets (e.g., ordering lab equipment) and manual ops data-entry with automated end-to-end purchasing and vendor outreach.
  * **Finance Reconciliation:** Operates financial software and multiple ledgers at month-end to reconcile expenses without needing custom APIs.
  * **Sales & CRM:** Gathers prospecting data and inputs records across disparate sales software and CRM systems.
* **Vertical Industry Adoption:**
  * **Restaurant Group Example:** Software uses back-storage photos and vision to project traffic, automate grocery ordering via platforms like Shopee, and adjust employee timetables in ERP systems based on historical data.

### Enterprise Controls & Guardrails
* **Deterministic Sandboxes:**
  * **Application and URL Restrictions:** Enterprise admins can create a sandbox around the agent to deterministically restrict which apps, websites, and data sources it can access.
* **Human-in-the-Loop Dials:**
  * **Auto-Approve System ("Guardian"):** A smart model evaluates every agent action to decide if it is safe and aligned.
  * **Custom Intervention Levels:** Enterprises can dial in specific requirements—such as mandating human approval for file downloads, uploads, or visits to new websites.
* **Design & Trust Building:**
  * **Visual Cues:** Features a visible cursor (affectionately called the "Codex cursor" by the team) and fun animations so users can watch the agent perform tasks cleanly and transparently.

### Consumer Use Cases, "Dots," & Proactivity
* **Automating Drudgery:**
  * **Daily Tasks:** Fills out DMV forms, unsubscribes from email lists, organizes personal finance spreadsheets, and manages meal planning.
* **Cloud Computers and "Dots":**
  * **Dedicated Cloud Environments:** Users can spin up "dots," each getting its own cloud computer and full web browser to execute tasks.
  * **Proactive Interventions:** James Sun shared how dots proactively catch missed calendar invites from external Gmail emails and monitor Slack channels to surface critical feedback and parse Sentry logs.
* **Consumer Growth & Form Factors:**
  * **YC-Level Growth:** Following the integration of browser use into existing products, OpenAI has seen YC-level week-over-week growth.
  * **Mobile Expansion:** The next inflection point involves bringing powerful cloud computing and agentic workflows to mobile devices for billions of everyday users.

### The Future of Software: Legacy vs. AI-Native
* **Coexistence of Legacy and New Software:**
  * **Prolonging Legacy Systems:** Because computer use interacts with software the same way a human does (via UI rather than APIs), it extends the utility of 10-to-40-year-old legacy software.
  * **New Software Standards:** Standards like **WebMCP** are being adopted in in-app browsers, allowing agents and humans to share the interface collaboratively (e.g., agents doing tedious work while humans handle final transactions).
* **Hyper-Personalized Software:**
  * **Micro-Applications:** The low cost and complexity of creating software means future applications can be custom-tailored for 100 people or even a single individual (such as custom tactical trainers built for family members).
  * **Shared Intelligence Layers:** The vision points toward coherent user experiences where applications, browsers, and cloud agents share context rather than operating as isolated silos.

## Why AI Is Reinventing How Businesses Buy Everything

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OTQ-lFsq7zA
- Published: 2026-10-02
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261002.html#video-OTQ-lFsq7zA
- Speakers: Vladimir Keil (Co-founder and CEO, Lio); Seema Amble (Host, a16z); Elena Burger (Host, a16z)

---

### TL;DR
AI-native startups beat incumbents by automating end-to-end work outside traditional systems of record, such as using multi-agent systems to handle complex enterprise procurement tasks like sourcing, RFQs, negotiations, and exception handling.

### The Incumbent vs. AI-Native Startup Dynamic
* **System of Record Limitations:** **Sema Amble** notes that legacy incumbents are limited to their systems of record and fail to complete end-to-end jobs (e.g., resolving a billing dispute requires hitting billing, reviewing chat history, and analyzing contracts across multiple systems).
* **The Incumbent Advantage & Conflict:** **Sema Amble** explains that while Salesforce (Agent Force) and other incumbents have massive distribution and customer trust, they face internal conflicts between workflow tools and full-resolution agents, as well as misaligned sales org structures.
* **Building External Trust:** **Vladimir Keil** emphasizes that while incumbents have pre-existing trust, shipping immature autonomous features too early can destroy it. Startups must methodically earn trust by starting small and scaling up autonomous thresholds.

### The Four Tiers of AI Agents in Enterprise
* **Retrieval Agents:** Basic chatbots sitting on top of systems of record to pull up contract terms, verify data, or run light analytics (**Sema Amble**).
* **Process Agents:** Agents that execute clear processes without judgment, such as automatically applying credit approvals based on policy handbooks (**Sema Amble**).
* **Policy Agents:** Agents that apply situational judgment where rules lack strict definitions, such as determining if a 20-minute service outage qualifies as significant (**Sema Amble**).
* **Principal Agents:** Autonomous agents that weigh relationship health and business value to make high-level decisions beyond standard policy (**Sema Amble**).
* **Lio's Spectrum:** **Vladimir Keil** notes that Lio spans all four categories depending on risk and budget, operating fully autonomously for low-risk tasks while retaining human-in-the-loop oversight for high-value operations.

### The Hidden Complexity of Enterprise Procurement
* **The Iceberg of Work:** **Vladimir Keil** points out that while an ERP system records a simple final price (e.g., $8K for aluminum), the actual work involves roughly 30 stakeholder meetings, 500 emails, and 20 Excel sheets.
* **High-Stakes Physical Impact:** **Vladimir Keil** highlights that physical-world manufacturing (aircraft, data centers, humanoid robots) involves thousands of suppliers. A part arriving two weeks late can cause hundreds of millions of dollars in damages, making automated email processing and shipment tracking critical.
* **Unlocking Unmanaged Spend:** **Vladimir Keil** notes that enterprises historically ignored spend below $50K due to human capacity constraints; Lio agents now capture savings on smaller, unmanaged transactions.

### Multi-Agent Collaboration and Human-in-the-Loop
* **Cross-Departmental Coordination:** **Vladimir Keil** explains that Lio utilizes a multi-agent system because procurement touches legal, finance, cost engineering, and operations across multiple software tools.
* **Gradual Trust Scaling:** **Vladimir Keil** states that enterprises never start with fully autonomous high-value negotiations. Lio uses a unit-in-the-loop approach, feeding agents feedback from human experts (like cost engineers and procurement specialists) to scale trust from 10K negotiations to 100K+ negotiations.
* **Direct vs. Indirect Procurement:** **Vladimir Keil** distinguishes between indirect procurement (laptops, MRO parts, marketing services) where agents run fully autonomously, and direct procurement (building airplanes or drones with 100 to 2,000 strategic suppliers), where agents assist expert human engineers behind the scenes.

### Model Commoditization and the Last 20%
* **Foundational Models as Commodities:** **Vladimir Keil** treats underlying foundation models as a commodity for reading PDFs and creating spreadsheets, but notes that specialized use cases require proprietary data, harness integration, and fine-tuning.
* **The Last 20% Rule:** **Vladimir Keil** explains that while engineers can build basic retrieval agents in 8 hours, reaching the final 20% of accuracy required for production demands heavy integration, memory workflows, and exception handling.
* **Internal Build Failures:** **Sema Amble** shares that Fortune 500 companies attempting to build internal cash collection tools over 3–4 months repeatedly hit roadblocks due to poor-quality context, fragmented data across multiple ERPs, and the sheer overhead of ongoing exception handling.

### Notable Quotes
> "No company and no enterprise starts with fully autonomous negotiation agents from day one. Why? Because they don't trust us and they don't trust the technology from day one."
> — **Vladimir Keil**

> "Procurement historically may have been more in a box and now it's touching legal, it's touching finance. It's touching a bunch of different software systems and people. The opportunity for the AI native startup is to say, we're going to own that entire end-to-end arc."
> — **Sema Amble**

> "You can get to 80%, but those last 20% really matter, and you can only get—they matter to get into production. So that's why you need this harness... you need all those like integrations, memory, workflows, and sometimes you need vertical data."
> — **Vladimir Keil**


## Software in the Age of Agents | The a16z Show

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mxs4erDxOEE
- Published: 2026-07-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260707.html#video-Mxs4erDxOEE
- Speakers: Seema Amble (Partner, a16z); Steven Sinofsky (Board Partner, a16z); Elena Burger (Partner, a16z)

---

**TL;DR**
As AI agents become the primary users of software, the value of enterprise applications is shifting from front-end user interfaces to the underlying data and logic ("headless" software). While AI can automate the mundane, replacing legacy systems like SAP remains difficult because these platforms codify a company’s unique business rules and exception-handling processes.

### The Rise of "Headless" and Agentic Software
* **Definition of Headless:** Seema Amble (Partner, a16z) explains that headless software decouples the data and logic from the user interface (UI). In an agentic world, the UI becomes optional because agents access systems via APIs rather than clicking through screens.
* **The Salesforce "Headless 360" Announcement:**
    * **Market Acknowledgment:** While largely a marketing rebrand of existing APIs, the move signals an industry-wide shift toward making systems of record accessible to agents.
    * **Increased Integration:** Usage of Slack agents and chatbots has surged (e.g., a 300% increase in Slackbot usage), allowing users to retrieve CRM data without ever logging into the Salesforce "EOS" interface.
* **API vs. Agent Capabilities:** Steven Sinofsky (Board Partner, a16z) distinguishes between three levels of agent interaction:
    * **Lookup:** Simple data retrieval.
    * **Action:** Changing the system of record (requires complex credentialing and seat-license management).
    * **Analysis:** Synthesizing unstructured information across multiple systems, which Sinofsky identifies as the most promising use case for LLMs.

### Why Legacy SaaS Remains Indestructible
* **The "Vibe Coding" Misconception:** Amble argues it is a mistake to believe a simple database and an API can replace heavyweights like SAP. The value isn't just the data; it’s the millions of lines of custom logic that define how a specific business operates.
* **Codifying the Business:** Sinofsky notes that companies like Ford, Toyota, and Walmart are defined by their internal processes. 
    * **Customization as a Moat:** Although Larry Ellison (Oracle) famously complained about software customization, it is those very customizations that differentiate how companies hedge currencies, buy materials, and manage workforces.
    * **Stickiness via "Inertia":** Software becomes "sticky" when it is tied to regulatory compliance (HIPAA), money collection (Stripe), or deep muscle memory (the "Excel as a platform" effect).
* **The "Export to Excel" Escape Valve:** Most enterprise software is limited by its UI. Sinofsky notes that the most used features in any SaaS product are "Export to CSV" or "PDF," because users invariably need to take data elsewhere to perform actual analysis.

### AI and the "Long Tail" of Exceptions
* **The Root of Enterprise Challenge:** Almost everything interesting in a business is an "exception."
    * **Context Graphs:** Agents currently struggle with "context"—the unwritten rules or "standard operating procedures" (SOPs) that exist in a human’s head but aren't logged in a CRM.
    * **Handling the Edge Cases:** Success in automation requires observing how humans handle exceptions and capturing that "data exhaust" to train more sophisticated agents.
* **The Productivity Paradox:** Sinofsky argues that automation does not destroy jobs but shifts them.
    * **Example (Amazon):** Amazon automated customer service for simple returns (e.g., toothpaste), but this created a new need for back-end analysis to prevent shipping errors in the first place.
    * **The Law of Expanding Work:** As software makes tasks easier, businesses simply dream up more sophisticated things to do. For example, legal contracts will likely become longer and more complex because AI makes them easier to draft, not shorter and simpler.

### Strategic Opportunities for Startups
* **Avoid Head-on Competition:** Startups should not try to build a "better" version of a legacy CRM or ERP by matching it feature-for-feature.
* **Target "The Middle":** The greatest opportunity lies in the gaps between established players or between functional departments (e.g., bridging the handoff between Sales and Finance).
    * **Incumbent Blindness:** Established players will "bolt on" AI to protect their existing margins; they are unlikely to reinvent their entire architecture. 
* **The Internal Network Effect:** While consumer network effects are rare in SaaS, a "viral loop" can occur inside a company when a tool (like ChatGPT or Figma) allows different teams to communicate and share expertise in ways that were previously siloed.
* **Vertical Software Focus:** Capturing data from the "physical world"—such as construction or manufacturing—remains a massive opportunity for AI to digitize previously unrecorded human interactions and field work.
