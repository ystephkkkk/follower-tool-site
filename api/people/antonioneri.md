## Tech Slides as Nvidia, AI Risks Take Center Stage

- Channel: Bloomberg Tech

**TL;DR:** Tech stocks tumbled on Tuesday—with Nvidia dropping 8% and Anthropic falling 12% below its $83 IPO price—as initial euphoria over Jensen Huang’s reinstatement as Nvidia CEO gave way to renewed anxieties over US tariffs, export controls, and ongoing federal investigations. Meanwhile, HPE CEO Antonio Neri reported durable AI demand and raised the company's full-year outlook, emphasizing that Huang's brief absence did not disrupt customer orders.

### Tech Selloff & Anthropic's Post-IPO Slide
* **Market Reversal:** Following Monday’s rally—which saw the Nasdaq 100 post its best day since April 2025 and Nvidia record its best session since 2003—tech stocks retreated on Tuesday, with the Nasdaq 100 declining up to 3% before recovering slightly, and Nvidia falling 8%.
* **Unanswered AI Questions:** Bloomberg Equities Reporter Carmen Reinicke noted that Huang’s return does not resolve broader market anxieties regarding the sustainability of AI capital expenditures (capex), fresh US tariffs, and potential export curbs to China.
* **Anthropic Underperforms:** Anthropic shares dropped roughly 12% to around $77, falling below its $83 IPO price and wiping out gains for early investors.
  * Reinicke pointed out that Anthropic lacks the institutional and retail buying support seen in recent IPOs like SpaceX.
  * Big Tech Editor Sarah Frier added that while Anthropic raised needed capital and employees face a 180-day lockup, the volatility—and capital shifting back to Nvidia—creates a challenging valuation benchmark for other frontier AI labs.

### Jensen Huang’s Return & Washington Scrutiny
* **Ongoing Investigations:** Bloomberg’s Ian King reported that while Huang has retaken control of Nvidia, his long-term tenure remains at risk. He admitted to breaking company rules, and intelligence and law enforcement agencies are still investigating potential national security implications and evidence of chip smuggling. In response, Nvidia plans to hire a Head of Compliance.
* **Congressional Hearings Next Week:** Bloomberg Tech Editor Mike Shepard outlined Huang’s upcoming Washington schedule:
  * **Tuesday:** Closed-door testimony before three House national security committees (Intelligence, China Select, and Foreign Affairs) regarding his affair with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, coinciding with the Nvidia Developers Conference in D.C.
  * **Wednesday:** Nvidia’s General Counsel will appear behind closed doors before the same three House committees to address export control compliance and potential influence over the Commerce Department.
  * **Thursday:** Huang will testify publicly before the Senate Banking Committee.
* **Presidential Intervention:** President Donald Trump directly engaged with Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang, a move Senator Dave McCormick (R-PA) criticized as a concerning precedent for executive branch involvement in corporate governance.

### Market & Geopolitical Implications of Huang's Comeback
* **Restoring the "Nvidia Put":** Bloomberg Intelligence Senior Semiconductor Analyst Kunjan Sobhani stated that Huang’s return removes succession risks and restores product strategy continuity, though SEC probes, California subpoenas, and export control scrutiny remain overhangs.
* **Analyst Confidence:** StoneX Financial Equity Research Analyst Cody Acree maintained a $270 price target on Nvidia, noting that while CFO Colette Kress could manage financials, she lacked the engineering leadership required to sustain Nvidia's architectural roadmap and industry relationships.
* **US-China AI Race:** Bloomberg Economics Analyst Michael Deng explained that Huang’s return accelerates US frontier AI progress while China remains constrained by strict export controls, including a recent Commerce Department rule closing remote cloud-access loopholes in Southeast Asia.
* **Export Control Skepticism:** Wiley Rein Partner Nazak Nikakhtar argued that China has been working to indigenize its semiconductor industry since the 1950s and is neck-and-neck with the US, meaning the US must pull back technology transfers rather than rely on Commerce Department enforcement alone.

### HPE's AI Demand & Diversified Compute
* **Raised Full-Year Outlook:** HPE CEO Antonio Neri reported record Q4 2026 performance and issued strong 2027 guidance: 31% revenue growth at the midpoint, 26% operating profit growth, 31% non-GAAP EPS growth, and 20% free cash flow growth. HPE is also returning 75% of free cash flow to shareholders via buybacks and increasing dividends by 90%.
* **Resilient Demand:** Neri stated that HPE saw zero change in customer demand during Huang’s 11-day absence. HPE recently opened an AI factory in Europe with Nvidia and is seeing accelerating demand for diversified compute, including the AMD Helios stack (which connects 72 AMD GPUs with six trays of networking).
* **Supply Constraints & Risk:** Neri noted that HPE's revenue conversion remains severely constrained by supply availability (with 56% order billing growth vs. 20% planned 2027 growth). He also clarified that HPE has no financial exposure to neoclouds and recently closed its China business.

### Other Tech News
* **Sanofi Acquires Insmed:** Bloomberg’s Isabelle Lee reported that Sanofi is buying Insmed for $41 billion (a 46% premium) to expand its rare disease portfolio.
* **Xiaomi Slump:** Xiaomi’s revenue fell 5% for a third straight quarter, with its EV/AI division reporting a loss.
* **DeepSeek Valuation:** DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng is now the world's richest AI model founder, with a $55 billion fortune following a funding round valuing the Chinese startup at $86 billion.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Inference Dominance:** By the end of the decade, 80% of AI demand will be in the inferencing space rather than training. — *Antonio Neri, CEO of HPE*
* **Nvidia Growth Outlook:** Nvidia’s 75% growth outlook for fiscal 2028 will not change, and there is a possibility it could even rise. — *Kunjan Sobhani, Senior Semiconductor Analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence*
* **Export Control Legislation:** Bipartisan calls in Congress for stricter export control measures on advanced chips will likely see a push to pass before the end of the year. — *Mike Shepard, Bloomberg Tech Editor*
