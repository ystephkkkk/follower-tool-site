## Inside the Urgent Effort to Prepare America’s Workforce for a Massive AI Disruption

### TL;DR
Former U.S. Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo leads the new organization RAISE US to prevent severe economic and gender inequality from AI disruption by partnering with state governors and business leaders on career transition pilots. Meanwhile, Maven CEO Kate Ryder emphasizes the enduring value of keeping healthcare human in an era of technological anxiety.

### Workforce Disruption & Economic Risks
- **Worst-Case Scenario:** Unchecked AI deployment could lead to tens of millions of displaced or underemployed workers, an unemployment rate of 10% to 20% among young people in their 20s, deep and long-term economic recession, street rioting, fraying democracy, and subsequent heavy regulation that halts innovation and cedes global leadership to China.
- **Vulnerable Job Categories:** Roles heavily clustered in clerical, administrative, and data tasks face immediate exposure, specifically call center workers, billing staff, claims assessors, healthcare coders, and researchers.
- **The Gender Gap Threat:** Raimondo expresses deep concern that AI will disproportionately impact women because they are clustered in vulnerable clerical/administrative roles and historically adopt new technology at slower rates than men.
- **Current Business Dynamics:** Corporate response is split; while some CEOs focus solely on short-term share price maximization, a coalition of 24 to 25 companies is partnering with RAISE US to proactively experiment with retraining and retention.

### RAISE US: Strategies & State-Level Pilots
- **Organizational Mission:** Serving as the leading organization to orchestrate a planned transition to an AI economy by providing private capital, partnering with governors, and redesigning workforce incentives.
- **Maryland Partnership:** Collaborating with the state on a government-supported year of service for young people modeled after AmeriCorps/Teach for America, focusing initially on healthcare and teaching roles where participants earn credentials and transition to full-time employment.
- **Arkansas Partnership:** Working with the governor to embed navigation tools directly into unemployment insurance collection points to connect displaced workers with community skills training and open jobs.
- **Proposed Policy Levers:** Developing wage insurance pilots (such as in Pennsylvania) to top up salaries for older workers taking lower-paying transitional jobs, and reforming outdated unemployment insurance systems and payroll taxes that currently penalize human employment over AI agents.
- **Business Accelerators:** Operating small business accelerators to help individuals use AI to start their own businesses.

### Human Skills & Individual Resilience
- **Advice for the Workforce:** Cultivate comfort with change, build continuous AI fluency rather than fear, and channel workplace anxiety into productive action and advocacy.
- **Enduring Human Capacities:** Judgment, reasoning, thinking, whole-context decision-making, and direct human-to-human interaction remain uniquely human and non-automated.
- **Alternative Sectors:** Non-college-educated individuals, particularly young women, are encouraged to consider high-demand, stable trades (such as plumbing, pipefitting, and electrical work) that require physical presence and are immune to AI automation.

### Healthcare & Human-Centric Care
- **Maven's Core Value:** Maven CEO Kate Ryder highlights the ongoing imperative to "keep healthcare human," noting that previous fears of robots replacing doctors have resurfaced as modern anxiety over AI replacing medical professionals.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Congress Inaction:** "I would bet you a lot of money that Congress won't pass effective bipartisan legislation helping American workers in the next three years. It's just not going to happen." — Gina Raimondo
- **AI-Powered Data Integration (by 2030):** By 2030, integrated data systems will enable seamless, rapid skill assessment and localized community retraining for displaced workers (such as a 55-year-old woman losing her job). — Gina Raimondo

## She's the youngest woman to have led a Fortune 500 company and now runs a $100B+ healthcare giant

- Channel: CNBC Television
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9xQXkCH0S8
- Published: 2026-02-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260217.html#video-v9xQXkCH0S8
- Speakers: Sarah London (CEO, Centene Corporation); Julia Boorstin (Senior Media & Tech Correspondent, CNBC)

---

**TL;DR**
Sarah London, CEO of Centene, applies an athlete’s mindset of individual accountability and "team-first" winning to lead a $100B healthcare giant through industry volatility and digital transformation. Her leadership focuses on radical transparency, bidirectional communication, and a "shuttle method" of work-life balance to sustain peak performance.

### The Athlete-Leader Framework
*   **The Competitive Edge:** London’s background as a Harvard Division 1 tennis player instilled an innate drive to win, but she emphasizes that "winning" means finding the best possible answer for the company, not necessarily being the person who provides it.
*   **Embracing Failure:** High-level sports taught her to view failure as a necessary process for improvement. She credits the CEO role with "beating the perfectionism" out of her because the scale and complexity of healthcare allow no room for perfect solutions.
*   **Accountability Dynamics:**
    *   **Individual Ownership:** Tennis creates a "singular accountability" where you win or lose alone on the court.
    *   **Corporate Leadership:** London believes a CEO should take 100% of the blame for failures while redirecting 100% of the credit to the team.

### Radical Transparency and Communication
*   **The "Why" Behind Decisions:** London is known for an unusual level of transparency, including regular video updates for Centene’s 60,000+ employees. She believes workers must understand the logic of a journey to stay committed to it.
*   **Bidirectional Dialogue:** She maintains a cadence of meeting with employees at all levels, particularly those early in their careers, to gather candid feedback on what is not "bubbling up" through traditional management layers.
*   **Vision as an Organizing Force:** 
    *   **Storytelling:** Effective leadership requires painting a vivid picture of the destination to create a "gravitational pull."
    *   **Decision Touchstones:** When the vision is clear (e.g., industry-leading outcomes at an industry-leading cost structure), employees can use those goals as "touchstones" to navigate independent negotiations and problems.

### Leadership Tactics and Startup Roots
*   **Listening as a Superpower:** London defines her primary role as making "51-49 percent calls"—the difficult decisions where there is no clear right answer. This requires taking in data from trusted sources and ignoring the ego-driven need to be the smartest person in the room.
*   **High-Trust Environments:** Drawing from her experience at the startup Humedica (later acquired by Optum), she emphasizes that high-talent teams perform best when there is deep trust.
    *   **The "Thanksgiving Massacre":** She cites a formative experience where the entire startup team worked through Thanksgiving weekend to meet a client deadline, creating a lasting bond through shared accountability.
*   **Technology as a Democratizer:** London views AI and digitization as essential forces for modernizing healthcare outcomes and managing the current "fraught" industry landscape.

### Sustaining Performance and Preventing Burnout
*   **The Burnout Reality:** Approximately nine months into her tenure as CEO, London hit a wall characterized by 4:00 AM starts, neglecting physical health, and losing connection with her family. 
*   **The Athlete’s Recovery Mindset:** Just as professional athletes (like Carlos Alcaraz) take full days off before major finals, London realized that constant "repetitive stress" in a single lane depletes energy and perspective.
*   **The "Shuttle Method" of Balance:** 
    *   Instead of a hard line between work and life, she uses a "shuttle" approach where work and family life are integrated and transparent. 
    *   **Support System:** Her husband stepped back to be a full-time father, providing the domestic stability required for her to lead a Fortune 500 company.
    *   **Mental Outlets:** She prioritizes seven hours of sleep and reads fiction (specifically mysteries and romance novels) to maintain mental wellness and "step out of the moment."
