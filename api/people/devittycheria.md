### TL;DR
MongoDB CEO Dev Ittycheria—who returned after a 10-month retirement following an 11-year run—breaks down the core job of a CEO into three non-negotiable pillars: setting strategy, putting the right people on the bus, and creating an environment where they can win. In an era of AI-driven speed, he argues that a CEO’s toughest calls test courage rather than intelligence, requiring radical candor, fast decision-making, and a relentless hunt for bad news.

### The Three Jobs of a CEO & The Boomerang Return
* **The Core Framework:** Credited to former Palo Alto Networks CEO Mark McLaughlin, Dev Ittycheria defines the CEO role by three responsibilities; doing 997 other things well while failing at these three means failing as a CEO:
  * **Strategy:** Defining where the company is going and what it aspires to be (which changes infrequently).
  * **People:** Ensuring the right core leadership team is on the bus to execute the strategy (which requires constant assessment).
  * **Environment:** Making capital allocation decisions and building a culture ("culture as a product") that attracts, retains, and sets people up for success.
* **The Return to MongoDB:** After retiring for 10 months to spend time with family and join Sequoia, Dev received a call in Tuscany from the board chair that his successor was resigning. He returned within a week out of responsibility to MongoDB's 6,000 employees, thousands of customers, and investor base—just ahead of an Investor Day and their largest user conference in New York.
* **Immediate Restructuring:** Upon returning, Dev simplified the organization by consolidating product—which his predecessor had split into two groups—and bringing in an interim executive to cover a vacant role, aiming to eliminate ambiguity and accelerate decision-making.

### Speed, Truth, and Confrontation
* **Decision Velocity:** A company is the sum of its decisions; the best companies make good decisions quickly over a long period.
* **The Vulnerability Secret:** Dev admits his intensity can intimidate people, so he uses vulnerability—admitting his own mistakes and knowledge gaps—to create psychological safety. When leaders admit mistakes, teams mimic them, surface truth faster, and solve problems more quickly.
* **Confrontation Over Conflict Aversion:** Avoiding issues causes them to fester; if a team knows an issue exists and the CEO ignores it, the CEO becomes the problem by lowering the standard.
* **The Asymmetry of Information:** Outside the company (Glassdoor, Blind, X, Wall Street), bad news is overrepresented. Inside the company, good news finds the CEO automatically, while bad news travels slowly upward and is often sanitized. Dev assumes any bad news he hears is both late and worse than reported, so he actively hunts for it.

### Scaling Leadership & Evaluating Talent
* **Courage Over Intelligence:** Over 11 years at MongoDB, Dev went through 23 C-level executives. The hardest calls were not intellectual, but emotional—deciding to put the company ahead of loyal early executives who could not scale to the next stage.
  * **Tells of an Unscalable Exec:** Lack of self-awareness, losing their team ("you can fool people above you, sometimes around you, never below you"), and declining hiring quality.
* **What Dev Looks for in a Successor CEO:**
  * **Ownership Mindset:** Treating the business as if it is 100% of their net worth, owned for 100 years (citing Warren Buffett).
  * **Long-Term Orientation:** Avoiding myopic, transactional decisions driven by 6-to-12-month stock price targets or short-term equity triggers.
  * **People Development:** Going beyond recruiting and execution to actively coaching, giving constructive feedback, and holding people accountable.
  * **Crisis & AI Thinking:** Distinguishing "builders" from "harvesters" during downturns, and having a clear point of view on how AI impacts org design, capital allocation, and innovation.
* **Evaluating Founders & CROs:**
  * **Founders:** Brian Halligan (HubSpot CEO & Sequoia partner) and Dev debate raw compute (e.g., math Olympiad/chess prodigies) versus wisdom, taste, and judgment. Dev argues that in a world where intelligence is ubiquitous and cheap, intellectual humility, taste, and the ability to learn ("student of the game") matter more than being the "smartest kid in the class."
  * **CROs:** Dev warns against "cut-and-paste" playbooks, noting that infrastructure sales (like MongoDB's) require winning over developers, security, ops, and economic buyers, unlike many application sales. He looks for CROs who succeeded selling a second- or third-tier product against a strong incumbent rather than riding inbound demand at a market leader, and stresses conducting blind reference checks.

### Operating in the AI Era
* **What Has Changed for Modern CEOs:**
  * **Shorter Decision Half-Lives:** The world moves too fast to punt on decisions; most decisions today are two-way doors, making speed a primary weapon.
  * **Token & Unit Economics:** With compute and GPU costs, CEOs must understand gross margins and the value delivered on top of consumed tokens.
  * **Talent Density:** Companies can do much more with fewer people, placing an even higher premium on top talent.
  * **Modulating Euphoria and Panic:** Founders must keep egos in check during inbound surges (warning against COVID-style overhiring and bloat) and keep teams steady when new models drop.
* **Planning & Focus:** While running 12-month planning cycles, Dev recasts plans every 90 days to align expenses with real revenue and ruthlessly cut underperforming projects.
* **Brian Halligan’s Takeaways for HubSpot:** Returning as CEO of HubSpot, Brian is applying Dev’s advice by encouraging employees to act as if they own 100% of HubSpot shares, making faster two-way-door decisions, obsessing over leading indicators (usage, NPS, unit economics), and maintaining a steady presence between high highs and low lows.
