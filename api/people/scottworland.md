### TL;DR
Successful enterprise AI deployment requires a solid data strategy and straight-through processing before building agents, and teams must be willing to throw away obsolete workflow scaffolding as frontier models and evaluation capabilities improve.

### Data Strategy & Straight-Through Processing
* **Foundational Data Requirements:** John Stecher (Blackstone) emphasizes that a cohesive data strategy moving transactional data into accessible, managed systems is mandatory before building AI agents, noting that failure to do so will cause agent initiatives to fail.
* **Straight-Through Processing Optimization:** Stecher notes that straight-through processing still matters; he uses development tools (such as Factory) to study existing workflows, write optimization code, and inject AI intelligence only where humans previously made decisions.
* **SDLC Offloading:** AI agents are heavily leveraged at Blackstone to handle tedious software development tasks including writing test cases (functional and performance) and versioning forward libraries underneath applications.

### Evals, Benchmarks, & High-Accuracy Environments
* **Financial Services Accuracy Bar:** John Stecher highlights that the duty to be accurate in financial services is extremely high, requiring rigorous evaluation sets to test agent output consistency against shifting model versions, harness changes, and data updates.
* **Norm AI Evaluation Dataset:** Scott Worland (Norm AI) shares that Norm accumulated tens of thousands of hand-labeled evaluation examples by domain experts, enabling the team to regression-test their platform and immediately benchmark new agentic approaches.
* **Scorecard-Driven Development:** Norm AI partners directly with attorneys at its affiliated law firm, Norm Law, to establish scorecards that measure success across multiple dimensions rather than traditional feature roadmaps.

### The Bitter Lesson & Throwaway Software
* **Revisiting the Bitter Lesson:** Scott Worland and John Stecher discuss Rick Sutton's bitter lesson (first articulated in 2019, with historical roots back to 1950s perceptrons and 1980s machine translation), noting that handcrafted workflow systems are ultimately dominated by systems that scale with compute.
* **Obsolete Scaffolding:** Worland explains that Norm AI built complex map-reduce workflows and scaffolding early on to ensure consistency, but later realized through their large eval set that most of this thoughtfully written code was obsolete and actively in the way.
* **Rebuilding the Platform:** Following this realization, Norm AI's engineering leadership drove to an Airbnb in upstate New York after a snowstorm to completely respec and rebuild their platform for agentic experiences and AI co-development.
* **Disposable Internal Applications:** Stecher advocates for leaning into AI software harnesses to rapidly build proofs of concept (POVs), put them in front of stakeholders, iterate quickly, and throw them away if unneeded—saving significant time and money compared to lengthy planning.

### ROI & Strategic Model Timing
* **Cost-Benefit Balance:** John Stecher weighs project ROI against the cost of building today versus waiting six months when more sophisticated models and higher compute availability will solve problems more cheaply.
* **Strategic Delays:** Stecher notes that Blackstone deliberately delays building certain applications when there is no pressing business value increase, waiting for frontier models to mature.
* **Targeted Investment Decisions:** Scott Worland describes navigating this gradient by investing heavily in foundational primitives (data capture, distillation, state management, benchmarks, evals) while remaining flexible enough to index into third-party frontier updates.
* **Claude Code Inflection Point:** Worland cites his experience updating hard on Claude Code—where an LLM could write code, execute it, and run a computer in a verifiable way—as the moment he recognized the arrival of truly generalizable intelligence capable of handling complex knowledge work.
