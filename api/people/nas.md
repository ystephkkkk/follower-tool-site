## Hip Hop’s Origin Story, Told by the Legends Who Lived It

- Channel: a16z

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261031.html#video-_d83IiEU7_0

### TL;DR
Hip-hop legends Kurtis Blow, Nas, Rev Run, and Roxanne Shanté celebrated the foundational roots of the culture, tracing hip-hop's birth from 1970s New York crises and disco into a global movement. The panel honored Kurtis Blow’s pioneering role as hip-hop's first major-label rapper and his mentorship of a young Run-DMC.

### Origins of Hip Hop and New York City in Crisis
* **Roots in a Broken City:** **Kurtis Blow** explained that early hip-hop emerged out of the 1970s New York fiscal crisis, civil rights trauma following the assassinations of Martin Luther King Jr., Malcolm X, and Robert/John F. Kennedy, and widespread arson in the Bronx and Harlem where landlords burned buildings for insurance money.
* **The "Ghetto Disco" Era:** **Kurtis Blow** described hip-hop as a direct mutation of disco culture, noting that after working all week, youth would seek escapism in downtown clubs featuring strobe lights and disco balls.
* **Underage Club Research:** At age 16 in 1975, **Kurtis Blow** used a phony ID from 42nd Street to enter major New York discos like Levigas, Nemos, Nell Gwyns, Superstar Cafeteria, Hotel Diplomat, Ipanemas, and the Garage to study DJ styles.
* **Early MCing Roots:** **Kurtis Blow** began MCing in 1974 at "The Wham"—an old police station on 152nd Street and Amsterdam—imitating his radio hero Hank Spangen (WWR-L Super 16).

### The Rise of Kurtis Blow and the Music Business Strategy
* **Building a Track Record:** **Kurtis Blow** studied communications at CCNY and learned that to succeed in a city with heavy competition (at least 50 MCs in 1978–79), an artist needed to build a track record on the outskirts of the city before breaking into the big market.
* **Connecting with Russell Simmons:** **Kurtis Blow** met Russell Simmons in college in 1977, brought him to a crowded 150-person house party in Harlem, rocked Chic's "Good Times," and eventually launched the Night Fevers disco in Simmons' neighborhood, helping establish Rush Productions.

### Mentorship and the Birth of Run-DMC
* **Taking Joey Under His Wing:** **Kurtis Blow** mentored a 13-to-15-year-old Joey (later **Rev Run**), protecting him from Russell Simmons, letting him stay at his house on weekends, and paying him $35 a show (which bought him Quantas turntables).
* **Touring and Stage Presence:** **Rev Run** recounted carrying two crates of records by train and bus, cooking breakfast for the crew, and developing his iconic stage persona while opening for Kurtis Blow and the Fat Boys.
* **Inspiration for the On-Stage Persona:** **Rev Run** noted that his aggressive stage antics—including smashing microphones every night and shouting *"This is my motherf***ing house!"*—were inspired by Eddie Murphy's stand-up comedy and watching Kurtis Blow tour alongside major acts like Bob Marley and Marvin Gaye.

### Hip-Hop Legacy and Mutual Respect
* **Revisiting a Classic:** **Nas** explained how deeply Kurtis Blow's 1980 movie appearance and song *If I Ruled the World* impacted him, inspiring him to later remake the track with Ms. Lauryn Hill.
* **Industry Validation:** **Kurtis Blow** recalled listening to the Lauryn Hill remake in his Nissan for two hours, calling Sony to guarantee it would go double platinum (which it did with proper promotion).
* **The Spirit of Paid in Full:** **Roxanne Shanté** concluded the discussion by emphasizing that *Paid in Full* celebrates the passion, accomplishments, and foundational contributions of pioneers like Kurtis Blow who fought to make hip-hop what it is today.

## Why the People Who Built Hip-Hop Ended Up With Nothing

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZO6VyDvysxY

- Speakers: Ben Horowitz (Co-founder, Andreessen Horowitz); Nas (Rapper and Co-founder, Paid in Full Foundation); Grandmaster Caz (Hip-hop pioneer)

### TL;DR
The Paid in Full Foundation pairs financial grants with deep personal recognition to honor and support the foundational pioneers of hip-hop who built a multi-billion dollar culture while capturing almost none of its wealth. Founded by Ben Horowitz, Steve Stoute, and Nas alongside Felicia Horowitz, the initiative fosters genuine community, healing, and cross-generational connection among hip-hop legends.

### Origins and Mission of the Paid in Full Foundation
* **Foundational Inspiration:** Ben Horowitz was inspired to start the foundation because he purchased the album *Paid in Full* for $10 and derived at least $5 million in career value from it, feeling a pressing need to pay that value back.
* **The Core Problem:** 
  * **Financial Disconnect:** Artists poured immense creativity into culture—changing fashion, dialect, and lifestyle brands like Tommy Hilfiger and Adidas—while their bank accounts and acknowledgments remained empty.
  * **Industry Division:** The music industry historically separated artists by success, status, old school versus new school, and competitiveness, creating an every-man-for-yourself culture.
* **Nas's Insight on Delivery:** When consulted, Nas noted that simply handing out money wouldn't work in hip-hop because artists would view it as charity or reparations; the support had to be paired with profound public honor, dignity, and celebration.
* **The Grandmaster Awards:** Created to honor true greats, the foundation initially faced widespread disbelief, with some artists rejecting the calls or awards because they thought it was a scam due to decades of industry mistreatment.

### Impact and Personal Stories from the Pioneers
* **Grandmaster Caz:** Wrote the rhymes that became "Rapper's Delight" (with his name in the lyrics), which was stolen from him without proper credit or reward.
  * **Life Transformation:** Receiving the 5-year grant allowed him to buy a house in Pennsylvania, move out of the Bronx projects, escape the day-to-day grind, and eliminate his fear of dying in the projects.
  * **Ongoing Hustle:** Emphasized that hip-hop lacks social security or health benefits, forcing pioneers to keep working park jams and tours to survive.
* **Scarface & Medical Crises:** Scarface needed a kidney transplant and open-heart surgery, but was initially in complete shock when Ben Horowitz called him from the South of France to cover his medical needs.
  * **Felicia's Intervention:** Felicia Horowitz personally flew to Houston and insisted Scarface go to the doctor, saving his life just as his aorta burst on the way in.
* **Roxanne Shanté:** Her child asked if she felt bitter seeing new female rappers win awards; she replied that she didn't want those awards—she wanted the *Paid in Full* award.
* **Cross-Generational Bonding:** At foundation events, multi-platinum icons like Dr. Dre requested introductions to legends like Slick Rick and Kurtis Blow, whom they had never met despite decades in the industry.

### Scaling, Funding, and the Annual Gala
* **Matching Model:** Ben and Felicia Horowitz match every donation 2.5 to 1.
* **Volunteer Operation:** There are zero paid employees at the foundation; 100% of donated funds go directly to the artists and grants.
* **The Gala Experience:** 
  * Described as feeling like a joyful family reunion with zero egos, deep mutual respect, and unscripted park-jam performances.
  * Past highlights include George Clinton (age 83) reciting Rakim's "Follow the Leader" lyrics on stage, and Ice Cube declaring he never dropped an album without first listening to Scarface.
* **The Quincy Jones Award:** Created in direct response to the Grammys mishandling Quincy Jones's tribute; this year's recipient is Nile Rogers (producer of Chic, Sister Sledge, and Daft Punk's "Get Lucky").
