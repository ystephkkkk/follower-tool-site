- Speakers: Yann LeCun (Executive Chairman, AMI Labs); Tom Mackenzie (Journalist, Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR:** Yann LeCun argues that current Large Language Models (LLMs) are fundamentally incapable of achieving true intelligence or understanding the physical world; his new venture, AMI Labs, is developing "World Models" (JEPA) to bridge this gap, enabling robots and autonomous systems to learn from observation and predict the consequences of their actions.

### The "Wall" of Generative AI and the Shift to World Models
* **LLM Limitations:** Yann LeCun notes that while LLMs excel at discrete symbols (text, code, math), they are "completely useless" for real-world signals and physical intuition.
    * **Predictive Failure:** LLMs use "auto-regressive prediction" (guessing the next token). This works for text but fails in video/physical reality because most world details (like the exact look of a person in a crowd) are unpredictable at the pixel level.
* **The JEPA Framework:** AMI Labs utilizes Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which avoids pixel-by-pixel generation.
    * **Abstract Representation:** Instead of "filling in the blanks" of a video, JEPA learns an abstract representation of a signal and ignores unpredictable details, focusing only on the underlying physics and logic.
    * **The "Cat" Benchmark:** LeCun points out that a cat has 800 million neurons (100x fewer than a human) but possesses far more common sense and physical intelligence than any current AI.

### AMI Labs: Strategy and Industrial Applications
* **Why AMI Labs Left Meta:** Despite support from Mark Zuckerberg, Meta’s internal focus shifted toward catching up in the LLM race. LeCun founded AMI Labs to pursue "Advanced Machine Intelligence" (AMI) independently.
    * **B2B vs. B2C:** Meta is a consumer-facing communication company, while World Models are best suited for industrial B2B applications.
* **Target Industries:**
    * **Complex Control:** Predicting and managing behavior in power plants, jet engines, pharmaceutical manufacturing, and human cell modeling.
    * **Defense and Robotics:** Moving beyond simple perception (image recognition) to "action planning"—allowing drones or robots to plan complex trajectories and manipulate physical objects reliably.
* **Global Footprint:** By design, the company has no presence in Silicon Valley or China, maintaining offices in Paris, New York, Montreal, and Singapore to ensure a global, independent perspective.

### AI Sovereignty and Project Tapestry
* **The Risk of Cultural Erosion:** LeCun warns that if AI assistants (the future "mediation" for all human information) are controlled by a few companies in the US or China, local cultures and languages will disappear.
* **Project Tapestry:** A proposed decentralized infrastructure for training foundation models.
    * **Sovereign Training:** Countries, universities, and entities can contribute local data and computing power to train a "global consensus model" without ever sharing their private data with a central server.
    * **Data Advantage:** This would allow models to be trained on vast amounts of non-public cultural and linguistic data (e.g., India's 22 official languages), potentially outperforming proprietary Western models.

### The Future of Physical AI
* **Data Bottleneck Myth:** LeCun dismisses the idea that we are running out of training data.
    * **Visual Wealth:** There are 20 trillion words of text on the public internet, but a four-year-old child sees as many "bytes" of visual data in their first few years as the largest LLM ever trained.
* **Achieving Level 5 Autonomy:** Current AI struggles with "common sense" tasks like inertia and gravity, which infants learn in nine months. Success in World Models is the prerequisite for Level 5 self-driving cars and domestic robots.
* **The European Opportunity:** While Europe "missed the boat" on the current LLM frontier, LeCun believes the race for Physical AI/World Models is wide open, providing an opportunity for Europe to lead through alternative research paths.

*   **Margin Strategy:** While selling physical chips may lower gross margin *percentages* compared to pure IP licensing, Haas expects a massive increase in gross margin *dollars*.
    *   **Example:** Haas noted that a 50% margin on a $100 billion market ($50B) is far more valuable than a 95% margin on a $3 billion market ($2.8B).

### The AI Growth Engine: "Agentic AI" and Compute Demand
*   **CPU Resurgence:** While GPUs handle token generation, Haas emphasizes that CPUs are "first-class customers" in AI because they handle orchestration, scheduling, and distribution.

*   **Market Projections:** Arm forecasts the general-purpose CPU TAM will grow from roughly $60–$70 billion today to $100 billion by 2030. 
*   **Total Opportunity:** Haas projects Arm’s total available market across all sectors (including automotive and edge devices) to reach $1 trillion by the end of the decade.

### Supply Chain Constraints and Macro Outlook
*   **Memory Shortages:** High-bandwidth memory (HBM) and NAND are facing massive supply shortages. Haas expects the memory market to remain "quite supply-constrained" for the next 12 to 18 months.
*   **Smartphone Resilience:** Despite memory tightness, Haas is "bearish" on volume risks. Even a 15–20% drop in smartphone volumes would only result in a 1–2% impact on Arm’s royalties due to their shift toward higher-value Compute Subsystems (CSS).
*   **Geopolitical Stability:** Arm has not yet seen material financial impacts or supply chain disruptions resulting from conflicts in the Middle East.

### Future Frontiers: Inference and Space
*   **Shift to Inference:** Haas views the industry shift from "training" models to "inference" (running models) as an "intellectually obvious" evolution that will lift demand across the entire ecosystem.
*   **Data Centers in Space:** Haas takes the concept of space-based data centers seriously, citing "profound" power benefits despite the obvious challenges of latency and hardware maintenance. 
*   **Security Infrastructure:** As data centers house increasingly valuable IP, Haas confirms that physical and digital security for these facilities is becoming a top-tier industry priority.

**TL;DR**
Quantum computing has transitioned from theoretical research to a critical "scaling" phase, with industry leaders projecting commercial viability and "practical quantum advantage" by the late 2020s or early 2030s. While the U.S. and China currently lead in massive corporate investment, European firms are carving out a position of strength by dominating the essential supply chain components of error correction, networking, and algorithms.

### The Path to Commercialization and "Quantum Advantage"
* **Timeline for Impact:** Industry experts anticipate that the "switch" to real-world commercial impact will occur toward the end of this decade (2029–2030).
    * **The 2025 Turning Point:** Panelists noted that 2025 was a pivotal year where investment increased by 50% and hardware reached the performance levels necessary for error correction.
* **Early Adopter Industries:** The first sectors to see material benefits will be those reliant on complex molecular simulation.
    * **Drug Discovery:** Solving chemistry and material science problems that traditional supercomputers cannot scale to handle.
    * **Advanced Materials:** Development of more efficient batteries, fertilizers, and high-temperature superconductors for power transmission.
* **The "Willow" Benchmark:** Google’s Willow chip (unveiled Dec. 2024) serves as a baseline for the industry, demonstrating the ability to solve problems in minutes that would take classical supercomputers septillions of years.

### Technical Hurdles: Scaling, Errors, and Networking
* **Quantum Error Correction:** Steve Brierley (Riverlane) identified this as the single most critical hurdle.
    * Current machines can manage roughly 1,000 operations before errors occur; commercial applications require **trillions of error-free operations.**
    * Error correction technology acts as a real-time "loop" that infers and corrects data flips as the computer runs.
* **Modular Scaling:** Carmen Palacios-Berraquero (Nu Quantum) emphasized that the future of the industry is not in building single, massive processors ("monolithic scaling"), but in networking smaller, robust processors together.
    * This mirrors how modern AI and cloud data centers scale by networking GPUs and CPUs.
* **Hardware Integration:** Quantum machines are increasingly viewed not as standalone units but as accelerators that will work hand-in-hand with existing GPU and CPU hardware (e.g., IQM’s partnership with NVIDIA).

### Geopolitics and the European "Sovereignty" Strategy
* **The European Gap:** Jan Goetz (IQM) noted that while Europe has more quantum startups than the U.S., it lacks the massive corporate investment seen from American giants like Google, IBM, and Amazon.
* **Avoiding Dependency:** Leaders argue that Europe must control its own quantum supply chain to avoid the strategic dependencies currently seen in the AI sector.
    * **The "European Way":** Rather than copying the U.S. cloud-first business model, Europe is focusing on infrastructure, building hardware specifically for on-premise data centers and supercomputing hubs.
* **Global Competitiveness:** Despite the funding gap, European firms are emerging as global leaders in specific "puzzle pieces," such as algorithms (Phasecraft), networking (Nu Quantum), and error correction (Riverlane).

### Investment, Talent, and Security Risks
* **The Talent Pipeline:** IQM is aggressively targeting the educational market, aiming to place a quantum computer in every university globally to ensure future workers are trained on their specific architecture.
* **Capital Totals:** Significant recent fundraises include:
    * **Riverlane:** $75 million Series C (2024).
    * **Nu Quantum:** $60 million round.
    * **Phasecraft:** Over $50 million raised to date.
* **The "Encryption Shock":** The arrival of large-scale quantum computers in the early 2030s poses a systemic threat to current public encryption (RSA).
    * Experts warn that industries requiring long-term data security (10+ years) are already late in transitioning to "Quantum Safe" encryption standards.
