### TL;DR
Erik Allebest and Sarah Guo discuss how Chess.com scaled to 250 million registered members, 10 million daily active users, and over $200 million in annual revenue by prioritizing pure user experience, community, and the cultural revival of chess.

### Company Backstory & Scale
* **Domain Acquisition:** In 2005, Erik Allebest and his co-founder/CTO bought the chess.com domain name for **$56,000** out of a bankruptcy auction with the vision of building a MySpace-like community for chess.
* **Bootstrapped Growth:** Facing rejection from venture capitalists who called the idea an "uninvestable niche passion project," the founders grew the business slowly funded by cash flow, revenue from online learning memberships launched in **2007** (within 18 months of launch), and personal savings.
* **Current Metrics:** Today, Chess.com employs **650 people**, boasts **250 million registered members**, **10 to 50 million monthly active users**, **10 million daily active users**, and projects slightly over **$200 million** in revenue this year.
* **Recent Milestones:** After explosive viral waves during the COVID-19 pandemic, *The Queen's Gambit*, short-form content, the Mittens bot, and online cheating scandals in **2023**, the platform successfully stabilized at a much higher baseline by **2024**.

### Private Equity & Investment Evolution
* **General Atlantic:** Entered around **2020 or 2021** as the first private equity firm to believe in the mission, buying secondary shares from existing investors without injecting new capital.
* **CVC Capital Partners:** Recently partnered with Chess.com to buy out earlier shares, bringing deep expertise in gaming, sports, and community businesses; General Atlantic also reinvested.
* **Operational Impact:** Allebest notes that working with General Atlantic and CVC has positively transformed the company through rigorous forecasting, operational excellence, and active, in-the-weeds product collaboration rather than top-down pressure.

### AI, Human Expertise, & Anti-Cheating
* **Evolution of Chess Engines:** Early chess engines like Stockfish made games overly mechanical and boring, but subsequent neural network engines like Leela Chess Zero introduced aggressive, unconventional play that reinvigorated human creativity and gameplay.
* **Accelerated Learning:** Modern AI tools, personalized puzzles, game reviews, and LLM-powered coaching help young players (including children and 7-to-10 prodigies) reach elite levels much faster.
* **Human-Centric Retention:** Despite computers outclassing humans for **30 years**, chess popularity has surged because humans fundamentally want to compete against other humans and value shared cultural touchstones.
* **Anti-Cheating Operations:** Chess.com dedicates substantial resources, statistical models, and machine learning models to detect cheating, protect game integrity for both casual and professional players with prize money, and ban bad actors.

### Product Expansion & New Ventures
* **Pure Software Focus:** Unlike capex-heavy businesses or hardware companies, Chess.com succeeded as a software and content platform driven by relentless focus on user experience and community.
* **Gambit.com:** Chess.com recently launched *gambit.com*, a poker platform designed to apply the chess playbook—introducing skill-based ratings rather than just chip volume or financial risk.
* **Future Games:** The company plans to roll out additional classic games built around wholesome, educational, and pure gaming principles.
* **Internal AI Stack:** The company utilizes LLMs for customer support automations, internal data analysis, and an internal authentication/knowledge layer called **GNS** built by CTO Josh to accelerate product development cycles.
