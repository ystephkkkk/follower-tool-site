### TL;DR
Driven by an AI boom requiring 16 times more transistors every two years, ASML CEO Christophe Fouquet and industry analyst Dr. Ian Cutress discussed deep tech collaboration, the evolution of Moore’s Law, and Europe's scaling and funding challenges at the Hello Tomorrow conference in Amsterdam.

### ASML's Unconventional Origin Story & Ecosystem Strategy
- **Origins as a Spin-off:** Spun out of Philips 40 years ago at a time when major semiconductor players like IBM pursued full vertical integration.
- **System Integration Model:** Unlike Japanese competitors Nikon and Canon, ASML chose system integration rather than vertical integration, allowing them to partner with the world's best suppliers for every part of the tool.
- **Key Partnerships:** Collaborates with Zeiss on optics (a 40-year partnership), VDL, Trumpf (for EUV), and Mistral (for AI).
- **Customer Co-Investment:** During the development of EUV when R&D funding was tight, ASML had customers co-invest in the technology to help share the financial burden and accelerate scaling.

### Redefining Moore's Law and the AI Infrastructure Boom
- **The Classical vs. AI Definition:** While the traditional definition of Moore's Law is the number of transistors per chip doubling every 2 years, AI has put Moore's Law "on steroids."
- **AI Transistor Demand:** Advanced AI products require a **16 times** increase every 2 years across both logic and memory.
- **Density Drivers:** Achieving required density demands scaling complemented by **3D integration** (advanced packaging and stacking wafers/dies).
- **Market Visibility:** AI now accounts for about **half** of total semiconductor production. The industry is currently catching up on a significant backlog with a visibility window of **2 to 3 years**.

### The Hard Reality of Deep Tech Innovation
- **Solving Real Problems:** Fouquet emphasized that creativity must serve a concrete purpose—identifying the exact problem to solve and finding customers who deal with that problem daily.
- **The EUV Development Cycle:** Theorized in the **1980s**, EUV took roughly **20 years** to develop, with the first 15 to 18 years consisting largely of a "succession of very painful mistakes" and resilience in solving source power issues.
- **Scaling Over Ideas:** Having a great idea is not enough; survival requires operational execution, robust supply chains, and the ability to scale production from one prototype to hundreds of units shipped globally.

### AI Infrastructure and the Mistral Partnership
- **Data as the Core Asset:** Fouquet stated that the most important part of AI is data, noting that companies must generate unique data to feed powerful customized models rather than relying solely on internet-scale LLMs.
- **Mistral Partnership:** ASML partnered with European AI startup Mistral because ASML lacks a DNA in model development despite generating terabytes of data. Mistral is helping build customized models for ASML's products, operations, and R&D.
- **Open Source Alignment:** Mistral supports open source because they believe the real value of AI lies in downstream applications and customization.

### Europe's Trillion-Dollar Company Challenge
- **European Strengths:** Europe maintains strong education systems and well-funded research facilities, consistently producing great ideas and deep tech startups.
- **Scaling Bottlenecks:** European startups struggle with access to capital when scaling up (hiring, securing space, purchasing equipment).
- **Regulatory Hurdles:** While US and Chinese governments act as enabling bodies with supportive regulations, European governments and the European Commission often create regulatory drag. Fouquet noted that the EU Chips Act 2.0 has improved market focus, but the Commission still needs to trust the industry to lead itself.

### Notable Quotes
> "When you look at the most advanced chips, the most advanced product, AI product, you're not looking at a two times every 2 years, you're looking at a 16 time every 2 years. So, the industry, the AI industry needs a lot more transistor both for logic and memory than Moore's Law used to provide."

Global demand for AI infrastructure remains enormous and supply-limited, driving robust long-term visibility for ASML. ---

### European AI Strategy and Geopolitical Standing

* **The Sovereignty Sequence:** ASML argues that Europe must focus on innovation and market demand before investing heavily in semiconductor manufacturing. 
    * **The Order of Operations:** Europe should target industrial AI applications and chip design first, followed by chip manufacturing once domestic demand is established.
    * **The Manufacturing Trap:** Building a two-nanometer fab in Europe today would be premature, as the lack of domestic buyers would result in the manufactured wafers simply being exported to the US.
* **Global Market Assessment:** The US is leading the AI race, followed by China, while Europe remains "quite behind."
    * **US Dominance:** The US is the clear winner, aggressively securing manufacturing capacity and purchasing 80% of advanced chips manufactured worldwide.
    * **China’s Position:** China is investing heavily across the entire ecosystem, specifically driving the adoption and use of AI applications more aggressively than other nations.
    * **European Market Potential:** Despite trailing, Europe holds a significant advantage with its highly attractive market, representing 22% of global GDP. 

### AI Infrastructure Boom and Supply Constraints

* **Supply-Limited Market:** The semiconductor industry faces a prolonged period where demand will outpace supply due to massive infrastructure build-outs.
    * **Lagging Industry Catch-up:** While AI software companies flagged the need for advanced architecture years ago, the semiconductor hardware industry only fully accelerated capacity building at the end of last year. 
    * **Customer Visibility:** ASML continues to see strong, long-term order visibility from customers rather than temporary pull-forwards.
* **Energy Solutions and Space-Based Data Centers:** Emerging concepts like space-based data centers represent attempts to bypass terrestrial infrastructure bottlenecks.
    * **Energy Efficiency:** Placing data centers in space is primarily a solution to solve the massive power and energy constraints facing earth-bound data centers, rather than a driver of net-new data capacity.
* **Gigawatt-Scale Projects:** Elon Musk’s proposed "Terafab" (a potential one-terawatt power project) represents a major future opportunity.
    * **Scale Comparisons:** ASML compares the scale of the Terafab to Korea's massive DRAM projects, which produce millions of wafers.
    * **Supply Management:** ASML is carefully tracking these major projects to ensure they do not face supply constraints when delivering lithography equipment.

### Regulatory Compliance and Emerging Markets

* **Strict Export Compliance:** ASML dismisses recurring rumors that it is bypassing US export controls to ship Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) lithography machines to China.
    * **Adherence to Law:** The company maintains an active dialogue with global governments and strictly adheres to changing regulatory frameworks for both DUV and Extreme Ultraviolet (EUV) technologies.
* **The India Growth Story:** ASML has partnered with Tata to support India’s nascent semiconductor ambitions.
    * **First Fab Timeline:** India’s first semiconductor fabrication plant is scheduled to open next year.
    * **Future Consumption:** By 2030, India is projected to consume 10% of all globally manufactured chips, making localized production a strategic necessity.
    * **Long-Term Outlook:** ASML views its partnership with Tata as a five-to-ten-year growth opportunity, focusing first on helping local teams master the steep learning curve of chip manufacturing.
