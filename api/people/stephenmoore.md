**TL;DR**
Former Trump economic adviser Steve Moore expresses strong optimism for 3–5% U.S. GDP growth driven by deregulation and tax cuts, though he maintains a "Reagan-style" preference for free trade and a strong dollar that occasionally diverges from President Trump’s protectionist leanings.

### Economic Indicators and 2026 Outlook
* **Current Economic Strength:** Moore characterizes the present economy as exceptionally robust, citing declining inflation, a booming stock market, and real wage increases.
* **Growth Projections:** While skeptical of Donald Trump’s suggestion of 15% growth, Moore predicts the U.S. could "easily" see 3% to 5% growth in 2026.
* **Core Policy Pillars:** Credits the current trajectory to three primary factors:
    * **Lower Tax Rates:** Incentivizing domestic investment.
    * **Energy Production:** Promoting home-country resources to lower costs.
    * **Deregulation:** Viewed as a "huge burst" to business productivity.
* **The "Shared Prosperity" Gap:** Moore acknowledges a disconnect between positive macro statistics and middle-class anxiety, noting that manufacturing-heavy regions in the Midwest often do not feel the effects of the "strong economy."

### Trade Strategy and the USMCA
* **Support for USMCA:** Moore strongly defends the U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA), calling it "enormously advantageous." He notes that Canada and Mexico—not China—are the United States' largest trading partners.
* **Free Trade vs. Tariffs:** Self-identifying as a "conventional Ronald Reagan free trader," Moore admits he does not always agree with Trump’s tariff policies but acknowledges Trump’s "dealmaker" approach.
    * **The "Hub and Spoke" Model:** Views the U.S. as the economic hub of the world, giving it the leverage to "flex muscle" and force better terms from other nations.
* **China Through the Backdoor:** Moore supports the administration's concerns regarding China using Mexico and Canada as "ports of entry" to bypass U.S. trade barriers.
* **Automation Trends:** Moore warns that regardless of trade policy, many traditional manufacturing and trucking jobs may vanish within 20 years due to AI and robotics.

### Energy Policy and the "Two Ns"
* **The Future of Power:** Moore identifies **Natural Gas** and **Nuclear Power** (the "Two Ns") as the essential fuels of the future because they are clean-burning and competitive.
* **Coal Subsidies:** Regarding the $175 million in government funds to bolster the coal industry, Moore expresses skepticism toward subsidies generally, arguing that natural gas is often cheaper due to market forces rather than just regulation.
* **Green Energy Critique:** Claims the "green movement" has relied on tens of billions in taxpayer subsidies and suggests that all energy forms should compete on a level playing field without government intervention.

### Monetary Policy and the Federal Reserve
* **Kevin Warsh Nomination:** Moore highly approves of the nomination of Kevin Warsh for the Federal Reserve, describing him as a "real professional" who will maintain Fed independence.
* **Strong Dollar Policy:** Moore advocates for a strong, stable dollar to ensure affordability for consumers. 
    * **Policy Disagreement:** He explicitly notes a disagreement with Trump on this point, as the President occasionally favors a weaker dollar to boost exports.
* **Reserve Currency Advantage:** Emphasizes that the U.S. benefits immensely from the dollar serving as the world’s reserve currency.
