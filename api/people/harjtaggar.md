### The Shift from Bits to Atoms: Hard Tech Resurgence
* **Hard Tech Growth:** Hard tech companies in YC batches have jumped from 8% to 20%, driven by technical founders with deep domain expertise (such as PhDs making up 1 in 6 founders in the current summer batch).
* **Defense & Aerospace:** Defense startups have grown from 1.5% to 5% of the batch.

* **AI Compute & Power Infrastructure:** 
  * Semiconductor stack and photonics companies grew from 1% to nearly 4% of the batch.
  * Power infrastructure companies grew from 1% to nearly 3%.
  * Alternative hardware innovators include **Lamb Labs** (building new compute processors), **Bot** (using ternary representation to bypass heavy floating-point precision needs), and **Dipole Labs** (building fully optical, photonics-based data center switches to eliminate electronic bottlenecks).

### AI, Data Labs, and the Evolution of Software
* **Explosive Revenue Acceleration:** Median YC companies now reach $20,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) by the end of the batch (up from $8K previously), with some startups breaking from zero to seven figures in revenue within just 3 months.

* **End-to-End Agentic Workflows:** Companies doing full-stack, end-to-end jobs (like automated insurance brokering, clinical intake, and medical billing) grew from 10% to over 25% of the batch.
  * **Juicebox:** An AI recruiting tool that doubled/tripled per-account revenue by expanding from search to executing automated outreach and interview scheduling.
* **Software Systems of Record vs. AI Harnesses:** Enterprise software leaders like Salesforce are expanding via AI harnesses (like Slack AI harnesses) to maintain their data moats as agents increasingly become the primary users of software.

### The Resurgence of Solo and Experienced Founders
* **Solo Founders Spike:** Solo founders have surged from roughly 5% to 18-19% of accepted YC companies, representing nearly one in five startups.
* **Experienced Founders:** Founders in their late 30s, 40s, and 50s are experiencing a major resurgence. Experienced leaders (like Peter Steinberger) excel by knowing "what to build" and successfully managing fleets of coding agents similarly to engineering teams.

### TL;DR
Patrick Collison, co-founder and CEO of Stripe, discusses the changing landscape of software development and entrepreneurship in the age of AI, reflecting on Stripe's origin story, the cost of dropping out of college, and why current Stripe data shows that startup formation and growth rates are at historic highs.

### Learning, Cognitive Cache, and AI
* **Cognitive L1 Cache vs. AI Agents:** Patrick Collison uses a computing analogy to describe human knowledge, comparing factual lookups to L1 cache versus RAM or network requests.
  * *Speed:* Even with advanced AI agents, neuronal lookups in the human brain are significantly faster than generating or reading prompts.
  * *Evidence:* High-end companies and labs continue to place an enormous premium on cognitive ability.
* **Personal Tooling Preferences:** Collison still writes his own code and essays rather than relying on automated writing models.
  * *Communication:* He views writing as a fundamental interpersonal medium and feels LLMs are still deficient at reasoning sensibly within multi-dimensional realities.
  * *Adoption:* He reports having sent zero pre-written suggestions from tools like Gmail or WhatsApp in his life.

### Dropping Out of College and Startup Urgency
* **Dual College Dropout:** Collison dropped out of MIT twice—first after his freshman semester to start a company with Harj Taggar, and later after another year to start Stripe.
* **Cost of Leaving School:** He notes that parents often fear dropping out will permanently damage a student's reputation, but in his experience, the cost is *de minimis* and nobody cares.
* **Reassessing Urgency:** 
  * *Past Mindset:* He felt a false sense of urgency in his youth, driven by a speed-running mentality and a fear that Silicon Valley opportunities were fleeting.
  * *Reality:* Silicon Valley consistently maintains a surplus of opportunities across decades.

### The Stripe Origin Story and Development Playbook
* **Inception at Startup School:** Stripe was conceived in 2009 after Patrick and John Collison attended Startup School in Berkeley and discussed the idea while walking back from sushi in Potrero Hill, reasoning "we might as well because it probably won't be that hard."
* **Early Obstacles:** 
  * *Credibility:* Fintech did not exist as a sector, and two young founders trying to build a financial services business looked like "squirrels in a trench coat trying to masquerade as a real business." Banks viewed them with extreme skepticism.
  * *Grounding:* The business succeeded because it was grounded in a concrete, universally felt user pain point.
* **The Long Pre-Launch Phase:** 
  * *Timeline:* Stripe spent two years building before its public launch in September 2011 (starting full-time in summer 2010).
  * *Production Feedback:* Despite a long internal build, they secured their first production user (Ross Buché at 28 North) in January 2010, just two months into coding, and added customers monthly in private beta to maintain real-world feedback loops.

### Macro Data and Enterprise Adoption of Startups
* **Surge in New Businesses:** 
  * *Stripe Data:* The number of new businesses starting on Stripe is currently growing at nearly **2x year-over-year**, the largest relative jump ever recorded outside of the early COVID-19 pandemic inflection (which saw ~50% growth).
  * *Performance:* The median business is performing better than a year ago, with improved probabilities of reaching $1M, $5M, and $10M revenue thresholds and declining time-to-revenue for Atlas-incorporated companies.
* **Enterprise Willingness to Buy:** 
  * *Risk Reversal:* Traditional corporate buyers (CIOs/CTOs) have a "terror of being left behind with archaic and antiquated ways of operating," making them far more willing to buy from early-stage startups than in past eras.
* **Decentralization vs. Hegemony:** 
  * *AI Concentration:* Despite fears of AI centralizing the economy around a small number of dominant labs, Stripe's transaction data shows intense retooling and startup formation across the board, pointing toward a decentralized world with thousands of winners.

## Solving the Blank Canvas Problem: Gusto's AI Co-Founder

---

**TL;DR:** Gusto Co-founder is an agentic AI product that automates end-to-end business workflows—such as payroll, compliance, and customer communications—via SMS and Slack, leveraging Gusto’s proprietary data to solve the "blank canvas" problem of traditional AI.

### Solving the "Blank Canvas" Problem
* **Beyond the "Glorified Search Engine":** Eddie Kim (Gusto Co-founder) observes that 99.9% of users still treat AI as a search or summarization tool rather than an autonomous agent that performs tasks.
* **Overcoming User Friction:** Most users struggle with "the blank canvas"—knowing what to ask an AI to do. Gusto Co-founder solves this by:
    * **Suggesting Automations:** Proactively offering to handle recurring tasks like approving timesheets or running payroll based on existing Gusto data.
    * **Leveraging Context:** Using Gusto’s "system of record" status to provide the AI with the necessary data (hours, employee roles, industry) to act without manual input.
* **System of Record Advantage:** Because Gusto holds the core business data, the AI can find hidden value, such as identifying a $50,000 R&D tax credit for a customer that they were unaware they qualified for.

### Product Interface and "The Work Before the Work"
* **SMS and Slack as the Primary UI:** The product avoids the web browser entirely. Business owners can approve payroll or trigger workflows via a simple text message.
    * **Low Friction:** Kim noted that users find the ability to manage a business via Telegram or SMS "mind-blowing" compared to logging into a complex dashboard.
* **Automating Data Manipulation:** Most small business labor is "the work before the work"—exporting data from one system (e.g., MindBody for spas), manipulating it in Excel, and then uploading it to payroll. 
    * Gusto Co-founder uses "connectors" (QuickBooks, Google Workspace, etc.) to automate these middle steps end-to-end.
* **Proactive Agency:** The tool is designed to be a "co-founder" that looks ahead, such as checking weather reports for a tour business and proactively texting customers to bring umbrellas if rain is forecasted.

### A New Paradigm for Product Development
* **Extreme Velocity:** The product was built from prototype to a "Tier 1" company launch by a team of only five people in just ten weeks.
* **The "Anti-Process" Approach:** To maintain speed, the team intentionally eliminated traditional corporate overhead:
    * **No Documentation:** No PRDs (Product Requirement Documents), no Jira boards, and no formal text specs.
    * **No Static Design:** The team bypassed Figma, moving straight from whiteboarding to code.
    * **24/7 Collaboration:** Instead of scheduled meetings, the team utilized a "perma-Zoom" room that stayed open 24/7 for instant, high-bandwidth communication.
* **Role Merging:** AI tools allowed the team to blur traditional boundaries:
    * **Designer-Coders:** The lead designer contributed as much production-grade code as the engineers.
    * **Engineer-Designers:** Engineers prototyped their own UI/UX, which the designer then refined directly in the codebase using AI coding assistants.

### Future Roadmaps and the "Abundance" of Startups
* **Channel Expansion:** Gusto plans to expand beyond SMS and Slack into WhatsApp and Telegram to meet international and vertical-specific communication needs.
* **Lowering the Barrier to Entry:** Future versions will allow users to use Co-founder before they even have an EIN, helping them register businesses and automate the "side hustle" phase.
* **Secular Trend:** Kim believes AI represents a "step function" in making business ownership easier, shifting the owner’s focus from "arcane HR rules" to strategy and growth.

---

**TL;DR**
AI agents have transitioned software development from a manual "writing" task to a high-leverage "director" role, enabling founders to achieve a 400x increase in output by "tokenmaxxing" and utilizing agentic workflows.

### The Return of the Builder: Garry Tan’s 400x Productivity Leap
* **The Comeback:** After a 13-year hiatus from active coding, YC CEO Garry Tan returned to building software, shipping hundreds of thousands of lines of code and launching viral open-source projects like *gstack*.
* **Cost and Velocity Collapse:** 
    * In 2008, building a blogging platform (Posterous) took $4M and 1.5 years.
    * In 2024, rebuilding a more advanced version with agentic research capabilities took $200 in tokens and 5 days.
* **The 400x Output Metric:** Tan calculated that by directing 15 agents simultaneously, his logical lines of code (LLOC) production is 400 times higher than his peak output in 2013.
* **The Ferrari Analogy:** Tan describes using modern AI agents as "driving a Ferrari"—exhilarating and incredibly fast, but requiring the user to be a "mechanic" who can pop the hood and fix the system when it breaks down.

### "Tokenmaxxing" and Agentic Engineering
* **Defining Tokenmaxxing:** The philosophy of intentionally spending heavily on high-quality model calls (e.g., $500/day) to "boil the ocean" of research, context, and testing. 
* **Journalistic Software:** Tan's project *Garry’s List* acts as an investigative journalist rather than just a CMS. It uses APIs (Perplexity, X, Grok) to ingest dozens of sources, cross-reference arguments, and produce sourced reports.
* **Latent Space vs. Deterministic Code:** Success in AI engineering depends on knowing which tasks belong in "LLM land" (handling generic cases, user intent) and which belong in "code land" (deterministic logic).
* **The "Rent" Mental Model:** Spending on tokens should be viewed like San Francisco rent for a startup—it is an expensive but necessary investment to create the serendipity and utility required for high-speed growth.

### The gstack Workflow: Thin Harness, Fat Skills
* **Thin Harness:** The core loop that manages LLM inputs/outputs and tool calls (e.g., Claude Code). 
* **Fat Skills:** The high-quality markdown, prompts, and logical checklists supplied by the human "director" to guide the agent.
* **The Multi-Stage Pipeline:** Tan uses a specific sequence of "skills" to ensure code quality:
    * **ASCII Visualization:** Forcing the AI to create data flow diagrams before coding to ensure it understands the architecture.
    * **The CEO Skill:** A meta-prompt inspired by Brian Chesky’s "10-star experience" that asks the AI to find a path to 10x value for only 2x effort.
    * **The Eng Review:** Automating the creation of unit and integration tests, reaching 80-90% coverage—a task human developers often neglect.
    * **Codex/QA:** Using high-IQ models to find bugs and Playwright-wrapped agents to perform automated browser-based testing of UIs.

### The Future of Personal AI and Human Agency
* **Personal vs. Corporate AI:** Tan argues the defining question of the era is whether individuals will control their own tools and data or be controlled by corporate-hosted algorithms (similar to a Facebook feed).
* **Open Source Golden Age:** Using tools like *Conductor* and *G Brain*, developers can now "extract" logic from existing repos and use AI to build complex RAG systems in a single weekend.
* **The AI Time Dividend:** AI allows busy executives to "buy back time" by utilizing machine consciousness to handle the "boring" parts of engineering (QA, boilerplate, research), leaving humans to provide taste and product intuition.

## The AI Agent Economy Is Here

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q8wVMdwhlh4
- Published: 2026-02-21
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260221.html#video-Q8wVMdwhlh4
- Speakers: Garry Tan (President & CEO, Y Combinator); Harj Taggar (Group Partner, Y Combinator); Jared Friedman (Group Partner, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
The "AI Agent Economy" has arrived, shifting software discovery and usage from human-centric to agent-centric, requiring founders to optimize documentation and APIs for autonomous AI decision-makers rather than human developers.

### The Shift from Autocomplete to Autonomous Agents
*   **The End of Micromanagement:** AI tools have evolved from "advanced autocomplete" (like Cursor) to autonomous agents (like Claude Code) that users trust to make independent architectural and tool-selection decisions.
*   **The "Cyber-Psychosis" Effect:** Garry Tan (President & CEO, Y Combinator) noted that the barrier to building has collapsed, allowing experienced founders to replicate years of previous startup work in just two weeks by running multiple simultaneous agent workers.
*   **Agent-Only Ecosystems:** Platforms like **Moltbook**—the first agent-only online community—demonstrate that AI agents are beginning to interact, collaborate, and transact without human involvement.

### "Make Something Agents Want": A New GTM Strategy
*   **The Oracle Effect:** Dev tool discovery is shifting from human recommendations on Stack Overflow to agent recommendations based on documentation quality.
*   **Documentation as the Front Door:**
    *   **Resend Case Study:** Founder Zeno Rocha observed that ChatGPT became a top-three customer conversion channel. By optimizing documentation for LLM-readability (structured snippets, clear headers), Resend became the "default" recommendation for AI agents.
    *   **The "llms.txt" Standard:** There is an emerging need for documentation optimized specifically for robot parsing rather than human reading.
    *   **Supabase Growth:** Agents frequently default to Supabase for database needs because its documentation is highly accessible and actionable for models.
*   **Agent-First Criteria:** Agents prefer **APIs over UIs**. They prioritize tools with low latency, clear code examples, and high-speed execution (e.g., agents favoring **Groq** over Whisper V1 for transcription due to a 200x speed advantage).

### Emerging Agent-Native Infrastructure
*   **Agent-Specific Services:** A new parallel tech stack is forming to serve non-human users:
    *   **AgentMail:** Provides email inboxes specifically for AI agents, bypassing the automation-blocking friction inherent in Gmail or Outlook.
    *   **Mintlify:** Powers agent-friendly documentation by auto-updating API docs directly from code, ensuring agents always have the most accurate "mental model" of a tool.
*   **The Liability Gap:** Despite their autonomy, agents currently lack legal standing. Humans remain the "liability sink" for signing documents and holding financial responsibility, though agents are beginning to manage "agent money" in parallel economies.

### Swarm Intelligence vs. God Intelligence
*   **Biological Scaling:** The hosts argue that AGI may not manifest as a single, massive "God Intelligence" (a trillion-parameter model), but as "Swarm Intelligence"—thousands of smaller, cheaper models collaborating like a social system.
*   **Dead Internet Theory:** As agents begin to generate the majority of code and text (e.g., agent-written Yelp reviews or documentation), the internet may shift toward a reality where agents primarily consume content created by other agents.
*   **Founder Advice:** Harj Taggar (Partner, YC) advises founders to develop an intuitive feel for agent limitations by using them daily. Success in the next cycle requires building tools that agents *want* to work with, rather than fighting the model's natural inclinations.
