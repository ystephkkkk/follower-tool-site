### TL;DR
Gary Cohn discusses the midterm election results, economic affordability pressures, potential bipartisan compromises, and the evolving outlook for AI data centers under a shifting political landscape.

### Economic Sentiment & The Affordability Crisis
* **Voter Dissatisfaction:** **Average citizen unhappiness** drives recent election cycles, with consumers voting for change regardless of party affiliation.
  * Sentiment indicators like the Michigan Consumer Sentiment Survey remain at rock bottom due to persistent financial pressure.
  * High-cost necessities—**food, housing, healthcare, and energy**—are squeezing household budgets.
* **The "L-Shaped" Economy:** **Asset owners vs. non-asset owners** divergence has worsened, transitioning from a K-shaped economy to an L-shaped dynamic where the bottom tier remains stagnant despite macroeconomic growth.
* **Macro Growth Disconnect:** Broad economic metrics do not reflect the lived reality of most Americans.
  * **GDP growth:** Reached **3.4%** previously.
  * **Job openings:** JOLTS data showed an increase of **330,000 new job openings**.
  * Despite corporate capex and economic expansion, growth is not working its way down broadly enough to satisfy the public.

### Political Reality & Bipartisan Opportunities
* **Divided Government Constraints:** The current legislative reality features a Democratic House, a Republican-led Senate requiring **60 votes to clear the filibuster** (with John Thune playing a major role), and a Republican president without a veto-proof majority.
* **Potential Compromise Areas:** President Trump may negotiate bipartisan legislation with Democrats in key areas:
  * **Entitlement & Social Security Reform:** Raising the cap on income taxed for Social Security would impose a **12.4% tax increase split evenly (6.2% each) between individuals and companies**, helping shore up the Social Security trust fund before it runs out of money in the early 2030s.
  * **Tariff Policy Codification:** Broad agreement exists on tariffs protecting U.S. workers, manufacturing, and jobs (especially against China), opening the door to pass tariffs through the legislative system rather than relying solely on executive orders.
  * **Border Friction:** Shutting down borders between Canada, Mexico, and the U.S. has proven problematic for policy, representing another area where pragmatic adjustments might be sought.

### AI Data Centers, Infrastructure & Tech Valuations
* **Regional Shifts & Energy Generation:** Texas politics are changing (including a newly elected Democratic governor and senator), impacting companies that recently redomiciled there from Delaware.
  * **Alternative data center hubs:** States like West Virginia offer enormous amounts of natural gas for behind-the-meter electrical generation, allowing data centers to be built without impacting the local grid.
  * **Local tax evaluations:** Jurisdictions must weigh whether data centers bring beneficial tax revenue for teachers and police against potential local roadblocks.
* **Tech Sector Impact & Cash Flow Dynamics:**
  * **Incumbent Advantage:** For mega-cap tech companies like **Amazon, Google, and Meta**, restrictions on new data center builds make existing infrastructure more scarce and valuable, allowing them to charge higher rental rates.
  * **Free Cash Flow Rebound:** Slower data center expansion forces these tech giants away from negative free cash flow (which dropped from **$40–$50 billion** annually into negative territory) and back toward cash-flow-positive operations.
  * **Supplier Uncertainty:** Pure-play hardware and component suppliers (such as CoreWeave or Nvidia) face greater questions regarding where and when the next new data centers will be built.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Social Security Insolvency Timeline:** The Social Security trust fund will run out of money in the **early 2030s**, forcing newly elected senators to address the issue within their current **six-year terms**. — Gary Cohn
* **Electoral Volatility:** If the financial position of the American consumer remains unchanged two years from now, voters will throw incumbents out of office once again. — Gary Cohn

## Former NEC Director Gary Cohn: Rising oil prices will have an impact on U.S. consumption power

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260430.html#video-5Gt8rjFHA24
- Speakers: Gary Cohn (Vice Chairman, IBM)

---

**TL;DR**
The U.S. economy is currently buoyed by a massive $1 trillion infrastructure and AI CAPEX boom that is offsetting the inflationary pressure of high oil prices. While high energy costs are depleting consumer disposable income, the physical build-out of data centers and domestic energy independence provide a unique structural advantage over global competitors.

### The Physical Reality of the AI CAPEX Boom
* **The "Caterpillar" Indicator:** Gary Cohn argues that the nearly $1 trillion in planned CAPEX for data centers translates directly into demand for heavy machinery.
    * **Infrastructure Foundation:** Building data centers requires moving dirt, laying foundations, and lifting steel—processes that rely on companies like Caterpillar.
    * **Policy Tailwinds:** The ability for companies to write off and depreciate equipment quickly (100% expensing) is a significant driver of this incremental machine buying.
* **Three Pillars of Earnings:** Cohn identifies the primary drivers of current economic resilience as:
    1. AI tech expansion
    2. GLP-1 (weight loss drug) development
    3. Physical data center construction

### Oil Prices and the American Consumer
* **Consumption Power:** Rising oil prices are acting as a direct tax on the American worker, particularly those who commute 50–100 miles daily. 
    * **Disposable Income:** Cohn emphasizes that consumers feel the immediate impact at the pump, which reduces spending power elsewhere in the economy.
* **U.S. Energy Independence:** Despite high prices, the U.S. remains in a superior position compared to Europe or Asia.
    * **Supply Security:** Unlike international peers who face potential fuel shortages or business shutdowns, the U.S. has no supply problem; the domestic debate is strictly about price, not availability.
* **Economic Tug-of-War:** Cohn notes the truth of the economy lies between two extremes: the "six weeks to a problem" bear view and the "growth will outweigh everything" bull view.

### AI Regulation and Anthropic’s Mythos
* **Government Scrutiny:** Cohn addresses reports that the White House is concerned about Anthropic’s new "Mythos" model due to its immense power.
    * **Cybersecurity & Compute:** The primary concerns in Washington involve the cybersecurity risks associated with such a powerful model and whether there is sufficient compute power for both government and private sector use.
* **Policy Intent:** Cohn dismisses "cynical" views that the administration is trying to slow Anthropic down to let competitors like OpenAI or Google catch up. He asserts the government is genuinely trying to understand the resource and avoid unintended consequences.

### Federal Reserve Internal Dynamics
* **Institutional Dissent:** Cohn points out the rare level of friction within the Fed, noting that four dissenters is a level of internal disagreement not seen since the 1990s.
* **Kevin Warsh's Potential Role:** Cohn praises Kevin Warsh as "unbelievably well-equipped" to lead, citing his critical experience at the Fed during the 2008 financial crisis.
* **Jay Powell’s Tenure:** Cohn describes Powell's current stance as "untraditional," suggesting he is breaking from his usual pragmatic/traditionalist mold by staying in his seat despite external pressures and ongoing investigations by the Fed's Inspector General.
    * **Short-term Outlook:** Cohn predicts Powell will not stay until 2028, suggesting an earlier exit would be in the best interest of Fed independence and productivity gains.
