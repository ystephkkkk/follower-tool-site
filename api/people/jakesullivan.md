*   **Responsible AI Integration:** As artificial intelligence, quantum computing, autonomous systems, and synthetic biology reshape national security, Western militaries must innovate rapidly but responsibly.
    *   **Network Defense:** Future defense relies on integrating advanced technology across allied militaries to multiply collective power rather than aggregating capabilities in isolation.
*   **Software-Defined Warfare:** Traditional procurement processes treat software like physical hardware, which is too slow for modern combat. 
    *   In modern conflicts like Ukraine, advantages go to those who can update code and deploy algorithms in hours, not years.
    *   Sullivan praises Canada’s new defense industrial strategy, its commitment to the 2% NATO defense spending pledge, and the creation of a dedicated acquisition agency to streamline these pipelines.

### Securing the "Electric Tech Stack" for National Power

    4.  Power electronics
*   **Industrial Mathematics:** If a nation cannot manufacture the electric tech stack securely and at scale, its defense architecture is structurally compromised. 
    *   **Allied Supply Chains:** Neither the U.S. nor Canada can secure this stack alone. Both nations must deliberately align their industrial strategies to build a resilient energy and technology backbone distributed across a global coalition of trusted nations.

## Rethinking Risk and Global Power with Jake Sullivan | The Outthinking Investor

**TL;DR:** The world has transitioned from the post-Cold War "unipolar moment" to an "old normal" of great power rivalry where economics and national security are inextricably linked, requiring the U.S. to move beyond punitive measures toward "offensive" economic statecraft like a national strategic investment fund.

### The Shift to the "Old Normal" and Spheres of Influence
*   **End of the "End of History":** For 30 years, global strategy assumed geopolitical and economic lanes were separate, but the world has reverted to a historical baseline defined by fragmentation, industrial policy, and great power competition.
*   **The Spheres of Influence Model:** Sullivan notes a shift toward a world where large powers dictate terms to smaller neighbors. 
    *   **The Trump Corollary:** Sullivan describes a "Trump corollary to the Monroe Doctrine," where the U.S. dominates the Western Hemisphere via coercion, a departure from traditional rules-based order.
    *   **Adversary Perceptions:** Chinese President Xi Jinping views recent U.S. military actions in Venezuela as confirmation of "imperial decline," believing a fading empire naturally lashes out at weaker neighbors.
*   **Geopolitical Signal:** While China feels empowered by the "spheres of influence" concept, Vladimir Putin remains unsettled by the unpredictability of current U.S. policy, despite his long-term desire for a carved-up world.

### The Technological Front Line: AI and Semiconductors
*   **The Nvidia H200 Controversy:** Sullivan criticizes the decision to license advanced GPUs to China in exchange for revenue cuts, arguing it gifts China a "generational leap" in AI.
    *   **The Compute Gap:** China possesses energy, talent, and resources but lacks "compute." Licensing H200s allows them to bridge this gap and train frontier models that compete directly with the U.S.
    *   **Market Realities:** Sullivan dismisses the argument that Nvidia needs the Chinese market to survive, noting Nvidia's market cap exploded even as its China revenue share dropped from 25% to 5%.
*   **China’s Strategy:** Beijing has issued binding directives to purge U.S. chips. Sullivan argues China uses Western chips only as a stopgap while pouring $150 billion into indigenizing its own tech stack.
*   **The "Czar" Influence:** Sullivan suggests U.S. hyperscalers are reluctant to speak out against Chinese chip sales for fear of crossing David Sacks, the administration's AI lead (the "Czar").

### Reimagining Economic Statecraft: The "Promote" Agenda
*   **"Protect" vs. "Promote":** The U.S. has become proficient at punitive tools (sanctions/controls) but lacks the "offensive" tools needed to build domestic strength and supply chain resilience.
*   **U.S. Strategic Investment Fund:** Sullivan advocates for a public, sovereign-wealth-style fund to crowd in private capital for "deep tech" (fusion, robotics, biotech) and "old economy" sectors like shipbuilding.
    *   **Governance:** The fund must be insulated from political cycles through a transparent public board, similar to the WWII-era Reconstruction Finance Corporation.
    *   **Additionality Principle:** To prevent "government stupidity," public funds should only be deployed when matched by private capital, ensuring market viability.
*   **China Shock 2.0:** Sullivan warns that without a proactive American model, China’s state-led control of "commanding heights" technologies will lead to massive market failures on the U.S. side.

### Risk Management and the "Human Element" of Statecraft
*   **Decision-making Frameworks:** Sullivan applies investment-style rigor to national security, utilizing "pre-mortems" (working backward from a hypothetical future failure) to identify current blind spots.
*   **The Risk Equilibrium:** Effective leadership requires shooting the gap between being "paralyzed by risk" and "dismissing risk" entirely.
*   **The "Situation Room" Reality:** Sullivan dispels the myth of a faceless, perfect "system" making global decisions.
    *   **Human-Centric Policy:** National security is "humans all the way down," driven by the specific collection of people in the room, their individual creative strengths, and their inherent weaknesses.

## Fmr. National Security Advisor Jake Sullivan on China, the U.S. & the AI Race | Amanpour and Company

**TL;DR**
The United States and China are engaged in a high-stakes race for AI supremacy that mirrors the Cold War’s nuclear and space races, requiring the U.S. to balance aggressive private-sector innovation with stringent export controls and sustained diplomatic safety talks. Success depends on maintaining the U.S. lead in computing power while establishing global norms to prevent AI from escalating to existential or nuclear risks.

### Strategic Scenarios and the "Winner-Take-All" Risk
* **Recursive Self-Improvement:** Sullivan outlines a scenario where AI achieves superintelligence, allowing it to invent new technologies autonomously. In this "hard-to-catch-up" model, the first nation to achieve this would gain an enduring, multi-domain advantage in military, economic, and scientific fields.
* **The "Distillation" Challenge:** A second scenario suggests that catching up may be easier than expected.
    * **DeepSeek Example:** Sullivan notes that while U.S. models remain ahead, the Chinese firm DeepSeek demonstrated that once a frontier model is released, competitors can "distill" those insights to rapidly close the gap. 
* **Physical vs. Digital AI:** There is an ongoing debate regarding whether Large Language Models (LLMs) will naturally unlock physical robotics or if "world models" (mapping the physical world) are required. Sullivan argues the U.S. must "lay chips on both bets" to ensure industrial and military superiority.

### Comparative Advantages: U.S. vs. China
* **U.S. Compute Lead:** The single most important advantage for the U.S. is its control over advanced AI chips and the equipment to manufacture them.
    * **Export Controls:** Sullivan criticizes any relaxation of chip controls, arguing that sustaining a "compute advantage" is vital to preventing China from closing the technological gap.
* **China’s Structural Strengths:** China possesses unmatched capacity for electricity production to power data centers, along with massive data sets and significant engineering talent.
* **Data and Privacy:** China leverages a lack of privacy constraints to build massive databases, whereas the U.S. must rely on democratic values and private-sector efficiency to compete.

### A New Framework for AI Diplomacy
* **Cold War Precedents:** Sullivan argues that AI safety talks with China must be as "serious, technical, and sustained" as Cold War nuclear arms negotiations, though they are more difficult because AI capabilities are harder to verify than missile counts.
* **Nuclear Safeguards:** A key diplomatic milestone was the 2024 agreement between Presidents Biden and Xi to ensure that the decision to use nuclear weapons remains in human hands, never ceded to an AI.
* **Institutional Innovation:** Sullivan proposes the creation of a new international institution—distinct from the IAEA—that includes governments, the private sector, and civil society to manage risks related to:
    * Bio-weapon intersections
    * Autonomous "rogue" systems
    * Global economic and financial disruptions

### The Government-Private Sector Partnership
* **Shift in Innovation Leadership:** Unlike the internet or nuclear energy, which were birthed by the government, AI is being driven almost entirely by a small number of private labs (e.g., OpenAI, Anthropic, Google).
* **Managing Corporate Concentration:** Sullivan identifies a risk in having transformative national security technology concentrated in the hands of a few private actors focused on shareholder returns.
* **The "Railroad" Analogy:** He argues against over-regulation but suggests the government must act like a "signaling and switching" authority—creating rules that actually allow the industry to move faster by ensuring safety and interoperability.
* **Democracy and Ethics:** Dominance in AI allows the U.S. to ward off risks like mass surveillance and disinformation. Sullivan asserts that the world is safer if built on "Democratic AI" rather than Chinese models, which are already used for state repression.

> "We are absolutely going to have talks that are as serious, as technical, as sustained as we did in the Cold War, except for the stakes are even higher because the impact goes beyond just one class of weapons and the problem is even harder." — **Jake Sullivan**

> "I would submit to you... that this is the single most important ingredient to powering the future of the AI revolution: computing power. And that we need to sustain our advantage here." — **Jake Sullivan**

> "In the case of large language models and generative AI, the government is on the sidelines. This is the companies doing it... it's going to require a new style, a new method of government partnership with the private sector." — **Jake Sullivan**
