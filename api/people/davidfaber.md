**TL;DR**
President Donald Trump defended his direct intervention with Nvidia’s board to reinstate CEO Jensen Huang, stating that national security and intelligence reviews cleared Huang of wrongdoing and that losing his "unbelievable" chip-design talent would be a far greater threat to the U.S. than his personal indiscretions. Trump also linked Huang's return to a $1 trillion rebound in Nvidia's market value and outlined aggressive tariff policies aimed at reshoring semiconductor and automotive manufacturing.

---

### Jensen Huang's Reinstatement & Administration Intervention
* **Direct White House Involvement:** President Trump confirmed to CNBC’s David Faber that his intervention—alongside administration figures including Vice President JD Vance, Scott Bessent, and Howard Lutnick—was "very important" in convincing Nvidia’s board to reinstate Huang.
  * Trump noted it was much easier to persuade the board after Nvidia’s stock dropped 40% to 50% than it would have been a week and a half earlier.
  * He stated he spoke with Huang the night before the announcement, telling him only to "work hard," and predicted Huang will be "more motivated right now than if it never happened."
* **Board & Governance:** While CNBC’s Jim Cramer criticized the board for acting hastily rather than placing Huang on leave, Trump defended the board as "very reasonable" for ultimately reversing course.
  * Trump acknowledged that Huang "made a mistake in judgment" regarding an affair with a subordinate (Alice Wong), but argued that Huang’s apology to his family, the company, and the country—combined with his irreplaceable technical knowledge—made this different from typical CEO scandals.
  * Trump confirmed he previously suggested Tim Cook as a potential replacement when the board initially said Huang was "never coming back," but said Cook could not match Huang's scientific chip-design capabilities.
* **No Government Equity Stake:** Trump told CNBC he did not consider taking a U.S. government stake in Nvidia, joking that he did not want the DOJ to sue the government for insider trading.

### National Security, Export Controls & China
* **Clearance on National Security & Criminal Liability:** In response to questions from CNBC’s Eamon Javers and Sara Eisen, Trump stated that both he and the intelligence community/Attorney General concluded Huang is "innocent" and does not pose a national security threat.
  * Trump said he gave the Nvidia board assurances regarding national security, noting that if he had not received proper clearance, he would not have backed the deal.
  * Regarding export controls, Trump said Huang is permitted to sell older, "semi-obsolete" chips like the 200, but not the advanced Blackwell chip. He argued that if a third party resells older chips to China, "that wouldn't be Jensen's fault."
* **The Alice Wong Reporting:** Asked about *New York Times* reporting that Huang's paramour, Alice Wong, was in contact with a Chinese MSS officer, Trump downplayed the concern, saying China "speaks to everybody" and that he did not believe she was a spy.
* **The "Brainpower" Threat:** Trump argued the real national security risk was alienating Huang and driving him to China, suggesting President Xi Jinping—who Trump said called Huang "a brilliant man"—would gladly pay him a fortune to head a company like Huawei.
* **Chip Complexity & Piracy:** Trump emphasized that advanced chips like the Blackwell are "2, 3, 4... maybe 10 years" ahead and are "uncopyable" due to their vast complexity, unlike rubber mats (citing WeatherTech) or movies that are pirated within minutes or 24 hours.

### Market Impact, AI Competition & Corporate Governance
* **Market Rebound:** Trump and Faber noted that Nvidia added $1 trillion in market value following Huang's reinstatement.
  * Trump contrasted this with a $5 trillion drop in the broader stock market in the week and a half following the recent election (after being up $25 trillion over two years), which he attributed to voter dissatisfaction with Democrats and noted he had held 30 to 31 rallies in 28 days drawing 25,000 to 30,000 people.
* **AI Bubble & Financing Concerns:** Trump dismissed concerns about an AI bubble, comparing Huang's return to getting a "superstar" back on a baseball team.
  * Responding to Jim Cramer’s question about whether the U.S. government would backstop Nvidia's financing if needed, Trump said he did not think Nvidia had a financing problem because its chip backlog is massive.
* **AI Safety & Other Tech Leaders:** Asked about AI safety concerns at companies like OpenAI and Anthropic, Trump said the DOJ would intervene if necessary, praised Anthropic's Dario Amodei as "a great guy" and "totally brilliant," and noted he also protected Elon Musk during his first term (describing Musk as "80% super genius, 20%... other problems").

### Tariffs, Domestic Manufacturing & Broader Economic Policy
* **Semiconductor Reshoring:** Trump credited tariffs for forcing foreign chipmakers to build in the U.S., citing "Mr. Wei" (TSMC) spending "hundreds of billions of dollars" in Arizona and starting a major plant in Texas.
  * Trump stated the U.S. will impose 100%, 200%, and 300% tariffs on chips—specifically noting a 200% tariff on chips in two years—which he said will bring "a big percentage of the chip business" back to the U.S. before he leaves office.
* **Automotive Manufacturing:** Trump highlighted auto tariffs rising to 25%, 35%, and 40% per car in two years, claiming this prompted Audi to spend $4 billion on a new plant, Toyota to build U.S. plants, and Ford to keep its 1906 Detroit plant open and running 24 hours a day.
* **China Trade & Legislation:** Trump said he recently had a "great summit" with President Xi (who runs 1.4 billion people) and announced a forthcoming trade deal in which China will double its purchases of U.S. agricultural products, including soybeans. He also noted he sold 250 Boeing planes to China and expects another 250.
  * On domestic policy, Trump pointed to the "Great Big Beautiful Bill" (18 bills in one), which he said delivered four years of legislation, the biggest tax cuts, and one-year depreciation/expensing.
