## Nvidia Argues to Wary Investors That It Can Thrive Without Huang | Bloomberg Intelligence

### TL;DR
Nvidia delivered a strong beat-and-raise quarter despite the board moving on from Jensen Huang, while Target’s traffic-led rebound and Lowe’s cautious outlook highlight a selective retail environment ahead of the holidays. Meanwhile, *Grand Theft Auto VI* is already tracking toward massive first-year sales and nearly recouping its $1 billion+ development cost through pre-orders alone.

### Nvidia's Post-Huang Strategy & Fundamentals (Gene Munster)
* **Low Probability of Huang's Return:** Gene Munster, Managing Partner at Deepwater Asset Management, noted the probability of Jensen Huang returning has dropped from over 50% over the weekend to sub-1%. While a return would spark a 10%–15% pop in the stock, the board (which has an average tenure of ~18 years vs. the 8-year tech average) is moving on due to a breach of trust—specifically, Huang allegedly lying to the board and General Counsel a year ago.
* **Thriving Without the Founder:** Nvidia can thrive without Huang, much like Apple did post-Jobs, and most key employees remain committed. However, Munster distinguishes between a "good investment" and a "generational investment," noting it is impossible to replicate a founder's "wartime" urgency once they are gone. Munster remains a shareholder and bought more shares the morning Huang resigned.
* **Strong Fundamentals Ignored by the Market:** Nvidia issued next-year guidance 6% higher than Street expectations and its own guidance from three months ago—even after factoring in potential China export curbs, new licensing rules, and circular financing caveats. Munster’s primary concern is that the market is currently ignoring these strong fundamentals because investors are fixated on Huang's departure.

### Target's Traffic Rebound & Holiday Outlook (Poonam Goyal)
* **Traffic-Driven Growth:** Poonam Goyal, Senior U.S. E-Commerce and Retail Analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence, highlighted that Target's 5.6% YoY comparable sales growth was led by a 5% increase in customer traffic.
* **Category Winners:** 
  * **Grocery:** Comps rose 7%. While grocery is only ~20% of Target's sales (compared to ~50% at Walmart), its differentiated, healthier assortment drives repeat store visits that cross over into higher-margin categories.
  * **Beauty & Value:** Beauty comps jumped 9% even without Ulta. Target's "Fun 101" initiative, offering differentiated items under $10, is also resonating with value-conscious shoppers.
* **Apparel Reset:** Apparel was the one category that missed estimates. Target needs a full reset—particularly in women's apparel—and management indicated the earliest product changes will not appear until spring next year.
* **Holiday Momentum:** Sales picked up in the first two weeks of November compared to October, with consumers largely holding out for Black Friday deals.

### *Grand Theft Auto VI*'s Blockbuster Trajectory (Nathan Naidu)
* **Massive Pre-Orders & Reviews:** Nathan Naidu, Bloomberg Intelligence Technology Research Analyst, reported that *GTA VI* (which features an 80-hour main story) has already logged nearly 16 million digital pre-orders—more than one-third of BI's first-year unit forecast—alongside near-perfect review scores on Metacritic and OpenCritic.
* **Multi-Year Revenue Model:** 
  * **Years 1 & 2:** Driven by upfront game sales at $80–$100 per copy, followed by a PC version release next year.
  * **Recurring Revenue:** The upcoming online multiplayer mode will underpin long-term recurring revenue; the previous iteration's multiplayer mode generated ~$500 million per year over 13 years.
* **Development Costs & Cloud Streaming:** Development reportedly cost upward of $1 billion (excluding marketing), though Take-Two noted it was less than the $1.5 billion cost of the Burj Khalifa. Pre-order trends have already nearly recouped this investment. Additionally, Xbox is allowing tens of millions of previous-generation Xbox One owners to play via cloud streaming.

### Lowe's Solid Quarter & Demand Uncertainty (Lindsay Dutch)
* **Q3 Performance:** Lindsay Dutch, Bloomberg Intelligence Consumer Hardlines and Beauty Retail Senior Analyst, noted Lowe's posted solid same-store sales growth of 1.8% (a 4-year best), driven by pro contractors and online sales.
* **Cautious Outlook & Weather Headwinds:** Lowe's only slightly raised its sales outlook, pointing to significant demand uncertainty for Q4 and next year. Management flagged tough weather comparisons in January; a warmer winter (such as from El Niño) without snow events would act as a headwind.
* **Housing Lock-In & Holiday Strategy:** Pending sales of existing homes hit a record low in October as homeowners cling to sub-3% mortgages, weighing on large renovation projects. However, repair and replacement demand (e.g., appliances) remains intact, and Lowe's is leaning into Black Friday/Cyber Monday promotions with curated assortments like exclusive DeWalt tools.

**TL;DR**

### Mobility and Travel: The Shift to Autonomous and Exclusive Supply
* **The Waymo Threat:** Autonomous vehicle (AV) expansion is pressuring the traditional ride-sharing model. Waymo is currently conducting 500,000 rides across more than 10 U.S. cities, nearly doubling its volume over the last 12 months.
    * Both Uber and Lyft are under pressure to move beyond "asset-light" models and add AV capacity to their platforms through partnerships.
* **Lyft’s M&A Potential:** Analysts suggest Lyft could eventually be an acquisition target for platforms like DoorDash.
    * A merger would allow for "agentic AI" use cases and cross-selling opportunities between food delivery and mobility, similar to DoorDash’s expansion in the U.K.
* **Airbnb vs. Expedia:** Airbnb continues to outperform competitors like Expedia due to its "exclusive supply."
    * **Direct Traffic:** Airbnb benefits from being a "verb" in the industry, attracting direct traffic that bypasses expensive marketing channels.
    * **Event Catalysts:** Large-scale events like the FIFA World Cup are expected to be massive demand drivers for the second half of the year across all travel platforms.

### Restaurant Sector: Value Leaders vs. Performance "Disasters"
* **Texas Roadhouse Winning on Volume:** Despite rising beef prices, Texas Roadhouse is seeing strong traffic because of its perceived value.
    * **Trade-Down Trends:** Customers are trading down to cheaper cuts of meat or chicken but are *not* decreasing the frequency of their visits.
* **Wendy’s Momentum Loss:** Described by analysts as a "disaster," Wendy's is struggling to find a turnaround as McDonald’s enters a period of aggressive discounting and the "chicken game" becomes oversaturated.
* **US Foods as a Defensive Play:** The food service distributor is taking market share through a 100% commission-based sales plan and a focus on branded products.
    * **Institutional Stability:** A large portion of their business is tied to "recession-proof" sectors like hospitals, government, and education, where contracts are often indexed to inflation.

### The Cultural Rebirth of Golf and Luxury Branding
* **Audience Expansion:** The U.S. golf audience has surged from 24 million pre-pandemic to 40 million today.
    * The demographic has shifted, becoming younger, more diverse, and highly influenced by social media and "non-traditional" stars from the NBA and NFL.
* **Dewar’s "19th Hole" Strategy:** Luxury brands are moving away from traditional sports sponsorship toward "lifestyle" integration.
    * **Ready-to-Drink (RTD) Innovation:** To attract a younger cohort, Dewar's is focusing on canned cocktails (like the "Lemon Wedge") and partnerships with tech-focused venues like Five Iron Golf and Topgolf.

### Intel’s Strategic Transformation and Political Backing
* **Unprecedented Alliances:** CEO Pat Gelsinger has repositioned Intel as the centerpiece of American manufacturing, securing a 5.5% stake from the U.S. government.
    * Gelsinger has successfully courted industry rivals and partners, including Elon Musk, NVIDIA’s Jensen Huang, and Michael Dell, to support Intel’s CPU role in the AI boom.
* **The "Detail" Challenge:** Despite the stock's massive recent tear (up 460% on a trailing 12-month basis according to the transcript), significant internal work remains.
    * **Manufacturing Quality:** The company must still prove it can fix the quality of its products and factories to move away from outsourcing and regain dominance.
    * **Leadership Style:** Gelsinger’s "venture capital" style of high-level management is a departure from Intel’s historically detail-oriented culture, representing a potential risk.
