## Fed Says More Data Will Help Next Decision as It Holds Rates: Fed Special

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261028.html#video-HkypQjwPtfg
- Speakers: Kevin Warsh (Chair, Federal Reserve); Tom Keene (Host, Bloomberg Surveillance); Jonathan Ferro (Host, Bloomberg Surveillance)

---

**TL;DR:** The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady under Chair Kevin Warsh, whose press conference left markets questioning the central bank's reaction function due to a perceived disconnect between strong economic rhetoric and a cautious hold. Warsh signaled that the FOMC will phase in a reduced schedule next year—voting at seven of eight meetings—and highlighted tighter financial conditions and market volatility as justifications for taking time to evaluate incoming data.

### Market Reaction & Economic Assessment
* **Asset Price Movements:** 
  * **Equities:** S&P 500 up 0.33%; Nasdaq up 0.4% during the afternoon session.
  * **Treasuries:** Yields moved up 2 to 3 basis points, with 10-year yields at 4.70% and 30-year yields at 4.30% (up 5 bps).
  * **Yield Curve & Dollar:** The yield curve steepened and the dollar weakened following the decision and press conference.
* **Economic Resilience:** Chair Warsh noted that domestic spending is resilient, productivity growth and capital investment are strong, robust job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little.
* **Persistent Inflation:** Warsh reiterated that inflation remains elevated and "too high," repeating his vow to get inflation down and deliver price stability with "no ifs, ands or buts."

### Communication Strategy & The "New Fed" Approach
* **Shift in Framework:** Warsh attempted to distance the central bank from modern monetary analysis and hyper-communication, signaling a desire to return to older protocols reminiscent of the 1950s (drawing comparisons to the 1951 Treasury-Fed Accord and William McChesney Martin).
* **Press Conference Mechanics:** Analysts noted structural changes to how the Fed communicates, including shorter press conferences, restricted questioning (limiting follow-ups), and a revamped statement style.
* **Meeting Schedule Adjustment:** Warsh revealed that the FOMC will phase in an adjusted meeting schedule next year, voting at seven of its eight meetings instead of all eight.
* **Mixed Signals on Guidance:** 
  * **Panelist Lisa Abramowicz:** Noted that Warsh attempted a "hawkish hold" but failed to clearly explain the decision, connect it explicitly to inflation data, or clarify whether the stance reflects risk management hikes, cuts, or neutral rate recalibration.
  * **Panelist Mike McKee:** Pointed out that the lack of concrete forward guidance leaves Wall Street struggling to price futures and interpret what the Fed is looking for "out the window" when only one CPI print arrives between meetings.

### Transmission Through Financial Conditions
* **Monetary Policy Transmission:** Warsh explicitly acknowledged that financial conditions have tightened, linking this tightening directly to interest rate sensitivity and the broader economy.
* **Market Doing the Work:** Panelists (including Tom Keene, Jonathan Ferro, and Lisa Abramowicz) suggested Warsh came close to admitting that recent market volatility and tighter financial conditions are "doing the work" for the Fed, reducing the immediate urgency to hike rates further.
* **The "Fed Put" vs. Market Sensitivity:** Jeff Rosenberg (BlackRock) noted that while it may not be a traditional "Fed put," Warsh demonstrated acute sensitivity to market stability and credit spread dynamics, acknowledging that market fluctuations act as a macro disinflationary event.

### Committee Dynamics & Institutional Alignment
* **Internal Division:** The Federal Reserve entered Warsh's regime already divided on whether ongoing price pressures stem from a supply shock or a demand shock.
* **Core Alignment vs. Chair:** Panelists observed that recent speeches by core committee members John Williams and Jefferson—emphasizing the luxury of taking a month to skip and gather data—pre-dated the data and served as more reliable policy guidance than Warsh's press conference.
* **International Parallels:** Jonathan Ferro compared the Fed's current communication challenges under Warsh to the European Central Bank (ECB) under recent leadership, noting a shared frustration from markets over vague institutional messaging.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Meeting Schedule Reductions:** The FOMC will officially phase in an adjusted meeting schedule next year, voting at seven of its eight meetings (as revealed by Chair Kevin Warsh).
