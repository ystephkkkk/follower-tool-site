## Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe on the R2, robotics & the future of EVs | Masters of Scale

- Channel: Masters of Scale

- Speakers: RJ Scaringe (Founder and CEO, Rivian Automotive); Jeff Berman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

**TL;DR** 
Rivian Founder and CEO RJ Scaringe outlines how the electric vehicle maker navigates extreme operational complexity through distributed decision-making, its strategic $5.8 billion software partnership with Volkswagen, and an aggressive expansion into autonomous driving and industrial robotics.

---

### Managing Organizational Complexity and Scaling Decision-Making

*   **Distributed but Coordinated Decisions:** Developing a single vehicle requires coordinating approximately 5,500 to 6,000 engineers managing an estimated 40 million distinct choices.
    *   **The Founder’s Pivot:** Transitioning from an engineer who designed CAD parts at age 26 to a leader who "designs teams," Scaringe learned that scaling requires letting go of micromanagement to avoid paralyzing the workforce.
*   **Preventing Tribal Silos:** To unify diverse engineering disciplines—such as battery chemists, structural engineers, and software developers—Rivian actively fosters an organizational culture of open debate to resolve natural friction points.
*   **The 50-Person "SWAT" Rule:** To avoid bloated decision-making processes, Rivian restricts early-stage vehicle development programs (such as the R2, R3, and future R4) to no more than 50 people for the first six months. This allows the core architecture and trade-offs to be defined rapidly without coordination bottlenecks.
*   **Fluidity in Organizational Structure:** Now managing over 17,000 employees, Scaringe treats organizational structure as a temporary efficiency tool rather than a permanent hierarchy, advising other founders to prepare for constant structural evolution.

### Vertical Integration and the $5.8 Billion Volkswagen Deal

*   **Zonal Electronics Architecture:** Rivian rejected the legacy automotive practice of using dozens of third-party electronic control units (ECUs). Instead, they consolidated vehicle networks into a small number of proprietary "zonal computers" running in-house software.
*   **The Volkswagen Licensing Deal:** This vertical integration enabled a $5.8 billion software joint venture with Volkswagen Group.
    *   **The Launch Vehicle:** The first product featuring this shared platform will be the Volkswagen ID.1, a low-cost electric vehicle designed for the European market.
*   **The Cannibalization Calculus:** While some board members feared licensing technology to a nominal competitor, Scaringe defended the move:
    *   **Market Diversity:** The $50,000 price segment is currently underserved and dominated by Tesla’s Model Y and Model 3; the broader industry requires more competitive variety to accelerate EV transition.
    *   **IP Protection:** Rivian licensed its software platform and electronics architecture, but held back its self-driving platform and physical vehicle designs.

### Product Strategy and Combating Tesla's Dominance

*   **Avoiding Carbon Copies:** Scaringe argues that legacy automakers fail against Tesla by building "copies" of the Model Y. Rivian focuses on delivering highly distinct form factors and brand philosophies.
    *   **R1S Premium SUV Success:** The R1S has successfully carved out its niche, becoming the best-selling premium electric SUV in the United States and significantly outselling the Tesla Model X.
*   **Unlocking the Mass-Market R2 Price Point:** Driving down costs for the upcoming R2 midsize SUV relied on three primary levers:
    *   *Supplier Leverage:* Transitioning away from the "first-time risk tax" paid to suppliers during the early, unproven R1 development days.
    *   *Engineering Simplification:* Consolidating components and redesigning manufacturing processes.
    *   *Subjective Content Trade-offs:* Using R1 consumer feedback to correct past mistakes—such as adding dual glove boxes on the R2, which were left out of the R1 to optimize dashboard space.

### Physical AI: Autonomous Driving, Uber, and Robotics Spin-outs

*   **The Evolution of Rivian Autonomy:** Rivian has transitioned from its original rules-based, supplier-dependent 1.0 driving platform to an entirely in-house, transformer-based neural net model trained on proprietary fleet data.
    *   **Autonomy Roadmap:** Rivian plans to roll out supervised point-to-point driving, followed by unsupervised driving, and eventually fully empty, driverless operations.
*   **The 50,000 Uber Robotaxi Partnership:** Rivian signed a strategic deal with Uber to deploy 50,000 robotaxi versions of the R2 beginning in 2028. This allows Rivian to focus strictly on self-driving technology while leveraging Uber’s massive customer distribution channel.
*   **Spinning out Mind Robotics:** Recognizing that industrial labor represents a third of global GDP, Scaringe spun out a dedicated robotics business to build mechatronic systems for manufacturing plants.
    *   **Capital and Focus:** The company was spun out to raise external capital independently of Rivian. Rivian will serve as the first customer.
    *   **Operational Philosophy:** The new robotics venture operates on a strict mandate to "make progress, not motion," avoiding time-wasting, repetitive demo loops in favor of long-term architectural planning.

## CEO Jason Robins on DraftKings’ next big bets | Masters of Scale

- Channel: Masters of Scale

- Speakers: Jason Robins (Cofounder and CEO, DraftKings); Jeff Berman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

**TL;DR**
DraftKings CEO Jason Robins details the company’s evolution from a fantasy sports startup to a $13 billion gaming leader, focusing on the strategic shift toward live betting and prediction markets while utilizing AI to enhance responsible gaming protocols.

### Super Bowl Strategy and Market Impact
* **The "Biggest Day":** The Super Bowl serves as the primary catalyst for customer acquisition, with DraftKings handling "hundreds of millions" of dollars in bets during the event. 
* **Creative Marketing Pivot:** After a two-year hiatus from Super Bowl television ads, DraftKings returned this year through a partnership with NBC.
    * **Live Betting Focus:** This year’s creative strategy focuses specifically on "live betting" activation—an experiment to keep users engaged during the game rather than just before kickoff.
    * **Trusting Expertise:** Robins emphasizes the "art over science" nature of marketing, noting that he defers to his CMO because high-performing ads often contradict executive intuition.
* **Post-Game Retention:** The ultimate goal of the Super Bowl surge is to migrate new users into other sports ecosystems, including March Madness, the NBA, and the NHL.

### Product Evolution: Prediction Markets and Live Betting
* **DraftKings Predictions:** A newly launched product designed to capture market share in states where traditional sports betting remains illegal, such as California and Texas.
* **The DFS Legacy:** The company transitioned from its 2012 roots in Daily Fantasy Sports (DFS) to a diversified betting platform following the 2018 Supreme Court decision to lift the federal ban on sports gambling.
* **Strategic Disruption:** Robins views prediction markets as "too disruptive to ignore," noting that tech leaders must invest in the "next thing" (like AI or predictions) before their core business is disrupted by others.
* **Women's Sports Growth:** The WNBA and women’s college basketball are among the fastest-growing categories on the platform. Interestingly, this growth is driven largely by existing male sports fans rather than a massive influx of new female users.

### Responsible Gaming and the Role of AI
* **Corporate Oversight:** DraftKings employs a Chief Responsible Gaming Officer, Lori Kalani, who reports directly to the CEO to ensure gaming remains sustainable and entertaining.
* **AI as a Safety Net:** The company is integrating AI to monitor customer service touchpoints and communication patterns to flag potential problem gambling behaviors that a human might miss.
* **User Empowerment Tools:** The platform provides granular tools for consumers to set daily or monthly spending limits and time-on-app restrictions to promote a "sustainable customer base."
* **Equilibrium in Betting:** DraftKings uses AI to set and manage betting lines to maintain a "playing field equilibrium" against sophisticated bettors who may be using their own AI tools to find an edge.

### Scaling, Culture, and Executive Advice
* **Organizational Growth:** Now at 5,600 employees, Robins maintains innovation by fostering a culture of "calculated risk" and speed.
* **The "Concentric Circle" of Hiring:** Robins attributes the company's success to its first group of hires. He advises founders that three people cannot build a company; the initial "concentric circle" of talent sets the bar for every hire that follows.
* **Navigating Politics:** In California, the path to legalization relies on collaboration with Native American tribes rather than just legislative lobbying. Robins views tribal sovereignty as the central factor in that state's regulatory future.
* **Advice for the Next Generation:**
    * **Skillsets of the Future:** Every professional, regardless of role (sales, engineering, etc.), must become an expert in utilizing AI to increase work efficiency.
    * **Identify Personal Greatness:** Robins urges young professionals to ignore the corporate tendency to focus only on weaknesses and instead identify the specific skills where they are "upper-tier" performers.

> "Most companies that benefit from innovation didn't invent the innovation. Most of them just figured out before others that they needed to be investing there... and they unleashed that talent." — **Jason Robins**

> "What AI can do is it can take all the communications... and find any sorts of patterns that we may want to look into [for responsible gaming]... so many people ask 'Are you using AI to get people to bet more?' and actually they're not thinking about using AI to improve responsible gaming." — **Jason Robins**

> "Creative, everyone has an opinion on. It’s not something that you can be that straightforward with. I’ve seen things where I and others are like, 'That’s terrible,' and it kills it on performance." — **Jason Robins**
