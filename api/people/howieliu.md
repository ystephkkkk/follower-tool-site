## Why Airtable’s founder is betting on teams of AI agents

- Channel: Sources Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DFf6t9Q4yss
- Published: 2026-11-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261117.html#video-DFf6t9Q4yss
- Speakers: Howie Liu (Founder, Hyperagent)

---

**TL;DR**
Airtable founder Howie Liu sold his nearly $500M-revenue SaaS company to Bending Spoons because the AI era has fundamentally rewritten software economics, distribution, and pricing models. He has now spun out Hyperagent to bet on "multiplayer" AI—teams of autonomous, role-specific agents designed to augment small business owner-operators rather than compete in the crowded personal assistant market.

### Selling Airtable to Bending Spoons
* **End of the SaaS Golden Era:** Liu founded Airtable in 2012 at the dawn of product-led growth (PLG) and rich browser-based single-page apps (alongside Figma and Miro). While Airtable reached nearly $500M in revenue, generated strong cash flow, and was still growing, Liu saw it as a mature business built on SaaS-era assumptions (like seat-based pricing) that are being disrupted by AI.
* **Why Bending Spoons:** Liu chose Bending Spoons—a publicly traded US company known for buying and holding brands forever (including Evernote, Vimeo, WeTransfer, AOL, and Miro)—because they are transparent, high-integrity, and disciplined about their playbook, avoiding the antitrust risks or drawn-out diligence traps seen in deals like Figma/Adobe.
* **Valuation & Equity Mechanics:** Airtable sold below its peak ZIRP-era valuation, a reality Liu believes many SaaS companies will face. However, because Airtable had raised over $1B and kept the vast majority of that cash on its balance sheet, the company was able to clear its preference stack and return dollars to common shareholders.
* **Spin-Out Structure:** Rather than keeping Hyperagent inside Airtable, Liu spun it out and gave Airtable shareholders a piece of upside in the new AI-native company.

### The Economics of SaaS vs. AI-Native Startups
* **Capital as a Crutch:** Liu argued against using Airtable’s cash flow to fund Hyperagent internally, noting that capital is rarely the primary constraint for 0-to-1 product-market fit and can actually distract from obsessive product iteration.
* **Structural Differences:** Traditional SaaS enjoys high margins and near-zero marginal distribution costs. AI-native businesses have fundamentally different margin profiles (due to variable token/compute spend), pricing models (outcome/autonomy-based vs. seat-based), and go-to-market motions.
* **Seed-Stage Discipline:** Hyperagent raised a small round ("many tens of millions of dollars") mostly from insiders after spinning out, operating with a lean, seed-stage mentality rather than relying on incumbent scale.

### Hyperagent 2.0: Betting on Multiplayer Agents
* **Origin Story:** Hyperagent began during the winter holidays when Liu used frontier models (like Claude Code, Cursor, and Opus 4.5) to build real, structurally sound software solo—an "inside-out" approach driven by new technological capabilities rather than traditional MBA-style "outside-in" product planning.
* **Escaping the "Knife Fight in a Phone Booth":** Liu is intentionally avoiding the 1-to-1 personal AI assistant market (e.g., ChatGPT Dots at $100/month, Instinct in private beta, Claw Duet, Muse), where tech giants like Meta, Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Microsoft are subsidizing compute with tens of billions of dollars.
* **From 1.0 to 2.0:** While Hyperagent 1.0 was praised for deep customizability, Liu acknowledged it was overly complex and intimidating. The upcoming Hyperagent 2.0 is a near-total rebuild focused on **multiplayer agents** with smart defaults:
  * **Distinct Identities:** Each agent acts like a hired employee with its own identity, provisioned accounts, guardrails, and auditability.
  * **Multi-Agent Coordination:** Business owners can hold group chats or group calls with multiple agents at once, allowing a marketing agent and a sales agent to coordinate work.
* **Dogfooding at Hyperagent:** Liu uses Hyperagent agents 24/7 for strategy, generating Super Bowl-quality video ads, tracking competitors, and even writing merge-ready pull requests directly into Hyperagent's codebase.

### Target Market: Empowering SMB Owner-Operators
* **Deprioritizing Enterprise:** Unlike Airtable, which scaled into Fortune 500 enterprises, Hyperagent is targeting SMB owner-operators (e.g., landscapers, insurance brokers, designers, creative agencies) to enable rapid product iteration and self-serve adoption.
* **Augmenting, Not Replacing:** Small business owners don't want another CRM; they want another hire they can't otherwise afford. Early users report getting the output of 10 incremental hires.
* **Concrete Use Case (Landscaping):** An agent can take a client inquiry, pull satellite imagery and past Zillow photos, use image models to mock up a landscaping design, estimate pricing, and send a polished proposal immediately—undercutting competitors who take five weeks just to call back—while also managing ongoing field project schedules.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AI's Economic Multiplier:** AI will double, triple, or potentially 10x the tens of trillions of dollars of human intelligence contribution to global GDP each year. — *Howie Liu*
* **Competitive Advantage:** Businesses in any category—even lower-margin ones like landscaping or insurance—that figure out how to deploy agents effectively will gain a 2x up to a 10x–100x advantage in efficiency, speed, and growth over competitors. — *Howie Liu*
* **SaaS Valuation Risk:** Many independent SaaS companies valued highly during the ZIRP era will never reach those peak valuations again, and attempting to maintain the status quo in the AI era risks underperforming the S&P 500 or eroding equity value. — *Howie Liu*

### TL;DR
Howie Liu of HyperAgent outlines the evolution of AI agent form factors—from completions to autonomous, always-on agent fleets—culminating in the HyperAgent Startup Battlefield finals where three agent-native startups competed for $100,000 in prizes, with Comment.io taking the grand prize.

### Evolution of Agent Form Factors
* **Progression of Models:**
  * **Completions:** Models accept a string of tokens and emit tokens until a stop token.
  * **Chatbots:** Instruct-tuned models enabling conversational back-and-forth interactions.
  * **Agents:** Models that recurse upon themselves with open-ended tool calls and reasoning steps rather than following prescripted linear workflows.
  * **Always-On Agents ("Claws"):** Agents capable of running for extended periods (minutes to hours) utilizing heartbeat mechanisms to wake up repeatedly, perform multi-turn loops, and creatively unblock themselves.
  * **Orchestrating Agents:** Models capable of complex, parallelized task distribution and agent-to-agent interactions to manage context windows and coordinate advanced workflows.

### Real-World Fleet Deployment: Landscaping Use Case
* **End-to-End Business Automation:** 
  * Highlighted a real customer case where a landscaping business runs end-to-end via a fleet of HyperAgent agents handling prospecting, surveying, pitching, and quote generation.
* **Agent Workflow & Human-in-the-Loop:**
  * **Triager Agent:** Takes inbound client inquiries, assesses project scope, and verifies lead quality.
  * **Surveyor Agent:** Uses a fully capable sandbox VM to manipulate files and analyze imagery/video (via tools like `ffmpeg`) to design bespoke pitch decks and interactive web pages.
  * **Human Oversight:** Human managers intervene only to unblock agents and authorize high-impact actions like final quotes or client pitches, serving as orchestrators of an agent fleet rather than executing micro-tasks.
  * **Real-Time Learning:** Agents accumulate memories, skill updates, and user feedback in real time (via Slack, email, or direct interactions) to adjust performance dynamically without waiting for retraining cycles.

### HyperAgent Startup Battlefield Finals
* **Competition Details:** 
  * Culmination of the "Founding 500" cohort pre-selected down to 20 competitors, featuring a live pitch in front of judges Howie Liu, Theo (T3 Chat), and Joshua (HeyGen).
  * Backed by $100,000 in prizes provided by HyperAgent.
* **Finalists & Winners:**
  * **Comment.io:** Grand prize winner ($50,000); a multiplayer markdown editor built specifically for real-time collaboration between humans and AI agents.
  * **Built by Foundry:** Runner-up ($30,000); deploys teams of agents to help content creators build recurring revenue businesses and launch custom products.
  * **Kamad:** Second runner-up ($20,000); agentifies physical commodity trade through a proprietary CFC state machine that verifies, executes, and settles global trade deals.

## Howie Liu On How To Future-Proof Your Company

- Channel: Forbes
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L45oOLFiqPs
- Published: 2026-04-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260424.html#video-L45oOLFiqPs
- Speakers: Howie Liu (Founder and CEO, Airtable)

---

**TL;DR:** The current AI revolution is a multi-layered "SaaS apocalypse" happening at 10x speed, requiring leaders to abandon traditional management techniques in favor of hands-on technical immersion and "agentic" business models where tiny teams generate billion-dollar revenues.

### Fundraising and Financial Resilience
* **Capital Strategy:** Airtable has raised $1.4 billion to date, maintaining over $1 billion on its balance sheet while generating $100 million in annual free cash flow.
* **Inflection Timing:** Liu advises raising capital only during or immediately before a company inflection point. Raising during a "slog" is significantly more difficult and less favorable for founders.
* **Safety Cushions:** A common mistake is raising only 6–12 months of runway. Liu recommends raising significantly more than needed to provide a buffer for errors and product iteration.
    * **Example:** In their first round, Airtable raised $3 million instead of $1 million to ensure they had several years of runway rather than just 12 months.
* **The "Money is Time" Philosophy:** Capital should be viewed as a tool to buy the time necessary to reach the next major milestone without the pressure of imminent insolvency.

### Navigating the "SaaS Apocalypse"
* **Multi-Layered Disruption:** Liu argues that AI is not a single shift (like the move to mobile) but rather 3–4 simultaneous disruptions occurring at unprecedented speeds.
* **The Billion-Dollar Solo Founder:** He agrees with Sam Altman’s prediction that agentic leverage will allow companies of 1–10 people to generate hundreds of millions or billions in revenue.
* **Structural Shifts:** The very nature of how companies operate is changing; instead of visiting websites (e.g., Nike.com), customers will likely use agents to shop and interact with services.
* **Wartime Leadership:** During this period of disruption, "business as usual" is a recipe for failure. Leaders must focus on winning the technological "war" rather than optimizing internal processes or engagement.

### Airtable’s AI Pivot: From No-Code to Hyperagents
* **Product Evolution:** Airtable began as a no-code app-building platform in 2013, moved into enterprise sales five years later, and is now returning to its roots as a Product-Led Growth (PLG) company powered by AI.
* **Omni AI:** This is Airtable’s internal AI feature that allows users to build applications through natural language conversations rather than manual UI configuration.
* **Hyperagent:** A new, separate product play from Airtable designed to allow users to build and deploy their own frontier-model agents (e.g., at the level of Claude/OpenAI) to run entire company operations.
* **Agentic Leverage:** Liu emphasizes that agents are moving beyond "chat" to autonomous work, capable of completing tasks in 30 minutes that previously took humans days.

### Strategic Advice for Executives
* **Stay at the "Edge":** Liu asserts that a CEO's primary job today is staying current with AI developments. Traditional management tasks (one-on-ones, all-hands) are secondary to understanding the technological frontier.
* **Real-Time Information Sources:** 
    * **X (formerly Twitter):** Liu identifies X as the essential source for AI news, noting that major companies often release groundbreaking updates there via developer posts rather than formal press releases.
    * **Technical Immersion:** Leaders must personally experiment with tools like "Claude Code" or "Hyperagent" to develop the "vocabulary" of what is possible in an agentic world.
* **The Speed of Innovation:** Traditional annual launch cycles (like Salesforce’s Dreamforce) are becoming obsolete because the technology changes too much within a single month to wait for a yearly event.

> "When apocalypse is coming... you don't maintain business as usual. What matters is winning or losing the war. And I think the only way to do this in this moment is to actually get hands-on with the most frontier AI experience that you can." — **Howie Liu**

> "I think it's going to be the default that you're going to have very, very small teams... that build enormously leveraged companies that generate hundreds of millions if not billions of revenue because they're able to be fully agentically leveraged." — **Howie Liu**

> "If you do nothing else as a CEO except to go and stay focused on what's happening every single week and constantly be playing around with it... you couldn't do much better than that." — **Howie Liu**
