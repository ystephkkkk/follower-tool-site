### TL;DR
Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller noted that a surge in long-term US Treasury yields is helping cool interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing and autos, functioning as a de facto rate hike. Speaking at Bloomberg's New Economic Forum, Waller also addressed how massive AI infrastructure spending is creating short-term inflation pressures before driving long-term productivity and growth.

### AI Economy, Global Disparities, and Productivity
- **Global AI Divide:** 
  - **U.S. Leadership:** The U.S. is the dominant builder of AI models, alongside China.
  - **Inputs & Infrastructure:** Inputs are largely produced in Taiwan and South Korea; data centers are deployed globally.
  - **GDP Implications:** There is a high correlation between GDP per capita and workplace AI usage, risking widened global gaps if adoption lags in certain regions. Europe is a major user though it produces little AI hardware or models.
- **Productivity Lag:** 
  - **Trial and Error:** Major technological changes take years to show up in measured productivity as firms experiment with use cases.
  - **Coding Impact:** Coding tools are shockingly good and fundamentally changing the job, shifting workers from basic coding to expanded output.
  - **Welfare vs. GDP:** Efficiency gains may manifest as increased leisure time rather than higher annual output per worker, challenging traditional GDP metrics alongside life-improving non-GDP inventions like Google Maps.

### Inflation Pressures from the AI Buildout
- **Short-Term Price Pressures:** 
  - **Inelastic Demand:** Hyperscalers are spending trillions with perfectly inelastic demand, bidding up prices for semiconductors of all types and spilling over into broader goods.
  - **Timeline:** Price pressures driven by the AI buildout could last **2 to 3 years**, based on current commitments from hyperscalers.
- **Long-Term Deflationary Supply Effects:** Once the massive buildout concludes, demand pressure will taper off and supply effects will allow more output produced faster and at lower prices.

### Global Bond Yields, Growth, and Fiscal Pressures
- **Drivers of Higher Real Yields:** 
  - **Real Growth, Not Inflation:** High yields are driven by real growth, productivity expectations, and AI investments rather than rising inflation expectations (which remain well-anchored).
  - **Corporate Bond Example:** Google issued a 40-year bond paying a **100 basis point** premium over US Treasuries.
- **Fiscal Concerns & Safety Premium:** 
  - **International Strain:** Advanced countries face fiscal challenges (e.g., France), and recent academic research indicates that the safety and liquidity premium on U.S. Treasuries—historically worth **50 to 70 basis points**—has disappeared over the last couple of years.
- **Monetary Policy Spillovers:** Rising 10-year yields, driven by global supply and demand rather than overnight Fed rates, are tightening demand in housing, autos, and other interest-rate-sensitive sectors, doing some of the work of tighter monetary policy.

### Central Bank Independence and Fiscal Dominance
- **Historical Parallels:** Following World War II, when the U.S. debt-to-GDP ratio exceeded **100%**, high inflation over the subsequent two years eroded **40%** of that debt in real terms, leading to the Fed-Treasury Accord.
- **Threats to Independence:** 
  - **Institutional Protections:** While the administration has pressured the FOMC to lower rates, U.S. institutions (Congress, the courts) and the bond market serve as the ultimate protectors of central bank independence.
  - **Bond Market Vigilance:** If an administration creates a high-inflation environment, bond markets will push yields up rather than letting interest rates fall.

### Predictions & Forward Calls

**TL;DR**
The Federal Reserve’s Fifth Conference on the International Roles of the U.S. Dollar focuses on how digital innovations, particularly stablecoins and distributed ledger technology, are reshaping global financial intermediation and the dollar's traditional dominance.

### Organizational Structure of the Federal Reserve
The briefing opened with an overview of the Federal Reserve System’s three-pillar structure designed to promote economic health and financial stability:
*   **Board of Governors:** Based in Washington, D.C., this seven-member governing body is appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate for 14-year terms. It oversees the system and sets policy direction.
*   **12 Federal Reserve Banks:** Operating independently under Board supervision, these regional banks monitor local economic health, supervise commercial banks, and act as the "bank for banks" and the U.S. Treasury.
*   **Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC):** This 12-member body establishes monetary policy to achieve the "dual mandate" of maximum employment and price stability, primarily by influencing interest rates and credit conditions.
*   **Educational Mandate:** Through the Federal Reserve Education Program, the Fed provides free resources and professional development for educators and students to promote financial literacy and career paths in economics.

### The Evolving Global Status of the U.S. Dollar
Beth Ann Wilson (Federal Reserve Board) and Governor Christopher Waller addressed the "profound change" currently impacting the international monetary system.
*   **Traditional Pillars:** The dollar’s global prominence remains supported by the size of the U.S. economy, deep financial markets, and trust in the rule of law and U.S. institutions.
*   **Technological Disruption:** Rapid innovation is changing how households and businesses access, transfer, and use dollars. New technologies are reshaping financial intermediation and creating new connections between emerging platforms and traditional institutions.
*   **Institutional Flexibility:** Beth Ann Wilson noted that policymakers must channel the "vision and flexibility" of late Chairman Alan Greenspan to navigate this era of technological shift, particularly in the payment system space.

### Impact of Digital Assets and Stablecoins
Governor Waller emphasized that the private sector is moving rapidly to expand access to dollar-denominated assets through non-traditional means.
*   **New Payment Rails:** Distributed ledger technologies (DLT) and tokenized assets, such as stablecoins, are creating channels for global dollar intermediation that operate alongside traditional banking systems.
*   **Benefits of Competition:** Waller noted that while innovation creates new vulnerabilities, it also fosters competition, which generally leads to better outcomes for consumers and society.
*   **Stablecoins and Safe Assets:** A critical area of research is how dollar-backed stablecoins might create a new channel linking global liquidity demand directly to the U.S. Treasury market.

### 2026 Conference Research Themes
The conference outlined several key areas of academic and policy focus regarding the "digital dollar" ecosystem:
*   **Infrastructure Transformation:** Documentation of rapid growth in stablecoin-based transactions and decentralized foreign exchange (FX) trading.
*   **Market Spillovers:** Research into whether stablecoin flows affect exchange rates, dollar funding conditions, and cross-border capital movements.
*   **Reinforcement vs. Tension:** Analysis of whether digital innovation bolsters the dollar's global role by extending access worldwide or introduces new systemic tensions by altering the nature of safe asset demand.
