**TL;DR:** Turing Award Laureate Prof. Yann LeCun reflects on the multi-decade history of neural networks, argues that current AI lacks physical-world understanding (calling for "physical AI"), and advises students to master timeless fundamentals like math and physics rather than fleeting technologies.

### Origins & The History of Neural Networks
* **Early Inspiration:** LeCun's interest in AI was sparked at age 9 in 1969 when he saw *2001: A Space Odyssey* in France, fascinated by space travel, human evolution, and machine intelligence.
  * He notes that the film (set in 2001, with Hal 9000 built in 1992 in the novel) and its MIT advisor Marvin Minsky were far too optimistic about timelines, proving that human-level machine intelligence is much harder than anticipated.
* **The Two Branches of AI:** When AI was coined in the 1950s, it split into logic/reasoning ("good old-fashioned AI") and cybernetics/pattern recognition, which birthed neural networks.
* **AI Winters & Revivals:**
  * **Late 1960s Collapse:** Interest in neural networks died after MIT's Marvin Minsky and Seymour Papert published a book showing the severe limitations of early neural nets, halting most Western research through the 1970s and early 1980s (though Japan maintained a separate research ecosystem).
  * **Mid-1980s to Mid-1990s Revival:** Physicists revived the field, and the back propagation training procedure appeared around 1995–96 (with LeCun's contribution). However, interest faded again in the mid-1990s because neural nets required "black art" that was hard to share without the internet or open-source code habits.
  * **2010s Deep Learning Boom:** Neural networks—rebranded as deep learning—re-emerged to revolutionize speech recognition (~2010), image recognition (~2013), natural language understanding, and eventually Large Language Models (LLMs).

### Real-World Impact & The "Physical AI" Gap
* **Bell Labs & ConvNets:** Inspired by the visual cortex, LeCun designed convolutional neural networks (which he prefers to call "conv nets" rather than CNNs) trained via back propagation.
  * Working with an engineering group at Bell Labs, this research became a commercial product used by banks in the late 1990s to read handwritten checks, zip codes, envelopes, and documents.
* **Current AI Limitations:** Despite excelling at manipulating symbols (math theorems, code generation, protein folding, chess, and Go) and regurgitating accumulated declarative knowledge, current AI systems are "terrible at understanding the physical world."
  * LeCun points out that AI still has less physical-world understanding than a cat, which is why we still lack domestic robots or Level 5 autonomous self-driving cars.
  * He is currently tackling this challenge—known as **physical AI**—at his new company, Emulate Labs.

### The Role of Humans & Personal Philosophy
* **Humans as "Bosses":** Rather than replacing humans, LeCun believes human-level AI will elevate people into "bosses" managing teams of AI staff that amplify human intelligence, creativity, and efficiency.
  * He compares this to a lab director or political leader hiring staff smarter than themselves, noting that humans will still be needed to provide moral fiber and figure out *what* good questions to ask.
* **Heroes & Hobbies:** LeCun's scientific heroes are physicists Albert Einstein and Richard Feynman, though his mechanical engineer father inspired his love for building things—leading him to engineering over theoretical physics.
  * Outside the lab, he builds model airplanes (a family hobby shared with his father and brother), plays jazz, and taught himself electronics as a teenager to build synthesizers.
* **Biology as Inspiration, Not Blueprint:** Just as aviation pioneers studied birds to derive aerodynamic principles rather than building mechanical birds, AI researchers should derive principles like learning from neuroscience and cognitive science, then validate them by building intelligent machines.

### Advice for Students & Future Scientists
* **Learn to Learn:** Because technological progress is accelerating and careers will inevitably shift, students should avoid overloading on short-shelf-life technology courses that will be outdated in 5 years.
  * Instead, they should master timeless fundamentals—physics, engineering, science, and mathematics. (e.g., choosing quantum mechanics over iOS programming).
* **Domain Science + AI:** Future scientists will need to know both their core domain (e.g., material science, biology, medicine, environmental protection) and enough AI to build custom AI-driven research tools, much like Galileo's telescope or the microscope unlocked new scientific eras.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Superintelligence Timeline:** We will *not* have superintelligent AI systems in 1 to 2 years; it will take much longer. — *Yann LeCun*
* **Obsolescence of Tech Skills:** Specific technology courses learned in college today will very likely be out of date within 5 years. — *Yann LeCun*
* **AI in Scientific Research:** Within 5 to 7 years, AI will be used massively in research not just to generate code, but to design and automate physical experiments (e.g., in chemistry labs), driving an acceleration of progress across material science, biology, medicine, and environmental protection. — *Yann LeCun*

## AI at 70 Award Presentation & Keynote: World Models: Enabling the Next AI Revolution

- Channel: Dartmouth
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Yr62DCtIVs
- Published: 2026-10-29
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261029.html#video-7Yr62DCtIVs
- Speakers: Yann LeCun (Founder/Chair, AMI Labs); Peter Chin (Faculty Chair, AI at 70 Conference)

---

#### TL;DR
Current generative AI models and Large Language Models (LLMs) are fundamentally incapable of reaching human-level intelligence because they rely on forward propagation and cannot understand the complex, high-dimensional physical world. True progress will require moving toward objective-driven architectures utilizing hierarchical world models and inference by optimization.

---

#### Limitations of Current AI
* **The Failure of Generative AI and LLMs:** 
  * **Limited Training Data Scope:** LLMs are pre-trained on roughly $10^{14}$ bytes of publicly available text (approx. 20-30 trillion tokens), which is equivalent to only about half a million years of reading. In contrast, a 4-year-old child processes roughly the same volume of data ($10^{14}$ bytes) through 16,000 hours of optic nerve visual input (2 million nerve fibers at ~1 byte per second).
  * **Inability to Generalize to the Physical World:** Despite passing bar exams and math olympiads, current AI fails to deliver domestic robots or level-5 self-driving cars. Imitation learning for driving has been a "complete failure."
  * **The Moravec Paradox:** As formulated by Hans Moravec in 1988, things that are easy for humans/animals are difficult for AI, and vice versa.
* **Flaws in Forward Propagation:**
  * **Lack of Variable Thinking Time:** LLMs use pure forward propagation through transformer layers, meaning the computational effort spent on a "yes/no" answer does not scale with question complexity.
  * **Chain-of-Thought Limitations:** Techniques like chain-of-thought force LLMs to generate intermediate tokens, but true reasoning in biological systems is an optimization/search process rather than feedforward propagation.

#### The Science of Human and Animal Intelligence
* **Intelligence Defined:** Citing a developmental psychology concept attributed to Jean Piaget, *"Intelligence is not what you know, it's what you do when you don't know."* Intelligence should be measured by how fast a system can learn a new task zero-shot.
* **Developmental Milestones:** Cognitive scientist Emmanuel Dupoux's research shows babies learn basic concepts sequentially:
  * **2 Months:** Animate/inanimate distinction and object permanence.
  * **9 Months:** Intuitive physics (gravity, inertia, conservation of momentum).
* **Non-Linguistic Reasoning:** Most animal and human reasoning happens in abstract mental representation space (e.g., mental rotation of a 3D cube), not through language. Language is largely restricted to narrow domains like coding and mathematics.

#### Objective-Driven Architecture & World Models
* **Core Components:** Instead of generative prediction, Yann LeCun proposes an objective-driven architecture containing:
  * **Perception Encoder:** Converts raw observations ($X_T$) into a state representation.
  * **World Model:** A trainable neural network predicting the next state ($S_{T+1}$) given the current state and a proposed action ($A_T$).
  * **Objective/Cost Function:** Evaluates whether the task is accomplished ($0$ for success, positive numbers for distance from goal).
  * **Guardrail Objectives:** Evaluated on predicted states to ensure safety and prevent harm.
* **Inference by Optimization:** Unlike forward-propagation, the system searches for an optimal sequence of actions that minimizes the objective function using model predictive control.
* **Hierarchical Planning:** 
  * Real-world problem solving requires multi-level abstraction (e.g., planning a trip to Paris breaks down into: going to the airport $\rightarrow$ hailing a taxi $\rightarrow$ taking the elevator $\rightarrow$ standing up from a chair).
  * Multi-level world models predict short-term details at lower layers (e.g., ant leg positions over 200 milliseconds) and long-term abstract goals at higher layers (e.g., overall trajectory over several seconds), drastically increasing planning success rates.

#### Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA)
* **The Problem with Generative Prediction:** Forcing a model to predict every pixel in a video (e.g., predicting what every person in a meeting room looks like) fails because most information in the real world is unpredictable. Generative models result in blurry outputs or hallucinations.
* **JEPA Mechanism:** Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures run both observations ($X$) and targets ($Y$) through encoders to predict *abstract representations* rather than raw pixel details, ignoring unpredictable elements.
* **Preventing Representation Collapse:** 
  * Without intervention, encoder networks tend to cheat by outputting constant representations to achieve zero prediction error.
  * **SIGREG (Sketch Isotropic Gaussian Regularization):** A technique that projects representation vectors against multiple random dimensions and uses Fourier transform/CDF distance to drive the empirical marginal distribution toward an isotropic Gaussian with variance 1.
  * **Alternative Anti-Collapse Methods:** Self-supervised distillation frameworks (like DINOv3 and V-JEPA 2/2.1) use teacher-student encoder pairs trained with exponential moving averages.
* **Emergent Properties of V-JEPA:** When trained purely on video to predict missing parts, V-JEPA models spontaneously discover the 3D structure of the world (enabling depth prediction and object segmentation without explicit supervision).

### AMI Labs & Company Overview
- **Corporate Setup:** Yann LeCun explains that AMI Labs (pronounced *Anidance*, meaning "friend" in French) is headquartered in Paris with major offices in New York and smaller offices in Montreal and Singapore.
- **Funding & Scale:** The company started operations in March, raised approximately $1 billion in a seed round (believed to be the largest seed round in history outside the US), and currently employs around 65 people.
- **Core Mission:** Focused entirely on building AI systems for the real physical world ("physical AI") through the creation of world models.

### Limitations of LLMs & The Moravec Paradox
- **The Core Fallacy:** LeCun notes a 70-year-old recurring mistake in AI where researchers assume that if a machine performs a complex human intellectual task (e.g., playing chess, proving theorems, or passing the bar exam), it possesses general or superintelligence.
- **The Moravec Paradox:** Machines easily manipulate discrete symbols, code, and mathematics (via LLMs), but struggle immensely with the real world, which is messy, continuous, high-dimensional, noisy, and unpredictable.
- **Real-World Failures:** Despite LLM advancements, the industry still lacks domestic robots and Level 5 self-driving cars that can learn to drive in a few hours like a teenager.

### World Models & JEPA Architecture
- **Definition of a World Model:** A system that starts with an idea of the state of the world at time $t$, predicts the resulting state at time $t+1$ after taking a specific action, and uses optimization against a cost function to plan a sequence of actions.
- **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA):** Unlike generative models that try to predict every pixel or token (which fails in high-noise environments like video or financial time series), JEPA learns an abstract representation of the input and predicts only what is predictable while ignoring unpredictable noise.
- **Analogy to Finance and Physics:** Likened to financial data (mostly noise with a little information) and scientific modeling (such as using the ideal gas law or Navier-Stokes equations without needing to simulate every individual air molecule).
- **Hierarchical World Models:** Published a paper last week showing how high-level goals (e.g., traveling from New York to Paris) break down into hierarchical sub-goals (hailing a taxi, walking out a door) because short-term predictions are accurate while long-term physical details are uncertain.

### Global Landscape & Competitiveness
- **Asian Leadership in Physical AI:** Countries like China, Japan, Korea, and Taiwan are heavily pushing robotics and physical AI due to their strong manufacturing foundations.
- **Shift from Silicon Valley:** Because LLMs are inadequate for physical robotics, many researchers in Asia (particularly China) are rapidly publishing and working on world models and joint embedding approaches, outpacing Silicon Valley, which LeCun describes as "hypnotized by LLMs."

---

#### **## The Fallacy of AGI and Specialized Human Intelligence**

* **Rejection of the Term AGI:**
  * **Yann LeCun** refuses to use the acronym "AGI" because human intelligence is incredibly specialized rather than universally generalized.
  * Internal teams at Meta prefer the term **AMI** (Advanced Machine Intelligence)—which conveniently translates to "friend" in French.
* **The Simplicity of Language Manipulation:**
  * **Yann LeCun** notes that LLMs impress people simply because they manipulate language well, but language evolution occurred very recently in human history (a few hundred thousand years ago).
  * Genomic and neurological evidence shows language requires a tiny fraction of the human genome (roughly a couple of gigabytes) and is localized to two small brain regions: **Broca's area** (production) and **Wernicke's area** (understanding).
* **Absence of Real-World Common Sense:**
  * **Yann LeCun** challenges current progress by pointing out missing everyday applications: *"Where is my domestic robot? Where is my level-5 self-driving car?"*
  * Machines still struggle enormously with basic physical tasks that humans learn effortlessly, such as learning to drive safely within 20 hours of practice.

---

#### **## The Impossibility of a Single Intelligence Test**

* **Failure of Traditional Benchmarks:**
  * **Yann LeCun** compares defining a single test for AGI to the **Drake Equation**, where parameters are subjective and lead to wildly variable conclusions depending on error bars.
  * Neither the **Turing Test** (focused on who is listening) nor hypothetical physics discovery tests (proposed by **Dr. Brian Keating**) can serve as a single valid benchmark for machine intelligence.
* **Historical Precedent of Specialized Superintelligence:**
  * **Yann LeCun** points out that computer science history is built on solving specific subproblems with superhuman efficiency.
  * Examples include chess-playing software, multi-language translation, shortest-path GPS search algorithms, and basic calculation tools that easily outperform humans in their specific domains.

---

#### **## Safety, Control, and the Drive to Dominate**

* **Refuting the Will to Power:**
  * **Yann LeCun** disagrees with **Geoffrey Hinton** and **Max Tegmark**, stating that intelligence is not intrinsically linked to a desire to dominate or take over.
  * Domination is a trait specific to certain social species (e.g., baboons, chimpanzees, wolves, humans) driven by resource competition, whereas solitary species like **orangutans** display no such territorial or dominating drives.
* **The Safety Flaws of LLMs:**
  * **Yann LeCun** explains that current LLMs are intrinsically unsafe and uncontrollable because they generate tokens auto-regressively without optimizing toward a structured objective or thinking ahead.
  * **Dr. Brian Keating** compares an LLM's stochastic nature to a toddler sitting on a lap randomly hitting a keyboard—chaotic, but ultimately lacking the power to launch major catastrophes unless humans build them irresponsibly.
* **Biological vs. Machine Drives:**
  * **Yann LeCun** emphasizes that human behavior is shaped by hardwired evolutionary objectives (such as the drive for 1-year-olds to stand up or newborn sucking reflexes) rather than pure cognitive compute.

---

#### **## Predictions & Forward Calls**

### The "Wall" of Generative AI and the Shift to World Models
* **LLM Limitations:** Yann LeCun notes that while LLMs excel at discrete symbols (text, code, math), they are "completely useless" for real-world signals and physical intuition.
    * **Predictive Failure:** LLMs use "auto-regressive prediction" (guessing the next token). This works for text but fails in video/physical reality because most world details (like the exact look of a person in a crowd) are unpredictable at the pixel level.
* **The JEPA Framework:** AMI Labs utilizes Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which avoids pixel-by-pixel generation.
    * **Abstract Representation:** Instead of "filling in the blanks" of a video, JEPA learns an abstract representation of a signal and ignores unpredictable details, focusing only on the underlying physics and logic.
    * **The "Cat" Benchmark:** LeCun points out that a cat has 800 million neurons (100x fewer than a human) but possesses far more common sense and physical intelligence than any current AI.

### AMI Labs: Strategy and Industrial Applications
* **Why AMI Labs Left Meta:** Despite support from Mark Zuckerberg, Meta’s internal focus shifted toward catching up in the LLM race. LeCun founded AMI Labs to pursue "Advanced Machine Intelligence" (AMI) independently.
    * **B2B vs. B2C:** Meta is a consumer-facing communication company, while World Models are best suited for industrial B2B applications.
* **Target Industries:**
    * **Complex Control:** Predicting and managing behavior in power plants, jet engines, pharmaceutical manufacturing, and human cell modeling.
    * **Defense and Robotics:** Moving beyond simple perception (image recognition) to "action planning"—allowing drones or robots to plan complex trajectories and manipulate physical objects reliably.
* **Global Footprint:** By design, the company has no presence in Silicon Valley or China, maintaining offices in Paris, New York, Montreal, and Singapore to ensure a global, independent perspective.

### AI Sovereignty and Project Tapestry
* **The Risk of Cultural Erosion:** LeCun warns that if AI assistants (the future "mediation" for all human information) are controlled by a few companies in the US or China, local cultures and languages will disappear.
* **Project Tapestry:** A proposed decentralized infrastructure for training foundation models.
    * **Sovereign Training:** Countries, universities, and entities can contribute local data and computing power to train a "global consensus model" without ever sharing their private data with a central server.
    * **Data Advantage:** This would allow models to be trained on vast amounts of non-public cultural and linguistic data (e.g., India's 22 official languages), potentially outperforming proprietary Western models.

### The Future of Physical AI
* **Data Bottleneck Myth:** LeCun dismisses the idea that we are running out of training data.
    * **Visual Wealth:** There are 20 trillion words of text on the public internet, but a four-year-old child sees as many "bytes" of visual data in their first few years as the largest LLM ever trained.
* **Achieving Level 5 Autonomy:** Current AI struggles with "common sense" tasks like inertia and gravity, which infants learn in nine months. Success in World Models is the prerequisite for Level 5 self-driving cars and domestic robots.
* **The European Opportunity:** While Europe "missed the boat" on the current LLM frontier, LeCun believes the race for Physical AI/World Models is wide open, providing an opportunity for Europe to lead through alternative research paths.

**TL;DR**
Yann LeCun argues that current Large Language Models (LLMs) are a technological dead-end for achieving human-level intelligence; the future lies in "World Models" and the JEPA architecture, which enable AI to plan, reason, and understand the physical world—a vision he is now pursuing through his new startup, AME Labs.

### The Limitations of LLMs and the Case for World Models
* **Technical Dead-end:** While LLMs are excellent for language manipulation and coding, LeCun insists they are not a path to human- or animal-level intelligence because they lack a fundamental understanding of the physical world.
    * **Autoregressive Issues:** LLMs predict the next token but cannot plan or reason through a sequence of actions to reach a goal.
    * **Data Inefficiency:** Humans (e.g., 17-year-olds learning to drive) require far less data than AI models because humans possess internal world models that predict consequences.
* **The JEPA Architecture:** LeCun pioneered the **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)** to move beyond "pixel-level" or "token-level" prediction.
    * **Abstract Representation:** Instead of generating every pixel or word, JEPA predicts the "representation" of a situation, allowing the model to ignore irrelevant noise and focus on high-level concepts.
    * **System 2 Intelligence:** Inspired by cognitive science, this approach aims to give AI "System 2" capabilities—deliberate, reflective behavior and planning—as opposed to the "System 1" instinctive reactions of LLMs.

### AME Labs: "AI for the Real World"
* **Startup Mission:** LeCun founded **AME Labs (Advanced Machine Intelligence)** to scale world models for industrial and physical applications that current tech giants are neglecting.
    * **Industry Focus:** Applications include manufacturing, power plant control, jet engine optimization, and healthcare (e.g., modeling cellular dynamics for chronic disease treatment).
    * **Robotics Gap:** LeCun argues that current robotics companies rely too heavily on imitation learning, which is brittle. World models would allow robots to solve new tasks "zero-shot" by anticipating the results of their actions.
* **Geographic Strategy:** The company is headquartered in Paris with a New York office, deliberately avoiding Silicon Valley’s "herd behavior" where most labs are "digging the same trench" with LLMs.

### Strategic Shifts and the Departure from Meta
* **FAIR’s Isolation:** LeCun reflects on his decade at Meta (formerly Facebook), noting that while FAIR (Fundamental AI Research) was once the center of innovation, it became increasingly isolated as the company pivoted to catch up in the LLM race.
    * **Product Pressure:** The creation of the "GenAI" organization shifted Meta’s focus toward short-term engineering goals (LLaMA-2, 3, 4) at the expense of exploratory blue-sky research.
    * **The "Alex Schultz" Factor:** LeCun suggests that the recent leadership shift at Meta, prioritizing product integration over open-ended research, made it clear that Meta was no longer the right place to build the next generation of AI.
* **Open Source Advocacy:** LeCun remains a staunch supporter of open source; he was the primary internal advocate for open-sourcing LLaMA-2, arguing that the safety risks were overblown and the benefits to the industry were paramount.

### Global AI Sovereignty and the Tapestry Project
* **Cultural Representation:** LeCun is concerned that a few AI assistants from California or China will dominate the global "information diet," potentially "brainwashing" users with Western or Chinese political and cultural biases.
* **The Tapestry Vision:** A proposed open, federated platform where different countries (India, France, etc.) contribute compute and parameter vectors without sharing their private data.
    * **Sovereign AI:** This would allow nations to build a "global consensus model" that can then be fine-tuned to reflect local languages, values, and political leanings.
    * **Linux Analogy:** LeCun compares current proprietary models (OpenAI, Anthropic) to the proprietary Unix systems of the 1990s, predicting they will eventually be "wiped out" by open-source infrastructure.

### Re-defining AI Safety
* **Intrinsically Unsafe LLMs:** LeCun argues LLMs cannot be made fully safe because they cannot predict the outcomes of their own actions, leading to hallucinations and unpredictable agentic behavior.
* **Objective-Driven AI:** The alternative is an architecture where safety is hard-coded as a cost function.
    * **Hard Constraints:** In a world model, a system is given an objective (e.g., "accomplish this task") and a set of constraints (e.g., "do not hurt anyone"). By construction, the system performs a search for actions that satisfy both, making it mathematically more controllable than a prompted LLM.

### TL;DR
Yann LeCun argues that current Large Language Models (LLMs) will never achieve human-level intelligence; instead, the future of AI lies in "World Models" that use Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) to understand physical reality and perform hierarchical planning.

### The Failure of the LLM Paradigm
- **Data Inefficiency:** Language data is insufficient for human-level intelligence.
  - LLMs are trained on roughly $10^{14}$ bytes of text, representing 400,000 years of reading.
  - A four-year-old child has processed $10^{14}$ bytes of visual data in just 16,000 hours of wake time.
- **Moravec’s Paradox:** AI can pass the bar exam but cannot perform basic physical tasks, such as clearing a dinner table or driving a car with the efficiency of a teenager (20 hours of practice).
- **The "Agentic" Flaw:** Current systems are not truly agentic because they cannot predict the consequences of their actions.
  - Scaling LLMs to reach human-level intelligence is described by LeCun as "complete BS."
  - Autoregressive token prediction is computationally inferior to inference via search and optimization.

### World Models and JEPA Architectures
- **Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA):** LeCun proposes moving away from generative models (which try to predict every pixel) toward joint embedding.
  - **Abstract Representations:** JEPAs predict the "state" of the world in an abstract space, ignoring unpredictable details (like the exact texture of a wall) to focus on relevant outcomes.
  - **Non-Collapsing Systems:** Tools like **SigReg** (developed with Brown faculty Randall Balestriero) help maximize information content and prevent the system from "collapsing" into useless, constant representations.
- **Hierarchical Planning:** Human intelligence relies on decomposing goals.
  - A trip from NYU to Paris involves subgoals (getting to the airport) and lower-level actions (hailing a taxi, walking).
  - Current AI cannot yet perform this type of multi-level planning; it remains a primary research frontier.
- **Inherent Safety:** Systems built on world models are intrinsically safer because they can use "guardrail objectives" during the planning phase to reject actions with dangerous predicted outcomes.

### Industry Impact and the Future of Ami Labs
- **Industrial Applications:** The first successful world models will likely operate "behind the curtain" in B2B sectors.
  - **Use Cases:** Optimizing jet engines, refineries, chemical plants, and drug production by predicting system states via thousands of sensors.
  - **Scientific Discovery:** AI can learn "phenomenological models" for complex systems that defy simple reductionist equations, such as discovering materials for more efficient catalysis or higher refractive indices.
- **The Open Source Advantage:** LeCun's new company, **Ami Labs**, intends to follow the open research model established at Meta’s FAIR.
  - Open source allows for "exploratory research" through resident PhD students and academic collaboration, which LeCun credits for the success of models like Llama.

### Guidance for Education and Career
- **Focus on Fundamentals:** Students should prioritize subjects with a long "shelf life."
  - **Core Recommendation:** Given a choice between mobile app programming and quantum mechanics, choose quantum mechanics.
  - Fundamental physics and math provide the framework for learning new technologies as they emerge.
- **Learning to Learn:** As technological progress accelerates, the ability to adapt to new situatons "zero-shot" is the ultimate competitive advantage for both humans and machines.
