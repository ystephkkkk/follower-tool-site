## The next infrastructure boom is in space (space architect Ariel Ekblaw & Reid Hoffman) | Summit '26

- Channel: Masters of Scale

- Published: 2026-11-23

- Speakers: Ariel Ekblaw (Space architect and CEO, Aurelia Institute); Reid Hoffman (Host, Masters of Scale)

---

**TL;DR**
Space architect Ariel Ekblaw and host Reid Hoffman argue that space has transitioned from science fiction to a near-term commercial market—just 250 miles up—designed to benefit life on Earth. Through self-assembling orbital infrastructure, microgravity biotech, and lunar resource extraction, humanity is entering a new era of in-space construction and exploration.

### Self-Assembling Infrastructure & Rendezvous Robotics
* **Breaking the Rocket Constraint:** Ariel Ekblaw explained that building in space has historically been limited by what fits inside a rocket payload fairing. Her MIT PhD research, **Tessera**, solves this using "smart Lego" tiles that self-assemble in orbit into massive structures like closed-shape buckyball habitats, large solar arrays, and potentially AI data centers.
* **Commercializing In-Space Construction:** Over the last 1.5 years, Ekblaw spun Tessera out of her nonprofit, the Aurelia Institute, into a for-profit startup called **Rendezvous Robotics**.
  * The company recently closed a successful seed round (to be announced the following week) to scale in-space self-assembly.
  * Target applications include massive-diameter telecom antennas for better ground communications and large-scale space-based solar power arrays that can beam clean energy to Earth even at night, solving green energy intermittency.

### Orbital BioLabs & Space for Earth
* **Space to Help Earth, Not Escape It:** Reid Hoffman emphasized the distinction between trying to escape Earth versus using space to improve life on Earth, noting his strong preference for the latter.
* **Microgravity Drug Formulation:** Ekblaw highlighted that biology behaves differently without gravity, enabling breakthroughs impossible on the ground:
  * **Merck's Keytruda:** Merck took its $30 billion cancer drug to space to grow higher-quality crystals, allowing the treatment to shift from a heavy IV infusion to a simple shot administered in a doctor's office—lowering costs and improving patient quality of life.
  * **Artificial Retinas:** Delicate retinal tissue can be manufactured in space without gravity causing it to sag.
* **A University Campus in Orbit:** Hoffman and Ekblaw have been collaborating on the concept of a space university, talking to potential founding donors.
  * With the International Space Station (ISS) decommissioning in **2030** and **4 to 5** credible commercial space station companies coming online to replace it, Ekblaw noted we are within a 10-year window where donors could name MIT's or Johns Hopkins' first building in space.
  * Near-term, this starts with an orbital biolab module for faculty and graduate students; long-term, it could enable study-abroad programs in orbit.

### The Lunar Economy & Space Tourism
* **Building a Commercial Ecosystem:** NASA's **Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)** program—contracting companies like **Intuitive Machines** and **Firefly**—is establishing a commercial "taxi to the moon," mirroring the SpaceX playbook for the ISS. Hoffman noted that building this commercial ecosystem is what makes lunar exploration sustainable compared to the Apollo era.
* **Helium-3 Extraction:** Ekblaw identified **helium-3** as the first major lunar resource that will benefit Earth. Measured only in kilograms on Earth, it is abundant on the moon (due to the lack of a magnetosphere) and is mission-critical for national security, MRIs, quantum computing, and fusion cooling.
* **Hospitality in Orbit:** Hoffman joked that a lunar resort (like an Aman) will inevitably follow, while Ekblaw noted that **Hilton DoubleTree** has already sent DoubleTree cookies to the ISS to test if they taste the same in space (astronauts approved).

### AI, Purpose, and Terrestrial Lessons
* **The Microgravity AI Dataset:** Ekblaw pointed to the intersection of AI and space as a major emerging market. Beyond orbital AI data centers, microgravity provides a rare, otherwise inaccessible dataset to inform synthetic protein generation and bioscience (connecting to Hoffman's work with Manas AI).
* **Expanding Human Purpose:** Beyond tactical spin-outs—like NASA JPL's CMOS sensors now used in smartphone cameras—Ekblaw argued that in an age of AI, space exploration preserves a uniquely human purpose: exploring and seeking new knowledge for its own sake.
* **Sci-Fi Influences:** Both speakers credited science fiction for inspiring planetary-scale thinking, citing Larry Niven's *Ringworld*, Isaac Asimov's *Foundation* trilogy (and its Apple TV adaptation), Neal Stephenson's *Seveneves* (which inspired Ekblaw's PhD work on biomimetic robot swarms), *Murderbot*, and *Ode to the Half-Broken*.

### Predictions & Forward Calls

* **Mainstream Space Tourism:** Within 15 to 20 years, traveling to low Earth orbit for a long weekend will be like commercial air travel. — *Ariel Ekblaw*
