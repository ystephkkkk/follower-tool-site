## OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar at Silicon Valley & The World | Semafor

- Channel: Semafor

- Speakers: Sarah Friar (CFO, OpenAI); Gina Chon (Executive Editor, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR:** OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar detailed the company's path to profitability and an eventual IPO, highlighting $122 billion in Q1 fundraising, a $1.25 trillion valuation in its latest opportunistic round, 1.2 billion weekly ChatGPT users, and a $1 billion advertising run rate achieved in under 200 days. To offset massive compute expenses, OpenAI is driving high-margin enterprise and ad revenue while building its own custom chip, Halapeno, to lower supply costs.

### Fundraising, Valuation, and IPO Strategy
* **Capitalization & Valuation:** OpenAI raised $122 billion in Q1 and is closing out an opportunistic fundraising round at a $1.25 trillion valuation.
  * Friar noted the latest round was unplanned and triggered by an inaccurate news story that generated investor inbounds, prompting OpenAI to execute a rapid, six-week raise ahead of the midterms.
* **Anthropic IPO & Market Access:** Responding to Semafor Executive Editor Gina Chon on Anthropic's upcoming IPO, Friar wished them well, calling it positive for the ecosystem because public market investors currently lack direct access to frontier model companies (having to invest in chips, infrastructure, or hyperscalers instead).
* **OpenAI IPO Criteria:** Friar views an IPO as a milestone toward OpenAI's mission of AGI for the benefit of humanity, which will also unlock debt-based financing and lower the overall cost of capital. She outlined four readiness criteria:
  * Business readiness (which she believes OpenAI has achieved).
  * Momentum (demonstrated by Q3 performance).
  * Market readiness.
  * Safety and alignment, noting that remaining private allows OpenAI to pace itself on safety work while staying well-capitalized.

### Consumer Scale, Advertising, and Search Growth
* **User & Subscriber Base:** ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion weekly users, adding 200 million users in Q3 alone, with over 100 million paying subscribers.
  * 90% of ChatGPT's customers are outside the U.S.
  * Weekly usage includes 300 million people asking health or wellness questions and 200 million asking personal finance questions, with interaction modes increasingly shifting toward voice.
* **Advertising Business:** With 90% of its user base on free tiers, OpenAI launched an advertising model that hit a $1 billion run rate in August—reaching that milestone in under 200 days, making it OpenAI's fastest-growing business.
  * Ads are now live in 87 countries, delivering high contextual and personal relevance.
* **Search Momentum:** ChatGPT now handles roughly 20% of the volume of all Google queries, up from approximately 11% in March.

### Enterprise Momentum and Product Roadmap
* **Enterprise Growth:** OpenAI's enterprise business grew 100% quarter-over-quarter in Q3 and is already a highly profitable model, supported by frontier APIs and a growing system integrator ecosystem (e.g., Capgemini).
* **Cybersecurity & Astros:** Cyber is a top priority for boards and C-suites. OpenAI released its most capable cyber model, **Astros** (branded as **Daybreak, Red and Blue**), which scores 100% on cyber benchmarks.
  * In Q3, OpenAI signed cyber-led deals with JP Morgan Chase and Visa, and expanded work with existing customer Amgen.
  * In August, OpenAI discovered two PHP vulnerabilities that, if left unpatched, could have taken down roughly 70% of the internet.
* **Agentic Coding & Virtual Coworkers ("Dots"):** 
  * Friar emphasized that coding has shifted to autonomous, agentic workflows, predicting that manual coding will soon feel like hand-knitting compared to using a machine.
  * OpenAI is rolling out **dots**—proactive virtual coworkers that sweep Slack and Gmail to prepare talking points and track tasks. After using dots for 6–8 weeks, Friar compared their necessity to Excel or email.
* **Vertical Expansion:** OpenAI is expanding into verticals including life sciences, manufacturing, the Office of the CLO, and the Office of the CFO.

### Compute Economics and Custom Silicon
* **Demand vs. Supply:** Addressing Chon's question on whether revenue can catch up to astronomical compute expenses, Friar stated that OpenAI has been short on compute in 2026 and must invest ahead of demand.
* **Custom Chip (Halapeno):** OpenAI is building its own chip, **Halapeno**, to enable "soup-to-nuts" self-builds.
  * This shrinks the timeline from demand signal to supply signal, eliminates third-party margin stacking, and strengthens OpenAI's negotiating leverage with hyperscalers.

### Public Trust, Foundation, and Community Engagement
* **OpenAI Foundation:** Created during last year's recapitalization, the OpenAI Foundation holds roughly $180 billion in value and is actively issuing grants in life sciences and the public good.
* **Georgia Data Center & Local Impact:** Responding to Chon on public distrust of AI, Friar stressed the need to show up in communities—citing OpenAI's potential ~3 gigawatt data center in Georgia.
  * **Water & Power:** A 1-gigawatt data center uses about two Olympic swimming pools' worth of water, which OpenAI will truck in and recirculate like a car radiator (with most water going to workers). OpenAI also pledged not to raise local electricity bills and is working with grid providers to potentially lower costs.
  * **Community Investment:** In Effingham County, Georgia, OpenAI sponsored free school lunches and breakfasts for all public school students (driving a 70% uptick in participation), sponsored a local park, and hosted an AI summit for small businesses.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AGI Multimodality:** "AGI will be multimodal. It will be LLMs, but it will be voice, it will be image." — Sarah Friar
* **Gross Margin Expansion:** Advertising will have "a really strong kind of upward push on gross margins overall" next year by covering the costs of free users. — Sarah Friar
* **Voice-Driven Interaction:** The mode of how consumers ask questions is turning to voice, which Friar identified as a key direction for the technology "over the next year or two." — Sarah Friar

## Former US Treasury Secretary Warns Of ‘Underestimating’ Risk Of AI Financial Crisis

- Channel: Semafor
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uUO4rn-ckqE
- Published: 2026-11-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261113.html#video-uUO4rn-ckqE
- Speakers: Hank Paulson (Former US Treasury Secretary); Rishi Sunak (Former UK Prime Minister); Gina Chon (Executive Editor, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR:** Former US Treasury Secretary Hank Paulson and former UK Prime Minister Rishi Sunak warned that while the US leads in AI innovation, China is winning the long game on distribution and domestic adoption—and the West is severely underestimating both the financial bubble risks and the potential for a catastrophic AI accident to trigger a market and regulatory collapse.

### The US-China AI Race: Innovation vs. Distribution
* **Bipolar Global Order:** Hank Paulson framed the current landscape as an intense competition between the US and China, each backed by different economic and political models competing for power, prestige, and economic advantage.
* **US Leads Innovation, China Wins Distribution:**
  * Paulson noted that the US remains the center of cutting-edge innovation, driven by companies like Nvidia, OpenAI, and Anthropic.
  * Recognizing they will not lead in frontier innovation, China is executing a long-game "Huawei strategy" focused on giving technology away, training users, and commercializing it through robotics and other products.
  * China is also winning on domestic adoption, whereas US companies face a competitive disadvantage due to the high cost of compute, chips, and enterprise software.

### Middle-Power Strategy, Sovereignty, and Public Trust
* **Three Pillars for Middle Powers:** Rishi Sunak outlined how countries outside the US and China should approach AI policy:
  * **Adoption and Diffusion:** Countries do not need to be at the frontier of innovation to use AI to benefit their economies and citizens.
  * **Public Trust:** Democratic societies cannot diffuse AI broadly unless citizens trust it is being deployed responsibly.
  * **Sovereignty:** Because middle powers cannot build an entire sovereign tech stack, they must rely on alliances (such as the UK's close relationship with the US) while diversifying options to maintain leverage.
* **Protecting National Champions:** Sunak noted that governments will increasingly scrutinize who invests in and controls key domestic firms—such as the UK's DeepMind and ElevenLabs—to preserve leverage in future technology supply chains.
* **The Western Trust Deficit:**
  * Public polling shows populations in India, the Gulf, and China are significantly more optimistic about AI than those in the US and Europe (where broader economic angst has fueled protests in France and Germany).
  * Sunak pointed out a silver lining: individuals' personal experiences with AI are much more positive than their abstract fears about its impact on society, giving governments and tech companies a foundation to build on through job-transition support and safety guardrails.

### Financial Bubble and Catastrophic Risk
* **Unprecedented Capital Concentration:** Drawing on his experience at Goldman Sachs and the US Treasury, Paulson warned he has never seen so much money chasing a single opportunity with so little earnings and ROI backing it up.
* **Underestimating Tail Risks:**
  * Paulson argued that investors and executives are brushing aside catastrophic risks, noting that multiple AI accidents have already occurred even as companies push forward to go public.
  * He warned that US capital markets—while the best in the world—were not built for the AI boom and lack the necessary checks and balances.
* **The Consequence of a Major Failure:** Both Paulson and Sunak agreed that a single catastrophic AI accident would trigger a severe public and regulatory overreaction that could shut down or drastically slow development.

### Governance, National Security, and US-China Dialogue
* **A Middle Way on Regulation:**
  * Sunak criticized the EU AI Act as overly complicated and an attempt to legislate every conceivable use case—a misstep he noted Mario Draghi has publicly acknowledged as Europe looks to roll parts of it back.
  * Instead, both speakers advocated for industry-government collaboration modeled on the President's Working Group on Capital Markets and Jay Clayton's Super Intelligence Task Force.
  * **Core Governance Mechanisms:** Sunak called for industry-defined common standards, independent third-party audits ("you can't have companies marking their own homework"), narrow focus on high-risk domains (cyber, bio, chemical), and financial-style incident reporting for near misses.
* **Lowered Barriers for Bad Actors:**
  * Gina Chon and Sunak highlighted that AI makes it cheaper and easier for non-state actors (such as the Houthis) and adversaries in ongoing conflicts (Ukraine, Iran) to cause catastrophic harm.
  * Sunak pointed to the UK's newly established AI threat assessment center and AI Security Institute as models for synthesizing intelligence and hardening critical infrastructure defenses.
* **US-China Safety Cooperation:**
  * Paulson revealed that an op-ed he wrote on existential AI risk reached China's Politburo and drew an official response, indicating that Chinese leaders share concerns about catastrophic AI risk.
  * He urged the US and China to establish a crisis hotline to contain potential catastrophic events, despite a massive bilateral trust deficit.
  * Sunak cited the UK's decision to invite China to the first AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park—where China engaged and signed the declaration—as proof that dialogue is worthwhile, while Paulson highlighted upcoming US-China AI talks led by Scott Bessent as an important first step.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Middle-Power Intervention:** "You're going to increasingly see middle powers, I think governments take a little bit more interest in those companies that they think can provide that national champion leverage for them in the technology supply chains of the future." — Rishi Sunak
* **Catastrophic Risk Shutdown:** "One catastrophic accident would shut it down." — Hank Paulson

### Notable Quotes
> "I've spent a lot of time looking at risk at Goldman Sachs and at Treasury, and I've never seen so much money chasing one opportunity with not a lot of earnings and ROI backing it up." — Hank Paulson

> "The problem people have with risk is never not identifying the risk, but it's not giving enough weight to the big risk." — Hank Paulson

> "You can't have companies marking their own homework. I think that the average person would think that's reasonable and it's good that people are talking about independent audits and evaluations." — Rishi Sunak

> "Whereas before you could keep a track of relatively large state actors who could cause you enormous amount of harm, this makes it much easier for a far greater number of smaller actors to do similar catastrophic damage potentially." — Rishi Sunak
