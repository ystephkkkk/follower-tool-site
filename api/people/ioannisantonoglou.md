## Reflection’s founders on building a DeepSeek of the West

- Channel: Sources Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BKnAeVej0dc
- Published: 2026-10-05
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261005.html#video-BKnAeVej0dc
- Speakers: Misha Laskin (CEO and Co-Founder, Reflection); Ioannis Antonoglou (CTO and Co-Founder, Reflection)

---

#### TL;DR
Reflection has raised $4.6 billion from investors like Nvidia, Sequoia, and Lightspeed, and is releasing **Beam**—a 500-billion total parameter (23-billion active) open-weight model trained completely from scratch—to establish a Western open frontier in AI. Co-founded by CEO Misha Laskin (former Gemini reward modeling lead) and CTO Ioannis Antonoglou (former AlphaGo/AlphaZero creator and Gemini RLHF lead), the company aims to provide enterprises and sovereign entities with proprietary control over their AI infrastructure and token economics.

---

### 1. Market Shift: From Rental to Ownership
- **The Real Estate Analogy for AI:** **Misha Laskin** compares the 3-year history of the commercial AI market to a rental real estate market where users "rent intelligence" via closed models. As startups scale into mature enterprises, demand has shifted toward ownership and control, which can only be achieved via open models.
- **Token Consumption Flip:** **Misha Laskin** notes that token consumption on platforms like Vercel and OpenRouter flipped over a 6-month period from approximately 70% closed / 30% open to roughly 70% open / 30% closed.
- **Capability Maturity:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** points out that open models have kept pace with closed models, closing the distance between the open and closed frontiers as the market matures.
- **Geopolitical Motivation in China:** **Misha Laskin** attributes the earlier dominance of Chinese labs in open-weights to a concentrated, geopolitically motivated push to kickstart an open-source ecosystem, catalyzed by the unexpectedly strong release of DeepSeek V3.

### 2. Beam: Architecture, Efficiency, and Training
- **Model Specs:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** and **Misha Laskin** describe **Beam** as a 500-billion total parameter model with 23 billion active parameters, built as a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture.
- **Key Capabilities:** **Misha Laskin** highlights that Beam is built as a workhorse model for enterprises and the public sector, excelling specifically in coding and agentic benchmarks.
- **Reasoning Efficiency:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** states that Beam achieves 3x to 4x greater reasoning and token efficiency compared to open frontier models like GLM 5.3 and closed models like GPT-6 Luna, measuring performance by how many tokens or compute are required to solve complex tasks.
- **Training from Scratch:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** emphasizes that Beam was built entirely from scratch in the West, avoiding distillation to ensure complete ownership of pre-training dynamics, router stability, and data ingestion.
- **Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** and **Misha Laskin** reveal that Beam’s training involved one of the largest openly documented RL runs in open source, utilizing roughly 10,000 H300 GPUs running continuously for weeks.

### 3. Safety, Alignment, and Openness
- **Linus' Law and Cybersecurity:** **Misha Laskin** argues that open-source software—including Linux, Android, and encryption protocols—is fundamentally safer due to Linus' law ("with enough eyeballs, all bugs become shallow"). He contrasts closed-lab safety teams of ~300 researchers against an open community of hundreds of thousands of inspectors.
- **Mitigating Unintended Consequences:** **Misha Laskin** cites an incident where Hugging Face needed to defend against a cyberattack but was blocked by closed-model safety guardrails, forcing them to rely on open models. He argues that an open ecosystem provides vital redundancy and defensive tools against unintended AI risks.
- **Alignment Methodology:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** explains that Reflection's technical safety pipeline mirrors closed labs: establishing rigorous evaluations across risk profiles, collecting behavioral examples, and applying supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning to balance safety and usefulness without over-refusal.
- **Capability Thresholds:** **Misha Laskin** and **Ioannis Antonoglou** acknowledge that if models reach a critical capability threshold, regulatory policies governing deployment and gating may be necessary—though they maintain that safety rules should apply based on capability rather than whether a model is open or closed.

### 4. Business Model, Commercial Strategy, and Sovereign Control
- **Open as a Demand Driver:** **Misha Laskin** compares open models to Kubernetes, explaining that open-weights serve as a demand driver for full-stack infrastructure, cluster management, inference serving, and agent deployment.
- **The Intelligence Partner Playbook:** **Ioannis Antonoglou** notes that enterprises, public sector organizations, and allied nations view AI as labor that cannot be outsourced. Reflection positions itself as an "intelligence partner" that externalizes battle-tested serving and infrastructure software.
- **Commercial Partnerships:** 
  - **Dell:** **Misha Laskin** discusses infrastructure and compute deployment partnerships, such as a 250-megawatt data center project in South Korea.
  - **Compute Providers:** Reflection has secured major compute backing, including deals cited in the context of Nebius and SpaceX.
- **Multipolarity & Trust:** **Misha Laskin** summarizes Reflection's revenue equation as: *Intelligence Density × Compute Volume × Enterprise Trust*, arguing that the market is large enough to support a multipolar ecosystem of multiple major intelligence providers rather than a centralized monopoly.
