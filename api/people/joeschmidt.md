## Why the Next Generation of Enterprise Software Looks Nothing Like Salesforce

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K5yGLO8c6T0
- Published: 2026-09-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260916.html#video-K5yGLO8c6T0
- Speakers: Alex Rampell (General Partner, a16z); Joe Schmidt (Partner, a16z); Keith Peiris (Co-founder and CEO, Lightfield)

---

### TL;DR
Keith Peiris shares the story of how his team abandoned Tome (an AI presentation tool with 25 million users) after realizing it lacked the context to serve as an indispensable business tool, ultimately pivoting to found Lightfield—an AI-native CRM and "business world model" that recently raised a $47 million Series A led by a16z.

### The Tome Journey & The Hard Pivot
* **The Tome Success & Limitations:** 
  * Started Tome with three former Facebook founders out of five founding members, targeting idea storytelling and presentations.
  * Reached 2 million users a month around the launch of GPT-3 and ChatGPT.
  * Ultimately decided to walk away because the technology was constrained to individuals and students; the model lacked context regarding the presenter, the audience, and their relationship.
* **Transitioning to B2B:** 
  * Followed B2B sales and marketing users from Tome, running free pilots with twelve 500-to-1000-person companies.
  * Realized that while customers wanted pitch decks, they also needed help with research, lead qualification, and company expansion.
  * Discovered that the hardest underlying problem was making sense of incomplete, conflicting data scattered across disparate systems (CRMs, call recorders, data warehouses).

### Building Lightfield & Reimagining the CRM
* **Negative Pricing Strategy:** 
  * After initial go-to-market assistant software failed to gain pricing power, the team built a CRM in the dark for 4 months.
  * Offered free office space in exchange for using their early CRM, attracting 10 startups that provided hourly feedback via Slack.
* **The "Business World Model" Architecture:** 
  * Modeled the relationship chronology first—building an activity log (emails, calls, meetings, documents, product usage, payments) rather than forcing manual data entry into rigid schemas.
  * Employs a semi-structured approach: storing vast amounts of unstructured data in the activity log, allowing AI to infer causality and power CRM updates, field filling, and workflows.
* **Real-World Impact:** 
  * Customer *Power* uses Lightfield to aggregate patients with illnesses (like Alzheimer's) and match them with frontier clinical trials and pharmaceutical companies by scraping the FDA and clinicaltrials.gov.

### Greenfield vs. Brownfield & Enterprise Strategy
* **Navigating the Brownfield:** 
  * Initially targeted new companies via LinkedIn prospecting to YC companies, growing with startups from 0 reps to 100 reps.
  * Avoided superficial features like basic email sending (which incumbents like Salesforce easily replicate), focusing instead on high-fidelity business modeling and company reality.
* **Overcoming the "Seasoned VP of Sales" Objection:** 
  * Offered the product for free across entire greenfield companies (engineering, customer support, finance, sales).
  * Created internal network effects where departments outside of sales rely on Lightfield, making it harder to rip and replace when a traditional, Salesforce-trained VP of Sales is eventually hired.

### Pragmatic Product Design & Flexible Pricing
* **Pragmatic UI Overdogmatism:** 
  * Blends natural language agents with traditional spreadsheet, table, and dashboard views because users still rely on familiar, deterministic workflows while transitioning to agentic workflows (e.g., chat-based sequences replacing complex trigger-and-arrow rule builders).
* **The Pricing Model:** 
  * Moved away from pure seat pricing (where power users consumed 10,000x more than others) and pure consumption pricing (where users hesitated to touch anything).
  * Landed on a **platform fee plus seat pricing** for core CRM workflows, combined with **consumption pricing** for pipeline generation, enrichment, workflow automations, and intelligence/forecasting.

### Company Culture, Speed, and Pivoting
* **Generalist-First Culture:** 
  * Rebuilt company operations to eliminate siloed "swim lanes" and playing house; with ~40 employees, everyone attends daily standups to stack-rank and tackle the most important problems (delivery, engineering, or customer success).
* **Continuous Planning:** 
  * Maintains a low bar for starting projects but a high bar for shipping (using company-wide bug bashes inspired by Instagram).
* **Advice on Pivoting:** 
  * Ignore outside noise, focus maniacally on customer pain, and be willing to completely abandon past work to follow the heat.

## Choosing Your Sales Strategy: Lighthouse vs. Landgrab

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aakZLqxRQfo
- Published: 2026-08-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260813.html#video-aakZLqxRQfo
- Speakers: Elena Burger (Investor, a16z); Andy McCall (Operating Partner, a16z); Joe Schmidt (Operating Partner, a16z)

---

**TL;DR:** AI startups facing go-to-market decisions must choose between a "Lighthouse" strategy (winning high-exposure, regulated marquee accounts for social proof) and a "Landgrab" strategy (rapidly deploying to mid-market buyers where math and existing budgets drive adoption). Founders should spend 99% of their time executing rather than over-analyzing strategy.

### The Lighthouse vs. Landgrab Framework

*   **Defining the Matrix:** 
    *   Co-host Joe Schmidt explains the 2x2 framework using Y-axis buyer exposure/risk and X-axis proof traveling in the market.
    *   **Lighthouse Model (Top Right):** High buyer exposure, proof travels. Fit for regulated industries with limited logos where a bad purchase carries legal or operational risk. Relies on social proof and missionary work (e.g., Harvey in legal, Further AI in insurance).
    *   **Landgrab Model (Bottom Left):** Low buyer exposure, low proof traveling. Fit for markets with established budgets where startups can show direct workflow math and ROI (e.g., Stu in accounts receivable, Pylon in customer support).
*   **The SFO/101 Freeway Fallacy:** 
    *   Founders frequently fall into the trap of targeting identical marquee logos in San Francisco or New York because it feels prestigious.
    *   Founders should be willing to pick up the phone and sell in markets like Ohio, Chicago, or St. Louis where customers actually need solutions.

### Historical Paragons: Samsara and Cisco Meraki

*   **Samsara & The ELD Mandate (Andy McCall):** 
    *   Founded in 2015; Andy McCall joined in 2017. 
    *   Rode the tailwind of the 2016 US federal Electronic Logging Devices (ELD) mandate for long-haul trucking.
    *   **Mid-Market Focus:** Instead of chasing giant, established telematics players (AT&T, Verizon) or top-tier trucking giants for social proof, Samsara targeted the mid-market for fast sales cycles and rapid product feedback loops.
*   **Cisco Meraki & Cloud Networking:** 
    *   Founded in 2006 out of an MIT research project (RoofNet); acquired by Cisco in 2012 for its cloud-managed Wi-Fi architecture.
    *   Executed a land-grab strategy against Cisco and HP by targeting the mid-market, which lacked trained IT command-line staff.
    *   **GTM Trial Program:** Used webinars to give away free access points, driving adoption by letting customers experience how much simpler the product was firsthand.

### Modern AI GTM Dynamics and POC Discipline

*   **The Current AI Window:** 
    *   Joe Schmidt notes that enterprise boards everywhere are mandating AI adoption, creating a rare window to sell "big software" and platforms again, as opposed to pure PLG (Product-Led Growth) wedge products.
*   **Proof of Concept (POC) Discipline (Andy McCall):** 
    *   **Strict Timeboxes:** POCs must have a hard end date (e.g., 30, 45, or 60 days) to prevent them from turning into endless science projects.
    *   **Upfront Success Criteria:** Both parties must explicitly define and agree upon what success looks like before starting the trial.

### Evolution from Landgrab to Lighthouse

*   **Sequential Strategies:** 
    *   Successful companies often deploy both strategies over time. Both Samsara and Meraki started with landgrab motions before maturing into lighthouse strategies by verticalizing into large enterprise segments like public sector and school districts.
*   **Team and Sales Profiles:** 
    *   **Lighthouse Sellers:** Seasoned enterprise reps who understand complex sales cycles, high-exposure accounts, and formal procurement.
    *   **Landgrab Sellers:** Aggressive reps hired for attitude, aptitude, and high-velocity deal stacking.

### Tactical Advice for Founders and Sellers

*   **The Execution Rule:** Spend 1% of time on GTM strategy and 99% on execution. There are no bonus points or revenue multipliers for hard-earned enterprise logos.
*   **Early Hires:** Andy McCall advises hiring for **Sales Operations** earlier than most startups do—even just a single person to handle territory alignment, name lists, and sales guardrails to prevent future speed bumps.
*   **Quota Realism:** Early-stage sales teams run on momentum. Founders should set attainable quotas that allow top sales talent to win; if only 40–50% of the team hits quota, quotas are likely too high or hiring profiles are off.
*   **Career Advice for Sellers:** Instead of chasing short-term title inflation or peak commission rates, early-career sellers should focus entirely on finding and joining the best growth-stage company.
