## Private Markets and The Future of Capital Allocation with Marc Rowan | The a16z Show

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oYK_MmZW9XI
- Published: 2026-05-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260527.html#video-oYK_MmZW9XI
- Speakers: Marc Rowan (Cofounder, CEO, and Chair of Apollo Global Management); David Haber (Host, The a16z Show)

---

**TL;DR:** As public markets face extreme concentration, Apollo Global Management is scaling past $1 trillion in assets by shifting from traditional private equity to investment-grade private credit—bridging long-term retirement liabilities with the massive, capital-intensive infrastructure demands of AI, energy, and robotics.

### The Evolution of Apollo: From Private Equity to Investment-Grade Credit

* **Asset Composition Shift:** Contrary to public perception, Apollo Global Management is no longer primarily a private equity firm. 
    * **Credit Dominance:** Of Apollo's $1+ trillion under management, 80% is in credit (mostly investment grade), 10% is in hybrid equity, and only 10% is in traditional private equity.
    * **Dual Economic Engine:** The business is anchored by its asset management arm and Athene, its retirement services business, making Apollo the world’s largest provider of retirement income.
* **Public Market Concentration Risks:** Marc Rowan (Cofounder, CEO, and Chair of Apollo) warns that the public markets no longer offer adequate diversification.
    * **S&P Concentration:** Just 10 stocks now comprise nearly 50% of the S&P 500, all leveraged to the same tech trends.
    * **Fixed Income Concentration:** The global fixed income market is undergoing a similar shift, soon to be dominated by just five large banks and five large technology companies.
    * **Private Market Necessity:** Private markets represent 80% of global economic activity, meaning investors must look to private assets for genuine diversification.

### The Infrastructure Boom: Bridging Venture Tech and Private Credit

* **The Shift to Capital-Intensive Tech:** The paradigm of capital-light software companies is shifting as breakthroughs in AI, defense, robotics, and energy require unprecedented physical infrastructure.
    * **Unprecedented Capex:** Just four public technology companies are projected to spend $800 billion in capital expenditures on chips, data centers, and energy transmission.
    * **Limits of Venture Equity:** Venture equity cannot efficiently fund hardware deployment at this scale. Capital must be partitioned, offloading hard, reusable infrastructure assets (like data centers and equipment) into the credit markets.
* **Opportunities in Robotics and Equipment:** Automation is transitioning from proof-of-concept to real-world industrial scale.
    * **Asset-Backed Financing:** Technologies like construction robotics will be funded via equipment rental structures and credit markets, using a much lower cost of capital than venture equity.
* **Apollo’s West Coast Expansion:** To better align with the growth and challenger ecosystem, Apollo is establishing a second headquarters to tap into technology talent and build direct partnerships with tech innovators.

### AI Disruption and the "SaaS-pocalypse"

* **Enterprise Software Devaluation:** Rowan argues that AI is rapidly commoditizing coding and software creation, turning historical software moats into liabilities.
    * **Overvalued PE Portfolios:** Roughly 30% of the private equity industry's capital over the past decade has been invested in enterprise software. 
    * **Declining PE Returns:** Rowan predicts "disastrous" returns for existing software-heavy PE portfolios because acquisition valuations did not account for AI-driven margin compression and new challenger competitors.
* **Rethinking Credit Collateral:** As a lender, Apollo evaluates credit on a 3-to-7-year horizon rather than a 20-to-30-year timeframe, staying highly diversified and paranoid about technological replacement risks (reminiscent of how the internet quickly replaced historically safe assets like yellow pages and cable TV).

### Democratization of Private Markets and Apollo’s Culture

* **Democratizing Credit Assets:** To serve individual investors, 401(k) plans, and retail wealth, private credit must conform to public market expectations.
    * **Daily Valuation Rollout:** Apollo is transitioning its investment-grade private credit suite to daily estimated values, standardized CUSIPs, and market-making liquidity by June 30th, expanding across its entire credit business by September.
* **Culture of "Playing to Win":** Rowan outlines key tenets from Apollo's internal cultural diagnostic:
    * **Clean-Sheet Thinking:** Inherited from Rowan’s formative years at Drexel Burnham under Michael Milken, this principles-first approach prioritizes building custom solutions over relying on industry norms.
    * **Fail Fast, Fix Fast:** Apollo maintains a "wall of shame" where senior professionals are transparent about lost investments, normalizing risk-taking and rapid course correction.
    * **Meritocracy Over Immutable Characteristics:** Rowan rejects traditional DEI metrics in favor of hiring based on "merit, adjusted for distance traveled"—evaluating the individual obstacles a candidate has personally overcome.
* **Moral Leadership:** Addressing his high-profile opposition to antisemitism at his alma mater (the University of Pennsylvania), Rowan asserts that business leaders should not remain silent on fundamental moral principles, choosing "right over easy."

### Notable Quotes

> "You either accept change or change is visited upon you." — Marc Rowan (quoting Michael Milken)

> "I personally expect the returns from private equity in the ground, to be disastrous, because so much of exposure is to enterprise software... The price that they paid reflected a future that did not have AI in it." — Marc Rowan

> "We hire for merit, adjusted for distance traveled... Show me the kid, show me the individual who's had to overcome something and still achieved. That's who I want." — Marc Rowan

## Apollo's Rowan Puts Current Credit Cycle Into Context

- Channel: Bloomberg Live
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BiQg5c8h16k
- Published: 2026-03-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260303.html#video-BiQg5c8h16k
- Speakers: Marc Rowan (CEO, Apollo Global Management); John Micklethwait (Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR:** Apollo is pivoting from its "buccaneering" roots toward investment-grade private credit to fund a "global industrial renaissance," arguing that moving credit risk from bank balance sheets to private investors has made the global financial system more resilient.

### Macroeconomic Outlook and Geopolitical Risks
* **The 70/30 Risk Split:** Marc Rowan notes that while 70% of the economic picture is positive (strong employment, high capital spending, open markets), the remaining 30% consists of unpredictable "disruptors" including geopolitics, government borrowing, and rapid technological change.
* **Fiscal Accountability:** Rowan warns that Western governments are spending beyond their means. While Japan sustained this for decades, he points to the UK’s "Liz Truss moment" as a template for how markets may eventually hold governments accountable for fiscal excesses.
* **Inflationary Pressures:** 
    * **Watch Items:** The restriction of labor and goods is fundamentally inflationary, though markets haven't fully priced this in yet.
    * **The "Skunk" at the Party:** Persistent inflation would handicap the Federal Reserve's ability to lower rates, potentially ending a long accommodative cycle.

### The "Global Industrial Renaissance" and Sector Strategy
* **Massive Capital Demand:** Rowan highlights a "global industrial renaissance" driven by energy transition, next-gen manufacturing, defense ramping, and AI infrastructure. 
    * **Scale of Spending:** He describes the current investment surge in data centers and energy as the world "spending every dollar since the creation of fire... all at once."
* **Software Caution:** Apollo holds **zero software** in its private equity portfolio and has minimal exposure in credit. 
    * **AI Threat:** Rowan argues that AI's impact on software is a fundamental risk that many leveraged lenders ignored until recently. 
    * **Market Concentration:** Software represents 30% of the leveraged buyout market, making it over-represented and vulnerable to technological disruption.
* **Infrastructure Bullishness:** Apollo prefers lending to diversified tech giants (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) for digital infrastructure rather than "equity-story" startups, provided they offer sensible underwriting.

### The Evolution of Private Credit
* **De-risking the Economy:** Rowan argues that the migration of credit from bank balance sheets to private markets is a successful post-2008 regulatory outcome. 
    * **Socialized Risk:** By moving credit into BDCs and insurance portfolios, the risk is distributed among investors who can price it, rather than sitting on government-guaranteed bank balance sheets.
* **The Investment Grade Shift:** While media focuses on the $1.5 trillion "leveraged lending" market, Rowan emphasizes that the vast majority of private credit is actually **investment grade.**
    * **Apollo’s Scale:** Out of $300 billion in originations last year, 80% was investment grade.
    * **Creativity in Credit:** Rowan rejects the idea that investment-grade lending is boring, noting it requires more creativity and scale than traditional "buccaneering" deal-making.

### Firm Strategy and Industry Consolidation
* **Growth Constraints:** Rowan asserts that Apollo's growth is limited by its capacity to **originate good risk**, not by its ability to raise capital. 
    * Over-expansion leads to "commoditizing" the business and taking on bad risks.
* **The Coming Shakeout:** A "reckoning" is expected for firms that failed to manage risk or over-concentrated in sectors like software. 
    * **The Winners:** Firms with "fortress balance sheets" and disciplined underwriting will gain market share, similar to how dominant banks emerged after the GFC.
* **Solving the Retirement Crisis:** Rowan positions Apollo as a bridge between the global need for retirement income and the massive capital requirements of industrial companies. 
    * **Critique of 401ks:** He notes that the current retirement system is overly concentrated, joking that the US has "levered the entirety of the retirement system... to Nvidia."

> "We're essentially spending every dollar since the creation of fire and we're doing it all at once... We will see the largest need for capital ever." — Marc Rowan

> "The formation of credit particularly in these private BDCs is a de-risking activity for the economy... it has moved it out of the levered government-guaranteed portion of our economy." — Marc Rowan

> "Public is not safe or risky, private is not safe or risky, they're both safe or risky—they're just different degrees of liquidity." — Marc Rowan
