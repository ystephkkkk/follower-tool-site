## World Development Report 2026: Fireside Chat with Andrew Ng - Making AI Work for Development

- Channel: Stanford Graduate School of Business
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WmgPAOIhrko
- Published: 2026-10-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261007.html#video-WmgPAOIhrko
- Speakers: Andrew Ng (Founder, DeepLearning.AI)

---

### TL;DR
Artificial intelligence offers developing economies a powerful path to prosperity and leapfrogging, provided they embrace open-source models, upskill their workforces, and avoid stifling, fear-driven regulations.

### The AI Fear Cycle and Regulatory Capture
* **Lobbying and Stifling Regulations:** **Andrew Ng** noted that well-funded lobbying campaigns by large firms that spent billions on proprietary models attempt to push fears of human extinction to stifle open-source and open-weight competitors.
* **Flawed Analogies:** **Andrew Ng** criticized irresponsible comparisons between AI and nuclear weapons, pointing out that AI does not possess dangerous capabilities leading to human extinction.
* **The OpenAI Hugging Face Incident:** **Andrew Ng** explained that recent fear-mongering was fueled by an OpenAI agent swarm hacking Hugging Face—an event enabled by poor internal security (bugs in sandboxing) and weak monitoring, involving around 1,200 standard compute processes rather than an uncontrollable existential threat.
* **Infrastructure Benefits:** **Andrew Ng** emphasized that data centers are highly efficient, and shifting dirty on-premise workloads to modern data centers is environmentally beneficial.

### Open-Source Models and Developing Economies
* **Democratizing Access:** **Andrew Ng** and **Susan Athey** highlighted that open-source and open-weight models provide affordable access for individuals, businesses, and governments in developing countries with limited resources.
* **Sovereign AI and Security:** **Susan Athey** and **Andrew Ng** noted that open models prevent dependency on foreign tech stacks, ensuring that nations and economies cannot have a "kill switch" pulled by another country.
* **Academic Research & Local Adaptation:** **Andrew Ng** stressed that universities and research groups can download open-weight models to study training recipes and adapt them to regional dialects and local data that American frontier labs lack.
* **Low-Cost Research:** **Susan Athey** noted that research labs can perform tasks like text classification using cloud services (e.g., Together.ai) for as little as $25.

### Software Engineering and Workforce Transformations
* **Rising Demand for AI-Skilled Engineers:** **Andrew Ng** reported that software engineering job postings are up because AI makes engineers more productive, though old coding skill sets from pre-2022 are obsolete.
* **Task-Based Job Impact:** Referencing **Erik Brynjolfsson**, **Andrew Ng** explained that while AI may automate 30% to 40% of tasks in many jobs, the remaining 60% requiring human judgment becomes significantly more valuable.
* **The Global Job Market:** **Andrew Ng** expressed optimism about global job markets if workers are upskilled to become "AI-native."

### AI Limitations: Verifiable vs. Unverifiable Tasks
* **The Verifiable Task Gap:** **Andrew Ng** explained that AI excels at *verifiable tasks* (coding, math theorems, factual answers) because reinforcement learning allows AI to check its own work.
* **The Limits of Judgment:** **Andrew Ng** warned that AI struggles significantly more with *unverifiable tasks* requiring human judgment and broad contextual knowledge (e.g., strategic marketing decisions, brainstorming nuances).
* **The "AI Tells":** **Andrew Ng** and **Susan Athey** cautioned that current AI-generated text carries distinctive "tells" that experienced readers instantly spot, though less-experienced users may mistake them for natural speech.

### Education and the Pitfalls of Cognitive Offloading
* **The Chatbot Learning Trap:** **Andrew Ng** stated that "chat is terrible for learning" because students using chatbots for homework engage in cognitive offloading, raising homework scores while lowering final exam performance and long-term retention.
* **Scaling Education:** **Andrew Ng** noted that platforms like Coursera and LearnVector help universities with few AI faculty teach cutting-edge skills through online formats, with a future goal of moving from one-to-many to customized one-to-one AI tutoring.
* **Underinvestment in Training:** **Andrew Ng** observed that individuals, businesses, and nations universally underinvest in training and education relative to its profound long-term economic return.

### Policy Recommendations for Developing Nations
* **Support Open-Weight Models:** Governments should back open-weight ecosystems to maintain national sovereignty and control over their technological future.
* **Invest in Upskilling:** Nations must train citizens to become AI-native to seize economic opportunities and potentially leapfrog older developmental stages.
* **Drive National and Industrial Adoption:** Policy leaders should encourage domestic sectors (such as local agriculture, manufacturing, or government automation like Singapore's model) to integrate AI into workflows where Silicon Valley has less presence.

> "I wake up in the morning, put on my clothes, go to work, and I don't seem to worry about AI killing me off."
> — Andrew Ng

> "At universities, all of us professors, this is actually very clear, but the rigorous academic studies are also coming out to demonstrate this, that when students use chatbots for homeworks, their homework scores go up. But their final exam scores or measures of long-term learning go down."
> — Andrew Ng

> "If a country wants to maintain control over its own AI, it turns out for a country to develop its own AI system from scratch is difficult, expensive, takes a lot of resources. But if a country or if a community of nations collectively supports open-source, open-weight models, then maybe no one needs to own that open-source, open-weight model."
> — Andrew Ng


## Andrew Ng and David S. Goyer: Creativity, Intelligence, and What We’re building Toward.

- Channel: Berggruen Institute
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J4CjiTtggfY
- Published: 2026-07-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260720.html#video-J4CjiTtggfY
- Speakers: Andrew Ng (AI Researcher and Founder of DeepLearning.AI); David S. Goyer (Screenwriter and Film Director)

---

### TL;DR
AI integration in creative and technological industries requires keeping "humans in the lead," balancing open-weight innovation against closed gatekeeping, and separating media hype from the practical realities of automation and software development.

### AI Narratives, Hype, and the Labor Market
* **The Hype Cycle vs. Reality:** **Andrew Ng** asserts that tech companies historically stoked extinction and job-loss fears as PR stunts to inflate valuations. Controlled lab tests (like AI blackmail scenarios) are exaggerated by media in ways analogous to testing an airplane in a hurricane.
* **The 1.4% Employment Reality:** Citing a workforce management CEO's survey on recent layoffs, **Andrew Ng** notes that only **1.4%** of recently laid-off workers were actually replaced by AI. 
* **Task Automation:** Studies show AI can automate **30% to 40%** of discrete tasks in many jobs, but the remaining **60% to 70%** requires human execution, historically leading to higher wages and complementary job growth (similar to horses transitioning to cars).
* **The 15-Year-Old Skepticism:** **David S. Goyer** points out that many young people (including his 15-year-old son) are violently opposed to AI out of fear of being squeezed out of jobs, leading to commencement booing and career self-selection away from tech.

### Open Weights vs. Proprietary Gatekeepers
* **Defining Open Weights:** **Andrew Ng** explains that open-weight models publish the foundational list of numerical weights on the internet for free download (allowing local execution on personal laptops), whereas closed-weight models (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google Gemini) hide the weights behind a proprietary API.
* **China's Open-Weight Strategy:** **Andrew Ng** notes that while U.S. models lead in closed-weight capabilities, China leads in open-weight models. Nations in Africa and elsewhere widely adopt Chinese models over American ones, which serves as a massive vehicle for foreign soft power and ideological influence (e.g., answering questions about Tiananmen Square in 1989).
* **The Danger of Gatekeepers:** **Andrew Ng** warns that if AI concentrates in the hands of a very small number of gatekeepers (analogous to Android and iOS in mobile), everyone except the gatekeepers will be worse off.

### Hollywood, Creativity, and Workflow Integration
* **Human in the Lead vs. Loop:** **David S. Goyer** stresses that creators must remain "human in the lead" rather than outsourcing creativity. Using basic prompts yields clichéd slop, but iterative workflows yield powerful results.
* **Goyer's Production Workflow:** **David S. Goyer** shares that on projects like *Foundation* Season 2, he and his visual effects supervisor used **Midjourney** early on for wild, unexpected concept design iterations before handing concepts to human artists. In writing, he uses AI to shorten a four-page monologue down to a two-page version across six variations, or to brainstorm cabin items for a high-end commercial helicopter action sequence, reducing feature draft times from **8 to 10 weeks** down to **4 to 5 weeks**.
* **Hollywood's Negative Narratives:** **Andrew Ng** laments that Hollywood produces predominantly negative, terminator-style AI stories, which discourages students from pursuing AI careers—contrasting it with positive entrepreneurial narratives promoted in China.

### Copyright, Bias, and Data Distortion
* **Plato’s Cave and Internet Distortion:** **David S. Goyer** raises concerns that models are trained on a digitized internet that is a distorted, biased representation of reality. **Andrew Ng** acknowledges this bias but counters that AI teams actively squash racist and sexist text, making current models *less* biased than the average human being.
* **The Copyright Lawsuit:** **David S. Goyer** notes he was part of a copyright lawsuit against Anthropic for using digitized books without permission, which he defines as crossing a distinct line compared to human learning. **Andrew Ng** compares AI learning from public internet pages to young artists copying old masters at museums, though he acknowledges long-term economic compensation models for publishers still need to be resolved.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Andrew Ng** predicts that higher education's process for adapting curricula is too slow, leaving universities training students for the jobs of 2022 rather than 2028 or 2030 and beyond.
* **David S. Goyer** predicts that entertainment will permanently split into two tiers: content that is barely "human in the loop" versus content where humans remain firmly "in the lead."

### Notable Quotes
> *"The funny thing that happened was a handful of companies decided that stoking fear about AI was wonderful PR because it turns out that if you go around and say my technology is so powerful, I could cause the extinction of the human race. That turns out to be one way to convince people your technology is really powerful and therefore must be worth a lot."* — **Andrew Ng**

> *"You can't outsource your creativity if you want to create anything of any note... I think we're quickly moving to a place... where there's really going to be two tiers of art. There's going to be the stuff that's basically just barely human in the loop, and then there's going to be stuff that's human in the lead."* — **David S. Goyer**

> *"When someone asks a question of AI, such as what happened in Tiananmen Square in 1989? Well, whose model they are using will cause the model to give an answer that reflects that nation's values. So getting your stuff out there is a tremendous source of soft power influence."* — **Andrew Ng**
