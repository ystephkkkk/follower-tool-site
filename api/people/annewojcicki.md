## Why 23andMe Is Betting Big On An Open Science Model

- Channel: Forbes
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xdBft7vW8Tw
- Published: 2026-07-09
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260709.html#video-xdBft7vW8Tw
- Speakers: Anne Wojcicki (CEO and Founder, 23andMe); Maneet Ahuja (Host, Iconoclast)

---

**TL;DR**
23andMe has transitioned into a nonprofit research institute to decouple genetic data from profit incentives, leveraging its 14-million-person "citizen-powered" database to bypass traditional clinical trial bottlenecks and accelerate preventative medicine through an open-science model.

### The Shift to a Nonprofit "Open Science" Model
* **Systemic Flaws in Healthcare:** CEO Anne Wojcicki founded 23andMe after a decade on Wall Street (1996–2006), observing that the healthcare system is structured to monetize illness rather than reward prevention. 
    * **The Prevention Gap:** Wojcicki notes that while hundreds of companies spend $300 billion on type 2 diabetes treatments, the CDC’s prevention program historically received only about $10 million.
* **The Transition to Nonprofit:** Following a difficult period as a public company—including a "devastating" bankruptcy and a major cyber incident—Wojcicki moved the company to a 501(c)(3) structure to "cement the mission."
    * **Incentive Alignment:** As a nonprofit, the company prioritizes mission impact over profit, allowing it to act as an "honest broker" of data without the pressure to sell "snake oil" or use ad-driven models.
* **Funding the Future:** The institute is currently raising a $100 million philanthropic campaign to support operations over the next four years, aiming to provide academic and nonprofit researchers with low-cost or free access to its massive datasets.

### Citizen-Powered Research at Scale
* **Unprecedented Data Size:** With over 14 million customers—90% of whom have consented to research—23andMe possesses a dataset significantly larger than major state-funded initiatives like the UK Biobank (500,000 participants) or the U.S. "All of Us" program (1 million).
* **Democratizing Clinical Trials:** Traditional research relies on "flyers at hospitals," which is slow and inefficient. 23andMe uses digital surveys to rapidly assemble cohorts.
    * **GLP-1 Study:** The team recently published a Nature study on the genetics of GLP-1 response using data from nearly 28,000 participants.
    * **Autoimmune AI:** The company developed an AI model to predict 18 autoimmune conditions based on data from 5 million people.
* **The "Escapers" Study:** One of the company's most unique research areas focuses on "escapers"—individuals who are genetically high-risk for diseases like Alzheimer’s or Cystic Fibrosis but never develop them. These individuals may hold the biological keys to new preventative treatments.

### Patient Agency and Advocacy
* **A Legacy of Self-Advocacy:** Wojcicki credits her mother’s medical advocacy—born from the tragic loss of a young brother due to a doctor's dismissal—as the foundation for 23andMe’s focus on the individual.
* **Challenging the "Incompetent Patient" Myth:** Wojcicki argues that the healthcare establishment often operates on the assumption that patients are too "incompetent" to understand their own data or change their behavior.
    * **The "Dirty Secret":** She asserts that the "dirty secret of healthcare" is that individuals are actually in charge of their own health, but the system is designed to make them feel powerless.
* **Personalized Prevention:** Genetic screening (such as BRCA variants for breast/ovarian cancer or prenatal screening) is often underutilized because it generates questions that the current system "doesn't currently monetize."

### Leadership Lessons and Data Integrity
* **The Public Market Challenge:** Wojcicki reflects on the lessons of leading a public company, noting that managing a public entity during a pandemic requires a specialized team and board with specific public-market experience.
* **Data Privacy and Trust:** Despite the nonprofit shift, Wojcicki maintains strict boundaries regarding data usage.
    * **Law Enforcement:** 23andMe is not involved in "cold case" solving or forensic genealogy. The company fights subpoenas and has a transparency report to document police requests.
    * **Consumer Rights:** Customers maintain the right to delete their data at any time, a core tenet of the company's ethics-first approach.
