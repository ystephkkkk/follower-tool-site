## The Frontier Ideas a16z Wants to Fund Now | Marc Andreessen, Ben Horowitz & Chris Dixon

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-9XQMHhpypc
- Published: 2026-10-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261022.html#video--9XQMHhpypc
- Speakers: Chris Dixon (General Partner, a16z); Marc Andreessen (General Partner, a16z); Ben Horowitz (General Partner, a16z)

---

**TL;DR:** a16z is expanding its crypto fund into a16z Frontier to back ambitious, "outer-planet" founders operating at the bleeding edge of AI, hardware, biology, and media. The speakers argue that AI is pulling decades of technological progress forward, creating an unprecedented capability gap between agile startups and legacy incumbents.

### Launching a16z Frontier and Expanding the Mandate
* **Strategic Evolution:** **Chris Dixon** and partners announced the expansion of the a16z crypto fund into **a16z Frontier**, a broader mandate to back founders building at the edges of technological possibility.
  * **Blurring Boundaries:** **Chris Dixon** noted that crypto technologies like stablecoins have mainstreamed into fintech and Wall Street (e.g., used by **Stripe**), blurring categorical lines and creating intersectional companies.
  * **The "Outer Planet" Metaphor:** **Ben Horowitz** explained that the fund targets "outer planet" entrepreneurs—those working on novel, first-of-their-kind concepts that fall outside traditional venture verticals.
* **Firm Roots:** **Marc Andreessen** and **Ben Horowitz** highlighted that the new fund continues a firm-wide philosophy of making larger, earlier, and more aggressive bets, rooted in historical principles like JP Morgan's motto of doing "first-class business in a first-class way."
* **Multidisciplinary Expertise:** **Marc Andreessen** emphasized that the Frontier team combines deep technical expertise (such as game theory, math, and cryptography) with robust policy and regulatory capabilities developed during the crypto journey.

### AI as a Catalyst and the Incumbent-Startup Divide
* **The Capability Gap:** **Marc Andreessen** stated that the capability delta between AI-native early-stage startups and traditional incumbents is the widest he has ever seen in his career, driven by rapid weekly advancements.
  * **Super-Empowered Individuals:** **Marc Andreessen** observed that AI is currently easier for individuals to adopt than institutions, leading to employees secretly using AI and paving the way for skyrocketing startup formation.
  * **Disruption Frame Shift:** **Marc Andreessen** argued that Christensen's disruptive innovation framework—where tech companies fight each other—is being replaced by modern technologists going out to transform the broader economy (e.g., full-stack food service company **Adams**).
* **Technical Risk and Timing:** **Chris Dixon** noted that nearly every good technology-driven idea eventually works, but predicting timing is difficult; the Frontier fund explicitly aims to err on the side of being too early.
  * **Accelerated Timelines:** **Chris Dixon** suggested that AI is pulling forward 20 years of technical progress, enabling small teams (such as 30-person groups) to build full-stack device and hardware companies that once required thousands of people.

### Redefining Industries: Hardware, Entertainment, and Media
* **Hardware Democratization:** **Marc Andreessen** and **Chris Dixon** highlighted open-source tools like **REA (Reverse Engineer Anything)**, which harnesses AI to reverse-engineer physical hardware and rewrite its source code, collapsing the barrier between software and hardware engineering.
* **Entertainment and Gaming Renaissance:** 
  * **Video Games:** **Chris Dixon** pointed to recent developments where AI models disassemble code, run Windows games on Unix faster than Windows, and mash up video games (e.g., combining Super Mario and GTA) within days.
  * **Film Economics:** **Marc Andreessen** contrasted the $200 million loss of the studio movie *Digger* against the massive profitability potential of producing similar artistic achievements on a $2 million AI-assisted budget.
* **New Forms of Media:** **Ben Horowitz** and **Chris Dixon** predicted that AI will not just optimize existing media like movies, but unlock entirely new, AI-native forms of media and collaborative expression.

### The Agent Economy and Open Source vs. Centralization
* **Agent Ecology:** **Ben Horowitz** and **Chris Dixon** predicted a future where the most numerous participants on the internet will be software agents (such as utility proxy agents like **Muse** and **Instinct**) interacting via stablecoins and decentralized infrastructure.
* **The Battle for Open Source:** 
  * **Current Landscape:** **Marc Andreessen** noted that open-source models are currently racing ahead—or lapping closed models by 6 to 12 months—supported by active open-source ecosystems in both the US and China.
  * **Avoiding Centralization:** **Marc Andreessen** and **Ben Horowitz** warned against draconian regulations that could ban open source, arguing that without open-source AI, power centralizes dangerously into the hands of just a few companies or governments, while open source allows the global academic and student community to tackle safety issues like reward hacking.
* **The Internet Covenant:** **Chris Dixon** warned that the traditional internet covenant (where search engines send clicks to content publishers) is breaking down as AI provides direct answers, forcing a decade-long reconfiguration of digital economics, attention, and collaboration.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Startup Formation:** Startup formation will skyrocket from current levels due to individuals leveraging AI superpowers to launch businesses — **Marc Andreessen**
* **Market Dominance of Agents:** Within the coming era, the most numerous participants on the internet will be AI agents rather than people — **Ben Horowitz**
