### Architecture of a Personal AI System
- **Memory Limits vs. Capacity:** Human working memory is strictly limited to seven items ($\pm 2$), while an AI agent holds a million tokens (roughly a thousand pages, or three Harry Potter books) and can find a needle in them instantly.
- **The Knowledge Library:** Garry Tan's personal system (GBrain) utilizes a Carpathy-style knowledge wiki with about 220,000 markdown pages, capturing 25 years of diarized life, emails, meetings, and notes.
- **Skill Files:** Markdown instructions in plain English act as employees (e.g., summarizing meeting transcripts, pulling commitments, and cross-checking facts).
- **Latent Space vs. Deterministic Code:** Judgment, taste, and reading user intent belong in latent space (steered by markdown), while arithmetic, SQL queries, and seating charts require deterministic code.

### The Economics and Politics of Skill Files
- **The Revenue Per Head Revolution:** AI-native companies are breaking historical metrics; Emergent (Summer 2024 batch) reached nine figures of revenue in 8 months with only 15 people when crossing $15M ARR, and Retail (Winter 2024) hit $60M ARR with about 40 people.
- **The Career Risk:** Skill files externalize your cognition; if they live in a company's repository under corporate IT policies, your career is extracted when you leave; if they live in your repo, your expertise compounds.
- **The Spinozan Choice:** Comparing the offer to the "thousand gilders" or Heidelberg professorship offered to Baruch Spinoza, Tan warns founders against comfortable arrangements that trade personal ownership for short-term salary and compliance.

### Five-Step Implementation Guide
- **Step 1 — Pick a Harness:** Run an agent locally using OpenClaw, Hermes Agent, or GBrain (available free at `gbrain.io`), or use lighter tools like Codex or Claude Code.
- **Step 2 — Start Your Library:** Create a single folder of markdown files containing notes, projects, and working relationships that only exist in your head.
- **Step 3 — Write Your First Skill File:** Automate your most hated weekly task (expense reports, meeting notes, status updates) by explaining it in plain English to your agent.
- **Step 4 — Wire Up Recurring Jobs:** Schedule background jobs (e.g., 7 AM daily briefings or Friday summaries) so work finishes while you sleep.
- **Step 5 — Skillify Everything:** Never do one-off work; at the end of every task, command the agent to extract and save the process as a reusable markdown file.

    * While median human output in 2013 was ~15 logical lines of code per day, Tan argues that current agentic workflows allow for 400x that volume.
    * Even with a "pathological verbosity penalty," the floor for AI-enhanced leverage is approximately 8x to 80x.
* **Wiring vs. Weights:** The competitive advantage does not lie in the LLM models themselves (the "weights"), as everyone uses the same APIs. 
    * **Key Insight:** True leverage comes from "how you wire the work"—the specific way a company integrates agents into its core operations.
* **Economic Anomalies:** Modern YC startups are hitting unprecedented revenue-per-head milestones.
    * **Emergence:** Reached nine figures in ARR in eight months with a tiny team.
    * **Retail:** Scaled to $60M ARR with only 40 people.
    * **Historical Context:** This level of efficiency has never existed in software, oil, or any previous industrial era.

### Encoding the "Workforce of Markdown"
Tan proposes that every component of a traditional 1,000-person organization can now be encoded as Markdown files and logical tables:
* **Skill Files as Employees:** Each file defines a specific capability or job description that an agent can execute.
* **Resolver Tables as Org Charts:** These tables decide which "skill" or agent should handle a specific incoming task.
* **Filing Rules as Internal Processes:** Standard operating procedures are codified into the system logic.
* **Trigger Evals as Performance Reviews:** Automated tests (evals) ensure that agents are executing tasks according to the required standards.
* **Management Layer:** In AI-native companies, engineers are no longer just writing software; they are hiring, training, and managing a "workforce made of Markdown."

### The "Company Brain": Library + Librarian
The bottleneck for AI agents is no longer just the model; it is context engineering—ensuring the agent has the right information at the right time.
* **Working Memory Limits:** Humans can hold roughly seven items in mind; an agent can hold about "three Harry Potter books" worth of tokens.
* **The Librarian Function:** A "Company Brain" (like Tan’s open-source project, **G-Brain**) acts as both a library of every meeting, email, and decision, and a librarian that selects which "three books" to put in the agent's context window for a specific task.
* **Data Hygiene:** A brain without curation becomes a "garbage dump."
    * **Requirement:** Companies must treat their internal knowledge base as production infrastructure, requiring provenance on facts and constant pruning by both humans and agents.

### The Discipline of "Skillification"
To prevent organizational "amnesia," Tan advocates for a strict operational discipline:
* **Never Do One-Off Work:** Founders and employees should not just use AI to complete a task once.
* **"Skillify" the Task:** Once an AI agent produces a successful result for a new problem, that process should be immediately converted into a reusable "skill file."
* **Compounding Knowledge:** If you have to ask an AI (or a human) to do the same thing twice without a template, the organization has failed to learn. 
* **Ownership:** Tan emphasizes that while model quality is "rented" from providers like OpenAI or Anthropic, the "Company Brain" and its library of skills are assets the company owns and compounds over time.

## How The Best Companies Defend Against Mediocrity And Rot

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7VKliOQXQ9M
- Published: 2026-05-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260522.html#video-7VKliOQXQ9M
- Speakers: Garry Tan (President & CEO, Y Combinator); Eric Ries (Author, 'The Lean Startup')

---

1. **TL;DR**  
Standard corporate governance frameworks prioritize short-term shareholder primacy at the expense of long-term innovation, frequently resulting in founders being ousted and company missions corrupted. To build enduring, century-scale companies, founders must adopt "mission-controlled" legal structures—such as Public Benefit Corporations (PBCs) or trust-owned models—that legally shield their core values from speculative financial pressures.

2. ## The Trap of Shareholder Primacy and Founder Oustings

* **The Fallacy of "Best Practices":** Modern corporate governance rules often force companies to prioritize quarterly stock gains over long-term product value.
    * **A Modern Invention:** Shareholder primacy is not a natural law of capitalism; it was popularized in the 1980s by academic and judicial consensus (such as Milton Friedman's doctrines) rather than legislative action.
    * **The Delaware Loophole:** Standard Delaware C-Corporation charters define a company's purpose using a "blank space" legal clause ("any lawful act or activity"), which courts have routinely interpreted as an absolute legal duty to maximize short-term investor returns.

* **The Jeff Lawson / Twilio Warning:** **Activists vs. Builders:** Founder Jeff Lawson successfully scaled Twilio to $4 billion in revenue, but agreed to a seven-year sunset clause on his founder-control supervoting shares.
    * **The 199-Day Window:** Just 199 days after his supervoting protections expired, activist investors representing less than 0.5% of shareholders forced him out of the company due to temporary post-pandemic stock volatility.

* **The Myth of "Independent" Directors:** **Misaligned Incentives:** Conventional governance advocates for "independent" directors to balance boards. In practice, because these directors are nominated and recommended by venture capital firms for future board seats, they prioritize investor desires over the founder's long-term vision.

### The Costco Origin Story: Placing Customers Over Shareholders

* **The Sol Price Philosophy:** **Fiduciary to the Customer:** Sol Price, the founder of FedMart and pioneer of modern retail, treated his relationship with customers as a legal fiduciary duty, ranking his priorities as: customers first, employees second, and shareholders third.
    * **Extreme Transparency:** If a competitor offered a lower price on an item, Price famously put up signs in FedMart directing customers to shop at his competitor instead, building immense consumer trust.

* **The FedMart Collapse vs. Costco’s Ascent:** **The Cost of Activism:** Price took FedMart public and later brought in private equity board members to help take it private. Instead, the board locked him out of his office in 1975 to chase higher prices and lower wages.
    * **The Counterfactual:** Under traditional management, FedMart went bankrupt in just seven years, destroying two decades of building. 
    * **The Costco Solution:** A protesting FedMart executive quit and helped Price launch Price Club, which ultimately merged to become Costco. Costco continues to protect Price's customer-first ethos using a highly defensive "governance fortress" that routinely receives poor ratings from standard governance agencies but delivers legendary market performance.

### The "Third Way": Building Mission-Controlled Structures

* **The Ethos and Integrity Formula:** **Systemic Protection:** Rather than relying solely on vulnerable "founder control" (which can suffer from hubris and ends upon the founder's retirement or death), companies should become "mission-controlled."
    * **The Formula:** $\text{Ethos} + \text{Integrity} = \text{Incorruptible}$. True structural integrity means the mission itself holds legal sovereignty.

* **Public Benefit Corporations (PBCs):** **An Accessible Shield:** Transitioning to a PBC is a straightforward, two-page legal filing in Delaware.
    * **Legal Airbag:** A PBC allows board directors to reject lucrative buyouts or cost-cutting measures from malicious actors by giving them the legal coverage to prioritize a defined public benefit or mission alongside profit. 

* **Industrial Foundations:** **A Proven Alternative:** Common in Europe but underutilized in the US, this structure places the voting control of a for-profit operating business inside a non-profit foundation or perpetual purpose trust.
    * **Survival Metrics:** Academic data shows that companies structured as industrial foundations are six times more likely to survive to year 50 (a 60% probability of survival compared to just 10% for traditional corporate structures).

### High-Stakes Governance: Novo Nordisk and Anthropic

* **The $600 Billion Novo Nordisk Case Study:** **Protecting R&D:** Founded in Denmark in the 1920s to commercialize insulin, Novo Nordisk was structured as a for-profit subsidiary of a non-profit foundation to prevent predatory price-gouging.
    * **Blocking the Merger:** In the early 2000s, investment bankers and the for-profit board pressured the company to undergo a massive $20 billion merger. The non-profit trustees vetoed the deal because it wasn't necessary for the company's survival.
    * **The GLP-1 Payoff:** This veto saved a quiet, 11-year-old research program that went on to create GLP-1 agonists (the active compounds in Ozempic and Wegovy), eventually driving Novo Nordisk's market valuation past $600 billion—greater than the entire GDP of Denmark.

* **Anthropic’s Long-Term Benefit Trust:** **Protecting AI Safety:** When founders Dario and Daniela Amodei left OpenAI to launch Anthropic, they designed a "Perpetual Purpose Trust" called the Long-Term Benefit Trust.
    * **Decoupling Control:** This trust uses a set of independent, non-investor trustees whose sole job is to appoint and dismiss board directors, insulating the safety-focused AI lab from being forced to prioritize profit-maximization at the cost of existential risk.
