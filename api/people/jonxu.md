## Groww: If Your Customers Don't Love It or Hate It, You've Already Lost

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ObBAxL2dFzw
- Published: 2026-06-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260615.html#video-ObBAxL2dFzw
- Speakers: Lalit Keshre (Co-founder and CEO, Groww); Jon Xu (General Partner, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Building a generational consumer fintech company requires obsessing over organic customer love, making bold strategic bets on transparency, and maintaining a deeply aligned co-founder value system. By prioritizing long-term customer trust over immediate monetization, Groww scaled to become India's largest retail investment platform.

### From Failed Iterations to Product-Market Fit
* **The Robo-Advisor Failure:** In 2016, Groww launched as a robo-advisor modeled after US platforms like Wealthfront and Betterment, but the concept failed to gain traction with Indian consumers.
* **The Pivot to Choice and Transparency:** Listening to early customer signals, co-founder and CEO Lalit Keshre realized Indian customers wanted selection, absolute transparency, and zero friction. 
* **Immediate Market Validation:** In May 2017, Groww launched its transparent, multi-product platform. 
    * While the team hoped for 100 signups in the first month, they acquired 600, signaling immediate product-market fit (PMF).
* **Unscalable Customer Research:** To gather early product feedback, the founders took extreme measures:
    * Set up direct WhatsApp communication groups with early users.
    * Intercepted moviegoers at theaters to conduct in-person consumer interviews.
    * Answered questions directly on Quora to build organic trust.
* **100% Organic Growth:** Groww achieved nearly zero customer acquisition cost (CAC) in its early years, relying entirely on word-of-mouth referrals.

### Strategic Bets on Regulation and Monetization
* **The Regulated Zone Mandate:** To eliminate business risk and establish trust, Groww made a strategic decision to play strictly within regulated spaces, proactively obtaining licenses rather than operating in unregulated "gray" areas.
* **Four Years of Zero Revenue:** Based on power-user feedback, Groww shifted from commission-based mutual funds to zero-commission direct mutual funds. 
    * This decision meant the company operated with zero revenue for its first four years.
    * The bet was built on the thesis that a high-retention, high-engagement consumer financial platform with massive capital flow would eventually find monetization channels.
* **Unlocking Revenue with Stocks:** Capitalizing on its highly retained user base, Groww launched stock trading, which unlocked its core monetization engine.

### Obsessing Over Product Design and Customer Love
* **Design as a Commandment:** "Obsess over design" is one of Groww's ten core company commandments.
* **Founder as a Power User:** Keshre personally uses the Groww app for at least two hours a day to experience the product exactly as customers do.
* **The Danger of Indifference:** Keshre teaches his product teams to aim for strong, binary reactions from customers on new features:
    * Feedback should either be "I love it" or "I hate it." 
    * "Don't care" (indifference) is considered a failure, as it indicates the product does not matter to the user.

### Co-founder Dynamics and the Future of Wealth Tech
* **Four Co-Founders, Zero Conflict:** Despite having four co-founders, Groww avoids misalignment by distinguishing between core values and business strategy:
    * **Value systems** (such as being "customer-first") are written in "pen" and remain permanent.
    * **Strategies** are written in "pencil" and constantly evolve.
    * Clear ownership of distinct domains prevents overlapping friction.
* **The Impact of AI on Startups:** Keshre, an active user of AI coding tools like Claude Code, notes that AI has dramatically lowered the barrier to building startups. 
    * In the "internet era," launching required a multi-person team of PMs, designers, coders, and operations staff.
    * Today, a single founder can leverage AI to handle product design, writing, coding, and automated operations.
* **The Shift to Wealth Management:** As Groww's user base matures and grows wealthier, the platform's focus is shifting toward sophisticated, smart wealth management products to serve customers throughout their financial lifecycles.

## How Razorpay Became India’s Largest Payments Company

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X5bABLCuIHA
- Published: 2026-05-06
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260506.html#video-X5bABLCuIHA
- Speakers: Harshil Mathur (co-founder and CEO, Razorpay); Jon Xu (Representative, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
Razorpay became India’s largest payments platform by treating regulatory hurdles as competitive moats, pivoting based on deep customer empathy, and maintaining "founder mode" through radical transparency and early bets on infrastructure like UPI.

### The Pivot from Education to Startups
*   **The Origin Problem:** Harshil Mathur and his team discovered the payments problem while building a crowdfunding side project, realizing that in India, it was significantly easier to accept cash than digital payments.
*   **Failed GTM Strategy:** 
    *   Razorpay originally applied to YC with a plan to digitize fee collections for educational institutes.
    *   **The Insight:** Schools had no incentive to modernize because they held all the power; students would wait in physical lines to pay regardless.
*   **Finding True PMF:** While working in a co-working space, the team realized fellow startup founders were the ones actually desperate for a tech-first payment solution. They pivoted the business entirely to serve the startup ecosystem.

### Regulatory Hurdles as a Competitive Moat
*   **The One-Year Wait:** It took Razorpay a full year after joining YC to process their first live transaction due to the complexity of getting bank approvals and certifications. 
*   **Building Conviction:** During the long wait, Mathur often questioned the business. He regained conviction by talking to customers; their consistent frustration with existing systems proved the market need was massive.
*   **The Regulation Moat:** Mathur views heavy regulation as "fair" because it applies to everyone. The same hurdles that frustrated Razorpay now serve as a barrier to entry for new competitors who lack the patience to endure the year-long approval cycle.

### Crisis Management and the Trust Economy
*   **The Near-Death Experience:** Two weeks after their YC Demo Day, Razorpay’s partner bank pulled the plug, shutting down all 50 live merchants overnight.
*   **The "Human Touch" Philosophy:** 
    *   Instead of hiding, the team called every single merchant to explain the situation personally and listen to their frustrations.
    *   **Trust over Efficiency:** Razorpay avoids using AI to replace customer support. They believe that in B2B financial services, the primary role of support is not just solving a ticket, but establishing human trust during a crisis.
    *   **Impact:** Many merchants who "abused" the team during that shutdown remain Razorpay customers today because of the transparency shown during the crisis.

### Strategic Bets and Capital Efficiency
*   **Betting on UPI:** In 2016, most large banks and payment gateways were skeptical of UPI. Razorpay bet early, becoming the first gateway to go live on the system.
    *   When demonetization hit and the market shifted to UPI, Razorpay was the only player ready. This allowed them to land major clients like Zomato and Swiggy in just two weeks.
*   **Logical B2B Growth:** Razorpay maintained extreme capital efficiency, even putting their $11M Series A into fixed deposits because their burn was so low. 
    *   Mathur argues that B2B is inherently logical: if you add value, the business pays. Burning money to "acquire" B2B customers is ineffective if the core product value isn't there.

### Scaling and "Founder Mode"
*   **The Manager Mode Trap:** Mathur admits to a period where he hired great executives and stepped back into "manager mode," which he now views as a mistake. 
*   **Returning to the Weeds:** He emphasizes that while delegating execution is necessary, a founder must never delegate the core vision or product direction. No leader will care about the company as much as the founder.
*   **The AI Shift:** Mathur is currently using AI to reinvent Razorpay’s platform from scratch to avoid "incumbent fallacy." 
    *   **Execution as the Only Moat:** As AI makes the "build" process faster and cheaper, the only remaining moats are deciding *what* to build and how fast a team can execute.

> "When a digital system is harder than a physical system, it does the reverse of democratization; it creates silos. And that felt like a completely incorrect, wrong thing for us." — Harshil Mathur

> "The regulation can seem unfair to you at times but the best part is it's actually very fair. Every company, no matter how big or small they are, they have to clear the same lot of guidelines... that itself creates a very deep moat." — Harshil Mathur

> "In customer support, in our case, support is not about solving the problem; it is the channel to establish trust. We’re using AI in everything except replacing customer support." — Harshil Mathur

> "The only way to survive is to figure out what the market is going to be and move today. Any company who takes the call that 'we're going to respond to the market' is already dead." — Harshil Mathur
