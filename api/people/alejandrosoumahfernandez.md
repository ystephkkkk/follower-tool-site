#### TL;DR
Most GPU compute capacity still trades out of public view via private bilateral contracts negotiated over Slack, X, and Telegram, creating a financing squeeze for mid-size companies that must pay steep upfront prepayments. Jump Trading’s compute trading leads Affaan Mustafa and Alejandro Soumah Fernandez explain how chips, power, capital, and evolving market structures drive this hidden "dark pool" economy.

### Market Structure & The "Dark Pool" of Compute
* **Unlit Market Dynamics:** Most physical compute trades in blocks through long, bilateral commercial agreements—incorporating MSAs, SLAs, and hardware-specific order forms—rather than on widely adopted public exchanges.
* **OTC Channels:** Tenured compute capacity for fixed terms and specific Total Contract Values (TCVs) is negotiated in private neo-chat channels, including Slack messages, X messages, Telegram group chats, and OTC venues.
* **Pricing Skew:** Negotiations center heavily on the chip SKU and base board (e.g., H100, H200, B300) alongside memory types (VRAM, CPU memory, additional storage), delivery dates, and precise workload specifications.

### Macroeconomics, Jevons Paradox, & Upstream Costs
* **Decoupling of Token and Compute Prices:** According to a16z data referenced by the speakers, token and inference costs have fallen sharply while GPU compute prices have remained robust.
* **Jevons Paradox in Action:** Cheaper tokens unlock vastly higher usage; teams consume significantly more tokens on the same budget, driving up underlying demand for compute.
* **Upstream Supply Chain:** Bringing a usable GPU hour online requires the convergence of chips, power, buildings, grid connections, and massive capital.
* **IEA Electricity Projections:** The International Energy Agency’s April update projects global data center electricity consumption to jump from **485 terawatt-hours in 2025 to 950 terawatt-hours in 2030**.

### The Middle-Market Financing Gap
* **The Hyperscaler Advantage:** Hyperscalers, frontier labs, and large neo-clouds leverage strong equity, debt access, and large balance sheets to secure multi-year commitments (such as OpenAI's 10-year deals).
* **The Mid-Size Squeeze:** Startups and Series B companies cannot easily underwrite $200M+ compute contracts over three years without eating an entire funding round.
* **Prepayment Hurdles:** CoreWeave public filings show median prepayments of **15% to 25% upfront** on cluster contracts; on a **$100 million TCV cluster**, a startup must put down **$15 million to $25 million upfront**, severely restricting operational runway.
* **Strategic Workarounds:** Growing companies must weigh turning away customer demand, bridging shortfalls via spot/on-demand pricing, or financing the prepayment component against the opportunity cost of building and operating their own full stack.

### Operating Models & Procurement Options
* **Build vs. Rent Spectrum:** Companies can build and operate the full stack (handling power, permits, assembly, and management), own servers in a colocation facility with partner operations, or buy off-take rental capacity.
* **Control vs. Cost Trade-offs:** Some businesses require root access, confidential compute, and topology customization, making full-stack ownership worth the premium despite rental alternatives.
* **Venture Capital Portco Impacts:** VC investors increasingly focus on compute procurement because payment terms directly dictate portfolio company runway.

### Market Participants, Brokers, & Policy
* **Ecosystem Roles:** The market relies on price discovery providers (indexes and benchmarks for future derivative contracts), physical matching venues (decentralized marketplaces, bare-metal resellers, and neo-clouds), bilateral brokers, and specialist equipment lenders.
* **Bespoke Deal Complexity:** Every deal features custom bills of materials, delivery lead times, and variable creditworthiness factors, making non-fungibility a major hurdle.
* **Export Controls & Open Weights:** U.S. policy heavily shapes the market; in January, the U.S. Commerce Department moved qualifying H200-class license reviews for China and Macau to case-by-case review with conditions.
* **U.S. AI Action Plan:** The plan explicitly encourages open-source and open-weight AI while recommending financial market improvements so startups and academics can access large-scale computing power.
