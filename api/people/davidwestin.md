---

**TL;DR:** White House National Economic Director Kevin Hassett expressed strong optimism for U.S. economic growth, projecting GDP expansion around 4% driven by capital spending, labor supply increases, and an AI-fueled productivity boom, while acknowledging that fiscal deficit targets will depend on outside forces and policy trade-offs.

### Economic Growth & Supply-Side Drivers
* **Current Growth Metrics:**
  * **Real GDP:** Currently tracking in the 5% range (specifically cited at 5.014 real), supported by strong incoming economic data.
  * **Growth Projections:** Base case growth is expected to cruise around 4% (rather than 3%), assuming no disruptions from the outside world.
* **Growth Accounting Breakdown:**
  * **Productivity Growth:** Estimated at approximately 2.5%.
  * **Capital Deepening:** Estimated at 0.7% to 0.8%.
  * **Labor Supply:** Estimated to contribute roughly similarly to capital deepening.
* **Labor Market Indicators:**
  * **Weekly Wages:** The administration emphasizes average weekly wages over hourly wages because tax stimuli are driving longer work weeks and increased work hours.
  * **Unemployment Claims:** Initial claims for unemployment insurance are at their lowest levels since World War II, showing minimal labor market stress.

### Artificial Intelligence & Productivity
* **AI Productivity Boom:** 
  * The current productivity boom is being observed in real time *before* benchmark revisions, signaling exceptionally strong momentum.
  * AI subscription prices have remained stable despite massive quality improvements, reflecting unmeasured deflationary productivity gains similar to the 1990s internet boom.
* **Community Pushback:** 
  * Local communities and states (such as Texas and New York) are enacting data center moratoriums.
  * **Voluntary Framework:** The administration’s AI policy remains strictly voluntary—no town is forced to host a data center, but firms building them must generate their own power.
* **Labor Impact of AI:** 
  * Academic studies show that firms adopting AI experience significant increases in sales, employment, and wages.
  * Conversely, the primary labor market stress is concentrated in firms that fail to adopt AI and consequently struggle or fail.

### Fiscal Policy, Deficits, & National Net Worth
* **Federal Workforce Reductions:** 
  * Federal employment has been reduced by approximately 300,000 workers, yielding roughly $500 billion in budget savings over 10 years.
  * Displaced workers transitioning to the private sector earn on average about $30,000 more than in government work, generating an additional $100 billion in tax benefits.
  * DC-area job numbers show no negative disruption from these cuts, as workers either retired or quickly secured private sector employment.
* **Deficit and Debt Perspectives:**
  * Reaching a 3% of GDP deficit target by the end of the term depends on avoiding unforeseen "force majeure" measures, though deficit reduction remains a priority for the president and key members of Congress.
  * **Household Net Worth:** While government debt is a liability for future generations, external debt sits at around $15 trillion, whereas household net worth net of debt has surged by roughly $90 trillion over the past decade.
  * **Capital vs. Consumption:** Federal policy has focused on building future capital (via tax and capital formation reforms) rather than just mailing consumption checks, resulting in historic capital formation numbers.

### Interest Rates & Yields
* **Real Return on Capital:** 
  * Rising long-term bond yields (including a 3% real yield on 30-year TIPS with 2.1% inflation) reflect a higher real return on capital driven by deregulation and pro-capital tax policies.
  * General equilibrium dynamics mean that high returns on capital (e.g., borrowing at 6% to fund a data center yielding 20%) naturally push borrowing rates upward until markets clear.

### Trumpanomics vs. Prior Economic Frameworks
* **Pillars of Trumpanomics:**
  * **Worker-First Supply Side:** Features worker-centric tax policies (such as no tax on tips and average tax refunds near $3,500 after major tax legislation) alongside traditional Reagan-style business supply-side incentives and faster deregulation.
  * **Trade Policy:** Aggressive use of tariffs is driving a massive wave of domestic onshoring.
* **Deficit Trade-Offs:**
  * Similar to Reaganomics, upfront tax reforms and pro-growth policies can initially expand deficits, but the long-term benefit of a high-capital, high-growth economy justifies the policy bundle.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Kevin Hassett** predicts the U.S. economy will "cruise around four [percent] instead of three" for growth, driven by capital spending, labor supply increases, and an AI productivity boom.
* **Kevin Hassett** claims that the deficit will track toward a 3% of GDP target over the next two years, though achievement will depend on whether "force majeure measures" arise.

And so, I'd say that we're really, really bullish on the momentum that we have going forward."* — **Kevin Hassett**

---

**TL;DR**
The Federal Reserve is maintaining a "higher for longer" interest rate stance due to geopolitical uncertainty and domestic fiscal stimulus, while experts suggest the AI revolution may be a gradual integration of "normal technology" rather than an immediate economic upheaval.

### Federal Reserve Policy and Geopolitical Uncertainty
*   **Monetary Policy Outlook:** Randal Quarles (Cynosure, former Fed Vice Chair) predicts no change in interest rates for the remainder of the year. While the Fed is "data-dependent," current drivers remain more inflationary than non-inflationary.
*   **The Neutral Rate:** The Fed’s estimate for the neutral interest rate ticked up from 3.0% to 3.1%. Quarles suggests this is an institutional "catch-up" to structural changes that have existed for some time rather than a direct result of AI investment.
*   **Inflationary Pressures:** 
    *   **Energy Markets:** Uncertainty surrounding the war in Iran could lead to "demand destruction" if higher prices persist beyond two months.
    *   **Fiscal Stimulus:** The "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" provides growth incentives but is currently pushing upward on short-term inflation.
*   **Labor Supply Shifts:** Quarles argues the market underestimates the shrinkage in labor supply due to immigration policy changes, effectively offsetting softness in job demand and supporting higher interest rates.

### The Reality of AI Integration
*   **"Normal Technology" Perspective:** Arvind Narayanan (Princeton Professor) argues AI is a powerful general-purpose technology similar to electricity, requiring gradual integration rather than signaling an impending superintelligence that overrides economic laws.
*   **Reliability vs. Capability:** High performance on benchmarks does not equal reliability. Narayanan cites the **Air Canada lawsuit**—where a chatbot invented a refund policy—as evidence that legal liability and organizational hurdles will slow adoption.
*   **Knowledge Worker Impact:** 
    *   **Expanding Inquiry:** Drew Matus (MetLife Chief Market Strategist) suggests AI doesn't eliminate tasks but allows workers to ask more complex questions, increasing their value to firms.
    *   **Abstraction of Labor:** Historically, technology moves workers to higher levels of supervision. In software engineering, despite heavy AI tool adoption, job postings for engineers continue to increase.
*   **Limits of AI:** Narayanan pushes back against the idea that AI can solve geopolitical strategy, noting that the inability to predict the future is a data and agency limitation, not a biological one.

### U.S.-Canada Trade Relations
*   **Economic Strain:** Following President Trump’s tariffs, Canada has seen real GDP contract in two of the four quarters of 2025, with annual growth slowing to 1.7%.
*   **The "Trust Gap":** Over half of Canadians polled no longer view the U.S. as a reliable ally. Author Louise Penny noted she redirected her book tours to Canada in protest, citing "ruinous" tariffs on consumer goods.
*   **Divergent Business Views:**
    *   **Investment Chill:** Goldy Hyder (Canada Business Council) reports that capital is "frozen" in trade-sensitive sectors like steel and forestry.
    *   **Contrarian View:** Barry Zekelman (CEO of Zekelman Industries) argues that while he pays $7 million monthly in tariffs, U.S. demand is "booming," and Canada should endorse similar pro-growth policies rather than "poking the U.S. in the eye" at global forums.

### Social Media Regulation: The Australia Experiment
*   **100-Day Review:** Australia’s ban on social media for those under 16 has seen roughly five million accounts removed across 10 platforms.
*   **Implementation Challenges:** Paul Litherland (Surf Online Safe) expresses concern that the "reasonable steps" required by legislation are being loosely interpreted by tech companies, allowing children to circumvent bans via new accounts.
*   **The Business Case for Youth:** 
    *   **Snapchat Operations:** Jennifer Park Stout (SVP, Snap) reports the company removed 400,000 users in Australia (out of 8 million daily users). She argues blanket bans ignore the "sweet spot" of community connection.
    *   **Long-term Growth:** Ravi Iyer (USC Marshall) notes that while youth aren't the primary source of current revenue, banning them disrupts "network effects" and habit formation, which could hurt the long-term growth engines that drive stock prices.
