## Building the Cloud for AI Agents | AWS CEO Matt Garman

### TL;DR
AWS CEO Matt Garman discusses how AWS is scaling its $169B business (growing at 37%) to support the massive infrastructure demands of AI agents, startups, and custom silicon like Trainium and Graviton. In conversation with a16z's Raghu Raghuram, Garman details Amazon's $220 billion CapEx buildout for 2026, the strategic importance of reserving GPUs for early-stage startups, and how AI agents are transforming both cloud architecture and internal software development.

### AI Infrastructure and the CapEx Buildout
- **Record-Breaking Capital Expenditure:** AWS's CapEx has accelerated significantly over the past 3 to 4 years, with a projected $220 billion CapEx budget for 2026. Garman notes that demand is so massive that AWS does not anticipate slowing down anytime soon.
- **Supply Chain and Constraints:** Moving beyond simple server planning, AWS now manages energy projects globally—purchasing renewable power, solar, and nuclear projects (both behind-the-meter and grid-connected) up to 20 years out. Garman notes that "the Goal" principle applies: as soon as power ceases to be a constraint, others emerge (e.g., memory, TSMC capacity, HBM, networking components, or connectors across a multi-tier supply chain).
- **GPU Allocation Strategy:** While top Frontier Labs (such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta) gobble up available GPUs, AWS is intentionally reserving capacity for newer startups that will become future enterprises. AWS recently announced plans to buy 2 million NVIDIA GPUs over the next couple of years.
- **Bubble Skepticism:** Garman dismisses the idea of an AI CapEx bubble, noting that unlike Neo-clouds with 30–60% customer concentration, AWS maintains single-digit (or lower) concentration per customer and sees enterprises achieving positive ROI on core compute, storage, and inference workloads.

### Re-Architecting the Cloud for AI Agents
- **Agent-First Optimizations:** AWS is adapting its cloud to serve autonomous agents rather than just human developers. New services and features include **AWS Context** (a context layer across S3 and Aurora data lakes) and streamlined AWS account creation (allowing sign-up via Gmail without credit cards, VPC, or IAM setup in under 30 seconds).
- **Transient vs. Durable Infrastructure:** Traditional cloud primitives (like Aurora databases with 5 nines of durability) are often "over-engineered" for transient agent workflows that spin up resources briefly and discard them. AWS is working to balance fast, low-overhead creation with long-term production durability.
- **Purpose-Built Building Blocks:** AWS has developed new primitives for agents, including sandboxes built on **Firecracker** (micro-VMs invented by AWS 10 years ago), gateways, compute sandboxes, and short-term, fine-grained service role permissions.

### Custom Silicon: Graviton and Trainium
- **Nitro and Graviton Success:** Starting with the Annapurna Labs acquisition, AWS developed **Nitro** to offload virtualization to specialized cards for bare-metal performance and security isolation. Building on this, **Graviton** ARM-based processors now land in larger volumes than any other chip family, offering 20% better price-performance and reducing customer server fleets by half. Over 90% of AWS's top 100 customers use Graviton.
- **Trainium Evolution:** AWS is now on its third generation (**Trainium 3**), with Trainium 4 announced. Despite the name, Trainium is heavily utilized for inference and powers much of Amazon Bedrock. Trainium capacity is currently sold out through late next year, with major backing from Anthropic, OpenAI, and select startups.

### Enterprise Adoption, Bedrock, and Security
- **Amazon Bedrock & Data Privacy:** Bedrock protects enterprise data by guaranteeing it never leaves the customer's VPC or goes back to model providers. Bedrock usage is exploding month-over-month as customers migrate workloads from proof-of-concept to production.
- **Open Weights and Fine-Tuning:** Enterprises are increasingly using **Amazon SageMaker** to host open-weights models and perform post-training/fine-tuning on their proprietary data to achieve better performance at lower costs.
- **Enterprise Agent Hurdles:** Enterprises are held back from autonomous workflows due to trust, safety, and fear of agents accidentally deleting production databases. AWS is deploying automated guardrails, policy-driven access controls, evaluation systems (Evals), and field-driven engineering (FDE) teams to train customer teams in 40–45 days rather than locking them into endless consulting contracts.
- **Continuum Security:** AWS launched **Continuum**, a service leveraging powerful AI models to scan enterprise environments for vulnerabilities, prioritize them using context, and deliver security at "machine speed."

### Internal AWS Operations and Agentic Development
- **Internal Deployment of Q:** Amazon rolled out **Amazon Q** to every employee, enabling non-technical line-of-business teams (HR, finance) to build agents and automate multi-week tasks into hours.
- **Agentic Software Development:** AWS's internal "frontier teams" use agent-first development rather than simple code completion. Engineers now manage swarms of coding agents, resulting in a massive turbo-boost in the speed of shipping new products and features.

---

### 1. Redefining Workplace Productivity with Amazon Quick
* **The Fragmentation Bottleneck:** Traditional AI assistants operate in walled gardens, functioning as isolated search tools with short-term memory that force humans to act as the manual "orchestration layer" to copy and paste data across silos (e.g., Slack, OneDrive, Google Drive).
* **Amazon Quick & The Knowledge Graph:** Amazon Quick uses an agentic search layer called the *Knowledge Graph* to reason across an organization's entire data estate—mapping relationships between people, documents, communications, and data lakes. It dynamically learns and compounds context with every query.
* **Key Visual and Autonomic Features:**
    * **Direct Data Queries:** Users can query complex structured databases using natural language. Quick automatically drafts SQL queries, samples schemas, and builds interactive visualizations using the QuickSight platform.
    * **Activity Feed & Proactive Highlights:** Features a personalized activity feed that learns project relevance and user preferences over time.
    * **Persistence Agents:** Allows non-technical users to build scheduled agents (such as a *Marketing Performance Agent*) that autonomously monitor and update reports 24/7.
* **Proven Enterprise Outcomes:**
    * **GoDaddy:** Eliminated 15,000 hours of manual work annually.
    * **Aderant:** Achieved 90% faster business insights and a 75% improvement in documentation accuracy.
    * **NBA:** Structured over 25 years of player prospect data into interactive, real-time leaderboards and benchmarks.

### 2. Bridging Speed and Safety with AWS Continuum
* **The Paradigm Shift:** Security operations are moving from a reactive model of "telemetry, storage, query, and dashboards for humans" to an active model of "context, reasoning, and actions for agents." 
* **Introducing AWS Continuum:** A comprehensive security agent suite designed to eliminate the historical friction between rapid software deployment and organizational risk.
    * **Threat Modeling:** Automatically generates threat models directly from raw code, mapping them to system architecture, source code, and design documents to mitigate risks across all six STRIDE categories.
    * **Penetration Testing:** SmugMug utilized Continuum to run full, context-aware penetration tests on demand, compressing a process that typically took weeks down to hours.
* **Code Vulnerabilities Capability (Preview):** Developed alongside design partners like Capital One, MongoDB, Rivian, and Robinhood, this model-agnostic feature operates across four core stages:
    * *Discovery:* Scans backlogs and active cloud environments to build a comprehensive map of vulnerabilities and attack paths.
    * *Prioritization:* Evaluates and contextually ranks threats by reasoning across AWS infrastructure, permissions, network topology, and unstructured business risk data.
    * *Validation:* To minimize false positives, the agent builds and executes reproducible test cases inside a sandbox environment, producing human-reviewable evidence.
    * *Remediation:* Determines and validates precise code fixes or blocking controls. Security teams can run the tool in a supervised "lone mode" before graduating it to automated "enforce mode."

### 3. The Continuous Software Delivery Loop: Kiro, DevOps Agent, and Transform
* **The Software Delivery Loop:** To stop buggy, machine-generated code from creating compounding technical debt, AWS introduced a unified, continuous loop: *write it right, ship it fast, and keep it modern*. 
* **Kiro (Write It Right):** Kiro is an engineering agent that drafts clear requirements, structured design documents, and validated tests *before* writing any code. 
    * **Fintech Case Study (Dhan):** Using Kiro, a single engineer built a highly complex charting platform featuring 170+ indicators in just 8 weeks—a project estimated to take 12 engineers up to 24 months.
    * **Kiro Mobile App (iOS):** Developers can kick off spec-driven coding workflows, review pull requests, and approve deployments from their mobile phones. The sessions run securely in the cloud and sync context instantly with the developer's laptop.
* **AWS DevOps Agent (Ship It Fast):** 
    * **Release Management (Preview):** Moves the DevOps Agent from a reactive SRE tool to a proactive release pipeline. It validates builds, runs production risk assessments, and conducts autonomous testing in sandboxes to catch regressions before they impact users.
* **AWS Transform (Keep It Modern):** 
    * **Continuous Modernization (Preview):** While previous versions of Transform required manual activation, this new continuous background agent automatically crawls repositories to upgrade frameworks, update dependencies, and resolve version compliance issues.
    * **Enterprise Adoption:** Southwest Airlines scaled Kiro to 2,700 developers (two-thirds of its engineering organization), utilizing the tool to accelerate legacy codebase modernization and pull its platform modernization timeline in by three years.

### 4. Scaling Custom Agents with Bedrock AgentCore and AWS Context
* **Amazon Bedrock AgentCore Harness (GA):** Building custom agent infrastructure (session memory, error recovery, orchestration, and API tool access) typically takes months. AgentCore Harness decouples this infrastructure from the model, allowing developers to spin up secure, isolated microVM agents with just three API calls.
* **AgentCore Policies:** Deterministic guardrails enforced *outside* the agent's code, preventing bad actors from manipulating models or bypassing security parameters via prompt injection. It will soon integrate signals from major security providers like CrowdStrike, Netskope, SentinelOne, and Zscaler.
* **The Unified Context Layer:**
    * **Web Search:** grounds responses in real-time, public web information. This managed tool operates entirely within the AWS secure boundary.
    * **Managed Knowledge Base:** Integrates unstructured organizational data (S3, SharePoint, Google Drive, Confluence) using an "agentic retriever" to answer multi-step questions. (Currently used by OpenAI to power its large-scale RAG pipelines).
    * **AWS Context (New Service):** Automatically infers relationships across structured databases (stored as S3 Iceberg tables) and unstructured files to build an enterprise-wide knowledge graph. This service applies semantic reasoning (e.g., understanding that "escalations" and "tickets" refer to the same concept) to serve custom agents with highly accurate business context.
* **Infrastructure Demands:** Swami Sivasubramanian highlighted that multiple agentic reasoning steps require massive scale. In Q1 2026, Amazon Bedrock processed more requests than in all previous years combined. GPT-4o and GPT-4o-mini are now fully integrated on the platform.

**TL;DR**
Companies often fail to transition AI from proof-of-concept to production due to undefined success metrics and scaling challenges, while AWS is building a natively integrated "AI-first cloud" platform, including proprietary silicon, to address these issues and emerging sovereign AI requirements.

### Challenges & Keys to Successful AI Deployment
*   **Gap between Experimentation and Production:** Many companies conduct AI proofs-of-concept (POCs) without defining clear success criteria or measurable goals, hindering full-scale deployment.
    *   **Defining Value Beyond Cost Savings:** An example cited was a hospital administrator who didn't save money with ambient listening AI, but significantly reduced doctor attrition, highlighting the importance of diverse metrics beyond direct cost reduction.
    *   **Metric Maturity:** Good metrics are prevalent for areas like customer service and code generation, but "general productivity" applications still lack robust measurement.
*   **Deployment Blockers:**
    *   **Security Concerns:** For agentic workflows, companies fear agents acting autonomously, leading to security breaches, sprawl, and identity management issues.
    *   **Operationalization and Scaling:** Moving from a single GPU POC to global deployment poses significant challenges in scaling, operational efficiency, and productization.

### AWS's AI-First Cloud Strategy
*   **AI Integration into Every Application:** Matt Garman, CEO of AWS, asserts that all future applications will have AI inference capabilities natively integrated, transforming every business and industry.
*   **Deep Infrastructure Integration:**
    *   **Natively AI-Aware:** AWS is integrating AI throughout its infrastructure, enabling agents to interact seamlessly with storage, VPCs, security settings, and permissions.
    *   **Bedrock Platform:** AWS's Bedrock platform offers extensive model choice, capabilities, and the robust security customers expect, operating within a VPC.
    *   **Ecosystem Approach:** AWS ensures its platform supports partners and the broader ecosystem, acknowledging that no single vendor can build every necessary component.
*   **Proprietary Silicon (Tranium):** AWS's investment in chips like Tranium aims to provide better price-performance, offer customer choice beyond dominant GPU providers, and deliver differentiated capabilities.
    *   **Margin vs. Price:** Garman suggests that while proprietary chips could improve margins, AWS's operating model typically involves passing cost savings to customers to drive faster growth and more new workloads, aligning with Jevons paradox.

### Future of Infrastructure: Constraints & Innovation
*   **Physical Infrastructure Bottlenecks:**
    *   **Construction Delays:** While software development accelerates, physical infrastructure buildout (pouring concrete, permits, power) remains time-consuming, limiting hyperscalers' expansion.
*   **Space Data Centers (Long-Term Vision):**
    *   **Theoretical Advantages:** Offer infinite power (solar) and easier cooling.
    *   **Current Hurdles:** Server weight, lack of permanent space structures, high cost and limited availability of space transport, and latency concerns make them impractical for the foreseeable future.
*   **Hyperscalers and "Full Stack" Control:**
    *   **Beyond Chips:** AWS's move into chip manufacturing (something unheard of 20 years ago) suggests a potential expansion into areas like power generation or construction.
    *   **Industry Scaling Challenges:** Traditional utilities, for example, are structured for slow growth (3-5% annually) with fixed pricing and returns, which does not meet the rapid scaling demands of hyperscalers. AWS may need to directly fund or own parts of this infrastructure to accelerate growth.
*   **Capacity Planning for AI:**
    *   **Sustained Demand for Older Chips:** Due to high demand exceeding supply, older generations of AI chips (e.g., A100s) remain in demand and are not retired, especially for tasks requiring higher precision (HPC).
    *   **Multi-Horizon Planning:** AWS plans across multiple timeframes, from 20-30 years for data centers, to 5-6 years for servers, managing a complex supply chain and investment risks across these horizons.
    *   **Chip Cycle Time:** New chip versions emerge every 18-24 months, with 9-12 months from first chip out to scaled production.

### Advancing AI with Internal Practices & Sovereign Solutions
*   **AI-Driven Coding at AWS:**
    *   **Significant Acceleration:** Teams starting with "AI-driven coding" from the beginning see 10x to 100x improvements by prompting models instead of writing code, leading to context-rich, self-documenting, and testable code. Some teams have a "no line of code" mandate.
    *   **Challenges with Legacy Systems:** This speedup is less pronounced for complex, integrated legacy systems (e.g., S3), where providing sufficient context to models for tasks like memory management or object durability is difficult.
*   **Addressing Sovereign AI Requirements:**
    *   **Geopolitical Concerns:** Countries increasingly desire sovereign AI infrastructure due to concerns about trusting foreign governments and maintaining strategic independence.
    *   **AWS EU Sovereign Cloud:** AWS has launched a fully separate subsidiary in the EU, beholden to EU laws, with an independent governing board. All data, including metadata and login credentials, resides within the EU, and the system has been tested for disconnection from the AWS backbone, co-designed with European regulators like Germany's BSI. This model addresses trust for large economies.

### Executive Guidance for AI Acceleration
*   **Build Guardrails for Speed:** CIOs and CISOs should focus on creating "safe places to run fast" by implementing guardrails and secure building blocks (like AWS Agent Core) for AI deployments. This alleviates fears of autonomous agents causing production issues or security breaches, allowing teams to accelerate adoption.

**TL;DR**
AWS CEO Matt Garman emphasizes that successful AI adoption hinges on clear success metrics, robust security frameworks, and scalable infrastructure, detailing AWS's "AI-first cloud" vision, custom silicon, and sovereign cloud solutions designed to enable rapid and secure enterprise deployment.

### Defining Success and Overcoming Adoption Barriers

*   **Gap in AI Deployment:** Many companies struggle to move beyond AI proofs-of-concept (PoCs) to production due to a lack of clearly defined success criteria from the outset.
    *   **Example**: One hospital leader saw no financial savings from AI-driven ambient listening, while another successfully used it to reduce doctor and nurse attrition (from a 30% risk) by improving work-life balance.
*   **Measurement Challenges**:
    *   **Strong Metrics**: Companies typically have good metrics for AI in customer service and code generation (e.g., lines submitted).
    *   **Fuzzy Metrics**: General workforce productivity gains from AI often lack clear, measurable metrics.
*   **Security and Operational Hurdles**:
    *   **Agent Risks**: Concerns about AI agents acting outside intended parameters, agent sprawl, and identity management.
    *   **Scaling PoCs**: Difficulty scaling AI PoCs (e.g., from a single GPU instance) to global production environments, leading to underutilized resources and operational complexities.

### AWS's "AI-First Cloud" Strategy and Economics

*   **Ubiquitous AI Integration**:
    *   **Vision**: AWS believes AI inference will become a fundamental component of *every* application, dissolving the distinction between "AI applications" and non-AI ones.
    *   **Core Principle**: This necessitates native integration of AI into all aspects of AWS infrastructure, including storage, VPC, security, permissions, and model testing.
*   **Platform and Ecosystem Approach**:
    *   **AWS Bedrock**: A platform designed to offer extensive model choice, capabilities, and the robust security expected by customers within their VPCs.
    *   **Partner Ecosystem**: AWS acknowledges it cannot build every component and actively partners with other providers to ensure a comprehensive ecosystem for customer success.
*   **Custom Silicon (Trainium Chips)**:
    *   **Strategic Investment**: Over a decade of investment in custom silicon like Trainium aims to offer differentiated performance and choice.
    *   **Economic Impact**: While potentially improving AWS margins versus solely relying on third-party GPUs, the primary goal is often to provide better price-performance for customers, fostering a flywheel effect where lower costs enable more customer innovation.

### Addressing Infrastructure and Supply Chain Realities

*   **Physical Infrastructure Constraints**:
    *   **Scaling Disparity**: While software development accelerates, physical infrastructure build-out (e.g., pouring concrete, securing permits, power access) remains time-consuming and doesn't scale at the same pace.
*   **Space Data Centers**:
    *   **Theoretical Benefits**: Offers appeal with infinite power and easy cooling.
    *   **Current Impracticality**: Significant barriers include the immense weight of servers, lack of permanent space structures, limited rocket availability, and "massive", uneconomical payload costs for transport.
    *   **Bottleneck**: The primary constraint is the cost and availability of transport into space.
*   **"Full Stack" Control and Utilities**:
    *   **Evolving Role**: AWS's journey into chip manufacturing indicates a willingness to integrate vertically when necessary.
    *   **Utility Gap**: Traditional utility industries, often with fixed/regulated pricing and 3-5% annual growth, cannot scale to meet the exponential demand of hyperscalers.
    *   **AWS Involvement**: AWS may need to directly fund or develop "behind-the-meter" power solutions to bridge this gap, even if not directly operating power stations.
*   **Chip Cycle and Capacity Planning**:
    *   **Compression**: Chip cycle times are compressing (18-24 months), though constrained by manufacturing processes (e.g., TSMC).
    *   **Demand & Longevity**: High demand often means older chips (like A100s) remain fully utilized. Some use cases (e.g., HPC requiring higher precision) still depend on older generations.
    *   **Long-Term Planning**: AWS manages complex capacity planning across multiple time horizons, from 20-30 year data center amortization to 5-6 year server lifecycles, and manages supply chain risks across all levels of investment.

### Transforming Software Development with AI

*   **AI-Driven Coding Acceleration**:
    *   **Significant Gains**: Teams adopting an "AI-driven coding" mandate from the start see 10x to 100x improvements.
    *   **Benefits**: AI systems gain deep context, understand interdependencies, auto-document code, and facilitate comprehensive automated testing.
*   **Current Limitations and Future Outlook**:
    *   **Complex Legacy Systems**: AI struggles to provide the same speed-up for highly integrated, large-codebase legacy systems (e.g., S3), especially in lower-level stack areas like memory management.
    *   **Ongoing Development**: AWS expects to address legacy code challenges within 6-9 months by developing novel ways for AI systems to comprehend existing codebases.

### Navigating Sovereign AI and Safe Deployment

*   **Sovereign AI Infrastructure**:
    *   **Customer Concern**: European companies, for instance, express trust in AWS but concern over potential U.S. government intervention ("What if the U.S. government decides to turn me off?").
    *   **Prioritizing Resilience**: For critical sectors like banking and telecommunications, national resilience and control often outweigh pure technological advantage.
    *   **AWS Solution (EU Sovereign Cloud)**: Launched as a fully separate subsidiary within the EU, beholden to EU laws, with an independent governing board. All data, including metadata and logins, remains within EU regions, and the system has been tested for disconnection from the broader AWS backbone. This model was co-designed with regulatory bodies like BSI Germany.
*   **Advice for CIOs and CSOs (Accelerating Adoption)**:
    *   **"Guardrails Analogy"**: Just as handrails enable running across a narrow board over a canyon, robust security and operational guardrails allow teams to adopt AI rapidly and safely.
    *   **Mitigate Fear**: Address concerns that unleashing AI agents could lead to production outages or security incidents.
    *   **Empowerment through Safety**: Provide teams with "safe places to run fast" in production settings through AWS building blocks like Agent Core, which offer necessary controls and guardrails.
