### Defining the Borderless Founder
* **Inclusive Definition:** Anyone trying to build a global company internationally, whether they start building primarily in their home market or land directly in the US with global ambitions.
* **The "Chip on the Shoulder" Mindset:** 
  * Borderless founders often come from overlooked countries (e.g., Gabriel Vasquez growing up in El Salvador) or small rural towns, driving a desire to prove they can achieve greatness regardless of birthplace.
  * They contrast with traditional US founders who rely on "everything as a service" at every stack layer (e.g., fraud-as-a-stack, KYC-as-a-stack); international founders in regions like Latin America often have to build entire infrastructure stacks locally.
* **Geographic Expansion:** Beyond Latin America, a16z spends significant time in the UK, Sweden, Israel, and Germany.

## Kavak's Playbook for Rebuilding a Company Around AI

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n34CIw3gk1k
- Published: 2026-08-10
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260810.html#video-n34CIw3gk1k
- Speakers: Angela Strange (General Partner, a16z); Gabriel Vasquez (Host, a16z); Alejandro Maza Ayala (Chief Product & AI Officer, Kavak)

---

### TL;DR
Kavak transformed its used-car marketplace into an AI-native company where agents handle 96% of customer interactions and 95% of transactions, outperforming human sales teams by over 2x.

### Company Overview & Business Model
* **Core Operations:** **Kavak** operates as a vertically integrated used-car marketplace in Latin America, handling car purchases, refurbishment, sales, financing, personal loans, and logistics.
* **Shift from Transactional to Relational:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** explained that Kavak shifted from measuring basic transactional metrics (cars bought/sold) to managing 10 million customers in its database where long-running agents maximize customer lifetime value (LTV).
* **Scale of Agent Deployment:** Between 100,000 and 200,000 agents are instantiated daily, each equipped with its own virtual machine, multi-year memory, and hard goals.

### Engineering the Agentic Architecture
* **Agentic Harness Over Workflows:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** noted that after previously using multi-agent graphs and workflows (which worked well until December), Kavak transitioned to a robust harness where each customer gets an individual virtual machine, CLI access to every company API/tool, and a long-term goal of maximizing LTV.
* **Evals as the Primary Engine:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** emphasized that Kavak spends as much engineering time, tokens, and money on building evaluations (evals) as it does on building the agents themselves, measuring first and foremost true business conversion and customer happiness rather than superficial KPIs.
* **Token ROI Tiers:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** outlined Kavak's token expenditure framework:
  * *Tier 3 (Most Valuable):* Direct ROI tracking through agents performing core organizational jobs.
  * *Tier 2:* Indirect evaluation via developer output in codebases.
  * *Tier 1:* Unmeasured usage of generic plugins or chat tools (where ROI is unknown).

### Performance and Real-World Applications
* **Superhuman Sales Agents:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** stated that Kavak built complex sales agents—not just customer support bots—that handle 20,000 SKUs, financing, insurance, and trade-in quotes. These agents tripled NPS, initially converted 50% better than human teams, and now convert over **2.1x** better.
* **Financial Underwriting at Scale:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** noted that car loans are typically approved in under 3 minutes in Mexico due to deep customer and vehicle data integration.
* **The "AI CEO" Experiment:** To test organizational limits, Kavak deployed an AI agent as the CEO of a single city in Mexico (**Cuernavaca**). Over its first 6 weeks, the AI CEO aimed to double profits, ultimately achieving a **1.5x (50% increase)** in monthly profit by micromanaging forecasts, customer data, and issuing daily voice-note plans to 800 physical workers.
* **Human-Agent Collaboration in Mechanics:** **Gabriel Vasquez** and **Alejandro Maza Ayala** discussed "El Mike," a physical-world sidekick inspired by *Ratatouille* that guides Kavak's ~800 mechanics in Mexico through inspections and repairs, reducing warranties by **26%**.

### Organizational Transformation & Training
* **Top-Down Restructuring:** **Alejandro Maza Ayala** stressed that successful AI adoption requires a top-down redesign of APIs, systems, and company structure rather than simply handing ChatGPT to existing teams.
* **The Jedi Academy:** Kavak launched an internal training program called the Jedi Academy, ranging from the CEO and AI engineers to mechanics, where participants spend 6 weeks learning to build and launch state-of-the-art AI agents to production.
* **Human-in-the-Loop Feedback:** Rather than discarding failed agent interactions to a dead-end support queue, Kavak routes human-assisted exceptions back into building new skills and training data for the broader agent fleet.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Alejandro Maza Ayala** predicts that by **2035**, with the current rate of capability improvement, AI agents will be fully capable of performing complex leadership roles such as a company CEO.
* **Alejandro Maza Ayala** claims that companies looking for incremental AI adoption (achieving only 6% to 10% efficiency gains by keeping old factory-style structures) will be disrupted by new AI-native companies undergoing Schumpeterian creative destruction, drawing a parallel to how factories failed to gain 3x productivity until they fully redesigned around electricity and small dynamos rather than coal shafts and belts.
