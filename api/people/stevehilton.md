## Steve Hilton & Spencer Pratt: Fixing California, Cheaper Gas, Ballot Fraud & CA Republicans

**TL;DR:** Steve Hilton and Spencer Pratt discuss California's political and economic decay under one-party rule, detailing crippling regulations, high living costs, and policy failures, while outlining their respective insurgent campaigns and reform proposals.

### California's Economic & Regulatory Collapse
* **Economic Paradox and the "Resource Curse":** Steve Hilton notes that California is ranked 50th out of 50 for business climate by Chief Executive Magazine, suffers from the highest poverty and unemployment rates in the country, and has a median house price near $1 million against a median salary of $65,000. 
  - Hilton attributes the state's GDP ranking to Silicon Valley's wealth generation and a doubled state budget over 10 years, noting that net private sector job creation since the pandemic is "basically zero."
  - Peter Thiel's prior observation describes Silicon Valley as a "resource curse" that generates enough tax revenue to insulate the state government from good governance.
* **Regulatory Bloat and Legislative Overload:** The Sacramento legislature passed 1,118 bills in the prior year, creating over 420,000 total state regulations.
  - Hilton points out that a 2018 state law legalizing street vending across California preempts local governments, ruining long-standing tax-paying small businesses (such as a 14-year-old Latino-owned shoe store in South Central LA) while allowing unregulated, non-taxpaying vendors to operate without licenses or health codes.
* **Hidden Taxes and Government Waste:** 
  - Hilton highlights that programs like the Low Carbon Fuel Standard and the cap-and-invest program add about $1.50 to gas prices and fund projects like high-speed rail.
  - Vehicle registrations in California cost hundreds of dollars ($800–$1,000+), whereas they are under $100 in most other states.

### The Path to Victory & Proposed Reforms
* **Polling and Persuadable Voters:** Hilton outlines his polling metrics, showing a race where 33% are solidly with him, 41% with opponent Javier Becerra, and 26% up for grabs.
  - While starting from a 30-point gap on primary night, recent polls show the gap closing down to 8 points.
  - The target demographic consists of non-college-educated, regular working people who are at a financial breaking point.
* **Hilton's 100-Day Reform Agenda:** 
  - **Tax Cuts:** Cancel high-speed rail, cut government headcount by 10% with a 5% efficiency target, stop state funding for nonprofits worsening homelessness, and use the savings to make the first $150,000 of income tax-free.
  - **Energy Policy:** Utilize California energy resources, eliminate outdated regulations and hidden gas taxes, and drive gas prices down toward $3.
  - **DMV Reform:** Abolish the DMV and cap flat vehicle registration at $73 per vehicle per year.
  - **Regulatory Target:** Cut the number of state regulations from over 420,000 down to under 200,000 by the end of a first term.
  - **New Cities Initiative:** Publish a plan to build 10 new cities in California to spur housing and business development.
* **The Case Against Javier Becerra:** Hilton cites Biden administration figures to criticize opponent Javier Becerra, noting that domestic policy head Susan Rice described Becerra as an "idiot" and an "ass," while another cabinet member stated that running a large executive agency is not his skill set.

### LA Governance, Nonprofits, and the Infrastructure Crisis
* **MacArthur Park and Tax-Funded Subsidies:** Hilton and Spencer Pratt discuss visiting MacArthur Park and Langers Deli in LA, where open fentanyl use and drug distribution occur in broad daylight.
  - Hilton asserts that taxpayers are funding nonprofits that hand out crack pipes, needles, and drugs.
* **The Palisades Fire and Reservoir Neglect:** Spencer Pratt details the destruction of his home and neighborhood in the Pacific Palisades.
  - Pratt asserts that Mayor Karen Bass and the Department of Water and Power (DWP) failed to repair a reservoir cover that cost $200,000, leaving vital fire-suppression reservoirs empty.
  - Pratt states that the DWP was fined $1 million for cutting dead brush around a downed power pole due to strict state park environmental protections favoring plants over human safety.
  - A mass tort lawsuit with 10,000 victims has been filed against Gavin Newsom, state parks, the city, and the DWP regarding negligence surrounding an 8-acre New Year's Eve fire that was left unmonitored and smoldered into high winds on January 6–7.

### Elections, Ballot Integrity, and the "Machine"
* **The Machine vs. The People:** Hilton and Pratt characterize the California political apparatus as an entrenched, corrupt machine heavily funded by public-sector unions, trial lawyers, and special interests.
* **Pratt’s LA Mayor Race Experience:** Pratt describes his insurgent campaign for LA mayor, utilizing grassroots truth-telling and social media content to challenge incumbent Karen Bass and Democratic Socialist (DSA) candidates.
  - Pratt recounts leading on election night before mail-in ballots shifted the race toward the end, arguing that the mechanics of ballot harvesting and legal rules make it exceedingly difficult for political outsiders to compete without adopting similar mobilization tactics.

## CA Governor Candidate Steve Hilton on Why California is Destroying Itself & How a Republican Can Win

- Channel: All-In Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WQu7w1tII0
- Published: 2026-04-29
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260429.html#video-9WQu7w1tII0
- Speakers: Steve Hilton (Republican Candidate for CA Governor); Chamath Palihapitiya (Co-host, All-In Podcast); Jason Calacanis (Co-host, All-In Podcast)

---

**TL;DR**
Republican gubernatorial candidate Steve Hilton proposes a radical "pro-worker" overhaul of California, centered on eliminating state income tax for households earning under $100,000 and slashing $80 billion in annual government waste through a "California Department of Government Efficiency" (CalDOGE).

### Fiscal Reform & The "CalDOGE" Initiative
*   **Tax Structural Overhaul:** Hilton’s "pro-growth" plan eliminates state income tax for the first $100,000 of household income and implements a 7.5% flat tax for all income above that threshold.
    *   **Affordability Impact:** Approximately seven million households (over one-third of the state) would pay $0 in state income tax. 
*   **The CalDOGE Audit:** To fund tax cuts, Hilton plans to reduce state spending by 18.5%, returning the budget to 2019-2020 levels.
    *   **Fraud Identification:** Hilton claims to have identified $425 billion in "fraud, waste, and abuse" over the last five years.
    *   **Example of Mismanagement:** Out of $1 billion allocated for solar panels for low-income housing, only $72 million reached the actual projects; the remaining $928 million went to political nonprofits and "environmental justice" campaigns.
*   **Billionaire Tax Opposition:** Hilton warns that proposed wealth taxes are driving the tech ecosystem and "wealth creation" out of the state, putting California near an economic "cliff edge."

### Housing, Energy, and "Climate Dogma"
*   **The Cost of Building:** California homes cost 2–3x more to build than those in neighboring states due to three factors: union power, litigation (CEQA), and "climate dogma."
    *   **CEQA Litigation:** 70% of California Environmental Quality Act (CEQA) lawsuits are used to block housing. Hilton notes unions use these lawsuits as leverage to force "Project Labor Agreements" that mandate union-only shops at 2–3x market wages.
*   **Energy Incoherence:** California imports nearly 80% of its oil—primarily from Iraq and the Amazon rainforest—despite having abundant local reserves.
    *   **Fuel Prices:** High gas prices are driven by regulatory refusals to issue drilling permits. Hilton aims for "$3 gas" by doubling local production every two years.
    *   **Refinery Decay:** The state has dropped from 40 refineries to just seven, largely because current regulations make refining local "heavy crude" nearly impossible.

### Education and Public Safety
*   **Education Outcomes:** California spends roughly $27,000 per student annually, yet 65% of students fail to meet basic math standards and over 50% fail English standards.
    *   **Proposed Reforms:** Hilton advocates for universal "phonics-based" reading instruction and "school choice" to break the union monopoly.
    *   **Accountability:** Implementing a "repeat third grade" policy for students who cannot pass basic reading benchmarks, a model that successfully revitalized Mississippi’s education system.
*   **Crime and "Catch and Release":** Hilton argues that closing state prisons has forced violent criminals into overcrowded county jails, leading to a "catch and release" culture where local law enforcement feels paralyzed.
    *   **Policy Shift:** Stop the prison closure program and increase capacity to ensure criminals are punished and rehabilitated rather than cycled back onto the streets.
*   **Homelessness Mandates:** Hilton supports a "rehab or jail" approach for the 80% of the homeless population struggling with addiction or mental health issues.
    *   **Infrastructure:** Plans to build large-scale mental health facilities by waiving the federal "16-bed rule" (IMD rule) that currently prevents Medicaid reimbursement for large institutions.

### The Path to a Republican Victory
*   **Working-Class Coalition:** Hilton believes he can win by building a multi-racial, working-class coalition focused on "Cal-fordable" policies (lower gas, lower electric bills, and no tax on tips).
*   **The "Top Two" Primary:** Currently leading the field on the Republican side; he expects a general election matchup against a Democrat that will serve as a referendum on "Change vs. More of the Same."
*   **Voter Turnout Strategy:** Hilton notes that Donald Trump received 6.1 million votes in California in 2024 without campaigning; Hilton estimates he needs 5.9 million votes to win the 2026 midterm.
