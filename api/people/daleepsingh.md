## Rethinking Risk and Global Power with Jake Sullivan | The Outthinking Investor

- Channel: PGIM
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=snvBIpzaLnI
- Published: 2026-02-10
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260210.html#video-snvBIpzaLnI
- Speakers: Jake Sullivan (Professor, Harvard University); Daleep Singh (Host, PGIM)

---

**TL;DR:** The world has transitioned from the post-Cold War "unipolar moment" to an "old normal" of great power rivalry where economics and national security are inextricably linked, requiring the U.S. to move beyond punitive measures toward "offensive" economic statecraft like a national strategic investment fund.

### The Shift to the "Old Normal" and Spheres of Influence
*   **End of the "End of History":** For 30 years, global strategy assumed geopolitical and economic lanes were separate, but the world has reverted to a historical baseline defined by fragmentation, industrial policy, and great power competition.
*   **The Spheres of Influence Model:** Sullivan notes a shift toward a world where large powers dictate terms to smaller neighbors. 
    *   **The Trump Corollary:** Sullivan describes a "Trump corollary to the Monroe Doctrine," where the U.S. dominates the Western Hemisphere via coercion, a departure from traditional rules-based order.
    *   **Adversary Perceptions:** Chinese President Xi Jinping views recent U.S. military actions in Venezuela as confirmation of "imperial decline," believing a fading empire naturally lashes out at weaker neighbors.
*   **Geopolitical Signal:** While China feels empowered by the "spheres of influence" concept, Vladimir Putin remains unsettled by the unpredictability of current U.S. policy, despite his long-term desire for a carved-up world.

### The Technological Front Line: AI and Semiconductors
*   **The Nvidia H200 Controversy:** Sullivan criticizes the decision to license advanced GPUs to China in exchange for revenue cuts, arguing it gifts China a "generational leap" in AI.
    *   **The Compute Gap:** China possesses energy, talent, and resources but lacks "compute." Licensing H200s allows them to bridge this gap and train frontier models that compete directly with the U.S.
    *   **Market Realities:** Sullivan dismisses the argument that Nvidia needs the Chinese market to survive, noting Nvidia's market cap exploded even as its China revenue share dropped from 25% to 5%.
*   **China’s Strategy:** Beijing has issued binding directives to purge U.S. chips. Sullivan argues China uses Western chips only as a stopgap while pouring $150 billion into indigenizing its own tech stack.
*   **The "Czar" Influence:** Sullivan suggests U.S. hyperscalers are reluctant to speak out against Chinese chip sales for fear of crossing David Sacks, the administration's AI lead (the "Czar").

### Reimagining Economic Statecraft: The "Promote" Agenda
*   **"Protect" vs. "Promote":** The U.S. has become proficient at punitive tools (sanctions/controls) but lacks the "offensive" tools needed to build domestic strength and supply chain resilience.
*   **U.S. Strategic Investment Fund:** Sullivan advocates for a public, sovereign-wealth-style fund to crowd in private capital for "deep tech" (fusion, robotics, biotech) and "old economy" sectors like shipbuilding.
    *   **Governance:** The fund must be insulated from political cycles through a transparent public board, similar to the WWII-era Reconstruction Finance Corporation.
    *   **Additionality Principle:** To prevent "government stupidity," public funds should only be deployed when matched by private capital, ensuring market viability.
*   **China Shock 2.0:** Sullivan warns that without a proactive American model, China’s state-led control of "commanding heights" technologies will lead to massive market failures on the U.S. side.

### Risk Management and the "Human Element" of Statecraft
*   **Decision-making Frameworks:** Sullivan applies investment-style rigor to national security, utilizing "pre-mortems" (working backward from a hypothetical future failure) to identify current blind spots.
*   **The Risk Equilibrium:** Effective leadership requires shooting the gap between being "paralyzed by risk" and "dismissing risk" entirely.
*   **The "Situation Room" Reality:** Sullivan dispels the myth of a faceless, perfect "system" making global decisions.
    *   **Human-Centric Policy:** National security is "humans all the way down," driven by the specific collection of people in the room, their individual creative strengths, and their inherent weaknesses.
