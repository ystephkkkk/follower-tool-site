## $1.5B VC: “Founders need a savage mindset”

- Channel: My First Million
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AGwNx57ABfM
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-AGwNx57ABfM
- Speakers: Shaan Puri (Host, My First Million); James Currier (Co-founder, NFX)

---

**TL;DR:** NFX co-founder James Currier ($1.5B VC) argues that speed is the ultimate startup advantage—rolling up all 48 founder traits he looks for—and that achieving it requires founders to adopt a "savage," emotionally fluid mindset, set aggressive time constraints, copy proven markets, and master the "magic words" that align teams and customers.

---

### The Speed Advantage & Breaking the "Speed Bar"
* **The Ultimate Metric:** Across 25 years of startups and investing $1.5B at NFX, James Currier found that all 48 attributes they look for in founders roll up into one metric: **speed**.
  * **Chess Analogy:** Shaan Puri noted that extra resources (like extra rooks) won't help you beat a grandmaster like Magnus Carlsen, but moving twice for every one move they make will.
* **Resetting the Speed Bar:** School and large corporations train people to move slowly (semesters, waiting for the bottom 10–20% of a class, 9-month corporate cycles).
  * **Mike Cassidy Benchmark:** In the 1990s, Cassidy sold a company for $500M in 500 days by building prototypes in 3 weeks (vs. 12 months), raising money in 3 days (vs. 3 months) by lining up VCs in 36 hours, and hiring in 72 hours (vs. 6 months) with offers ready at the interview.
* **Time Constraints Over Outputs:** Instead of asking how long a dream product will take, set an arbitrary deadline (e.g., 30 days) and ask what you can build within it.
  * **Currier’s Incubator:** Launched 24 products in 3 years at 1/10th the cost and time of competitors ($600K in 2 months vs. $6M in 1.5 years), yielding 3 successful companies (including early versions of Yammer/Monday.com and Nextdoor).
  * **Meerkat & Houseparty:** After wasting 18 months and 8 months on failed products, Currier forced the Yeo team to whiteboard 5 products (named by colors) to launch every 30 days. "Yellow" became Meerkat (built in 60 days); when Twitter crushed it, they pivoted immediately to "Blue," which became Houseparty and sold.
  * **8 Days LLC:** Hot or Not founder James Hong named his company "8 Days" and refused to build any product taking longer than 8 days.
* **The "Fuck It" Moment:** Founders often make their best, fastest, and simplest decisions when they are nearly out of money (e.g., Nikita Bier with TBH, Dan Porter’s OMG Pop pivoting to *Draw Something* in the final 30 days and selling for ~$200M). Currier hates hearing founders say they have 36 months of runway; they need the urgency of 3 months.
* **Speed is Quality:** V1 will be low quality, but moving fast lets you reach V6 in the same time a perfectionist takes to ship V1.
  * **Hardware Proof:** SpaceX (which raised $10B vs. NASA's hundreds of billions) and Stoke Space blow up rockets to iterate faster. Elon Musk built the Colossus data center in Nashville in 18 months vs. the industry standard of 3–5 years.
  * **Job Title as a Tell:** Currier advises teams to fire their VP of Product, whose ego is tied to product perfection, and instead pair the CEO (VP of Progress) directly with designers.

### Borrowing, Copying, and Market Risk
* **Inventing Nothing:** Currier argues that **Google** and **Facebook** invented almost nothing; they copied existing products, nailed distribution/branding, and hired better.
  * **Google:** Copied search from Infoseek (1994), maps from MapQuest, email from Hotmail, and bought AdSense for $80M. They won via distribution deals (AOL, MSN) and branding.
  * **Facebook:** Copied Friendster, the Winklevosses' `.edu` real-name exclusivity, Bebo's newsfeed (which Currier’s team had 18 months prior), Snap Stories, and TikTok.
  * **Bebo:** Michael Birch and Currier launched from the exact same codebase (Birch grew to 53M users, Currier to 47M); Birch later pivoted his into a social network and sold it for $850M.
* **Execution Risk vs. Market Risk:** After PayPal, Elon Musk avoided market risk—people already bought cars (Tesla) and paid $400M to launch satellites (SpaceX)—and focused purely on execution/technology risk. Similarly, Airbnb only worked when it copied VRBO's whole-home rental model (charging 10% instead of $300/month).

### Founder Psychology & The "Savage" Mindset
* **Gladiator Sport:** Entrepreneurship is a psychologically violent environment. Raising VC locks you into a "prison" until an exit or shutdown. Competition has exploded from 150 VCs in 1994 to 35,000 today.
* **Savage Founders vs. Wantrepreneurs:** Since *The Social Network* (2010), being a founder became high-status. "Savage founders" are ultra-competitive, fast, and disagreeable—often shaped by childhood trauma (e.g., bad father relationships) or being outsiders (autistic, gay).
  * **Avoid Comfort:** Currier refuses to invest in startups coming out of cushy, subsidized accelerators or government-funded spaces. Comfort breeds weakness; startups should stay in gritty environments (like Bezos's door-desks).
* **Emotional Fluidity:** Currier’s top coaching lever (often trading 1–2% equity to coach CEOs on growth) is founder psychology. Founders must let fear, shame, and ego pass through them quickly.
  * **90 Seconds of Suffering:** Shaan Puri shared Tony Robbins's rule of giving yourself a strict 90-second budget to fully feel negative emotions (anger, shame) before moving on.
* **"Kick Ass and Get Your Ass Kicked":** Founders must surround themselves with people smarter than them and explicitly invite brutal feedback.
  * **VCs as Free Consultants:** Since 90% of VCs will say no, founders should use pitch meetings to ask open-ended questions (*"What am I missing?"*) to uncover blind spots.

### The Power of "Magic Words"
* **Language Drives Everything:** Whether selling B2B SaaS (selling a PowerPoint) or pitching LPs, words trigger budgets and action.
  * **NFX's $150M Raise:** Despite having 6 unicorns out of 70 accelerator companies (5–7x better than YC), 6 LPs rejected NFX because "accelerators are a bad asset class." After attending an LP conference in Boston, Currier rewrote the deck to say NFX was *just like every other VC* (with better deal access) and raised $150M in 6 weeks.
* **Words Before Product:** Figure out the magic words first, then build the product to match.
  * **Honda Accord:** Spent 8 months aligning on the phrase *"rugby player in a tuxedo,"* leading to 8–12 years as America's bestselling car.
  * **Currier’s Photo Site:** Changed homepage copy from *"Store your photos"* to *"Share your photos,"* forcing engineers to build sharing features in 48 hours and igniting growth.
  * **Lemonade Stand:** Changing *"Lemonade 20¢"* to *"Lemonade Cleanse $20"* completely alters perceived value.

### AI, Psychedelics, and the Five Lenses
* **AI Trajectory:** Currier expects 12 more years of "more and better" (more/better companies, healthcare, podcasts) rather than mass unemployment.
  * **The Real Risk:** Not killer robots, but humans using AI to create biological weapons.
  * **Silicon-Based Life:** Referencing Hans Moravec’s *Mind Children* (1991), Currier views AI as our "mind children"—a superior silicon species operating at lower wattage (e.g., 60 watts vs. 3,500 calories/day) and extreme temperatures (-273°C) that will eventually feel things humans cannot (like Earth's 4,500 Richter scales).
* **Psychedelics:** Currier sees a "dam bursting" for non-addictive, opening chemicals: psilocybin (curing suicidal depression), MDMA (originally created by the US government for Vietnam PTSD; saving marriages in 4 hours vs. 15 years of therapy), and 5-MeO-DMT (synthesized from Sonoran frog venom, currently in Phase 3 FDA trials backed by $400M in VC funding).
* **The Five Lenses of Reality:**
  1. **Status:** The atomic kernel of the brain (*better than / worse than*), driving all human suffering, politics, and VC brand selection.
  2. **Networks:** Driven by *preferred attachment* (the Matthew Principle—the rich get richer, e.g., Forbes putting Mark Andreessen on the cover over #1 investor Alfred Lin).
  3. **Energy:** All living things and geopolitics (e.g., Japan in WWII, US in Venezuela) are fights for energy.
  4. **Language:** The battle for magic words.
  5. **Mental Models:** The underlying assumptions (e.g., Spain's youth unemployment tied to expecting government jobs vs. US "bootstraps" mentality) that drive behavior and wars.

### The Modern Malaise: Technology, Addiction, and Disconnection

- **Breaking the Cycle:** 
  - **Cold Turkey Withdrawal:** Leave the phone behind, lock it in a drawer, and go outside into nature where you are forced to interact with the non-human world.

- **Viktor Frankl & Concentration Camp Survival:** 
  - **Meaning as Survival:** In Auschwitz, survivors were overwhelmingly those who had a distinct purpose, such as a book to write or a family member to save.

- **Core Lessons from *The 48 Laws of Power*:** 
  - **Say Less Than Necessary:** In the social media era, people reveal too much; power requires a balance of absence and presence.
  - **Disappear:** Celebrities like Beyoncé disappear for a year and a half before dropping a record, letting speculation build an air of mystery.
- **The Psychology of Charisma:** 
  - **Childhood Trauma Origin:** Many charismatic figures (e.g., Marilyn Monroe, Malcolm X) come from deeply troubled or abandoned backgrounds, making them outerdirected and desperate for public love.
  - **Confidence as a Core Trait:** True charisma stems from inner security and an intense drive behind a cause, whereas fake confidence reads as empty bravado.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **AI and Legal Professions:** AI will "decimate" the legal profession because individuals will be able to get answers to any legal problem instantly, as stated by Robert Greene.
- **AI and Negative Human Nature:** The dark side of human nature (irrationality, grandiosity, phishing, scams, and the creation of biological weapons/dirty bombs) will increasingly hijack AI technology over the coming years, according to Robert Greene.

### Professional Background & Trading Philosophy
- **Origin of Wealth:** Bill Perkins made his initial capital trading natural gas derivatives and commodities, starting as a clerk on the New York Mercantile Exchange floor before moving to Houston, Texas, in 1995 to run a derivatives desk.
- **The Role of a Trader:** Perkins compares traders to glorified insurance agents who do not compete directly with other smart players, but instead take on risk based on market distribution, fair value, and theoretical edge.
- **Shorting Strategy:** Most of Perkins's wealth came from being short on natural gas over time, capturing profits as winter risk premiums bled out.
- **Career Regrets and Advice:** Perkins notes that while he made close to a billion dollars in profit for his employer, he regrets not concentrating his capital and maximizing risk even more aggressively when he was young, stating he should have bet the farm since he could always get another job. - **Investment Success Factors:** Combining insights from guests like Howard Marks and Ray Dalio, Perkins identifies superior temperament (stoicism during losses) and an informational edge as the core drivers of trading success, avoiding pure arbitrage.

### Time Bucketing vs. The Traditional Bucket List
- **The Flaw of a Traditional Bucket List:** Waiting until the end of life or retirement to check off adventures fails because physical health and energy decline with age.
- **Life as Tetris:** Perkins argues that life consists of multiple distinct periods (or "deaths" of past versions of oneself), and specific experiences (like skiing 500 times or backpacking) must be scheduled into optimal age brackets.
- **Net Worth Peak as a Date, Not a Number:** Because the body is a decaying asset whose ability to convert money into fulfillment approaches zero near death, net worth peaks are best defined by a timeline rather than an arbitrary financial figure.

### Breaking Cultural Programming & Maximizing Accountability
- **Cultural Osmosis:** Perkins realized that many personal desires—such as career ladders, white picket fences, and lifestyle milestones—are merely default cultural programming inherited from society and movies.
- **Ego as the Enemy of Risk:** People often avoid taking life chances not because of financial risk, but due to ego, fear of status drops, and worry about societal judgment.
- **Maximum Accountability:** True personal growth requires treating oneself with radical honesty, adopting an internal locus of control, and rewriting past victim stories with 100% personal ownership.

### The Art Collector and Unique Investments
- **The Sugar Shack Acquisition:** Perkins flew to Sotheby's and purchased Ernie Barnes’ historically significant painting *The Sugar Shack* (famous from Marvin Gaye's album and *Good Times*) for **$15 million**, dramatically resetting the market for African-American masters.
- **The Art Market Arbitrage:** Perkins views collecting as a way to signal to future generations what is worth preserving, noting an historical arbitrage caused by past racism in the art community that undervalued foundational African-American works.

### Current Ventures, Health, and Future Goals
- **Current Business Portfolio:** Perkins runs two startups (**SkyFi**, a geospatial intelligence platform acting as an "Uber for satellites," and **Simax**, an intelligence firm) while continuing to manage a hedge fund.
- **Health Adherence Dissatisfaction:** Perkins expresses dissatisfaction with his current health metrics (body fat around 16–18% and diminished VO2 max compared to his peak at 8% body fat), noting he is currently "screwing over future Bill" and looking to hire a traveling trainer to enforce accountability.
- **The Farm Vision:** Ultimately, Perkins plans to transition into a quieter chapter centered around a large garden, beekeeping, family, and sharing memories.

2. **Inside the Mind and Management Style of Jeff Bezos**
   - **Flaws and Evolution:** **Constructive Criticism Only:** Early on, Bezos struggled with giving praise, focusing exclusively on corrective feedback. Over time, he worked with an executive coach to intentionally add positive reinforcement.
   - **Attack Mode & Accountability:** **Relentless Problem-Solving:** When frustrated by failures, Bezos often went into attack mode—not just at the team, but out of frustration with himself. For example, after a poorly received price-matching app idea, he expressed disbelief at the oversight.

   - **Anecdotes Over Data:** Bezos famously prioritized customer anecdotes over macro data, noting that even a 99% success rate at Amazon scale still leaves 25 million unhappy customers a day.
   - **The Twitch Acquisition:** Amazon acquired Twitch for ~$970 million. Driven by Amazon stock 5Xing during the earnout, engineer vest values spiked to $2 million/year, leading to a massive cliff and attrition when salaries normalized.

   - **Post-Amazon Happiness:** Ethan Evans reported his happiness jumped from a 7/10 at Amazon ($900k–$1M+ target comp working 50–60 hours/week) to an 11/12 working for himself (making $700k–$800k working ~20 hours/week).

## How to find your arbitrage (from the guy who’s done it 3x)

- Speakers: Shaan Puri (Host, My First Million); Nick Kokonas (Founder, Tock and Alinea)

---

#### TL;DR
Nick Kokonas reveals how he mastered multiple fields—from options trading and building the world-class restaurant Alinea to creating the software company Tock—by viewing every industry through the lens of time-scale arbitrage, intense daily execution, and adjacent-industry theft.

---

#### Trading Floor Origins & The Art of Arbitrage

* **The Reality of High-Stakes Trading:** 
  * **Capital Scarcity:** Nick Kokonas started trading with just $60,000 ($20,000 of his own money and $40,000 from a backer), which he describes as drastically undercapitalized for the floor.
  * **The 1-Year Goal:** Kokonas set a hard target to be trading on a badge within one year of taking a $5.25/hour runner job at the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, hitting his goal almost to the day.
  * **Day 1 Trial by Fire:** On his first day in the pits, the strict rule was that he had to complete at least one trade in a zero-sum, hostile, and chaotic physical environment to break the psychological seal.
* **Bypassing the 30-Second Gap:**
  * **The Time-Scale Arb:** Recognizing that a trade execution process took 30 to 40 seconds via paper tickets passed between New York and Chicago, Kokonas sought to bridge the gap directly.
  * **Regulatory Scrutiny:** By meticulously reading the voluminous CFTC rules—which lawyers and board members rarely read in full—he discovered that direct communication between identical firm tax IDs was not explicitly barred.
  * **Union Workarounds:** When the Merc blocked dedicated fiber-optic line permits to protect broker group interests, Kokonas bribed union cable workers with cases of Jameson to run the line.
  * **Peak Revenue:** The direct telecommunications link generated instant, zero-risk arbitrage profits, scaling to over $100 million in trading revenue at its peak.

#### Restaurant Innovation & Alinea's Blueprint

* **Open-Sourcing a Masterpiece:**
  * **Building in Public:** Before launching Alinea, Kokonas created the "Alinea Project" forum thread on eGullet, open-sourcing the business plan, name choices, and menu development.
  * **Marketing Leverage:** The public documentation generated hundreds of thousands of views and early demand, securing heavy press coverage from the *New York Times*, *Wall Street Journal*, and *Gourmet Magazine* before opening day.
  * **The Michelin Goal:** The explicit target was to build the best restaurant in America and make it so exceptional that the Michelin Guide (which had not yet expanded to the U.S.) would look bad for omitting it.
* **Operations, Margins, and Scale:**
  * **The Universal Restaurant Calculator:** Kokonas built a single-tab spreadsheet with roughly 80 cells to track every possible cost input, realizing that the low barrier to entry causes most restaurants to fail due to poor business fundamentals.
  * **Financial Success:** Unlike the popular narrative that restaurants only net 2-3%, Alinea scaled to roughly $20 million in annual revenue with 18% to 20% profit margins, ultimately selling in 2023 for an 8x return on initial investment (totaling roughly 27x to 28x overall with quarterly distributions).

#### Software & The Tock Revolution

* **Solving the Host Stand:**
  * **The KYC Problem:** Kokonas founded Tock out of frustration with OpenTable, which acted as an intermediary and left restaurants completely blind to who was walking through their doors (Know Your Customer).
  * **Dynamic Pricing:** Tock introduced variable pricing for time-slotted businesses, matching the reality that a Tuesday 9:00 PM table has fundamentally different demand than a Saturday 7:00 PM table.
* **The M&A Playbook:**
  * **Diligence Control:** When selling Tock during COVID-19, Kokonas, along with co-founder Jeff Kaplan and CFO Steve Bernaki, converted an 896-question diligence list into a Google Doc and color-coded it.
  * **Bypassing Intermediaries:** By refusing to act as passive passengers and completing the rigorous data request in just three days, they saved millions in advisory fees while keeping the deal on track to close before the buyer's Q1 S1 filing deadline.

#### Stealing from Adjacencies: Magic & Theater

* **Borrowing from Magicians:**
  * **Attention Control:** Instead of reading standard hospitality books, Kokonas studied magic books by authors like Nate Stanforth and Darren Brown to understand how magicians handle attention, sight lines, and audience positioning.
  * **Service Positioning:** Applying magical principles, Alinea servers were trained to stand *alongside* the diner rather than directly across from them, creating a collaborative "same team" dynamic.
* **The "Strange House" Venture:**
  * **Immersive Illusion:** Kokonas partnered with magician Nate Stanforth and game designer Alistair Lee to build *Strange House*—a 90,000-square-foot purpose-built experiential venue beneath Wall Street.
  * **Human Connection:** The project aims to combat societal isolation by leveraging theatrical staging, psychological illusion, and Disney-style room throughput to evoke childlike wonder and foster deep human connection among strangers.

High-performance coach Joe Hudson breaks down the mental, emotional, and nervous system patterns of elite tech founders with hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr, explaining how to untangle self-reliance, conflict avoidance, and negative self-talk through counter-intuitive emotional acceptance and weekly experimentation.

### Mind, Heart, and Nervous System: The Tri-Level Framework
* **The Three Levels of Transformation:** Real personal or professional change requires addressing three distinct systems rather than just intellectual understanding:
  * **The Mind (Prefrontal Cortex):** The easiest level for high-achievers to grasp intellectually, though knowing what to do rarely translates to doing it.
  * **The Heart (Mammalian/Emotional):** The emotional center where underlying fears and avoided feelings must be processed.
  * **The Nervous System (Reptilian):** The primal, physical layer (triggering a physical startle response to a Slack message or a late arrival) which changes not through a-has, but via repetition and expanding baseline pleasure.
* **The Self-Reliance Pattern:** Traced back to childhood where individuals learned they had to fend for themselves and others (e.g., dealing with critical or addicted parents), creating adults who:
  * Struggle to ask for help, own their desires, or announce when they are hurt.
  * Harbor a complicated relationship with authority—both rebelling against external authorities and refusing to fully step into their own.
* **The Golden Algorithm of Emotion:** The emotion you are trying to avoid is the exact emotion you end up inviting into your life by the way you try to avoid it.
  * *Example:* Avoiding the feeling of helplessness by over-grinding leads to burnout, poor decisions, and ultimately, a deeper state of helplessness.
  * *Solution:* Fall in love with the emotions you are avoiding; treat them as messengers and signals rather than enemies.

### Rewiring Self-Talk and Negative Thought Loops
* **Abusive Self-Talk Patterns:** High-achievers are frequently their own worst abusers, trapped in loops of overt criticism ("Why did you do that?"), endless pressure ("You've got to do this now"), or subtle undermining ("You can do it, Mike").
* **Reaction Over Content:** Trying to change the self-talk directly is inefficient; instead, change your *reaction* to the self-talk by treating it playfully or as bad management (e.g., responding to an internal critique with, "Oh, I see how scared you are. Prove it.").
* **Debunking the Narrative:** List the top 20 things the negative voice in your head says and find at least 7 concrete ways each is false (or ask friends to help prove it wrong).

### The Psychology of Founders and Conflict Avoidance
* **Conflict as Progress:** Avoiding conflict in a marriage or business is equivalent to ignoring a blinking warning light on a factory machine; conflict is the exact mechanism through which efficiency and alignment are gained.
* **Organizational Health Metrics:** Joe Hudson evaluates corporate health using just two metrics: how quickly an organization has hard conversations, and how quickly it resolves them.
* **Conducting Hard Conversations:** 
  * Do not solve the problem for the person (which is just a manifestation of CEO self-reliance); instead, ask them to present a plan to solve it by tomorrow.
  * Prioritize understanding their context via open-ended questions before delivering feedback.
  * Express your own vulnerability (e.g., "I feel like we've lost trust and that sucks for me") rather than making it entirely about attacking the other person.
* **The Psyche of Top 1% Performers:**
  * Exceptional fast-paced CEOs rarely second-guess themselves because the velocity of decision-making demands decisiveness.
  * Approximately 60% to 70% of high performers harbor an underlying paradox: they simultaneously feel superior to everyone else (conscious mind) and deeply broken (underneath), using achievement to prove they are not broken.

### Practical Weekly Experiments for Growth
* **The 10% Enjoyment Experiment:** Take what you are already doing and find a way to enjoy it 5% to 10% more without changing your actual actions, doing this roughly 5 times a day for a week.
* **The Do-Only-What-You-Enjoy Week:** Commit to doing only what you enjoy for a week; when forced to confront an unenviable task (like taking out the trash), figure out how to genuinely enjoy the process rather than enduring it.
* **The Gratitude Shift:** Consciously look around every day and thank 5 people for what they did for you to actively dismantle the self-reliance pattern.
* **Visualization of the Worst-Case Scenario:** Like samurai or stoics visualizing their own death or the destruction of their company, thoroughly feel the worst-case outcomes (such as homelessness or corporate collapse) so that fear no longer dictates your business decisions.
