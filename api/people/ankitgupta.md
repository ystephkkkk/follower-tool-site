## How Meesho Became India’s Biggest Shopping App

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49L8lVe_PVo
- Published: 2026-06-11
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260611.html#video-49L8lVe_PVo
- Speakers: Vidit Aatrey (Co-Founder & CEO, Meesho); Ankit Gupta (General Partner, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
Meesho became India's dominant Android shopping app by relentlessly focusing on mass-market consumers and pivoting its business model through five distinct iterations—transitioning from a localized directory to a WhatsApp social commerce tool, and finally to a direct-to-consumer e-commerce giant processing 2.5 billion annual orders.

### Meesho's Scale and Market Dominance
* **Massive Transaction Volumes:** Meesho currently processes **2.5 billion orders per year**, with its user base averaging 10 purchases annually. 
* **Unrivaled User Base:** The platform has **250 million unique annual buyers** (growing at over 30% year-over-year) and close to **1 million active sellers**.
* **Market Share:** Out of India's estimated 350 to 400 million annual online shoppers, more than half purchase on Meesho.
* **App Store Leadership:** Since launching its direct-to-consumer app on July 5, 2021, Meesho has held the **#1 position in the shopping section of India's Android Play Store every single day**.

### The Journey of Iterations: From FashNear to Meesho Supply
* **The Core Thesis:** Co-founders Vidit Aatrey and Sanjeev Barnwal launched the company in 2015 to bring e-commerce to "mass India" outside of major tech hubs like Bangalore, where online shopping was virtually non-existent.
* **Version 1 (FashNear):** A localized directory to help physical fashion shops sell to nearby consumers. 
  * *The Failure:* The team shut it down in three months after realizing they had only spoken to sellers and ignored the consumer experience, which ended up offering the worst of both physical malls and online shopping.
* **Version 2 (Meesho - "Meri Shop"):** Developed during their YC Summer 2016 batch, this software helped physical shopkeepers manage inventory and collect payments over WhatsApp groups. 
  * *The Monetization Bottleneck:* While hundreds of thousands of shops used it, local Indian businesses were unwilling to pay for software.
* **Version 3 (Meesho Supply / Product-Market Fit):** The founders noticed "power users" who were online-native drop-shippers (mostly homemakers looking for zero-upfront-cost businesses). They launched a separate app to connect these resellers with wholesale suppliers.
  * *Organic Explosion:* With zero spend on marketing due to extreme capital constraints, Meesho Supply doubled in transaction volume every single month for 10 consecutive months, prompting the founders to kill the original app and rebrand.

### The High-Stakes D2C Pivot in 2021
* **The Paradigm Shift:** By 2020, cheap mobile data (driven by Reliance Jio) and the COVID-19 pandemic forced mass-market Indian consumers to learn how to shop online directly, making the data-saving, WhatsApp-mediated model obsolete.
* **The Hard Decision:** Despite having **10 million active entrepreneurs** running WhatsApp groups on their platform, Vidit and his team realized they had to bypass these intermediaries and go direct-to-consumer (D2C). 
* **The Boardroom Risk:** Investors were hesitant and wanted a slow experiment, but Vidit committed fully, knowing a half-hearted pivot would alienate sellers without winning over consumers.
* **The Result:** Meesho launched its consumer app in July 2021. Within five months, it scaled from 10 million users to **100 million Monthly Active Users (MAUs)**. Competitors who failed to pivot quickly went out of business or were acquired.

### The Next Frontier: AI-Driven "Invisible" Commerce
* **Targeting the Next 750 Million:** To expand from 250 million users to 1 billion, Meesho must onboard rural demographics who struggle with digital literacy, app navigation, and written search.
* **Voice-First Shopping with "Vani":** Meesho recently launched a voice AI agent named *Vani* designed to eliminate the need to read, type, or click buttons. 
  * *The Workflow:* Consumers navigate the platform entirely through voice prompts and sliding images, speaking their address and OTP verbally to complete orders.
* **The Strategy:** The company prioritizes long-term consumer accessibility over legacy software patterns. Vidit predicts that within a few years, e-commerce may not even require a traditional app interface, but rather a ubiquitous, conversational voice agent.

## Why Two IIT Engineers Turned Down $550K Jobs To Build A Startup

---

**TL;DR** 
Varun Vummadi turned down a $550,000 quant finance offer and a Stanford PhD to build GigaML, eventually leveraging Y Combinator's network and a lean 8-person team to secure major enterprise clients like DoorDash over well-funded, 400-person competitors.

---

### The Non-Linear Path to Product-Market Fit
* **Rejecting Elite Safety Nets:** Driven by the launch of ChatGPT, Varun Vummadi and his co-founder turned down highly lucrative opportunities—including a $550,000 New York City quant trading offer and a Stanford PhD program—just three days before they were set to begin.
* **The YC Pivots:** 
    * The founders originally entered Y Combinator with an LLM-based ed-tech idea.
    * During the interview, YC Partner Harj Taggar told them the ed-tech market was unviable but accepted them anyway due to their strong engineering backgrounds.
    * After briefly pivoting to an LLM fine-tuning and caching service (which topped Hugging Face benchmarks and raised a $4 million seed round), they noticed their only growing customer use cases were coding and customer support. 
    * This insight led them to pivot fully into AI-driven customer support, signing Zepto as their first major customer.

### Defeating Giants: How a Lean Team Won DoorDash
* **Beating a 400-Person Competitor:** Operating as a lean team of just eight people, GigaML went head-to-head with a massive, heavily funded incumbent competitor to win DoorDash's enterprise customer service contract.
* **The YC Network Multiplier:** To get his foot in the door, YC President Garry Tan introduced Varun directly to DoorDash CEO Tony Xu. Because both startups were YC alumni, an inherent layer of trust allowed GigaML to bypass traditional enterprise sales friction and secure a three-month pilot.
* **Superior Deflection Metrics:** 
    * Traditional customer support IVR and chatbots yield a 10% to 15% deflection rate.
    * GigaML's conversational AI agents achieve a 60% to 70% deflection rate, with target goals of 90% to 95% for top-tier enterprise clients.
    * The core of their technical edge relies on "iterating markdown policy files" to consistently move customer KPIs like CSAT and resolution rates.

### Driving Hyper-Leverage via Internal AI Automation
* **A 7x Engineering Force Multiplier:** GigaML keeps its internal headcount incredibly low by heavily utilizing coding agents. Varun estimates that without tools like Claude, GigaML would require six to seven times more engineers to maintain their current shipping velocity.
* **"Vibe Coding" Interview Tactics:** GigaML tests job candidates by allowing them to "vibe code" alongside AI assistants during the interview. To ensure deep technical understanding, they then strip away the AI tools and ask candidates to manually debug and modify the generated codebase.
* **Enterprise AI Deployments:** Varun identifies the traditional "forward deployed engineer" as the primary bottleneck in enterprise AI adoption. GigaML is currently building an autonomous "AI forward deployed engineer" designed to join Slack channels and Google Meets, synthesize notes, and automatically deploy system changes.

### Hard-Earned Advice for College Founders
* **Burn the Boats:** Varun attributes their survival to the psychological pressure of rejecting their high-paying job offers. Having no fallback plan forced them to make the business work.
* **Charge Customers Early:** Startups should avoid offering free pilots for too long. If a business problem is painful enough, a customer will pay real money or time for it. If they refuse to pay, the founder is solving a "fake problem."
* **Product Over Sales:** In the generative AI era, product quality dictates success. Top AI companies like OpenAI and Anthropic scale rapidly due to value-delivery, not aggressive sales commission structures.

## Beyond Bigger Models: Recursion As The Next Scaling Law In AI

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DGtUUMNYLcc
- Published: 2026-05-01
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260501.html#video-DGtUUMNYLcc
- Speakers: Ankit Gupta (Y Combinator); Francois Chaubard (Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR:** Recursive AI models like Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM) and Tiny Recursive Models (TRM) achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance—outperforming models 1,000x their size on tasks like the ARC Prize—by utilizing weight reuse and latent memory states at inference time to bypass the "reasoning ceiling" of standard feed-forward LLMs.

### The Structural Limits of Standard LLMs
* **Feed-Forward Constraints:** While modern LLMs excel at next-token prediction, they are essentially one-shot feed-forward processes at training time. This limits their ability to solve "incompressible" problems that require iterative computation.
* **The Sorting Analogy:** Francois Chaubard explains that a transformer with 30 layers cannot solve a comparison sort for a 31-element list because the problem has a theoretical lower bound of $N \log N$ steps. Standard models literally "run out of layers" to complete the necessary logic.
* **Discrete vs. Latent Reasoning:** 
    * **Chain of Thought (CoT):** This is a "hack" that forces recursion in the visible token space, but it is limited by human-labeled data and training traces.
    * **Recursive Latent Space:** True recursive models reason within a continuous, high-dimensional latent space (memory), making them more expressive and efficient than models that must "snap back" to discrete tokens.
* **The Einstein Test:** Standard models are bounded by the limits of human knowledge. They can use tools (like a Python sort function) because they have seen them before, but they struggle to discover new algorithms (e.g., discovering Merge Sort if they only knew Bubble Sort).

### Hierarchical Reasoning Models (HRM)
* **Architecture:** Inspired by the human brain's varying processing frequencies, HRM uses three nested levels of recursion:
    * **Low-level (L-net):** Operates at high frequency for local features.
    * **High-level (H-net):** Operates at a lower frequency for broader context.
    * **Outer Refinement Loop:** Scales the reasoning depth at inference time.
* **The "Stop Grad" Innovation:** To avoid the "vanishing gradient" problem common in old Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), HRM uses a fixed-point iteration method. 
    * **Memory Persistence:** The model performs 16 iterations but only backpropagates through a small portion, essentially treating different memory states as a "mini-batch" rather than resetting activations to zero.
* **Performance:** With only 27 million parameters and no pre-training, HRM achieved ~70% on the ARC Prize, while massive models like o3 initially scored zero.

### Tiny Recursive Models (TRM): Complexity through Simplification
* **Model Efficiency:** TRM refines the HRM approach by collapsing the dual networks into a single shared-weight transformer layer. Despite having only 7 million parameters, it outperforms HRM.
* **Architectural Shifts:**
    * **Weight Sharing:** TRM uses the same network for both high and low-level features, proving that variable scoping (distinct memory states) is more important than having distinct physical layers.
    * **Deep Supervision:** It utilizes "truncated backprop through time," backpropagating through one full recursive loop rather than a single step, which significantly boosts performance.
* **Task Specificity:** While small, these models are currently task-specific. A TRM trained for Sudoku cannot inherently solve ARC puzzles, marking a departure from the general-purpose nature of LLMs.

### The Future of Scaling: Depth over Width
* **Compute Depth:** The "next scaling law" involves increasing compute depth (recursion steps) rather than just parameter width. 
* **Merging Paradigms:** The ultimate frontier in AI research is combining the representation power of giant foundation models (like Gemini or GPT-4) with the recursive reasoning engines of TRMs.
* **Latent Reasoning:** Future architectures will likely move reasoning into the latent "embedding" space rather than relying on the inefficient, token-by-token Chain of Thought method.

### Notable Quotes

> "It is actually impossible for the [standard LLM] model to map from an unsorted list to a sorted list on a one-shot basis... if I have a list that's 31 elements long and my transformer is 30 [layers], I run out of steps to do comparisons." — **Francois Chaubard**

> "This is why this paper is so exciting: [TRM] is a 7-million parameter model that can solve what a trillion-parameter model can't solve, even when the latter is trained on the entire internet." — **Francois Chaubard**

> "In the case of RNNs in general, they remain in this continuous latent space which is much, much higher dimensional... It’s much more expressive to be in continuous space, but we couldn't train it that way because you're inhibited by backprop through time." — **Ankit Gupta**

> "We found mapping from pixels to some really cool latent space where things are nicely semantically separated... But now in that space, use these tiny reasoning models—use some type of recursion inside that—and I think that’s going to really work." — **Francois Chaubard**
