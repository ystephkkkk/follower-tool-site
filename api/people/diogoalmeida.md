### TL;DR
AI is exceptionally capable of reasoning and natural language, yet it remains largely useless for daily automation because existing software treats AI as either a human-facing chatbot or a raw text generator. TypeSafe’s Jev introduces a new programming primitive—combining natural language and state machines inside the software itself—to enable reliable, probabilistic software that finally automates real-world tasks and fulfills the promise of "do what I mean."

### The Automation Paradox: Why AI Hasn't Automated Real Work
* **The "Where is the Automation?" Problem:** **Diogo Almeida** argues that despite AI's remarkable intelligence, it remains largely useless for practical automation outside of simple chat interfaces or writing raw code.
* **Limitations of In-Time Software:** **Diogo Almeida** points out that coding agents and cloud-code tools (like Codex or Cursor) merely accelerate code generation; they still produce traditional software that works the way it always has, leading to faster-written but fundamentally unchanged applications.
* **The Long-Tail and Misguided Benchmarks:** **Diogo Almeida** notes that the AI industry has fallen into the trap of over-promising on flashy demos and outlier benchmarks (like GPQA) rather than building reliable background automation for rote tasks like customer service—which OpenAI has struggled to automate since 2020.

### Jev and the Rise of Probabilistic Software
* **A New Software Primitive:** **Diogo Almeida** explains that Jev acts as an intelligent library embedded directly into program state, allowing developers to use natural language combined with state machines to make probabilistic, confident decisions rather than just generating text for humans to interpret.
* **Reliability Over Raw Generative Power:** **Diogo Almeida** emphasizes that achieving deep reliability—focusing on robustness, predictable intelligence, and robustness rather than simple uptime—is the fundamental barrier that enables developers to enter a flow state and program against AI without constant manual example queries.
* **Transitioning from LLM Loops to Internal Guts:** **Diogo Almeida** highlights that previous attempts to embed AI into software resulted in awkward "while loops" (agents talking to LLMs) or human-in-the-loop chat interfaces, whereas Jev aims to push intelligence deep into the "guts" of software infrastructure (comparable to the reliability tiers of TCP/UDP).

### The SaaS "Inverse Apocalypse"
* **The Software Capabilities Gap:** **Ben Horowitz** and **Diogo Almeida** discuss how standard PRs for large companies are often only ~10 lines of code, meaning coding agents only automate superficial edits rather than expanding core software capabilities.
* **SaaS as the Ultimate Beneficiary:** **Diogo Almeida** rejects the "SaaS apocalypse" narrative, arguing that established SaaS companies are best positioned to leverage Jev because they deeply understand user workflows and enterprise needs.
* **Rebuilding Critical Systems:** **Martin Casado** suggests that due to both new probabilistic software capabilities and mounting cybersecurity challenges, critical infrastructure and systems are primed to be entirely rebuilt using these new primitives.

### Diogo Almeida’s Unorthodox Journey & Philosophy
