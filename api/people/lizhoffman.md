## Is Walmart Backing America’s Next Financial Super-App? | Compound Interest

- Channel: Semafor
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T60ttyZVYBY
- Published: 2026-07-21
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260721.html#video-T60ttyZVYBY
- Speakers: Omer Ismail (CEO, OnePay); Liz Hoffman (Host, Compound Interest); Rohan Nadkarni (Host, Compound Interest)

---

**TL;DR** 
One (formerly OnePay) is a Walmart-backed "financial super-app" leveraging a massive, built-in distribution network of 150 million monthly shoppers and 1.5 million employees to provide integrated banking, credit, and investment services with virtually zero customer acquisition costs.

### Scale and the "Super-App" Strategy
* **Growth Metrics:** In four years, One has reached 7 million monthly active customers and processes approximately $50 billion in annualized payment flow.
* **The "One-Stop Shop":** CEO Omer Ismail describes the platform as an "open-loop" financial ecosystem designed to help users manage money holistically, moving beyond the single-product focus of early fintechs.
* **Product Suite:** The app currently offers digital banking, credit building, high-yield savings, installment loans, credit cards, and investment tools (including crypto).
* **Usage Depth:** One focuses on "high-engagement" products—the "Google toothbrush test" of items used daily. Currently, users average more than two features per person, with a goal of reaching five or six.

### The Distribution Moat: Walmart’s Ecosystem
* **Zero CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost):** While most fintechs struggle with high marketing costs, One acquires customers for free through Walmart’s existing digital and physical infrastructure.
* **Associate Integration:** Walmart employees (associates) can sign up for One in "20 seconds" during HR onboarding. Walmart shares data (with permission) to pre-fill applications, creating the fastest onboarding in the industry.
* **Point-of-Sale Acquisition:** Shoppers on Walmart.com are prompted to use One for installment loans at checkout, converting retail traffic directly into banking customers.
* **Open-Loop Ambitions:** While Walmart is the primary anchor, One is expanding into other employer ecosystems (e.g., UKG and Workday) to reach an additional 30–40 million employees.

### Operational Structure and Partnerships
* **Independent Governance:** Though Walmart is the majority owner, One operates as an independent company outside the "four walls" of the retail giant. This structure was designed by Walmart CEO Doug McMillon and the board (including OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar) to ensure agility and talent retention.
* **Asset-Light Model:** One is not a bank. It utilizes a partner-banking model to avoid the heavy regulatory and capital requirements of a banking license:
    * **Banking Partners:** Coastal Community Bank and Lead Bank.
    * **Credit & Loans:** Partnered with Klarna (installment loans) and Synchrony (credit cards).
    * **Brokerage:** Partnered with DriveWealth for clearing and execution.
* **Data Integration:** The platform uses "North Star" principles to never ask a customer for information the ecosystem already possesses, using Walmart’s data to personalize financial offers.

### Competitive Positioning and Market Outlook
* **Lessons from Goldman Sachs’ "Marcus":** Ismail, who previously led Marcus, noted that One is different because it focuses on high-frequency "front door" products (banking/credit) rather than niche, "set-it-and-forget-it" products like high-yield savings or personal loans.
* **The "Rabbit" Culture:** Ismail describes the company culture via a fable where a wolf hunts a rabbit: "The wolf was running for his dinner; the rabbit was running for its life." This emphasizes the urgency of a startup despite having a Fortune 1 backer.
* **Market Share:** Despite the rise of fintech, legacy banks still hold the vast majority of the market. Ismail argues that One’s distribution edge and multi-product integration allow it to compete where other "point-solution" fintechs have hit a ceiling.

### Notable Quotes
> "We have the ability to acquire customers at zero CAC [Customer Acquisition Cost] through the Walmart ecosystem... it’s not just distribution that’s a competitive moat, it's also the ability to use data with the customer's permission to offer them products catered to them." — **Omer Ismail, CEO of One**

> "I am a big believer that distribution beats content. You don’t have to have a perfect product—you have to have a good product, but you have to get it to people in a way that makes sense for them and that is cheap for you." — **Liz Hoffman, Semafor Business & Finance Editor**

> "One big lesson is structure... [One] is not inside of [Walmart's] four walls... which has allowed us to be able to get the business to the stage where it's at, with the right team and the right incentives in place." — **Omer Ismail, CEO of One**


## Big Tech’s Political Fixer On AI’s Unfixable ‘Darth Vader’ Problem

- Channel: Semafor
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z_grjvUpLos
- Published: 2026-06-30
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260630.html#video-z_grjvUpLos
- Speakers: Bradley Tusk (Political Fixer and Venture Capitalist); Liz Hoffman (Host, Semafor); Rohan Silva (Host, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR** 
Silicon Valley political fixer Bradley Tusk warns that AI companies face unprecedented, bipartisan regulatory backlash because the public lacks enthusiasm for the technology and sees no "hero" in the space. Consequently, Tusk is adapting his classic "ask forgiveness, not permission" playbook, winding down his traditional venture fund to focus on equity-for-advising deals in highly regulated sectors like AI data center energy and alternative compute.

***

### The Complex, Bipartisan Regulatory Threats Facing AI

* **Widespread Public Anxiety:** Unlike previous tech waves, AI faces a deep, bipartisan undercurrent of public distrust that is already driving aggressive local and state-level legislative action in both red and blue states (including Texas, Tennessee, Minnesota, Indiana, and New York).
    * Bradley Tusk warns that the current regulatory climate is likely the most permissive AI companies will ever experience, with restrictions only tightening from here.
* **The Four Regulatory Buckets of AI:** Tusk advises analyzing and tackling AI policy by segmenting the landscape into four distinct categories:
    1. **Consumer Protection:** Managing the direct human impacts of AI, such as local data center resource strain, AI chatbots, and automated workflows in healthcare, insurance, hiring, and education.
    2. **Catastrophic Risks:** High-profile, existential policy concerns surrounding frontier models.
    3. **Job Displacement:** Short-term economic pain and labor displacement, which Tusk notes will cause severe political friction despite the long-term creation of new industries.
    4. **Positive Utility:** Harnessing AI for high-upside public goods like climate change mitigation, drug discovery, and governmental efficiency.
* **The "Luke Skywalker" vs. "Darth Vader" Dynamic:** Tusk argues that AI lacks a clear "hero" to combat entrenched incumbents. He notes that Anthropic is successfully positioning itself as the responsible "Luke Skywalker," while OpenAI has willingly embraced a villainous "Darth Vader" persona.

### Why the "Ask Forgiveness, Not Permission" Playbook Fails for AI

* **The Problem of a Utility-Like User Base:** Uber succeeded in beating local regulations because its early users found the app "magical" and actively lobbied politicians to keep it. In contrast, Tusk notes that services like ChatGPT are treated like utility companies—widely used, but unloved and incapable of inspiring grassroots political defense.
* **High-Stakes Legal Risks:** While early Uber could absorb low-stakes penalties like impounded cars to force its way into markets, the regulatory and compliance risks for frontier AI models are massive and potentially criminal, making a "brute force" strategy highly dangerous.
* **Regulatory Moats as a Defense:** Tusk advises best-in-class startups in highly regulated sectors (like healthcare or insurance) to lean into public anxiety by actively lobbying for exceptionally high regulatory standards. 
    * By helping write stringent laws that they can meet but competitors cannot, startups can build highly defensive regulatory moats.

### The Impending Jurisdictional Battle Over Prediction Markets

* **The FanDuel Playbook:** During the 2015 fantasy sports crisis, Tusk kept FanDuel alive by mobilizing passionate, highly targeted user bases in key legislative districts, threatening local politicians with direct electoral consequences if they banned the service.
* **An Impending Supreme Court Showdown:** Prediction markets (e.g., Polymarket and Kalshi) face a looming 50/50 jurisdictional battle in the Supreme Court over whether they should be regulated by the federal Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) or individual state gaming boards.
    * **State Jurisdiction Scenario:** If the Court hands authority to the states, platforms like Polymarket will face sluggish state gaming boards and will need to launch massive, high-budget user advocacy campaigns to secure licenses.
    * **Federal Jurisdiction Scenario:** If the CFTC wins sole jurisdiction, traditional sports betting giants may pivot to prediction markets to escape high state taxes, creating massive, unexpected tax revenue deficits for state governments.
* **Underage Betting Safeguards:** Watching his own teenage children bypass age gates on sports betting apps led Tusk to work with New York Governor Kathy Hochul on a proposal to mandate biometric face-screening for logging into state-regulated betting platforms.

### Tusk's Venture Capital Exit and Focus on the Data Center Energy Crisis

* **Winding Down Tusk Venture Partners:** Tusk is winding down his $300 million venture fund to return to his original model of advising early-stage startups in exchange for equity rather than managing LP capital.
    * Tusk cited a dislike for LP relations, board seats, and compliance, noting that mid-sized funds are structurally inefficient because they must return their entire pool of LP capital before general partners see a dollar of carry.
* **The Data Center Energy Backlash:** Tusk has created a dedicated investment vertical targeting alternative compute and "behind-the-meter" power sources to capitalize on a growing political crisis: local taxpayers rejecting rate hikes driven by AI data center demands.
    * In major data center hubs like Northern Virginia and Georgia, residents are seeing utility bills rise by 30% to 50%, prompting active voter backlash and political defeats for local leaders.
* **Investing in Nuclear and Alternative Compute:** Tusk believes data centers will inevitably be forced to pay for their own power infrastructure. He is backing startups focused on behind-the-meter nuclear reactors and high-efficiency compute methods (such as biological or dendritic computing) to bypass the public grid entirely.

## Blackrock Infrastructure Summit: Future Builders | Semafor Media Partner

- Channel: Semafor

- Speakers: Larry Fink (CEO, BlackRock); Liz Hoffman (Business and Finance Editor, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR**
BlackRock is launching a $100 million "Future Builders" initiative to address a critical skilled labor shortage that threatens U.S. infrastructure and global competitiveness. As AI disrupts white-collar professions, leaders emphasize a cultural and economic shift toward the trades, where high demand and massive infrastructure investments are creating lucrative, non-degree career paths.

### The Skilled Labor Crisis and the "Future Builders" Initiative
* **Closing the Gap:** The BlackRock Foundation has committed $100 million to a national philanthropic initiative focused on connecting workers to skilled trades training.
    * **Market Demand:** According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, hundreds of thousands of net new skilled trade jobs are required to meet current infrastructure demand.
    * **The "Work" Gap:** There are currently 7.6 million open positions in the U.S., while 6.9 million prime-working-age men are affirmatively not looking for work.
* **The "Shop Class" Consequence:** Mike Rowe attributed the current crisis to the removal of vocational training from high schools in the 1970s.
    * **Cultural Stigma:** The trades were framed as a "vocational consolation prize," leading to a generation that views aspirational careers as exclusively degree-based.
    * **Retirement Wave:** For every five skilled tradespeople who leave the workforce, only two enter, creating an existential threat to the maritime, energy, and construction industries.

### AI Disruption and the Recalibration of Education
* **The Value of Degrees:** While Larry Fink acknowledged the social value of college, he noted that tuition has outpaced inflation for 30 years, undermining the value proposition for many students.
    * **AI Impact:** AI is expected to disrupt white-collar "whiteboard" jobs faster than manual trades, potentially leading to high unemployment among college graduates even in a growing economy.
    * **High-Earning Trades:** Mike Rowe cited data center electricians under age 30 earning $240,000 to $280,000 annually without college debt, noting they are frequently "poached" by competitors due to scarcity.
* **Corporate Shifts:** BlackRock is seeing revenue growth outpace headcount growth as technology allows the firm to "do more with fewer people," a trend expected to accelerate across the corporate sector.

### Infrastructure as a National Security Imperative
* **Global Competition:** Fink warned that if the U.S. does not develop abundant, affordable power and modern infrastructure, "China wins."
    * **Energy Strategy:** China is currently building 100 gigawatts of nuclear power and miles of solar fields; the U.S. must accelerate solar, battery storage, and hydrocarbon development to compete.
    * **Capital Requirements:** An estimated $10 trillion of investable money is needed over the next eight years to rebuild American infrastructure.
* **Onshoring Manufacturing:** Fink advocated for a "51% ownership" model—similar to China’s requirements—to bring Korean or Chinese battery and solar manufacturers to the U.S. to create local jobs.

### Financial Literacy and the Wealth Gap
* **Broadening Participation:** Currently, 40% of Americans have no capacity to invest in the financial markets. Fink argues that the wealth gap is driven by capital appreciating faster than salaries.
    * **Retirement Solutions:** BlackRock advocates for "childhood accounts" (starting with $1,000) to allow every citizen to benefit from the "technological boom" and long-term market growth.
* **The "Jumpstart Bill":** Mike Rowe highlighted proposed legislation (the Jumpstart Act) that would allow 529 plans to be used for "tools of the trade," such as trucks and equipment, rather than just tuition.
* **Economic Outlook:** Fink expressed minimal concern regarding long-term inflation, suggesting that if wage growth is coupled with technology-driven productivity, the net result is a "good outcome" for the economy.
