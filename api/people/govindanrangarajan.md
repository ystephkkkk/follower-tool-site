## Demis Hassabis On AGI, Advice For Indian Engineers, AI In Gaming & More

**TL;DR**
Demis Hassabis highlights how AI is transitioning from digital assistants to physical-world discovery, potentially compressing drug development timelines from years to weeks while evolving into a "general" intelligence capable of scientific breakthroughs like General Relativity.

### AI in Science and Healthcare
* **Drug Discovery Velocity:** Hassabis predicts that AI will reduce the average drug discovery timeline from 10 years to mere months or weeks.
    * **AlphaFold & Isomorphic Labs:** While AlphaFold solved protein folding, Isomorphic Labs is now targeting biochemistry, toxicity, and absorption to ensure compounds work safely in the human body.
    * **Impact in India:** Google DeepMind already collaborates with Indian contract research organizations to accelerate the production of generic drugs and treatments for rare genetic diseases.
* **Scientific "Taste":** Prof. Govindan Rangarajan and Hassabis discuss the difficulty of teaching "taste"—the intuition for which problems are worth solving.
    * **The Mentor Model:** Historically, taste is passed from PhD mentors to students. Rangarajan suggests future AI might be "apprenticed" to human masters to inherit their specific schools of thought.
    * **Active Experimentation:** Hassabis argues that taste cannot be learned passively; it requires active experimentation at the frontier of discovery.

### The Evolution of Gaming and Creativity
* **The "Genie" World Model:** Google’s Genie 3 can generate playable worlds from a single prompt. While currently limited to one-minute "dreams," Hassabis expects this to expand into a foundational tool for the industry.
* **New Gaming Genres:** Beyond asset creation (3D models and concept art), AI will enable massive multiplayer games populated by "smart" NPCs that dynamically advance storylines.
* **Small Team Renaissance:** AI tools are lowering the cost of prototyping, potentially ushering in a new "golden era" similar to the early 90s, where small, 10x teams can experiment with high-risk, creative ideas.

### The Path to AGI and Research Strategy
* **Defining AGI:** Hassabis maintains a consistent definition—a system capable of all human cognitive abilities. 
    * **Current Gaps:** Today’s models lack "continual learning," "long-term planning," and "consistency" (referencing how models can solve Olympiad-level math but fail at simple logic).
    * **The Ultimate Test:** A true AGI would be given a knowledge cutoff (e.g., 1911) and tasked with independently discovering a breakthrough like General Relativity (1915).
* **Organizational Balance:** At Google DeepMind, Hassabis splits his team 50/50 between "Blue Sky" research (long-term AGI goals) and commercial foundation models like Gemini.
* **Robotics & Physical AI:** The next five years will focus on AI understanding the physical world, leading to a "breakout moment" in robotics and wearable AI (smart glasses) that provides real-time contextual assistance.

### Advice for Engineers and the "Polymath" Advantage
* **Software Disruption:** Addressing the $200B Indian IT services sector, Hassabis views AI as a "continual abstraction." Much like the shift from Assembler to C++ or Python, "prompting" is simply the next higher-level language.
    * **Key Differentiator:** As technical skills become commoditized by AI, human "taste" and creative direction will become the most valuable assets for software engineers.
* **Breaking Silos:** Prof. Rangarajan notes that India's education system is often too specialized. Hassabis encourages engineers to become "polymaths" by mastering two or more distinct domains (e.g., ML and Biology).
    * **Learning Technique:** Hassabis advises experts to have the "humility to be a beginner again," applying first-principles thinking to bridge different fields where the most significant modern inventions occur.
