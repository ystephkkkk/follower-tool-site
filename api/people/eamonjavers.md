**TL;DR:** During a high-stakes summit in Beijing, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent signaled a shift toward establishing formal AI safety protocols and bilateral trade boards with China, while asserting that the U.S. negotiates from a position of strength as an energy superpower and the undisputed leader in artificial intelligence.

### AI Leadership and Global Guardrails
* **Bilateral AI Protocol:** The U.S. and China are moving toward a formal "AI safety protocol" to establish best practices and ensure non-state actors do not obtain advanced models. 
    * **Strategic Dominance:** Bessent emphasized that the U.S. remains the "undisputed leader" with a significant lead over China, allowing the U.S. to set the values and best practices for the technology.
    * **Corporate Collaboration:** The administration is consulting with leading firms—including Anthropic (Mythos), OpenAI, and Google (Gemini)—to ensure safety without "stifling innovation."
* **Cybersecurity in Finance:** Treasury is currently working with the 11 largest U.S. banks to compile AI-related vulnerability data, which will eventually be shared with regional and community banks.

### Trade Normalization and Investment Frameworks
* **New Bilateral Bodies:** The administration plans to form a "Board of Trade" and a "Board of Investment" to manage non-strategic economic relations.
    * **CFIUS "Pre-gaming":** The Board of Investment aims to identify non-sensitive areas for Chinese investment upfront, preventing these deals from being referred to or blocked by the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS).
* **Strategic Tariff Reductions:** Negotiators are discussing cutting tariffs on approximately $30 billion worth of imports.
    * **Targeted Sectors:** Reductions would focus on non-critical, low-end consumer goods (e.g., fireworks) that the U.S. does not intend to re-shore.
* **The "Three Bs" Strategy:** Discussions continue regarding major Chinese purchases of Boeing aircraft, beef, and soybeans to narrow the current $200 billion trade deficit.

### Technology Exports and the Nvidia Logjam
* **Chip Export Status:** Despite reports that 10 Chinese firms were cleared to buy Nvidia’s H200 chips, CNBC’s Eamon Javers reports that zero deliveries have been made.
    * **Chinese Hesitation:** Sales are stalled not by U.S. blocks, but by Chinese concerns over U.S. intelligence manipulation of chips and a domestic push to support Huawei. 
    * **CEO Diplomacy:** Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang joined the delegation in an attempt to "break the logjam" and finalize these long-delayed sales.

### Energy Independence and Geopolitical Pressure
* **U.S. as Energy Alternative:** Bessent highlighted that the U.S. is a record-breaking energy superpower. He is pushing China to diversify away from Middle Eastern oil in favor of U.S. crude and LNG, specifically from Alaska.
* **Iran Economic Squeeze:** Bessent characterized the current blockade of Iran as a "resounding success."
    * **Production Shutdown:** Satellite imagery suggests Iranian storage is full and loadings at Kharg Island have ceased, leading to a forced shutdown of Iranian production.
* **Taiwan Warning:** President Xi Jinping issued a stern warning to the U.S. delegation, stating that Taiwanese independence is "incompatible with peace" and could lead to a "dangerous confrontation."

### U.S. Macroeconomic Outlook
* **Fed Leadership:** Following the confirmation of Kevin Warsh as Fed Chair, Bessent expects an "open-minded" approach to monetary policy.
* **Inflation Projections:** While current inflation data remains "hot," Bessent argues the spike is a "transient supply shock" caused by Middle East tensions that will reverse quickly as energy prices come "trundling back."
