#### TL;DR
The Federal Reserve is reevaluating its communication regime under Chairman Kevin Warsh to improve the signal-to-noise ratio and avoid over-committing via rigid forward guidance. Simultaneously, the Fed is navigating severe crosscurrents—including sticky inflation, supply-side overlapping shocks, and the multi-year economic impacts of AI adoption—requiring a flexible, forward-looking policy framework rather than rigid reliance on conventional playbooks.

---

### Federal Reserve Communications Regime and Strategy

* **Shift from Historical Over-Communication:** 
  * Over the past three decades, central bank communications evolved from near-silence to heavy engagement, amplified significantly after the Global Financial Crisis (GFC) to manage expectations.
  * **Mary Daly on Tool Overuse:** Daly notes that "if I have a hammer, I'm always looking for a nail," arguing that central banks must step back to ensure communication tools build trust and clarity rather than creating low signal-to-noise ratios.
* **The Role of Chairman Kevin Warsh:** 
  * Daly views the transition under Chairman Kevin Warsh as an opportune catalyst to rigorously review Fed communications and balance-sheet tools alongside external task forces.
  * **Avoiding Short-Term Misinterpretation:** Frequent remarks about immediate meeting outcomes (e.g., the upcoming October meeting) risk mischaracterizing the Fed as short-term thinkers rather than forward-looking policymakers acting with 6-to-18-month lags.
* **Revisiting Forward Guidance and Balance Sheet Tools:** 
  * **Forward Guidance Discipline:** Daly suggests forward guidance should be reserved for times when the Fed has high clarity on its direction, rather than during periods of balancing active risks.
  * **Balance Sheet Coupling:** Daly questions whether the size and composition of the Fed's balance sheet should remain tightly coupled with the federal funds rate, or evaluated as a distinct operational tool.

### Inflation Dynamics and Supply-Demand Shocks

* **Conventional Wisdom vs. Current Reality:** 
  * The traditional economic rule of thumb dictates that central banks should "look through" exogenous, one-time supply shocks (such as tariffs or oil price spikes) because policy lags prevent effective offsets.
  * **Daly's Shift:** While Daly favored looking through these shocks six months ago, the persistent duration and growing willingness of businesses to pass price increases downstream have pushed her to be more cautious about overlapping inflationary pressures.
* **Inflation Expectations and the Risk Dashboard:** 
  * **Compensating Pressures:** Daly monitors a specific "inflation risks dashboard," noting that market-based inflation compensation rose ahead of the September FOMC meeting, signaling upward drift.
  * **Upside Risks:** Daly stresses that current risks skew entirely to the upside, requiring the Fed to remain ready and willing to tighten further if necessary.
* **Labor Markets and Real Wages:** 
  * Nominal wage stickiness downwards means that negative supply shocks inherently lower real wages and reduce purchasing power. 
  * While restrictive monetary policy can prevent wage-price spirals during demand overheating, applying it against supply-side shocks risks over-constraining the labor market and worsening real wage compression.

### Artificial Intelligence and Economic Implications

* **The 1-to-3-Year Horizon vs. Decadal Productivity:** 
  * While AI represents a generational general-purpose technology comparable to electrification, monetary policy operates on a 1-to-3-year window. 
  * Long-term productivity gains 10 years out do not directly solve current full-employment and price-stability mandates.
* **Near-Term Demand Pressures vs. Supply Adaptation:** 
  * **Hyper-scaler Bottlenecks:** The current phase is dominated by heavy infrastructure buildouts—data centers, GPUs, and memory chips—creating immediate demand shocks and input constraints (e.g., water, power, and chip availability).
  * **Private Sector Mitigation:** Firms are actively bypassing chip shortages by engineering products to use existing hardware longer, adopting small language models (SLMs), utilizing open-weight models, and deploying internal "gatekeepers" to route queries efficiently.
* **AI Boom-Bust Cycles:** 
  * Similar to historical semiconductor cycles, the rapid AI investment boom will eventually transition into a steady-state maintenance phase. 
  * Daly highlights that a potential "bust" or slowdown in AI infrastructure spending could emerge within the 1-to-3-year horizon, naturally dissipating upward price pressures.

### Financial System Stability and Cyber Risk

* **Commercial Bank Resilience:** 
  * When questioned about systemic threats like catastrophic cyber attacks disabling large financial institutions, Daly emphasized that private bank leaders (such as JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Diamond) and banking regulators are actively addressing cyber threats.
  * **Fed Oversight:** Vice Board Governor Michelle Bowman and the broader Federal Reserve system treat cyber risk as a top-tier supervisory priority, continuously ring-fencing internal IT data while pushing commercial institutions to fortify operational defenses against rapidly evolving technology.

## Fed's Daly Says Forward Guidance Could Be Misleading

- Channel: Bloomberg Technology
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rUB9XUNEFyw

- Speakers: Mary Daly (President, Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco)

---

**TL;DR**
Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco President Mary Daly remains highly bullish on AI's long-term economic potential despite a current lack of macro productivity gains, while cautioning that elevated economic uncertainty makes near-term interest rate forward guidance potentially misleading.

### AI Adoption, Productivity, and Financial Stability

* **The Productivity Lag:** Widespread, transformative productivity gains from AI have not yet materialized in macroeconomic data. 
    * **Litmus Test Timing:** Businesses are actively transforming their internal processes, with many citing next year as the true "litmus test" for realized return on investment (ROI).
    * **Beyond Tech:** Daly is particularly bullish on non-tech sector investments. Examples include machining companies training AI on 50 years of technical blueprints and robotics firms optimizing shipping logistics.
* **No Financial Stability Risks:** Despite high valuations and market enthusiasm, Daly does not see current AI investments posing systemic financial stability risks.
    * **Capital Sources:** Much of the current capital expenditure is funded directly by mega-cap technology companies (the "Magnificent Seven") rather than highly leveraged bank debt.
    * **Systemic Spread:** For a market dip to trigger a financial stability concern, it would need to spread directly to consumers, businesses, and the banking sector—which is not occurring.
* **Differences from the Dot-Com Era:** While San Francisco is experiencing enthusiasm reminiscent of 1996, the current AI wave is far more pervasive and integrated into core business operations than the dot-com boom.

### Monetary Policy and the Risks of Forward Guidance

* **Misleading Guidance:** Daly warns that providing explicit forward guidance on interest rates right now is "misguided" due to an unusually high number of economic crosscurrents.
* **Geopolitical Disruptions:** Geopolitical tensions (such as conflicts impacting oil and fertilizer prices) present ongoing inflation risks that could counter the disinflationary potential of technology. 
    * **Oil Projections:** Despite current volatility, the futures market projects oil to stabilize around $80 a barrel by the end of the year.
* **Policy Readiness:** Current monetary policy is well-positioned to react flexibly to economic data as it evolves in either direction. Daly’s top priority remains returning inflation to the Fed's target.

### Labor Market Stabilization and Hiring Caution

* **Stabilized Conditions:** The U.S. labor market has stabilized following supportive policy adjustments made late last year.
* **AI-Driven Hiring Hesitancy:** Businesses are displaying extreme caution regarding adding headcount.
    * **Agent Evaluation:** Rather than aggressively hiring, firms are actively evaluating what tasks AI agents can perform first to avoid overhiring and subsequent painful layoffs.
    * **Skill Alignment:** Companies are pausing recruitment to better understand the exact skill sets they will require once AI tools are fully integrated.

### Capex Bottlenecks and Fed Modernization

* **Infrastructure Capex:** Massive capital commitments for data centers and electricity grid demands are causing short-term bottlenecks in sourcing generators and infrastructure equipment.
    * **Disinflationary Timeline:** While these projects create localized price competition today, they build the physical infrastructure that will ultimately lower operational costs in the long run.
* **San Francisco Housing Supply:** Rising regional housing costs and local displacement are driven by a severe lack of housing supply, rather than excessive demand or speculative bubbles.
* **Fed Institutional Leadership:** Commenting on the transition to new leadership under Chair Walsh, Daly emphasized the Fed's dual mandate as fiduciary stewards of public trust and public funds.
    * **Safe Technology Adoption:** The Fed is exploring safe, highly regulated methods to adopt AI in its own operations to increase efficiency without compromising security or human judgment in monetary policymaking.

## San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly Talks AI, Economy | Bloomberg Talks

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vJcAWM99ADA
- Published: 2026-02-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260218.html#video-vJcAWM99ADA
- Speakers: Mary Daly (President, Federal Reserve Bank of San Francisco)

---

**TL;DR**
San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly views AI as a potential driver of sustained productivity and disinflation, but emphasizes that the Federal Reserve remains in an "evidence-based" observation mode, waiting for clear data before adjusting monetary policy.

### AI and Economic Productivity
* **Productivity Trends:** Recent U.S. productivity has tracked at an annual rate of roughly 2.7% over the last 10 quarters, significantly higher than the 50-year average of 1.9%.
    * **Causality Gap:** Daly notes it is currently impossible to definitively link these gains to AI versus general cost management or one-time pandemic-era adjustments.
* **Level vs. Growth:** A key distinction exists between "one-time adjustments" (e.g., automating a single loan process) and "sustained productivity growth" where technology fundamentally changes revenue generation and product design across all industries.
* **Marginal Protection:** Firms are likely using AI to protect profit margins as their power to pass through price increases to consumers diminishes.

### Inflation and the "Next-Later" Energy Crisis
* **The Inflation Tug-of-War:** AI investment creates a dual impact on inflation.
    * **Demand-Side Pressure:** The massive build-out of data centers raises prices for construction labor and raw materials (metals).
    * **Supply-Side Relief:** Long-term productivity gains from AI act as a disinflationary force by lowering the cost structure of firms.
* **Energy Imbalance:** Within the 12th District (which includes Silicon Valley), demand for electricity is currently outstripping supply.
    * **Infrastructure Delays:** Supply is lagging due to aging infrastructure, regulation, and supply chain issues for components like transformers.
    * **Disinflationary Energy:** Daly argues that energy will only become disinflationary once supply catches up to or exceeds demand, which is a "next and later" prospect.

### Labor Market: Replace, Augment, and Create
* **Task Displacement:** Daly identifies a three-stage evolution of AI in the workforce: Replace (tasks), Augment (workers), and Create (new roles).
* **Net Employment:** Historically, no technology has ever reduced net employment; instead, it shifts the nature of work.
    * **Current Environment:** The U.S. is in a "low firing, low hiring" state, which contributes to worker anxiety despite low unemployment.
    * **Skill Longevity:** Roles like "prompt engineers" are already being automated, signaling that workers must focus on "durable skills" and stay "AI-ready."
* **Sectoral Concentration:** Most current job growth is concentrated in healthcare and education, while other sectors show stagnation or slight declines, creating a "vulnerability" for workers trained in non-growing fields.

### AI at the Federal Reserve
* **Operational Integration:** The Fed is actively using AI for internal operations, including coding assistance, research discovery, and back-office efficiency (e.g., Fedwire and ACH systems).
* **Exclusion from Monetary Policy:** AI is not used to set interest rates or determine the "neutral rate" of interest.
    * **Model Limitations:** Current models produce a massive range for the neutral rate (from -3% to +11%), making them theoretical constructs rather than surgical tools for policy.
* **Fiduciary Stewardship:** The Fed maintains "ring-fenced" proprietary environments to test AI, ensuring they do not introduce risk to public funds or trust.

### Monetary Policy Outlook
* **Evidence-Based Approach:** While monetary policy is forward-looking, Daly insists it must remain evidence-based. The Fed will not cut rates based on *anticipated* AI gains until those gains manifest in disaggregated inflation data.
* **Restrictive Stance:** Policy is currently "modestly restrictive." If the neutral rate is approximately 3%, the Fed has roughly 75 basis points to go before reaching a neutral stance, though this depends on inflation reaching the 2% target.

> "The neutral rate of interest is not a truth with a capital T, it's an estimate... No one calibrates monetary policy surgically with a neutral rate of interest estimate." — **Mary Daly**

> "If an employee using AI gets much more productive, you hire more of them, not fewer of them. The economy grows faster, product development goes faster, and demand gets stronger." — **Mary Daly**
