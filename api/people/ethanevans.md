## Ex-Amazon VP: What it's really like working with Jeff Bezos

- Channel: My First Million
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0_hpTKQqKOQ
- Published: 2026-10-28
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261028.html#video-0_hpTKQqKOQ
- Speakers: Shaan Puri (Host, My First Million); Sam Parr (Host, My First Million); Ethan Evans (Former VP, Amazon)

1. **TL;DR:** Former Amazon VP Ethan Evans shares raw insights from his 20-year career working alongside Jeff Bezos, detailing Amazon's relentless operational culture, high-bar standards, and strategies for scaling leadership through systems like "question mark" emails.
2. **Inside the Mind and Management Style of Jeff Bezos**
   - **Flaws and Evolution:** **Constructive Criticism Only:** Early on, Bezos struggled with giving praise, focusing exclusively on corrective feedback. Over time, he worked with an executive coach to intentionally add positive reinforcement.
   - **Attack Mode & Accountability:** **Relentless Problem-Solving:** When frustrated by failures, Bezos often went into attack mode—not just at the team, but out of frustration with himself. For example, after a poorly received price-matching app idea, he expressed disbelief at the oversight.
   - **Pattern Recognition & Brilliance:** **10,000 Hours of Reviews:** Bezos’s ability to pinpoint the core leverage or flaw in a proposal within minutes came from thousands of hours spent reading documents and conducting business reviews.
   - **The Hubris Trap:** Like many great leaders, Bezos occasionally believed his vision was infallible, leading directly to high-profile failures like the Fire Phone ($170M hardware write-off).

3. **Amazon’s Operational Playbook: Scale, Speed, and Metrics**
   - **Question Mark Emails:** **24-Hour Accountability:** When customers complained directly to Bezos, he forwarded the emails to executives with a single question mark (?), giving leaders 24 hours to explain or fix the issue.
   - **Strategically Patient, Tactically Impatient:** **Long-Term Vision, Daily Urgency:** Major initiatives like Prime required decades of patience, but execution demanded maximizing every single quarter and daily metric.
   - **Web Labs & Design:** **Speed Equals Sales:** Amazon prioritized site loading speed and conversion rates over aesthetic design. Every page element and feature was rigorously AB tested via web labs.
   - **The Origin of Prime:** Originally conceived as "Project 100" (a 100-day rollout) focused purely on fast delivery during a difficult financial quarter, it later evolved to include perks like video and music. Free shipping thresholds ($25) were engineered around basket sizes for multi-item profit margins (e.g., two DVDs).

4. **Culture, Hiring, and the High-Performance "Sweatshop"**
   - **Work-Life Harmony vs. Balance:** Amazon rejected the concept of work-life balance, favoring "harmony"—expecting long, hard, and smart work. Employees were regularly reminded that high-performance careers have different seasons.
   - **Loyalty Over Pure Genius:** While smarts mattered, Bezos deeply valued loyal "fixers"—operators who would throw themselves on a fire to turn a failing "D" product into an acceptable "B" product when a "B" was all the system required.
   - **Anecdotes Over Data:** Bezos famously prioritized customer anecdotes over macro data, noting that even a 99% success rate at Amazon scale still leaves 25 million unhappy customers a day.
   - **The Twitch Acquisition:** Amazon acquired Twitch for ~$970 million. Driven by Amazon stock 5Xing during the earnout, engineer vest values spiked to $2 million/year, leading to a massive cliff and attrition when salaries normalized.

5. **Transition to Independence and Coaching**
   - **Post-Amazon Happiness:** Ethan Evans reported his happiness jumped from a 7/10 at Amazon ($900k–$1M+ target comp working 50–60 hours/week) to an 11/12 working for himself (making $700k–$800k working ~20 hours/week).
   - **Building a Platform via Twitch:** Evans used streaming on Twitch not as a gamer, but to gain reps answering 10,000+ live career questions, securing CEO approval beforehand to bypass strict PR rules.
   - **Honest Empathy in Coaching:** Drawing from *Leadership and Self-Deception*, Evans notes that delivering hard truths effectively requires seeing the person as a human first—caring about them so the underlying intent of help outweighs harsh feedback.
