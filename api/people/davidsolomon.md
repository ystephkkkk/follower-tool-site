## The AI Productivity Boom with David Solomon

- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNlIASgAQic

- Speakers: David Solomon (Chairman and CEO, Goldman Sachs); Becky Quick (Host, CNBC)

---

**TL;DR**  
While AI will cause near-term job dislocation and require significant labor upskilling, it is poised to unleash a generational productivity and economic growth boom supported by massive capital market liquidity, even as businesses face rising cyber risks and macroeconomic pressures.

### The AI Productivity Boom and Labor Dynamics

* **Overblown Job Apocalypse:** David Solomon argues that a doomsday employment narrative is counterproductive. He anticipates a major productivity and economic growth boom over a five-to-ten-year horizon.
* **Labor Force Adaptability:** The US economy is highly dynamic, historically creating and destroying an average of 30 million jobs annually. 
    * **Service-Driven Economy:** Approximately 65% of US job creation comes from small and medium-sized enterprises, largely focused on human-to-human service roles that are less vulnerable to immediate automation.
    * **White-Collar Disruption:** AI will primarily disrupt white-collar roles. While this dislocation will move faster than past technological shifts, history shows the labor force can successfully adapt.
* **Shared Mitigation Responsibility:** Because the pace of AI deployment is rapid, business and government have a joint responsibility to establish policies and upskilling programs to soften the transition for dislocated workers.

### Enterprise AI Adoption and Infrastructure Constraints

* **Permitting and Power Bottlenecks:** For data center builders, capital constraints have been replaced by regulatory hurdles, community pushback, and power infrastructure limits. 
    * Solomon warns that the surge in energy demand from AI must not increase the cost of power and utility services for average Americans.
* **Non-Linear Compute Demand:** Despite aggressive projections, the demand for compute will not follow a straight, compounding line. Advancements in chip efficiency, manufacturing, and distribution costs will continually alter the trajectory.
* **Cautious Enterprise Spending:** Many corporations will adopt AI slower than markets expect due to high token and operational costs.
    * **Margin Protection:** Low-margin businesses will be highly cautious with experimental AI spend, as even fractional margin changes significantly impact their bottom line.
    * **Base Model Utility:** Rather than deploying expensive, high-powered frontier models, many enterprises will opt for cheaper base models to improve standard operational processes.

### Capital Markets, Liquidity, and the "Greed vs. Fear" Dynamic

* **Abundant Market Liquidity:** The capital required for the AI infrastructure build-out is readily available. Global equity markets hold approximately $150 trillion, US equity markets stand at $100 trillion, and US money market funds hold $8 trillion.
    * **Alphabet’s Mega-Deal:** Alphabet's $80 billion follow-on equity raise—the largest in history—is trading exceptionally well, proving the market's capacity to absorb massive funding rounds.
* **Greed Outpacing Fear:** Solomon notes that global markets are in a phase dominated by greed over fear. His foundational advice to capital-consumptive firms is to secure funding now while capital markets remain highly robust and receptive.
* **The Cycle of Exuberance:** Drawing a parallel to former Fed Chairman Alan Greenspan's 1996 "irrational exuberance" speech, Solomon reminds investors that market optimism can continue for years (as the Nasdaq did until 2000) before undergoing a major retracement.

### Macroeconomic Headwinds and Escalating Cyber Risks

* **S&P 500 Concentration:** Market performance is highly concentrated, with just 10 mega-cap stocks driving returns and representing over 30% of the S&P 500's market capitalization. The remaining 490 stocks have largely trended sideways.
* **Macroeconomic Pressures:** Persistent inflation, rising energy prices, and supply chain friction are beginning to impact consumer wallets (with gas accounting for roughly 14% of the average American consumer's spending). This could shift consumer sentiment and slow economic growth in late 2024.
* **Cyber Security as the Top Threat:** Over 60% of surveyed CEOs identify cyber security as their primary risk. 
    * **Systemic Banking Vulnerabilities:** While major financial institutions have the capital to invest heavily in cyber defense, medium and small-sized banks lack the same capacity, creating systemic vulnerability if a breach occurs.
* **Model Security and Testing:** Goldman Sachs is currently testing Anthropic's Mythos model. Solomon emphasizes the critical need for testing transparency and collaborative government guardrails to secure both proprietary and open-source models before wide deployment.

## Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon on Running a Bank in the Age of AI | Odd Lots

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P4y38x2t2KA
- Published: 2026-06-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260604.html#video-P4y38x2t2KA
- Speakers: David Solomon (CEO, Goldman Sachs)

---

**TL;DR**  
Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon argues that while AI will dramatically boost productivity and reshape job structures, fears of a white-collar "job apocalypse" are overblown because emotional intelligence, trust, and human relationships remain irreplaceable business drivers. Furthermore, the massive capital demands of the AI transition are shifting market dynamics, forcing tech giants to execute historic equity raises and prompting large private firms to seek public listings.

---

### AI, Productivity, and the Myth of the "Job Apocalypse"

* **The Historical Context of Disruption:** 
  * **"Never Different This Time" Principle:** Solomon rejects the narrative of a jobless future requiring universal basic income. Historically, technological accelerations shift the makeup of the labor force and create new opportunities rather than causing permanent mass unemployment.
  * **Leveraging Human Capital:** Rather than replacing workers, AI will serve as a tool of leverage. Young professionals will be freed from manual processing tasks and pushed into client-facing, relationship-building roles much earlier in their careers.
* **The Irreplaceable Value of EQ:**
  * **Interpersonal Domination:** High-value corporate transactions and advisory services rely on trust, emotional intelligence (EQ), and creative problem-solving.
  * **The Power of Direct Communication:** Solomon stresses that human-to-human contact is a premium skill. He advises professionals to utilize the phone rather than relying entirely on digital messages, noting that personal calls carry exponentially more impact than texts or emails.

### The Evolution of Junior Roles and the Apprenticeship Challenge

* **Historical vs. Modern Workflows:** 
  * **Primitive Beginnings:** Solomon contrasts modern banking with his early career in 1984, when performing a common stock comparison took six hours using microfiche, physical copies of the *Wall Street Journal*, and hand-drawn graph paper. 
  * **Immediate Answers:** Today, technology delivers these outputs instantaneously, massively inflating the baseline productivity of employees.
* **The "Apprenticeship" Training Gap:** 
  * **Loss of Foundational Absorption:** Because AI and modern tools automate analytical tasks, junior employees no longer have to "work" to get basic answers. Solomon warns this makes it harder for their brains to absorb underlying financial mechanics.
  * **Systemic Adaptation:** The core challenge for financial institutions over the next decade is finding new ways to apprentice and educate junior staff when the traditional manual tasks used for training have been completely automated.
* **Quantifiable Productivity Gains:** 
  * **Operational Overhauls:** Under Goldman Sachs' "1GS 3.0" initiative, the firm is utilizing technology to remake core operations. For example, they aim to streamline client onboarding and KYC processes, reducing the number of employees touching the workflow from 3,800 to just a few hundred.

### Data Integrity and Systemic Cybersecurity Vulnerabilities

* **The Imperative of Clean Data:**
  * **"Garbage In, Garbage Out":** AI models perform exceptionally well on highly curated, clean proprietary databases, but they fail on public datasets. Solomon cites a personal example where an AI model incorrectly omitted Tiger Woods when asked about back-to-back Masters winners due to unverified online source material.
  * **Goldman's Proprietary Database:** Goldman Sachs is cleaning and leveraging its massive proprietary data repository, including its 40-year-old "SecDB" trading and transaction system, to train highly accurate internal models.
* **Cybersecurity Inequality in Banking:**
  * **Concentrated Resources:** The top eight largest global banks have invested heavily in cybersecurity defense and are relatively well-protected.
  * **The Mid-Tier Threat:** Solomon expresses deep concern for mid-sized and regional banks (and local utility infrastructures) that lack the resources of major Wall Street firms. A successful cyberattack on a regional bank could spark systemic panic, similar to the run on Silicon Valley Bank (SVB).

### Shifting Capital Markets: AI Capex, Equity Raises, and IPOs

* **Alphabet’s Unprecedented Capital Raise:**
  * **Goldman-Led Mega Deal:** Goldman Sachs solely managed the initial stages of Alphabet’s historic secondary equity offering over five months, raising approximately $85 billion to $90 billion. This transaction stands as one of the largest follow-on equity offerings in history.
  * **Strategic Shift to Equity:** Large tech enterprises face voracious capital demands for AI infrastructure over the next five to ten years. To avoid over-leveraging with debt, tech giants are utilizing strong equity markets and high multiples to raise cash.
* **The Demise of the "Permanently Private" Model:**
  * **Voracious Capital Demands:** Although abundant private capital allowed companies to delay public listings for years, the massive infrastructure costs of AI mean companies can no longer fund 100% of their growth privately.
  * **Incentive to Transition:** Solomon notes that companies must eventually go public to access public currency and massive capital pools, despite the regulatory frictions and the differing pressures of managing a public entity.
* **SpaceX Relationship Banking:**
  * **Decades of Groundwork:** Commenting on Goldman Sachs securing the lead on the SpaceX IPO, Solomon emphasizes that the mandate was won over a 20-year period of relationship building with Elon Musk and CFO Brett Johnson, rather than a short-term pitch.

### AI and Creative Expression

* **The Evolution of Music Production:**
  * **From Vinyl to Laptops:** Solomon, a recreational musician and DJ, compares Bruce Springsteen recording *Darkness on the Edge of Town* (1978) using a full suite of physical studio musicians to modern digital production platforms (Logic Pro and Ableton) that allow a single user to simulate a full horn section in seconds.
* **The Limitations of AI in Art:**
  * **Absence of Personal Voice:** AI-generated music and screenwriting lack a true human "voice" because the technology does not possess lived experiences, personal trauma, or emotions. Without human curation, AI outputs default to mediocrity.
  * **Unresolved IP Conflicts:** Solomon highlights the legal and ethical friction surrounding AI models training on existing artist IP without offering fair compensation or recognition to the creator community.

**TL;DR**
While geopolitical instability in the Middle East and structural stagnation in Europe present significant short-term risks, long-term economic optimism remains high due to the transformative productivity potential of AI and the continued compounding power of global equity markets.

### Geopolitics and Market Volatility
*   **Middle East Conflict:** Jens Stoltenberg characterized the current situation as "serious and dangerous," noting that while the primary tragedy is human suffering, the economic fallout—reduced energy supplies, higher prices, and inflationary pressure—is inevitable.
*   **Market Disconnection:** David Solomon expressed surprise that equity markets have not "derisked" more in response to geopolitical tension.
    *   **Underpriced Risks:** While rate and commodity markets have reacted, equity risk premia remain low. Solomon suggests the market is discounting the high probability of a "longer conflict" that could leave "sand in the gears" of global growth.
*   **Private Credit Resilience:** Despite concerns about higher rates, Solomon argues that the private credit asset class (approx. $1.7 trillion) is manageable. 
    *   **Historical Context:** During the Financial Crisis, leveraged lending saw a 10% default rate but a 50% recovery rate; with current coupons at 9-10%, the net loss remains sustainable for diversified portfolios.

### The Growth Gap: Europe vs. the United States
*   **Shifting Allocations:** The Norwegian Sovereign Wealth Fund has halved its European exposure (from 42% to 21%) over the last decade while increasing US exposure to 55%, driven largely by the divergence in earnings growth.
*   **Structural Impediments:** Solomon identified a widening gap between US trend growth (~2%) and European trend growth (~0.7%).
    *   **Fragmentation:** Europe functions as 27 independent nations rather than a single economic bloc, inhibiting the creation of "European champions" in sectors like banking.
    *   **Bureaucracy:** Excessive regulation and a failure to implement reforms (such as those suggested in the Draghi report) act as headwinds.
*   **Risk Culture:** Solomon argued that a "risk-taking culture" is notably absent in Europe compared to the US. He noted that the "risk-taking genes" largely migrated to America, leaving Europe with a more cautious, status-quo-oriented population.
*   **Investment Strategy:** Stoltenberg defended the fund’s "conservative" index-based approach, owning roughly 1.5% of 7,200 companies globally as the most effective risk management tool for a democratic nation.

### AI and the Productivity Revolution
*   **Enterprise Transformation:** Solomon views AI as a "powerful technology acceleration" that allows firms to move beyond simple automation to "remaking operating processes" from scratch.
    *   **Capacity for Growth:** Productivity gains from AI allow enterprises to reinvest saved capital into growth areas where they were previously resource-constrained.
*   **The Ford Analogy:** Solomon compared the AI revolution to Henry Ford’s implementation of the assembly line, which allowed for a shift from a six-day to a five-day work week without losing productivity.
*   **Norway’s Digital Exception:** Stoltenberg noted that while Europe lags generally, Norway maintains high productivity per hour worked due to early and aggressive adoption of digital solutions in both the public and private sectors.

### The China Outlook
*   **Shifting Predictions:** Solomon noted that in 2014, economists predicted China would be the world's largest economy by now. While China hit its GDP targets, the US outperformed expectations so significantly that China’s path to the top spot is no longer "cast in stone."
*   **Capital Allocation:** Solomon expressed skepticism that a centrally controlled economy can compete indefinitely with a market-driven one.
    *   **Interdependence:** Despite friction, Solomon emphasized that "we need China, and China needs us," suggesting a dynamic of "gives and gets" rather than total decoupling.

### Notable Quotes

> "In 1996, [the Sovereign Wealth Fund] was zero. Now it is two trillion... 27% of every krone we spend on the Norwegian budget is financed by the return from the fund." — **Jens Stoltenberg**

> "A lot of the risk-taking genes left with the Mayflower. The risk-takers per definition said, 'Let’s take some risks, let’s go to America.' The people who are left in Europe are the people who don’t want to change." — **David Solomon**

> "Find me a 25-year period of time where the world was not better 25 years in the future from where it was 25 years in the past. You can’t find one for the last thousands of years." — **David Solomon**

### TL;DR
The current macro environment offers a "sweet spot" for financial assets, driven by significant stimulus and a capital investment super cycle that's poised to drive strong M&A and IPO activity. Both Goldman Sachs and Andreessen Horowitz are strategically adapting to this landscape, with Goldman focusing on scale and funding diversification, while a16z is growing its venture influence and actively shaping crucial policy for emerging technologies like AI and crypto.

### Current Economic & Market Outlook
-   **"Sweet Spot" for Financial Assets:** The U.S. macro picture is highly favorable for financial and investable assets.
    -   **Fiscal Stimulus:** Significant and continuously increasing, with recent legislation adding to an already stimulative environment.
    -   **Monetary Stimulus:** In a rate-cutting cycle, with a few more cuts anticipated.
    -   **Capital Investment Super Cycle:** Unprecedented levels of investment; last year, the four largest companies contributed 1% to GDP growth with $400 billion in spending.
    -   **Deregulatory Wind-back:** A shift from a massive regulatory surge, further stimulating the economy.
    -   **Economic Resilience:** The economy is hard to slow down due to this "cocktail of stimulus" and enormous financial leverage, despite average Americans feeling stress from 25-30% higher prices.
-   **Market Dynamics:**
    -   **AI Productivity Gains:** The market is "pulling forward" expectations for AI-driven productivity gains over the next 1-4 years.
    -   **Geopolitical Fragility:** Moving to a multipolar world, with higher geopolitical risks and increased volatility due to social media.
-   **M&A and IPO Expectations:**
    -   **Regulatory Shift:** The previous "no" to M&A questions from regulators is now "maybe," even for very significant deals, due to a changing regulatory environment.
    -   **Projected Surge:** Anticipated to be the "biggest M&A year in history" and a significant year for IPOs, with many large companies finally going public.
    -   **AI-Driven IPOs:** The rapid erosion of competitive leads in AI (due to the ability to "throw money at the problem" with data and GPUs) will compel companies to seek capital through IPOs.
    -   **FTC Uncertainty:** Concerns remain regarding the FTC's aggressive stance, especially on big tech, potentially favoring IP transactions over traditional M&A.

### Goldman Sachs: Evolving for Future Relevancy
-   **Entrepreneurial Heritage & Evolution:** The firm was built "brick by brick" by entrepreneurial partners, maintaining a partnership culture even 25 years post-IPO.
    -   **1999 IPO:** Essential for long-term relevance, providing the permanent capital needed to participate in the global expansion of capital markets.
    -   **Post-IPO Culture:** Maintaining an aspirational partnership culture (450 partners, compensated relative to enterprise performance) while evolving from a small private partnership to a public company requiring top-down strategic direction for growth.
-   **Strategic Priorities for the Future:**
    -   **CEO's Role:** Owns firm strategy, ensures execution for growth, and manages big-picture strategic risks.
    -   **Scale is Paramount:** As an institutional firm, scale provides "enormous leverage and latitude" in mature businesses.
        -   **Current Gap:** Goldman Sachs has a $1.9 trillion balance sheet compared to JP Morgan's $4.5 trillion.
        -   **Future Goal:** Goldman Sachs aims to be "at least three and a half" times its current size if JP Morgan reaches six, necessitating growth beyond purely organic means.
    -   **Funding Diversification:** Reducing reliance on wholesale funding, a major strategic risk.
        -   **Shift from Wholesale:** Once the "largest wholesale funder in the world" 10 years ago, a position not desired.
        -   **Digital Deposit Platform:** Built from zero to ~$500 billion in total deposits, now funding ~40% of the firm, offering more stability than institutional wholesale funding.
    -   **Core Values:** Client Service, Partnership, Integrity, Excellence guide the firm's operations.

### Andreessen Horowitz: Scaling Innovation & Shaping Policy
-   **Founding & Evolution:** Started in 2009 during a challenging market, recognizing "the best time to raise money is when nobody has money."
    -   **"Top-Tier" Strategy:** Achieved top-tier status by offering a "better product" for entrepreneurs, enabling founders to run their companies as CEOs with enhanced brand, power, and access.
    -   **Scaling for "Software is Eating the World":** Designed the firm to scale and address a market beyond the traditional "15 significant companies" per year to potentially 150, necessitating a different organizational structure than traditional VC.
    -   **Market Leadership:** Now "the biggest," having raised ~18.3% of all U.S. venture capital in the last year.
-   **Future Ambition: Growing the Market:** Inspired by Andy Grove, a16z views itself as responsible for growing the entire industry and ensuring technological leadership for the U.S.
    -   **American Dynamism:** Focuses on how the country wins technologically and remains competitive with China.
-   **Key Policy Agendas:**
    -   **Crypto Policy:**
        -   **Importance:** A "profound breakthrough" in financial technology and society (property rights, business architecture for creatives, stakeholder capitalism).
        -   **Past Challenges:** Faced a "complete ban" by the previous administration through "abuse of power," including debanking and "wells notices" for private companies.
        -   **Legislative Wins:** Successfully pushed for the Genius Act and Stable Coin Bill.
        -   **Current Focus:** Advocating for the "Clarity Act" (market structure bill) to establish clear rules for classifying tokens (security vs. other assets), countering the "everything's a security" approach.
    -   **AI Policy:**
        -   **Existential Threat:** Concerns that banning or infringing on AI development (e.g., regulating math) will lead the U.S. to lose the AI race to China, with "hundred-year implications."
        -   **Key Protections:** Advocating to:
            -   **"Don't Regulate Math":** Regulate the *applications* of AI (e.g., using AI for illegal activities), not the underlying mathematical models themselves, as models are not sentient.
            -   **Prevent State-Specific Laws:** Avoid 50 different state AI laws that would stifle innovation across states.
            -   **Enable Copyright Training:** Allow building statistical models over copyrighted works (not reproduction) to create smarter AI, acknowledging China's lack of copyright respect.

### AI's Transformative Impact & Operational Challenges
-   **Internal Adoption & Productivity:**
    -   **Goldman Sachs:** Views AI as an "acceleration" of productivity for its professional services workforce, enabling more impact through tools and applications.
    -   **Andreessen Horowitz:** Aggressively automating non-preferred tasks, leveraging data lakes for insights, and enhancing customer support with AI.
-   **Reimagining Enterprise Processes:**
    -   **Goldman Sachs (1GS 3.0):** Focusing on "completely reimagining" fundamental operating processes across the massive enterprise to automate and make them more efficient.
    -   **Strategic Investment:** Efficiency gains create capacity to invest in growth areas; for example, finding $2 billion in savings could allow spending $8 billion on technology instead of $6 billion while maintaining returns.
    -   **Challenges:** Requires top-down leadership to overcome cultural resistance to change "empires" and navigate significant regulatory hurdles for new technology adoption.
-   **AI in Investing:**
    -   **Limitations:** Models rely on available facts, but major investment shifts often stem from "completely new and unexpected" events not found in past data.
    -   **Potential:** While models can quickly incorporate new information, it's "interesting to see" if they can outperform human investors, many of whom underperform due to biases.
