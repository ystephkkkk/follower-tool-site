### TL;DR
The Texas Stock Exchange (TXSE) has raised $440 million in total equity backing—including a newly announced investment from State Street—and surpassed $300 billion in listings within its first 22 days of trading. CEO James Lee argues that the 30-year listing duopoly of NYSE and Nasdaq is over, driven by a mission to reduce regulatory and legal frictions for public companies.

### Market Context & The Public Company Decline
- **Shrinking Public Markets:** **James Lee** noted that the U.S. has half the number of public companies it had at its peak 30 years ago (dropping to around 8,100), despite record-high indices.
- **The IPO Backlog:** **James Lee** stated there are currently 31,000 sponsor-backed companies stuck in a heavily backlogged pipeline, representing $4 trillion of marks on sponsors' books. At current exit rates, clearing this backlog will take 9.5 years.
- **Larger Issuance Sizes:** **James Lee** highlighted that today's average IPO market cap at issuance is $2.5 billion—four times larger than 20–23 years ago—meaning everyday investors miss out on the steepest parts of growth curves.
- **Burdensome Disclosures:** **James Lee** contrasted historical prospectuses with modern filings, noting that Anthropic’s recent filing featured a 400-page prospectus with 100 pages dedicated entirely to risk disclosures.

### TXSE Capital, Backing, & Ownership
- **Breaking News - State Street Investment:** **James Lee** announced that State Street has joined TXSE with a significant equity investment, bringing total equity capital to $440 million—an unprecedented amount for an exchange applying for SEC registration.
- **Institutional Backing:** **James Lee** detailed that TXSE’s ownership structure includes BlackRock, Vanguard, JP Morgan, Goldman Sachs, and Bank of America.
- **Liquidity Provider Support:** **James Lee** reported that 9 of the top 10 U.S. liquidity providers (representing 90% of daily U.S. equity volume) are equity holders in TXSE.
- **Underwriting & ETF Concentration:** **James Lee** noted that equity holders include lead bookrunners for 91% of IPO proceeds over the last 5 years, and sponsors representing two-thirds of the nation's $17 trillion in ETF assets.

### Exchange Competition & Market Fragmentation
- **End of the Duopoly:** **James Lee** declared that the day of a listing duopoly (Nasdaq and NYSE) is over, establishing TXSE as the third registered listing exchange in the U.S.
- **Trading vs. Listing Disconnect:** **James Lee** addressed concerns about startups lacking track records by explaining that under the National Market System (Reg NMS), there is no longer a correlation between where a company lists and where it trades. Intraday volume for NYSE-listed companies is only 8–9%, with 50% of U.S. volume occurring off-exchange across 20 exchanges and 200 venues.
- **Strict Listing Standards:** **James Lee** emphasized that TXSE has not relaxed standards, adopting quantitative standards higher than its competitors, rendering nearly 35% of current U.S. public corporates ineligible to transfer to TXSE.

### Legal Jurisdictions & Regulatory Alignment
- **The Texas Legal Exodus:** **James Lee** pointed to major companies re-domiciling into Texas—including Tesla, Coinbase, SpaceX, Dillard's, and Exxon (which moved from New Jersey after 144 years on May 27)—creating a viable alternative to Delaware.
- **Quantifiable Cost Reductions:** **James Lee** stated that a standard Fortune 100 company reorganizing into Texas and listing on TXSE can reduce cost structures by tens of millions of dollars annually in insurance savings, franchise taxes, and proxy preparation.
- **Regulatory Tailwinds:** **James Lee** praised current SEC leadership for unravelling reporting apparatus costs, noting the repeal of climate disclosure mandates and the movement to rescind Rule 14a-8 regarding shareholder proposals, aligning with Texas state legislative models like SB 1057.

### Issuer-Aligned Economics
- **Transparent Revenue Sharing:** **James Lee** explained that TXSE’s financial model is entirely issuer-aligned, sharing overall economics (such as market data, corporate actions, and auction revenues) so that public companies experience a net-positive cash relationship rather than just paying opaque listing fees.
