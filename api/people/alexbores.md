**TL;DR**
The U.S. economy is currently defined by a "bifurcated" consumer and a concentrated labor market, while a bipartisan House vote to overturn Canadian tariffs signals growing Congressional pushback against executive trade powers. Amidst this, leaders in fast food and technology are pivoting toward AI and "trustworthiness" as the primary drivers of future global competitiveness.

### Economic Indicators and Consumer Bifurcation
* **Job Market Concentration:** While the latest jobs report doubled expectations, growth remains heavily concentrated in the healthcare and social assistance sectors. 
    * **Steve Liesman’s Outlook:** There is hope that January’s numbers indicate business stability despite tariff uncertainty, but "at least three months" of consistent data are needed for a confirmed trajectory.
* **Fiscal Deficits:** The CBO projects deficits could reach $3 trillion by 2036, keeping the 10-year Treasury yield elevated (hovering around 4.17%).
* **The "Bifurcated" Consumer:** Restaurant Brands Chairman Patrick Doyle notes a distinct split in spending habits based on household income.
    * **Income Threshold:** Households earning over $75,000 remain positive and active; those under $75,000 have become significantly more conservative over the last 18 months.
    * **Value Strategy:** Brands like Burger King are leaning into consistent $5 and $7 price points and restaurant renovations to win back lower-income diners.

### Trade Policy: Congressional Pushback on Tariffs
* **Canadian Tariff Reversal:** In a 219-211 vote, the House passed a resolution to revoke President Trump’s tariffs on Canada by ending the "national emergency" used as their legal basis.
    * **Bipartisan Action:** Six Republicans joined Democrats in the vote, citing negative impacts on local farmers and steelworkers.
    * **Political Retaliation:** President Trump has threatened "consequences come election time," including primary challenges, for any Republican voting against the tariffs.
* **Institutional Power:** Analysts suggest Congress is attempting to "claw back" emergency trade authorities (under IEEPA) that have historically granted the executive branch broad power over tariffs.

### AI Regulation and National Competitiveness
* **The RAISE Act:** New York Assemblymember Alex Bores is advocating for nationalized AI regulation focused on an eight-point plan including child safety, democracy protection (deepfakes), and workplace impacts.
* **Trust as a Product:** Bores argues that American AI will only win globally if it is perceived as more "trustworthy" than Chinese alternatives.
    * **Market Mispricing:** Bores believes the market currently undervalues AI safety and trustworthiness, which will be the long-term differentiator for winning users in Europe and elsewhere.
* **Public Investment:** New York’s $400 million "Empire AI" compute cluster is cited as a model for federal policy to reduce the cost of academic research and keep the U.S. ahead of global rivals.

* **McDonald’s (MCD):** Reported Q4 sales above estimates, fueled by a "value push" in the U.S. that successfully countered consumer concerns over "shrinkflation" and rising meal costs.
