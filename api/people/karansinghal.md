## Should You Trust ChatGPT With Your Health?

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9n0MI4D7TCM
- Published: 2026-10-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261020.html#video-9n0MI4D7TCM
- Speakers: Karan Singhal (Researcher, OpenAI); Jay Rughani (Partner, a16z)

---

### TL;DR
More than 300 million people use ChatGPT weekly for health and wellness, and OpenAI is integrating medical expertise directly into general-purpose frontier models to serve as a safety net for both patients and clinicians. While AI will not replace traditional medical care, OpenAI is actively building proactive personal health assistants, clinical copilots, and EHR integrations (such as with Epic) to make trustworthy health intelligence abundant and reduce administrative friction across the healthcare system.

### Scaling Health Intelligence via General-Purpose Frontier Models
* **Specialized Training in General Models:** OpenAI avoids using smaller, specialized healthcare models because they fall behind frontier capabilities too quickly. Instead, specialized healthcare data is added to every stage of training for ChatGPT models, blending general reasoning with clinical expertise.
* **Global Expert Networks:** OpenAI created a global physician network of hundreds of experts across dozens of specialties, languages, and countries to establish consensus, build evaluations, and train models on uncertain medical scenarios.
* **Massive Consumer Reach:** Over 300 million people use ChatGPT weekly for health and wellness questions, with the vast majority of these users on the free tier and located outside the U.S.

### Clinical Copilots and Enterprise Integrations
* **EHR and Workflow Connectors:** OpenAI has integrated ChatGPT into Epic, allowing clinicians to use ChatGPT in a sidebar for tasks like chart review without needing to switch tabs or platforms.
* **Administrative Cost Reductions:** Early enterprise adopters like Advent Health have reported roughly an 80% reduction in administrative workflow costs using AI across their workspaces.
* **Clinician Safety Nets:** Experiments with Panda Health deployed clinician copilots that proactively flagged medication interactions and helped clinicians avoid issues like antibiotic overuse.

### Patient Navigation and the "Personal Doctor in Your Pocket"
* **Proactive Personal Assistants:** OpenAI is developing proactive health agents designed to act as a health "quarterback"—managing schedules, integrating health records securely, and prompting users during chronic care journeys (e.g., flagging a rising A1C in a diabetic patient for medication review).
* **Bridging Information and Care:** While ChatGPT provides abundant health information and helps patients navigate care journeys (such as interpreting lab reports or preparing for doctor visits), OpenAI views AI as a complement to, rather than a replacement for, traditional medical care.
* **Democratizing Second Opinions:** Access to fast, cheap AI-driven health intelligence acts as a safety net for patients to double-check treatment plans, medication interactions, and diagnoses.

### Trust, Uncertainty, and Mental Health Evaluation
* **Handling Medical Uncertainty:** OpenAI trains models to recognize and explicitly state their own uncertainty (`Healthbench` includes evaluations on hedging when consensus is lacking).
* **Rigorous Mental Health Benchmarks:** Partnering with licensed mental health experts worldwide, OpenAI developed mental health benchmarks to measure both safety floors and positive model behaviors in complex, tricky scenarios.
* **Safety Priority:** When user preferences conflict with clinical safety guidelines, the model is trained to prioritize safety and direct users to seek professional care.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Cost of Intelligence:** Karan Singhal predicts that the cost of health intelligence will plummet dramatically once the required level of trustworthy intelligence is reached.
* **Administrative Compression:** Karan Singhal predicts an overall compression of healthcare spend over the coming years specifically driven by back-office, documentation, and administrative workflow automation.
* **Proactive Prevention as a Macro Factor:** Karan Singhal predicts that over the next 1 to 2 years, better proactive and preventative AI navigation will help patients avoid downstream costs of expensive procedures.
* **5-10 Year Treatment Innovations:** Karan Singhal predicts that over the next 5 to 10 years, AI-guided generation of new molecular targets and AI-guided prioritization/validation of clinical trials will lead to significantly improved, more personalized medicines.

## Building AI for better healthcare — the OpenAI Podcast Ep. 14

- Channel: OpenAI
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VAzryGwnJW8
- Published: 2026-03-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260316.html#video-VAzryGwnJW8
- Speakers: Nate Gross (Head of Health, OpenAI); Karan Singhal (Health AI Research Lead, OpenAI)

---

**TL;DR**
OpenAI is developing specialized AI foundations for healthcare by integrating physician feedback into every stage of model training, aiming to transform the industry from a reactive system into a proactive, accessible "safety net" for both clinicians and patients.

### Strategy for Health-Specific AI Development
* **Consumer Demand:** Approximately 1 in 4 ChatGPT users (40 million people per day) currently use the platform for health-related queries, necessitating a dedicated health strategy.
* **The ChatGPT Health Foundation:** A specialized framework designed to provide secure, context-aware interactions.
    * **Data Privacy:** Uses encrypted "one-way valves" to protect conversations; OpenAI explicitly does not train its models on user healthcare data.
    * **Context Retention:** Unlike search engines that have "amnesia," the health foundation allows patients to ground AI responses in their own medical history and personal context.
* **The Three-Tiered Mission:** Dr. Nate Gross defines the team's objectives as:
    * **Raise the Floor:** Making medical expertise accessible to everyone globally.
    * **Sweep the Floor:** Automating administrative and bureaucratic burdens to give doctors more time with patients.
    * **Raise the Ceiling:** Accelerating medical discovery and achieving "transformative" outcomes that exceed current human capabilities.

### Rigorous Training and "HealthBench" Evaluation
* **Physician Collaboration:** OpenAI worked with a cohort of 250 physicians to generate data and evaluate model performance across 5,000 simulated clinical conversations.
* **Multidimensional Rubrics:** The models are evaluated using "HealthBench," which measures approximately 49,000 dimensions of performance, including:
    * **Seeking Context:** Training the model to ask follow-up questions (e.g., if a user says "it burns") rather than providing immediate, potentially unsafe advice.
    * **Adaptive Literacy:** The ability to tailor responses based on the user’s role, such as explaining a condition differently to a pharmacist in Kenya versus a patient with a 3rd-grade literacy level.
    * **Uncertainty Calibration:** Reducing "overconfident hallucinations" by training the model to admit when it does not know an answer and suggesting specific clinical follow-ups.
* **Deep Integration:** Unlike other models that focus on passing medical board exams, OpenAI integrates health-specific data into every phase: pre-training, mid-training, and post-training.

### Clinical Utility and Real-World Impact
* **Clinical Co-pilot Study:** In a partnership with Panda Health involving 20+ clinics in Nairobi, OpenAI deployed an AI "safety net" to monitor electronic health records.
    * **Error Reduction:** The study showed a statistically significant reduction in diagnostic and treatment errors when the AI flagged concerning data for clinicians.
* **Multimodal Integration:** The future of the platform involves "zero-to-one capabilities" where models analyze decades of history, lab tests, and wearable data (rings, watches, biosensors) to predict health trends a human might miss.
* **Unified AI Layers:** OpenAI aims to use the API to connect fragmented, siloed healthcare systems—bridging the gap between analog, digital, structured, and unstructured data.

### Emerging "Aha" Moments in Development
* **Medical Breakthroughs:** The research team is seeing instances where AI identifies new, direct clinical values for medications that have been "sitting on a shelf" for years.
* **The "Waymo" Effect:** Karan Singhal notes a shift in sentiment where clinicians are beginning to feel it is "dangerous" to work *without* AI, comparing it to the safety of an autonomous vehicle compared to a human driver.
* **Solving "Miracle Cases":** The technology is increasingly being used to solve "unsolved diagnoses" for patients who have struggled within the traditional medical system for years.
