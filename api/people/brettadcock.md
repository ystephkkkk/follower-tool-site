#### TL;DR
AI and automation will ultimately grow 100x larger than the internet, completely disrupting human-computer interaction and physical labor over the next five years. Brett Adcock—founder of Figure.ai, Hark, and Cover—shares insights on building frontier companies in humanoid robotics and digital AI-human symbiosis.

### The State of Humanoid Robotics & Figure.ai
- **The Core Thesis:** Brett Adcock notes that Figure.ai is building humanoid robots as a single piece of hardware capable of doing everything in the world, addressing the massive global labor shortage and high turnover rates in industrial and logistics settings.
- **Product Milestones:** 
  - Figure recently manufactured its 1,000th EVT robot for Figure 3.
  - Robots are actively deployed with commercial customers like BMW, handling logistics and package sorting.
  - A recent live stream demonstrated a Figure robot sorting packages off a conveyor belt at human speeds (2.9 seconds per package) for 200 hours straight.
- **Why Humanoids over Quadruped Robots:** Adcock argues that humanoid robots are only about 3x to 4x harder to build than four-legged dog robots, but offer a million times higher TAM (Total Addressable Market) and ROI.
- **The Home Robotics Frontier:** While commercial logistics provides near-term revenue, Adcock spends 3 to 4 hours daily, 7 days a week, working on the harder problem of making robots operate autonomously in new domestic environments they have never seen before (e.g., folding laundry in varying lighting and table heights).

### Hark and the Post-iPhone Era of AI Devices
- **The Digital Symbiosis Thesis:** Alongside physical robotics, Adcock founded Hark to build digital AI-human symbiosis—acting as a "Jarvis from Iron Man" style superhuman assistant with near-perfect memory, multi-modal capabilities, and autonomous computer use.
- **Solving Computer Use:** Most AI agents rely on APIs or MCPs, but only 1 in 1,000 websites have APIs. Hark solved general-purpose computer use by spinning up a virtual computer sandbox for every agent, allowing them to look at a screen and use a mouse and keyboard autonomously via a custom reinforcement learning process.
- **Reinventing Hardware:** Hark is designing next-generation AI devices to replace the iPhone and MacBook, which Adcock calls "complete rubbish for AI." 
  - The team features a hardware design leader who worked on the last several generations of iPhones and MacBooks.
  - Adcock dismisses Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses as "horrible" products that fail to operate independently without an iPhone tether.
- **Launch Timeline:** Hark will launch traditional browser and mobile app versions in about a month, with sci-fi-level custom hardware currently being tested in the lab.

### The Talent Wars & Meta's Aggressive Hiring
- **Extremely Selective Engineering Bar:** At Figure, mechanical engineering candidates must pass rigorous design case studies (building actuators, gearboxes, and bearings from scratch). Out of 10 case studies a week for six months, Figure hired zero candidates.
- **Meta Buying the AI Race:** Meta has been aggressively paying top dollar to monopolize the 20 to 30 people in California who know how to build advanced AI pre-training models.
  - Senior AI infra engineers are receiving offers from Meta worth **$36 million in RSUs over four years** of guaranteed cash.
  - Junior engineers in their late 20s are commanding total compensation packages between **$750k and $2 million per year**.
- **Adcock's Take on Meta's Strategy:** While Meta's mercenaries are purely money-driven, Adcock respects the execution and admits he would have done the same if he were Mark Zuckerberg to buy into the AI race. However, he prefers a smaller, mission-driven team of 20 to 30 people.

### Cover: Re-engineering K-12 School Safety
- **Pivoting the Tech Stack:** Cover aims to stop school shootings by deploying full-scale weapon-detection scanning systems. Adcock pivoted the entire technology stack a year ago to design custom silicon chips in Europe, reducing the price by 90% and making it vastly more scalable.
- **Leadership:** The company is led by a chief engineer from NASA's JPL.
- **Timeline:** The team is building out the full-scale system in October 2024 and aims to bring it up before the end of the year, though initial beta testing in a K-12 school might slip by a quarter due to a 6-month silicon fabrication lead time.

### Founder Psychology & Operating Frameworks
- **The Three-Bucket Life Strategy:** Adcock divides life into three buckets: Work, Family (three young kids), and Social/Travel. To achieve an A+ in work and family, he completely zeroed out the social bucket, turning down even 10-day visits from college roommates.
- **Rock Bottom as a Baseline:** During his first startup, *Veterary* (2012–2017), Adcock was severely broke, took out $50k–$100k in personal loans, and operated in negative bank balances before selling the company for **$110 million**. At Archer, he took out a second mortgage on his house and funded Figure using falling stock lockups.
- **Handling Lows:** His mental framework for extreme stress is to build a punch list and take things "day by day," echoing the mindset used when training for ultramarathons (focusing on a tiny distance ahead before considering quitting).
- **The Advantage of Hard Things:** Adcock believes working on hard things is easier than easy things because there is less competition, higher investor appetite for binary payouts, and they attract top-tier overachievers.
