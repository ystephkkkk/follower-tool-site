**TL;DR**
Jeff Bezos and Prometheus co-founder Vik Bajaj unveiled details of their $14.5 billion "AI for atoms" startup—which is already compressing months-long engineering tasks into hours for early alpha partners like Blue Origin—while Bezos defended his outreach to President Trump, urged Nvidia’s board to hire internally, and revealed how Amazon Studios cast Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond.

### Prometheus: "AI for Atoms" & The Manufacturing Economy
* **Thesis & Technology:** Unlike LLMs that manipulate symbols by reading the internet, engineering requires specialized models trained on physics and real-world constraints. Prometheus builds neural surrogates for numerical simulation using a single multiphysics model.
  * **Scale & Funding:** Bezos invested at the seed stage in late 2024; the company now employs 250 people and has raised approximately $14.5 billion, with much of it committed to compute.
  * **Early Benchmarks:** Prometheus tools are designing analog chips and radios in 12 hours—work that typically takes a team of 10 to 20 people 6 to 9 months. A teammate from JPL also prompted the tools to redesign the Mars helicopter; the model explored quadcopters, counterrotating helicopters, and fixed-wing aircraft before calculating the blade chord for the Martian atmosphere.
* **Go-To-Market & Business Model:**
  * **Private Alpha:** Currently in private alpha with early customers, including Blue Origin, which is using the tools on two active projects.
  * **Open Ecosystem over Walled Garden:** Vik Bajaj noted that the manufacturing market is worth tens of trillions of dollars, making a "walled garden" impossible. Prometheus will both make its tools broadly available and invest directly in some of the companies it helps, benefiting as a principal rather than exclusively as a toolmaker.
* **Data Trust & Supply Chain Impact:**
  * **Protecting IP:** Addressing fears of frontier labs acting like "the fox in the hen house," Bajaj emphasized that Prometheus respects proprietary engineering traditions, trains on publicly observable product data, and generates much of its own synthetic data.
  * **Accelerating Legacy Processes:** Bezos cited precision aerospace casting—which still relies on trial-and-error X-rays and mold insulation—as an area where AI can create repeatable processes, potentially cutting 9-to-12-month part wait times down to a month.

### Politics, Democrats, & Root-Cause Problem Solving
* **Engaging with Presidents:** Facing questions from Liz Hoffman about whether his closeness to President Trump is a liability now that Democrats hold the gavel, Bezos stated he has worked with every president since Clinton (noting interactions with Biden were the most limited). He described Trump as uniquely communicative and accessible, but insisted his outreach has always been for the country's benefit rather than to advantage Amazon.
* **The "Pick a Villain" Trap:** Bezos argued that while targeting a villain—historically lawyers, Wall Street, landlords, and now Big Tech—is effective politics, it fails to solve systemic problems.
  * **Housing Affordability:** Using housing as a core example, Bezos noted that high prices stem from subsidizing demand while constraining supply. He pointed to Austin, which lowered housing costs over the last 10 years despite significant population growth by increasing supply (such as eliminating requirements for two parking spots per apartment unit).
* **Tech & Democratic Priorities:** Bajaj added that rapid technological advancement gives Democrats a rare opportunity to achieve long-standing policy goals in education, infrastructure, and healthcare at scale.

### Nvidia, AI Investing, & Life Sciences
* **Advice for Nvidia's Board:** Declining to weigh in on whether Jensen Huang should return due to a lack of inside boardroom details, Bezos nonetheless praised Huang's foresight. His advice to Nvidia's board: hire from within, as outside CEOs in complex companies have a "vanishingly small chance of succeeding." He dismissed comparisons to Amazon, noting his own departure was a multi-year, planned transition.
* **Investor Discernment:** Bezos observed that early technology cycles fund every experiment ("the good, the bad, and the ugly"), but AI investors are now becoming more discerning and diverting capital to stronger ideas—a healthy sign for the market.
* **Healthcare & Biotech Timelines:** Bajaj, who previously ran Google's life sciences business, stressed the need to "underpromise and overdeliver" in biology.
  * Early cancer detection has been a 10-year journey, though machine-learning blood tests are now on the market.
  * Drug discovery and development (a ~15-year effort) is on the verge of accelerating cures, driven by specialized AI firms like Isomorphic, Zera, and Initro.

### Casting the Next James Bond
* **The Selection Process:** Bezos confirmed he was heavily involved in Amazon Studios' search for the next 007. After narrowing a large pool to five finalists, the team ran traditional screen tests featuring full costumes, real sets, other actors, and three different scenes per finalist.
* **Why Robert Pattinson:** Despite an initial slight bias against Pattinson due to his existing fame, Bezos said his screen tests made the choice "immediately undebated" alongside director Denis, leading to a final decision on Friday.

### TL;DR
Jeff Bezos and Vik discussed their physical AI startup Prometheus—which has raised ~$14.5 billion and grown to 250 people to transform industrial manufacturing—alongside Bezos's views on political engagement, Nvidia's leadership transition, AI market discernment, and Amazon's decision to cast Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond.

### Prometheus & "AI for Atoms" in Manufacturing
* **Limits of Traditional LLMs:** Jeff Bezos explained that while frontier LLMs are strong symbol manipulators (math, law) trained on the internet, they cannot learn physical engineering by reading—just as six top physicists (e.g., Newton, Einstein, Maxwell) could not build a toaster oven.
* **Specialized Multi-Physics Models:** Prometheus builds neuro-surrogates for numerical simulation using a single multi-physics model to handle the heterogeneous, physics-heavy demands of engineering.
  * **Scale & Funding:** The company has 250 employees and has raised approximately $14.5 billion, with a large portion already committed to compute.
  * **Speed & Capability:** Prometheus is designing analog chips, radios, and valves in 12 hours—work that typically takes a team of 10 to 20 people six to nine months.
  * **Mars Helicopter Test:** A teammate from JPL prompted Prometheus tools to redesign the Mars helicopter; the model explored quadcopters, counter-rotating helicopters, and fixed-wing designs, calculated blade chord for the Martian atmosphere, and converged on a viable Mars helicopter design.
* **Accelerating Invention:** Bezos framed Prometheus's mission as accelerating the "dream-build loop" of invention, noting that all civilizational wealth stems from inventions like the plow (6,000 years ago) and the steam engine.

### Business Model, Data Trust, & Early Traction
* **Open Enabler vs. Walled Garden:** Bezos stated Prometheus cannot be a walled garden because the manufactured world represents tens of trillions of dollars and a massive, complex supply chain requiring cooperation with thousands of companies.
  * **Hybrid Approach:** Prometheus will make its tools broadly available while also investing in some of the companies it helps, benefiting as a principal rather than exclusively as a toolmaker.
* **Early Adoption:** Prometheus is in private alpha with early customers, including Blue Origin, which is actively using the tools on two internal projects. Bezos noted there are no plans to merge or tether Prometheus, Blue Origin, and Amazon together.
* **Addressing Data Mistrust:** Responding to concerns that frontier labs act like "the fox in the hen house" to steal corporate alpha, Vik emphasized that Prometheus respects proprietary engineering processes, generates much of its own training data, and focuses on non-proprietary data sets.
  * **The Casting Example:** Bezos illustrated how traditional manufacturing (like precision aerospace casting) relies on trial-and-error X-raying and mold insulation. Making this repeatable could cut wait times for complex cast parts from 9–12 months down to roughly a month, benefiting the entire supply chain.
  * **Data Strategy:** Bezos advised companies with truly unique, valuable proprietary data sets to guard them closely, though he noted such data sets are rarer than many companies assume.

### Politics, Policy, & Root-Cause Problem Solving
* **Engaging with Presidents:** Bezos defended his outreach to political leaders, stating he has worked with every president since Clinton to benefit the country rather than to advantage Amazon. He described Donald Trump as quantitatively the most communicative and accessible president he has dealt with, while noting he still reaches out to Barack Obama for advice.
* **Value of Business Input:** Bezos argued that shutting out business leaders is a mistake because politicians on Capitol Hill lack "ground truth" about the free-market engine that funds wealth creation and redistribution.
* **Root Causes Over "Pick a Villain":** Addressing affordability and housing, Bezos criticized the political tactic of blaming "villains" (lawyers, Wall Street, landlords, Big Tech) instead of fixing root causes.
  * **Housing Supply:** He pointed out that housing costs rise when policies subsidize demand and constrain supply, citing Austin as a city that lowered housing costs over the last 10 years despite significant population growth by removing burdensome rules (such as requiring two parking spots per apartment unit).
* **Tech-Enabled Policy Progress:** Vik added that rapid technological advances create an opportunity to make progress on longstanding priorities in education, infrastructure, and healthcare.

### Nvidia Leadership, AI Market Cycles, & Life Sciences
* **Nvidia & Jensen Huang:** Bezos declined to speculate on whether Jensen Huang should return to Nvidia, citing a lack of visibility into boardroom details, though he praised Huang's foresight and hard work.
  * **Succession Advice:** Bezos strongly advised against hiring an outside CEO for a complex company like Nvidia, calling it a "last resort" with a vanishingly small chance of success, and noted that Amazon's multi-year planned transition is not a comparable analogy.
* **AI Investor Discernment:** Bezos observed that in early technology cycles, "the good, the bad, and the ugly" all get funded. He believes the AI market is now entering a healthy phase where investors are becoming more discerning and diverting capital to stronger ideas.
* **Life Sciences & Healthcare Timelines:** Vik urged scientific rigor and realistic expectations ("underpromise and overdeliver") in healthcare AI, noting that early cancer detection has been a 10-year journey and AI drug discovery/development has been underway for ~15 years. He cited specialized companies like Isomorphic, Xaira, and Insitro as turning these tools into real products.

### Casting Robert Pattinson as James Bond
* **Selection Process:** Bezos was heavily involved in Amazon's first James Bond casting decision, which concluded on Friday after a tight process that narrowed a large group down to five finalists.
* **Old-Fashioned Screen Tests:** Each of the five finalists filmed three different scenes in full costume on real sets with other actors.
* **Unanimous Choice:** Despite an initial bias against Pattinson because he is already so famous—and a preference toward someone more unknown—Bezos, director Denis Villeneuve, and the team spent a couple of hours reviewing the tapes and immediately agreed that Pattinson "is James Bond."
