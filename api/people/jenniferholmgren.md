## How cutting emissions is future-proofing industries beyond the ‘green premium’

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Urn67odHEaE

### TL;DR
Decarbonizing heavy industry requires driving clean-tech "green premiums" down through mass deployment and strict unit economics, moving past early-stage ambition into commercial delivery.

### First Movers Coalition (FMC) Overview
* **Coalition Purpose:** Formed around 2021 by the World Economic Forum, the First Movers Coalition aggregates collective purchase demand for clean tech by 2030 to give investors and project developers commercial confidence.
* **Hard-to-Abate Sectors:** The initiative covers seven high-emissions sectors—aluminum, steel, cement/concrete (materials); shipping, trucking, aviation (mobility); and carbon dioxide removal (cross-cutting).
* **FMC Day Focus:** The mid-year summit assesses progress among senior decision-makers, emphasizing delivery over ambition amid energy market disruptions and geopolitical conflicts.

### Commercializing Carbon Recycling and Sustainable Fuels
* **LanzaTech Process:** **Jennifer Holmgren** explains that LanzaTech captures carbon monoxide and CO2 from steel mills and uses bacteria fermentation taking seconds to convert waste gas into 90% protein animal feed and ethanol.
* **Commercial Scale & Products:** 
  * Four commercial plants are operating in China (converting steel/ferro alloy gases to ethanol) alongside a Gent, Belgium facility with ArcelorMittal.
  * Ethanol is used in marine fuel (boasting 40% more energy density than methanol) and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF) via a 10-million-gallon-per-year plant in Atlanta, Georgia (LanzaJet).
* **Oberon Fuels Operations:** **Rebecca Boudreaux** states Oberon converts organic waste streams (tree pulp methanol, food waste, agricultural waste) into renewable dimethyl ether (DME) used as a propane blend for vehicles and an aerosol propellant.
* **Corporate Partnerships:** Oberon works with Volvo Trucks, Mack Trucks, Ford, Suburban Propane, Aeropress, and L'Oreal (selecting Oberon out of 1,000 global startups for their accelerator).

### Economics, the "Green Premium," and Market Architecture
* **The Green Premium as a Bridge:** Both Holmgren and Boudreaux view the green premium—the higher cost of sustainable alternatives—as a temporary bridge that shrinks through industrial scaling, much like early solar and mobile phones.
* **Security Premiums:** **Noam Boussidan** notes that the green premium is increasingly viewed as a "security premium," offering energy diversification and supply chain resilience during disruptions like the Strait of Hormuz blockages.
* **Techno-Economics First:** **Rajesh Swaminathan** of Khosla Ventures emphasizes that Khosla avoids factoring subsidies or the U.S. Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) into investment memos, focusing instead on achieving lower capital expenditure (capex) and operating expenditure (opex) than fossil fuel commodities.
* **Investment Examples:** 
  * **Hera (Steel):** Backed by Khosla for its single-step, low-equipment capex architecture producing high-purity iron or commodity steel.
  * **Verdigy (Hydrogen):** Repurposed a chlor-alkali system designed for PVC pipe manufacturing to produce green hydrogen at $3 per kilogram without subsidies.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Jennifer Holmgren (LanzaTech):** 
  * In 5 years, the industry will transition from building skepticism to active construction, with western plant pipelines breaking ground and financing timelines dropping from 5 years to 2 years.
  * In 10 years, carbon-recycling feedstocks will be fully cost-competitive with current resources.
* **Rajesh Swaminathan (Khosla Ventures):** 
  * Verdigy’s new gigafactory will scale green hydrogen production down to $2 per kilogram within 2 to 3 years.
  * Super hot rock geothermal will reach commercial viability earlier than nuclear fusion, with both delivering sub-5-cents-per-kilowatt-hour 24/7 base load power.
