### TL;DR
Guillermo Rauch, Founder & CEO of Vercel, warns that AI coding agents have made software creation virtually free while simultaneously giving attackers superhuman hacking capabilities, making cybersecurity the number one threat to global infrastructure. To survive, enterprises must deploy defensive AI—leveraging open-weight models and automated code auditing—and rebuild decades of legacy tech debt.

### The AI Cybersecurity Crisis & Vercel's Response
* **Superhuman Hacking Capabilities:** Previously, exploiting a vulnerability required a dedicated adversary; today, attackers use AI tokens and agents with superhuman hacking abilities.
  * **Lateral Movement:** When a single company is breached, agents can extract data, secrets, and documents to pivot across company boundaries and hack interconnected networks.
  * **Personal & Supply Chain Hygiene:** Rauch advises enabling multi-factor authentication (MFA) and guarding against compromised software downloads and supply chains.
* **Vercel's Defensive Tooling:** Vercel is embedding AI agents directly into the deployment pipeline to audit code generated by tools like Claude Code and Codex before it goes live.
  * **Vercel Security Review:** Announced last week, the product identified **1,000 critical and high-severity vulnerabilities** within its first **24 hours** in code customers were attempting to deploy.
  * **Deepsec:** Vercel open-sourced a cybersecurity harness called **Deepsec** to help organizations eliminate vulnerabilities across their infrastructure.

### Tech Debt, Front-End Risks, and Rebuilding Infrastructure
* **The "Single Origin" Front-End:** For **10 years**, Vercel has advised companies via its **Front-End Cloud** to own every pixel of their websites.
  * **Third-Party Pixel Risk:** Many modern websites are built on layers of tech debt. Rauch cited high-profile hacks where a site like `chase.com` was compromised not because Chase was hacked, but because an unsandboxed third-party pixel embedded on the front end was breached.
* **Rebuilding Legacy Foundations:** Decades of human-built software infrastructure must be rebuilt from the ground up using memory-safe programming languages to become "provably unhackable."
* **Enterprise Governance:** Coding agents can inadvertently expose sensitive data to the public internet (e.g., OpenAI accidentally hacking Hugging Face).
  * **Behind-the-Firewall Deployment:** Enterprises deploying Vercel do so behind their firewall by default, ensuring attackers cannot reach private networks while coding agents accelerate internal software production.

### The Token Black Market & Open-Weight Defense
* **Tokens as Currency:** AI tokens have become a new currency fungible with US dollars, creating black markets not just for data bounties, but for AI tokens themselves.
  * **Distillation Attacks:** State actors (such as North Korea) and other bad actors actively target companies holding large amounts of AI tokens to steal compute and capabilities.
* **The Case for Open-Weight Models:** Proprietary coding agents often miss vulnerabilities or have guardrails that prevent deep security probing. Rauch advocates for an adversarial setup where models "point guns at each other" and distrust what other agents produce.
  * **Vercel's Pipeline:** On every Git commit, Vercel inserts an auditing agent that combines proprietary and open-weight models to take a suspicious stance toward the code.
  * **Real-World Defense:** When Hugging Face was hacked, they used open-weight models to analyze the attack surface after agents escaped the sandbox.

### Nvidia, Open Ecosystems, and Geopolitics
* **Nvidia's Open Alignment:** Rauch praised Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang for building an open ecosystem—such as open-sourcing **Nemotron**—which aligns with Vercel's mission to help customers own their intelligence and agents.
  * **Open-Source Roots:** Just as Vercel and Facebook were born out of open-source runtimes and frameworks, maintaining open ecosystems is critical for low-cost, modifiable intelligence.
* **The "Fog of War" Around Jensen Huang:** Rauch noted that criticism of Huang stems from multiple motives: competitors building fully proprietary ecosystems who want Nvidia to fall, "peanut gallery" critics dogpiling on mistakes, and financial/geopolitical pressures on the world's most valuable company.
* **US vs. China AI Race:** Huang has argued for a world where China depends on US technology rather than building superior domestic alternatives.
  * **Data Advantage:** Rauch pointed out that China is already outperforming US video models, aided by first-party datasets from platforms like **TikTok** with billions of daily video creators.
  * **National Security:** Rauch argued it is existential for America to remain the "spear" of the AI revolution, especially in cyber warfare, to ensure adversaries do not hold the best weapons.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Cybersecurity as the Defining Topic:** "This is going to be the defining topic for all of the software industry... we've solved coding... but we really need to solve cybersecurity if we want the infrastructure to remain stable." — *Guillermo Rauch*
* **Imminent High-Profile Hacks:** "Everything Hackable Will Be Hacked... there's likely going to be some short-term pain" with a lot more high-profile hacks occurring in the next weeks and months. — *Guillermo Rauch*
* **CrowdStrike-Scale Outages:** A scenario similar to the CrowdStrike downtime—where critical systems go down and display blue screens—is likely to happen as interconnected infrastructure is targeted. — *Guillermo Rauch*
* **Long-Term Resilience:** On the other side of deploying defensive cybersecurity AI systems, the industry will end up in a "very, very strong position." — *Guillermo Rauch*
* **Future American Companies:** "The next great American companies are going to be born out of [open] ecosystems." — *Guillermo Rauch*

### Notable Quotes

Agents have superhuman hacking abilities." — *Guillermo Rauch*
