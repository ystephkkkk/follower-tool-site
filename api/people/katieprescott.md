### TL;DR
Bill Gates warns that artificial intelligence is "ultra-different" from any previous technology and criticizes a dangerous "vacuum of US leadership" in AI regulation, while urging society, academia, and governments to wake up to immediate cyber, bio, and societal risks.

### Bill Gates on AI Risks, Urgency, and the Need for Societal Engagement
* **The "Ultra-Different" Nature of AI:** Bill Gates emphasizes that AI is unlike any prior technology because it rapidly concentrates power and accelerates invention across both cognition and, eventually, physical robotics.
  * **Immediate vs. Long-Term Risks:** While a world of abundance via humanoid robots may arrive in more than 20 years, near-term risks—such as job displacement, cyber threats, biosecurity hazards, and psychosocial impacts—are here today.
  * **Hardware and Software Velocity:** Gates notes that models are becoming far more powerful even within short timeframes because underlying hardware and software layers continue to advance rapidly.
* **The Complacency Gap in Society:** Gates expresses surprise that universities, think-tanks, and sectors outside the tech industry are not engaging deeply with AI's downsides or uses.
  * **The Black-and-White Trap:** Without sophisticated thinking from social scientists, economists, and politicians on issues like kids and the job market, society risks defaulting to a simplistic "let's ban it all" mentality that jeopardizes beneficial advancements.
  * **Failure of Past Intuition:** People resist the message that this time is different because past technologies were less disruptive and humans intuitively assume this technology will peak or retain permanent flaws.

### The Role of Tech Leaders, Self-Regulation, and Global Governance
* **Industry Limitations:** Gates argues that tech companies cannot be expected to self-regulate or understand whole-of-society impacts because they are not social scientists, psychiatrists, or biosecurity experts.
  * **Philanthropic Intent vs. Expertise:** Companies like Google, Microsoft, Anthropic, and OpenAI show strong philanthropic intent (such as providing back-end capacity), but they lack deep local context in regions like Africa or cultural expertise regarding children.
  * **The Multi-Player Reality:** Early industry designs assuming a single company would lead and hold back releases no longer apply in a world featuring multiple competing firms across the United States, China, and elsewhere.
* **The Vacuum of US Leadership:** Gates highlights that the world is unaccustomed to a vacuum of U.S. leadership on cutting-edge global issues.
  * **Historical Precedent:** Since World War II, the U.S. has typically acted as a benign leader for global initiatives (such as the UN, nuclear weapons oversight, the ozone layer, and aviation safety).
  * **Current Administration Shortfall:** The depth of understanding and concern about AI's bad outcomes is currently too low within the U.S. government relative to the industry itself.

### Personal Scrutiny and Philanthropic Focus at the Gates Foundation
* **The Epstein Association:** Addressing congressional scrutiny over his past association with Jeffrey Epstein, Gates acknowledges it was a dead end and a mistake in terms of reputational risk, noting he was originally trying to raise billions for global health to save millions of lives.
  * **Lessons Learned:** Gates states he is now much more careful about associations for himself and the foundation, though it does not change the foundation's mission or partners.
* **Accelerating Positive Impacts:** Through the Gates Foundation's GoalKeepers report and a $1+ billion commitment, the organization is infusing AI into strategies for global health and agriculture.
  * **Localized AI:** The foundation is partnering with African governments and donors to ensure agricultural weather data and health information are formatted properly and supported by foundational models, bringing miraculous tools to countries with severe doctor shortages.

### Personal Milestones and Discoveries in AI
* **ChatGPT's Shocking Arrival:** Gates describes a pivotal moment a little over three years ago when Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever, and others visited his house to show him ChatGPT roughly six months before its public release.
* **Coding Thresholds Crossed:** Gates points to late last year—when AI coding capabilities (specifically Claude) surpassed his own ability to write code—as a profound "Oh my" moment that signaled immediate thresholds for cyber and bio risk.

## Sam Altman on Trump, Elon Musk and the race to AGI

- Channel: Times Business
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1URzcc9uAaw
- Published: 2026-08-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260807.html#video-1URzcc9uAaw
- Speakers: Sam Altman (CEO, OpenAI); Katie Prescott (Technology Business Editor, The Times); Danny Fortson (West Coast Correspondent, The Sunday Times)

---

### TL;DR
Two years after launching ChatGPT, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman predicts that 10-year AI advancements will give every person more leverage than today's most impactful individuals, while acknowledging that the economic transition for knowledge workers will likely be painful.

### The State of AI & The "AGI" Milestone
- **Evolution and Timeline:** ChatGPT launched 2 years and a quarter ago, driving exponential capability gains.
- **Deep Research Launch:** Introduced roughly a week prior to the interview; Altman estimates this single feature handles about **5% of all tasks in the economy today** (e.g., summarizing research, baby crib shopping, consulting reports, complex financial analysis) by saving days or weeks of human work.
- **Defining AGI:** Altman tried to ban himself from using the term "AGI" due to poor definitions, but gave up because it is too sticky; internally, OpenAI uses **five levels of AI progress** rather than a single AGI threshold.
- **Democratizing Genius:** Envisions AGI as perfect collaboration providing as much genius-level intelligence as needed across every area.

### Infrastructure, Investments, and Competition
- **DeepSeek Moment:** Admits it was surprising that the capability dropped on *that specific day*, praising their nice chain-of-thought product work and massive free-tier availability, though noting it was not a massive research update for OpenAI.
- **Cost Scaling Law:** Observes that the price for a given level of intelligence falls by about **10x per year** (a 100x drop over the last few years).
- **Stargate Project:** A massive **$500 million project** to build a very large training and inference system (noting future clusters may reach the trillion-dollar scale).
- **Big Tech Spending:** The top seven tech companies are investing roughly **$560 billion** into new AI data centers and infrastructure this and last year.
- **Nvidia's Dominance:** Describes Nvidia as an incredible company and a "real powerhouse," noting it is easier for commentators to predict their downfall than for competitors to actually take their business.

### Societal Transition & Knowledge Work Impact
- **Knowledge Worker Disruption:** The transition will be messy and "pretty painful" for knowledge workers, who will feel the impact well before physical work (though robotics work is ramping up behind the scenes).
- **Work and Meaning:** Rejects the idea of a miserable UBI-driven existence of video games and drugs, arguing humans are hardwired to be useful and create; societal labor market transitions can be absorbed over generations, but the compression into a 5-to-10-year window creates friction.
- **Democratic vs. Authoritarian AI:** Cautions that the biggest unresolved fork in the road is heading toward authoritarian vs. democratic AI; democratic AI empowers individuals to an unprecedented degree but comes with societal growing pains.

### Geopolitics, Regulation, and Government Relations
- **Global Standards:** Suggests looking to the IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency) for nuclear material—despite its imperfections—as a model for an international regulatory body with global safety standards, rather than using a rigid "arms race" framework with China.
- **Europe and Regulation:** Notes that strict European regulations (e.g., a 3-to-5-month testing requirement for models) risk leaving European deployment perpetually out of date compared to 3-month improvement cycles.
- **US Political Climate:** Welcomes a vibe shift under President Trump, praising his focus on infrastructure, manufacturing, permitting, and energy that enables domestic mega-projects like Stargate, contrasting it with the Biden administration's friction on infrastructure.
- **Military Collaborations:** Explains that OpenAI is exploring work with the US military (such as a deal with Anduril) because it is critical that the US government understands AI and its impacts.

### Elon Musk & Silicon Valley Relationships
- **Relationship with Musk:** Confirms he no longer speaks much with Elon Musk, does not miss him as a friend, and feels Musk "really changed" while still respecting his past accomplishments.
- **Social Platforms:** Uses X (Twitter) because it's habitual, but plans to try Blue Sky after hosts recommend it as a better product with less noise.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Ten-Year Leverage:** "In a decade, perhaps everyone on Earth will be capable of accomplishing more than the most impactful person can today." — Sam Altman
- **Inference Price Decline:** The price for a given level of intelligence falls by about **10x per year**. — Sam Altman

### Notable Quotes
> *"I think it will be... democratizing genius, right? It's like we can all just tap into genius-level intelligence to do whatever we want. In every area, as much as you need, all working together super well, no one's not getting along."*
> — Sam Altman

> *"It's easy for politicians to sit around and talk about how we're going to re-skill people... That feels a little facile... society can absorb any amount of technologically driven labor market change [over two generations], but we've never had to do this in like 10 years or 5 years."*
> — Sam Altman
