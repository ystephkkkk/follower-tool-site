Jeff Bezos and Prometheus co-founder Vik Bajaj unveiled details of their $14.5 billion "AI for atoms" startup—which is already compressing months-long engineering tasks into hours for early alpha partners like Blue Origin—while Bezos defended his outreach to President Trump, urged Nvidia’s board to hire internally, and revealed how Amazon Studios cast Robert Pattinson as the next James Bond.

### Prometheus: "AI for Atoms" & The Manufacturing Economy
* **Thesis & Technology:** Unlike LLMs that manipulate symbols by reading the internet, engineering requires specialized models trained on physics and real-world constraints. Prometheus builds neural surrogates for numerical simulation using a single multiphysics model.
  * **Scale & Funding:** Bezos invested at the seed stage in late 2024; the company now employs 250 people and has raised approximately $14.5 billion, with much of it committed to compute.
  * **Early Benchmarks:** Prometheus tools are designing analog chips and radios in 12 hours—work that typically takes a team of 10 to 20 people 6 to 9 months. A teammate from JPL also prompted the tools to redesign the Mars helicopter; the model explored quadcopters, counterrotating helicopters, and fixed-wing aircraft before calculating the blade chord for the Martian atmosphere.
* **Go-To-Market & Business Model:**
  * **Private Alpha:** Currently in private alpha with early customers, including Blue Origin, which is using the tools on two active projects.
  * **Open Ecosystem over Walled Garden:** Vik Bajaj noted that the manufacturing market is worth tens of trillions of dollars, making a "walled garden" impossible. Prometheus will both make its tools broadly available and invest directly in some of the companies it helps, benefiting as a principal rather than exclusively as a toolmaker.
* **Data Trust & Supply Chain Impact:**
  * **Protecting IP:** Addressing fears of frontier labs acting like "the fox in the hen house," Bajaj emphasized that Prometheus respects proprietary engineering traditions, trains on publicly observable product data, and generates much of its own synthetic data.
  * **Accelerating Legacy Processes:** Bezos cited precision aerospace casting—which still relies on trial-and-error X-rays and mold insulation—as an area where AI can create repeatable processes, potentially cutting 9-to-12-month part wait times down to a month.

### Politics, Democrats, & Root-Cause Problem Solving
* **Engaging with Presidents:** Facing questions from Liz Hoffman about whether his closeness to President Trump is a liability now that Democrats hold the gavel, Bezos stated he has worked with every president since Clinton (noting interactions with Biden were the most limited). He described Trump as uniquely communicative and accessible, but insisted his outreach has always been for the country's benefit rather than to advantage Amazon.
* **The "Pick a Villain" Trap:** Bezos argued that while targeting a villain—historically lawyers, Wall Street, landlords, and now Big Tech—is effective politics, it fails to solve systemic problems.
  * **Housing Affordability:** Using housing as a core example, Bezos noted that high prices stem from subsidizing demand while constraining supply. He pointed to Austin, which lowered housing costs over the last 10 years despite significant population growth by increasing supply (such as eliminating requirements for two parking spots per apartment unit).
* **Tech & Democratic Priorities:** Bajaj added that rapid technological advancement gives Democrats a rare opportunity to achieve long-standing policy goals in education, infrastructure, and healthcare at scale.

### Nvidia, AI Investing, & Life Sciences
* **Advice for Nvidia's Board:** Declining to weigh in on whether Jensen Huang should return due to a lack of inside boardroom details, Bezos nonetheless praised Huang's foresight. His advice to Nvidia's board: hire from within, as outside CEOs in complex companies have a "vanishingly small chance of succeeding." He dismissed comparisons to Amazon, noting his own departure was a multi-year, planned transition.
* **Investor Discernment:** Bezos observed that early technology cycles fund every experiment ("the good, the bad, and the ugly"), but AI investors are now becoming more discerning and diverting capital to stronger ideas—a healthy sign for the market.
* **Healthcare & Biotech Timelines:** Bajaj, who previously ran Google's life sciences business, stressed the need to "underpromise and overdeliver" in biology.
  * Early cancer detection has been a 10-year journey, though machine-learning blood tests are now on the market.
  * Drug discovery and development (a ~15-year effort) is on the verge of accelerating cures, driven by specialized AI firms like Isomorphic, Zera, and Initro.

### Casting the Next James Bond
* **The Selection Process:** Bezos confirmed he was heavily involved in Amazon Studios' search for the next 007. After narrowing a large pool to five finalists, the team ran traditional screen tests featuring full costumes, real sets, other actors, and three different scenes per finalist.
* **Why Robert Pattinson:** Despite an initial slight bias against Pattinson due to his existing fame, Bezos said his screen tests made the choice "immediately undebated" alongside director Denis, leading to a final decision on Friday.
