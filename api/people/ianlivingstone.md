## An AI Agent Got Its User Banned From Resy — Keycard, JPMorgan Chase & AAuth

- Channel: AI Engineer
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IavxrdVnwXM
- Published: 2026-10-17
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261017.html#video-IavxrdVnwXM
- Speakers: Allie Howe (Moderator, Keycard); Ian Livingstone (CEO & Co-founder, Keycard); Michael Davis (Global Chief Security Architect, JPMorgan Chase)

---

### TL;DR
As companies push for higher AI agent autonomy, legacy security models like OAuth prove too coarse-grained for runtime authorization, necessitating a shift toward intent-based auth, mission-driven guardrails, and decoupled brain-and-hands architectures.

### The Failure of Legacy Auth & Coarse-Grained Access
* **Enterprise Security-First Constraints:** 
  * Regulated industries like financial services prioritize security and business continuity over rapid AI innovation (**Ian Livingstone**).
  * Existing enterprise security systems were designed for deterministic human decision-making, not reasoning LLMs (**Ian Livingstone**).
* **Inadequacy of OAuth Scopes:** 
  * OAuth was originally built for website-to-website communication with pre-registered participants and browser redirects, making it fundamentally misaligned with dynamic agent workflows (**Dick Hardt**).
  * Existing enterprise systems often rely on overly broad scopes (e.g., Salesforce's single API scope) or broken permissioning where resources marked as read-only are actually read-write (**Dick Hardt**, **Ian Livingstone**).
* **Real-World Incident Risks:** 
  * Unchecked agent autonomy leads to incidents like an Instinct agent making 200 API calls an hour on Resy and getting its user banned (**Dick Hardt**).
  * Without fine-grained intent, agents can trigger "Pac-Man style incidents" such as accidentally deleting production databases instead of staging ones (**Ian Livingstone**).

### Intent-Based Authorization & The "Mission" Concept
* **Defining Intent and Motive:** 
  * Intent runtime authorization provides necessary context at the moment of execution, similar to how historical paper checks included a memo field and price limits (**Michael Davis**).
  * Understanding the user or business motive allows systems to determine whether different chat sessions (e.g., shopping vs. debugging production) warrant different permission profiles (**Ian Livingstone**).
* **The Agent "Mission":** 
  * **Dick Hardt** and AAuth define a "mission" as the big-picture objective given by a person (organization or human) to an agent. 
  * Agents negotiate parameters with users upfront, and the mission identifier tracks through every transaction to enable auditing and policy enforcement.
* **Separation of Concerns:** 
  * **Dick Hardt** advocates for a supervising agent that evaluates policy and decides what a worker agent is allowed to do, keeping guardrails independent of the worker.

### Onboarding Agents Like Junior Developers
* **Graduated Capability Access:** 
  * Successful enterprises onboard AI agents much like human developers—starting with minimal privileges and adding capability levels as they mature (**Michael Davis**).
* **Deterministic Policies vs. Specific Tasks:** 
  * Organizations establish broad-based deterministic policies for what must never happen, layered with dynamic intent to calculate a safe bounding box for agent actions (**Ian Livingstone**).
* **Cross-Organizational Intersections:** 
  * Intent-based approaches allow different parts of an organization to decouple policies and bring them together dynamically based on complete context (e.g., whether an agent is acting on behalf of an administrative assistant vs. an unauthorized user) (**Michael Davis**).

### Agentic Commerce & The Need for Open Protocols
* **The "Know Your Agent" Challenge:** 
  * Financial services are extending "Know Your Customer" principles to "Know Your Agent," coupling the agent, user intent, operator intent, and processor intent (**Michael Davis**).
* **Unregistered Traffic on the Web:** 
  * Much like Vercel being blocked in Spain due to shared IP addresses, agents lack standardized ways to prove trustworthiness when arriving at a website's front door (**Ian Livingstone**).
* **Federated Standards vs. Closed Boxes:** 
  * While proprietary systems exist, the industry must decide whether to build federated protocols (akin to OIDC or TLS) or settle on systems that favor large players (**Dick Hardt**, **Ian Livingstone**).

### Production Architectures, Budgets, and Forensics
* **Brains vs. Hands Separation:** 
  * Decoupling the model (the brain) from the sandbox tools and credentials (the hands) prevents agents from holding broad standing access or raw API keys (**Ian Livingstone**).
* **Budgets Over Rate Limits:** 
  * Instead of traditional rate limits that hard-cut access, AAuth implements time-limited tokens and resource budgets, giving agents continuous feedback on resource scarcity (**Dick Hardt**).
* **Robotics-Inspired Context Sensors:** 
  * **Michael Davis** draws parallels to robotics, where decisions rely on non-deterministic probabilistic sensor data (environment state, system vulnerabilities, time of day) alongside user intent.
* **Tamper-Resistant Audit Logs:** 
  * Immutable, agent-isolated audit logs transform debugging into forensics, helping map liability to the human or organization that employed the agent (**Ian Livingstone**).

## Episode 50: Ian Livingstone - Co-Founder of London Regional Properties

- Channel: Generating Alpha Podcast

**TL;DR**
Ian Livingstone, co-founder of London Regional Properties, built a $10B AUM real estate empire by executing a "contracyclical" strategy—buying distressed assets when institutions flee—and prioritizing direct operational control over passive ownership.

### From Optical Retail to Real Estate Titan
* **The "Entrepreneurial Professional" Path:** Originally trained as an optometrist, Livingstone opened his first eyewear store in 1989. Within three years, he acquired the David Clulow chain, growing it to 170 stores before selling to Luxottica in 2011.
* **Capitalizing on Crises:** During the 1990-1991 financial downturn, Livingstone expanded his retail footprint by leveraging landlord desperation.
    * **Incentives:** He secured two-year rent-free periods and capital for fit-outs, meaning many stores cost nearly nothing to open.
    * **Efficiency:** He succeeded by being "nimble" and "quick to change products" in a market dominated by inefficient, slow-moving groups.
* **The 1987 Pivot:** Livingstone and his brother Richard founded London Regional Properties during a commercial property crash. 
    * **Contrarian Conviction:** While institutions were exiting, they bought assets at 50% discounts, convinced that panic selling had caused massive mispricing.
    * **Leverage:** Because they lacked significant capital, they relied on smaller banks willing to provide high loan-to-value (LTV) ratios on distressed assets.

### Investment Philosophy: The "Two Pockets" Strategy
* **Contracyclical Timing:** Livingstone’s core strategy is simple: "Buy when the institutions are selling and sell when they are buying."
* **Asset Segmentation:** He manages the portfolio through two mental "pockets":
    * **Trading Stock:** Assets bought to be turned around and resold for a profit within a few years.
    * **Family Office/Safe Havens:** Long-term trophy assets (like the Fairmont Monte Carlo or Cliveden House) held for future generations. These prioritize capital appreciation and stability over immediate high yield.
* **Finding the "Non-Institutional" Gap:** In the early years, the firm specialized in niches that pension funds and public companies ignored, such as sale-and-leaseback models for "mission-critical" real estate (cinemas, petrol stations, sports clubs). 
* **Operational Risk:** While the firm initially sought long-term leases to avoid risk, they evolved to embrace operational risk. They now prefer buying unbranded or "untried" hotels because they understand how to manage the underlying business performance.

### Direct Operations and the "Cody" Platform
* **Eliminating Agency Conflict:** Unlike many investors who use third-party managers (e.g., Marriott or Hilton), London Regional operates most of its properties directly through its "Cody" management platform.
* **Alignment of Interests:** Livingstone argues that big hotel brands prioritize their own shareholders and fee generation over the property owner’s bottom line.
    * **Control:** Direct management allows for faster decision-making regarding staffing, food and beverage brands, and CapEx.
    * **CapEx Tensions:** He notes that franchised brands often demand "Property Improvement Plans" (PIPs) that are inconsistent with a specific hotel’s business model.
* **Institutional Partnership:** While the firm has remained 100% family-funded for 40 years, they are now open to partnering with institutional capital as the "operating partner," provided they can maintain a significant equity stake (20–50%).

### Current Market Outlook and Global Scale
* **Market Stance:** Livingstone is currently "neutral to slightly negative" on the global property market. He is "sitting on his hands" regarding major acquisitions, citing significant headwinds and a lack of conviction in any specific asset class at current pricing.
* **Panama Pacifico:** One of the firm’s most ambitious projects is a $2B–$3B development in Central America. 
    * **Strategic Bet:** He views Panama as a "safe haven" in the region due to the US dollar, transparent rule of law, and its status as a beneficiary of neighboring instability.
* **Building a Team:** To maintain an opportunistic edge, Livingstone emphasizes hiring "curious" people and remaining hands-on. He and his brother still speak to senior management daily and personally vet ideas.
