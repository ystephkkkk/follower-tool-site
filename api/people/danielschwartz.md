## The Secretive PE Firm Behind Burger King, Tim Hortons, Skechers and Hunter Douglas (3G Capital)

- Channel: Invest Like The Best
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YyBQMiiSnY8
- Published: 2026-02-10
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260210.html#video-YyBQMiiSnY8
- Speakers: Alex Behring (Co-Managing Partner, 3G Capital); Daniel Schwartz (Co-Managing Partner, 3G Capital); Patrick O'Shaughnessy (Host, Invest Like the Best)

---

**TL;DR** 3G Capital operates a high-conviction "one investment per fund" model, leveraging an operator-first mindset to transform iconic but under-optimized brands through extreme talent density, ownership incentives, and long-term strategic patience.

### The "One Investment Per Fund" Model
*   **Deep Concentration:** 3G avoids broad diversification, believing there are only a handful of truly great businesses and even fewer great CEOs. By making only one investment per fund, they can focus 100% of their "A++ players" on a single opportunity.
*   **Downside Protection:** The model necessitates a rigorous focus on capital preservation. Decisions are driven by a "worst-case" analysis where the downside must still result in capital preservation or a small return.
*   **Skin in the Game:** The firm’s partners are the largest investors in every deal, aligning their personal reputations and capital directly with their Limited Partners (LPs).

### Investment Selection: Moats and Disruption
*   **Simplicity and Moats:** 3G seeks "easy-to-understand" businesses with strong brand franchises (e.g., burgers, shoes, window shades). They prefer businesses that are "high-IQ-proof" and resistant to technological disruption.
*   **Relationship Ownership:** A key criteria is whether a company owns the relationship with the end customer. 
    *   **Example:** In CPG, retailers like Amazon or Walmart can disintermediate brands via private labels (e.g., Kirkland). Conversely, if a customer wants a Whopper, they must go to Burger King, giving the brand pricing power and protection.
*   **Brand vs. Business Mismatch:** 3G looks for "brands bigger than the business." 
    *   **Burger King Case Study:** At the time of acquisition, Burger King had a global brand presence comparable to McDonald’s, but its market cap was a fraction of its peers' ($1B equity vs. $90B for McDonald's). The opportunity lay in fixing the business (franchisee relations, overhead, global expansion) to match the brand's stature.

### The Operator’s Playbook: Ownership and Urgency
*   **Management by Walking Around:** Alex Behring emphasizes that value creation happens on the "shop floor," not in a PowerPoint. As CEO of a Brazilian railroad, he spent weeks in overalls with engineers to identify simple efficiencies, like fixing locomotive cabs and dormitories to boost morale and fuel performance.
*   **Centralized "What," Decentralized "How":** Leadership sets the ambitious "what" (goals/strategy) but gives operators freedom on the "how" (execution). This autonomy is essential for attracting top-tier talent who want to solve problems.
*   **Zero-Based Budgeting (ZBB):** While famous for ZBB, 3G views it as a tool for visibility and "intellectual hygiene" rather than just cost-cutting. 
    *   **Growth Focus:** Daniel Schwartz notes that in most 3G offsites, 90% of the content is dedicated to growth and operations, while only a small fraction covers costs. The goal is to free up capital to reinvest in the business’s trajectory.
*   **Sense of Urgency:** 3G treats mature, cash-flow-heavy businesses with the urgency of a tech startup, pushing for results to happen "this month" or "now" rather than over several quarters.

### Talent Strategy: Extreme Meritocracy
*   **Early Bets on Talent:** 3G is known for "betting on children," placing young, high-potential leaders in massive roles.
    *   **Alex Behring:** Became CEO of Latin America's largest railroad at age 30.
    *   **Daniel Schwartz:** Became CFO of Burger King at 30 and CEO at 32.
*   **Talent Over Tenure:** The firm ignores traditional career paths, instead recruiting "superstars" from firms like Goldman Sachs or McKinsey and offering them founder-like economics and immediate responsibility.
*   **Radical Incentives:** Compensation is strictly meritocratic and non-political. 3G avoids "fair" (equal) distribution of stock, instead giving outsized awards to those who contribute the most to value creation.

### Strategic Patience and The Long Game
*   **Persistence in Acquisition:** The acquisition of Tim Hortons involved months of "radio silence" and multiple rejections. 3G’s partners succeeded by being "not shy and persistent," eventually convincing the board that the brand would retain its Canadian identity while growing globally.
*   **Multi-Decade Relationships:** The Hunter Douglas and Skechers deals were the result of 15+ years of relationship building. Alex Behring met Ralph Sonnenberg (Hunter Douglas) in the mid-2000s, waiting for a succession "window" to present a proposal in 2021.
*   **The "Forever" Home:** 3G positions itself as a long-term partner for founder-led or family-controlled businesses (like Skechers and Hunter Douglas) that want to maintain their legacy while professionalizing operations.
