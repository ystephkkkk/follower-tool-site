## Andrew Feldman on Building Cerebras and the Future of Chips | Ep. 57

#### TL;DR
Cerebras Co-Founder and CEO Andrew Feldman and Benchmark Partner Eric Vishria discuss the grueling 10-year journey of building the world's largest AI chip company, the Wafer Scale Engine, overcoming nearly fatal technical hurdles, and rethinking the semiconductor supply chain to tackle incumbent giants like Nvidia.

### The Genesis of Cerebras & The 100x Strategy
* **The 2016 Insight:** As a computer architect, Feldman recognized that AI workloads would be exceptionally compute-intensive, requiring a radically different architecture rather than remodeled existing chips.
* **The Goliath Strategy:** Feldman and Vishria argue that attacking an incumbent like Nvidia with a 10% or 2x better product is a losing game because incumbents possess massive margin flexibility to cut prices and bundle products.
* **The 100x Rule:**
  * **Scale timeline:** A new compute company takes at least 5 years to reach scale.
  * **Incumbent compounding:** If an incumbent doubles every year ($2^5 = 32\text{x}$ over 5 years), a new entrant must target a **100x to 500x performance multiple** to successfully intersect the market ahead of them.
* **Control Your Own Stack:** To achieve radical innovation, Cerebras brought chip, board, system, software, and API development entirely in-house.
  * **No Off-the-Shelf Parts:** Building a chip the size of a dinner plate meant no commercial catalog items (like heat sinks or packaging tools) existed; they had to pioneer world-class packaging through years of failure.

### The Valley of Death: Surviving the 18-Month Nightmare
* **The Boardroom Reality:** For 18 months, Cerebras faced a continuous existential hurdle: burning **$8 million a month** while walking into board meetings every six weeks with the exact same update: *"Still can't make it."*
* **The Mantra:** The team operated on a strict engineering discipline: **"Only new mistakes."** Every failure underwent rigorous analysis so they never repeated the same error twice.
* **The Breakthrough:** In **July 2019**, after months of shattering wafers in seconds, then minutes, a test run maintained a flat temperature in a makeshift lab with a hole drilled in the wall for ventilation—solving a problem that had baffled computer architecture for 75 years.

### Demystifying the Semiconductor Supply Chain
* **The Modern Pyramid (TSMC & ASML):** A cutting-edge fab costs **$40 to $50 billion**, takes 4 to 5 years to build, and utilizes football-field-sized facilities powered by continuous concrete pouring.
* **The Lithography Monopoly:** ASML builds the $500M+ photolithography machines (50–60 feet long, 20 feet high) that etch transistors into silicon wafers—representing a true technological monopoly.
* **The Geopolitical Bottleneck:** Three decades of domestic policy pushed fabs and packaging vendors (like Amcor and ASE) overseas. Feldman advocates for waiving local ordinances to build robust US domestic fab capacity with partners like TSMC, Samsung, and GlobalFoundries.

### Data Center Constraints & Energy Realities
* **The Real Estate Mismatch:** AI moves at the speed of software while data centers move at the speed of real estate, creating a permanent lag.
* **Power and Grid Hurdles:** 
  * The US grid relies on 1940s-1950s technology.
  * Data center expansion is driving intense innovation in backup power, including jet-engine power adaptations (from Boom), fuel cells (Bloom Energy), and renewed interest in nuclear power.
* **The Water Fallacy:** Data centers utilizing closed-loop water systems consume between **4x and 7x less water** than California almond growers.

### Scaling Throughput & Disaggregation
* **Speed Creates Markets:** Just as fast internet transformed Netflix from mailing DVDs into a movie studio, instant frontier intelligence unlocks entirely new applications.
* **Disaggregation Partnerships:** Cerebras leverages its architecture to partner with other chipmakers (such as AMD and AWS), achieving up to **5x additional throughput** while maintaining constant speeds by splitting inference workflows.

### Notable Quotes
> *"The more you work on companies and you see the challenges and you see how hard it is you build up scar tissue... you actually need some naivety... like you need to have this like 'can do' attitude and this naive bet around it to attempt it."* — **Eric Vishria**

> *"We're a professional David in the battle with Goliath and I I wake up every day with that mentality... We're only interested in solving problems that other people can't solve."* — **Andrew Feldman**

### The Reality of Going Public and the IPO Pendulum

*   **The Overhead of IPOs:** Andrew Feldman (CEO, Cerebras Systems) noted that the process of going public involves massive administrative friction, including "garbage meetings" of up to 130 attendees and endless document revisions. However, the event serves as a massive cultural milestone and point of pride for long-term employees and their families.
*   **Corporate Legitimization:** Will Marshall (CEO, Planet Labs) emphasized that being a public company provides critical validation, reassuring enterprise and government clients that the business is financially stable and structurally permanent.
    *   **Revenue Mix:** Security and defense contracts now account for roughly 60% of Planet Labs’ business, driven by geopolitical demands for early threat detection.
*   **Post-IPO Value Accrual:** Panelists agreed that the vast majority of historical tech investment returns occur *after* a company goes public, rather than during its private stages. 
    *   **Market Shifting:** While mega-caps like SpaceX and OpenAI have kept their massive valuation jumps in the private markets, Brad Gerstner (CEO, Altimeter Capital) noted that the pendulum is swinging back. More companies are aiming to go public at earlier valuations ($1 billion to $5 billion) to let public markets participate in their hyper-growth phase.
*   **Lock-up Innovations:** Cerebras and their banking partners pioneered a "dribble lock-up" structure, allowing investors to systematically liquidate shares over a six-month window based on specific performance hurdles. SpaceX is reportedly preparing a similar mechanism.

### The Horizon for Space-Based Data Centers

*   **Launch Cost Milestones:** Satellite launch costs have dropped nearly 10x over the last decade to roughly $1,000 per kilogram. Once launch costs reach $200–$300 per kilogram—a milestone expected in 2 to 3 years via SpaceX's Starship—it will be cheaper to run data centers in space than on Earth.
*   **The Space Power Advantage:** Terrestrial data centers face massive energy and battery storage constraints. In space, servers placed in sun-synchronous "dawn-dusk" orbits can view the sun 24/7, capturing five times more solar energy per panel than on Earth without requiring complex battery backups.
*   **Hardware Deployments:** 
    *   Planet Labs has already launched Nvidia GPUs into space and is currently partnering with Google to test Google TPUs in orbit.
    *   Marshall projects that within 10 years, a majority of the world's heavy compute will be deployed in space.
*   **Technical Caveats:** Feldman cautioned that space-based compute faces immense hurdles, particularly regarding the high-speed clustering and inter-chip networking protocols that remain incredibly difficult to optimize even on Earth.

### Redefining AI Silicon Architecture

*   **The AI Data Bottleneck:** The primary limitation in modern computer architecture is not processing speed, but the latency and energy required to move data from memory to the compute core.
*   **Wafer-Scale Innovation:** To solve this bottleneck, Cerebras rejected standard GPU design architecture. Instead of building standard postage-stamp-sized chips, they engineered a dinner-plate-sized "wafer-scale" chip that places massive amounts of memory directly adjacent to the processing cores.
*   **Real-World Speed Advantage:** This specialized design allows Cerebras systems to run OpenAI workloads 15 to 18 times faster than standard Nvidia GPUs. 
*   **The Real-Time Mandate:** Feldman highlighted that just as the market for "slow web search" or "dial-up internet" went to zero, the market for slow AI will also collapse. Real-time inference speeds are non-negotiable for future consumer and enterprise adoption.

### The Convergence of AI and Earth Data

*   **Beyond Text-Based LLMs:** Marshall observed that current Large Language Models are "blind" to physical reality because they are trained almost exclusively on internet text. They lack direct observation of physical events, agricultural trends, and ecological disasters.
*   **Large Earth Models (LEMs):** By combining space-based imaging (Planet Labs captures a complete visual scan of the Earth daily) with space-based AI compute, developers can construct "Large Earth Models" to automate decision-making for agriculture, energy grid management, infrastructure permitting, and global security.
