## The New AI Agents That Shop, Plan, and Gossip

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dSiTwPl0t1U
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261104.html#video-dSiTwPl0t1U
- Speakers: David Pawlan (Creator, Assistant Benchmark); Anish Acharya (General Partner, a16z); Josh Elman (Investor, a16z)

---

**TL;DR:** As AI assistants evolve from general tools into specialized, multiplayer agent networks, consumers are moving past basic productivity tasks toward complex preferences, social experiences, and trust-building. However, a major hurdle remains: 22% of power user messages center on the fear of trust, privacy, and embarrassing data leaks.

### 1. The State of the Agent Landscape & Meetups
* **The Agent Community Boom:** David Pawlan hosted an agent meetup in San Francisco with over 100 attendees, highlighting that founders and users are craving in-person community and sharing best practices openly rather than acting cagey.
* **Categories of Assistants:** David has tested over 147 different assistants, categorizing them into four primary types:
    * **General**
    * **Work-oriented**
    * **Multiplayer**
    * **Travel**
    * **Finance:** Josh Elman and David noted that finance remains an unfulfilled category waiting to fully take off.

### 2. Trust, Privacy, and "Fences"
* **The Dominant Fear:** Out of 3,600 messages across AI power-user group chats, **22%** of discussions focus on trust as the primary fear in the assistant space.
* **The "Picnic" Analogy & Shane Mack's Leak:** Shane Mack experienced a major privacy failure when his personal agent accidentally crossed into his work environment and blasted his personal finances onto his company Slack. 
    * **The Fence Solution:** Josh Elman emphasized the need for proper "fences" (permissions, boundaries, and monitoring) so personal agents don't bleed into professional channels.
* **Stated vs. Revealed Preferences:** Anish Acharya pointed out that consumers will gladly share all their data for trivial upside (e.g., Hark offering $30 of free pizza in exchange for total access to Google Workspace emails).
* **The Embarrassment Factor:** Josh Elman argued that the true high-stakes fear is social embarrassment (e.g., agents revealing dietary restrictions or miscommunicating on dates), making discretion a mandatory trait for agents.

### 3. Testing Judgment, Cost, and Constraints
* **Flower Delivery Benchmark:** David tested agents on ordering the cheapest flowers for same-day delivery:
    * **Instinct:** Performed exceptionally well at balancing price and time constraints.
    * **Kicker:** Secured an edge by utilizing a local Chrome browser extension.
    * **Season:** Also performed well. 
    * *Other agents failed by prioritizing delivery speed while completely ignoring the price component.*
* **LA Weekend Travel Test:** David tested Grockbot, Dots, and Instinct to book a luxurious yet cost-efficient weekend in LA:
    * **Dots:** Prioritized cost (e.g., choosing a redeye flight).
    * **Grockbot:** Prioritized time and luxury, and was the only agent in a multi-agent chat room that methodically absorbed and implemented value from the other agents' proposals.
* **Onboarding & Memory Portability:** 
    * Only **3%** of 354 surveyed power users use ChatGPT as their primary agent, though it retains massive context.
    * David tested the top 15 benchmarked agents on memory portability: **all but one** allowed users to export their memory file (markdown) to switch assistants easily.

### 4. Commerce, Shopping, and the Rationality Debate
* **Shopping Behavior:** In David's survey, Amazon and retail shopping represented **35%** of what consumers are purchasing through AI.
* **Direct-to-Consumer & Longtail Winners:** Josh Elman and Anish Acharya discussed how agents have infinite patience to search the longtail of the internet, bypassing aggregators and empowering platforms like Shopify to drive direct purchases.
* **Patrick Collison's Rationality Vision vs. Alien Preferences:** 
    * Patrick Collison envisions consumers becoming more rational via agents.
    * Anish Acharya countered that agents will develop their own "alien" revealed preferences—such as an irrational affinity for clean APIs with great documentation.
    * Josh Elman added that "paternalism will kill any product," warning that agents shouldn't force artificial rationality or strict diets on humans who ultimately want to enjoy life.

### 5. Agent-to-Agent Social Networks and Serendipity
* **Agent Gossiping:** Josh Elman highlighted playful agents that chat in the background and share minor updates (like a friend's power being out), creating a healthy "social indirection" and acting as a social lubricant.
* **Multiplayer Apps:** 
    * **Wabby:** Launched recently, allowing users to interact with multiple threads of life and media watchlists.
    * **Tommo:** Features "interest groups" (e.g., grouping users studying for the LSAT) to connect humans based entirely on shared goals.
* **Creating Serendipity:** The panel discussed how agent networks could ultimately form a new kind of social network—moving beyond Facebook's model of connecting only with people you already know, and instead pairing strangers based on real-world desires and complementary goals.

### 6. Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Enterprise Shift (David Pawlan):** The industry will shift more attention toward enterprise use cases over the next few weeks, moving beyond purely consumer-focused assistant hype.
* **Secret Superpowers (Anish Acharya):** Agents will eventually evolve from doing tedious productivity tasks to becoming "dark agents" or "secret superpowers" that help users achieve their deepest personal aspirations and desired identities.


## The Current State of Consumer AI

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aCvrzhwUxg0
- Published: 2026-10-05
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261005.html#video-aCvrzhwUxg0
- Speakers: Elena Burger (Editorial Partner, a16z); Olivia Moore (Investing Partner, a16z); Josh Elman (Investing Partner, a16z)

---

### TL;DR
Consumer AI is heavily dominated by a high-spending power-user economy, where only about 4.5% of US consumers pay for subscriptions, while massive white space remains in social, shopping, and entertainment.

### 7th Edition of a16z’s Top 100 Consumer AI Apps
* **Traffic Plateaus vs. New Spenders:**
  * **Fewer Newcomers:** Only 11 new products appeared across web and mobile lists combined—the lowest in any prior edition.
  * **Spend Data Disconnect:** Of the 50 products that ranked for consumer card spend (via Yipit data), 29 did not appear on either traffic list.
* **The Power-User Spend Economy:**
  * **Low Paid Penetration:** Roughly 4.5% of US consumers pay for an AI product subscription (about double from a year ago).
  * **Concentrated Dollars:** The top 10% of paying users drive over half of total revenue, and the top 1% spend $93/month personally. The median spender puts $25/month toward AI.
  * **Top Categories for Spend:** Developer tools, productivity tools, and creative tools (e.g., n8n, Granola, Higsfield, Manis) are heavily over-represented among top spenders.

### Personal Agents and Prosumer Assistants
* **The Shift from Search to Execution:** AI has evolved from a tool for faster search/productivity into agents that actually get things done.
* **Early Pioneers and New Entrants:**
  * **OpenClaw:** Pioneered local, open-source agent setups (with users buying Mac Minis to run local data), but traffic has since declined following team acquisition/pivots.
  * **Instinct:** Announced 100,000 users growing 10% day-over-day; 40% of users connected a credit card in the first 3 weeks, spending over $1,000 on average in month one.
  * **Muse:** Reached ~500,000 downloads and 250,000 active users in its first 12 days (available only in US and Canada). Compared to Threads (which hit ~16 million downloads in its first 22 days in the same markets), Muse sits around 5 million over a similar period due to higher server costs and a more personal nature.
* **Adoption Friction & Privacy:** True mainstream adoption is still nascent. Intimate personal data sharing creates tension regarding privacy, security, and trust. Furthermore, power users still heavily use assistants for coding and technical automation (driving high server costs in the hundreds/thousands per month), whereas mainstream consumers face a "blank box" problem without clear use cases.

### The Evolution of Business Models: Beyond Subscriptions
* **The Subscription Inversion:** Around 85% of the web list monetizes via subscriptions and 62% via credits/tokens, with only 13% using ads or transaction fees. This is an unnatural inversion compared to the historical consumer internet.
* **OpenAI’s Advertising Milestone:** 
  * OpenAI reached an annualized run rate of **$1 billion** in advertising (as of August data, with 1.2 billion weekly active users).
  * This scale was achieved much faster than traditional ads products due to massive density and rich user context enabling high-intent targeting.
* **Verticalized Ad Success:** Products like **Open Evidence** have captured 50–60% density of all US physicians, proving that targeted verticalized audiences can monetize effectively via advertising.

### Lab Trajectories: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini
* **Usage & Spend Hierarchy:**
  * **ChatGPT:** Dominates globally, sitting roughly 6x ahead of Claude and 2x ahead of Gemini on web traffic, and holding ~3x more paid consumer subscribers in the US than Gemini and Claude combined.
  * **Claude:** Surpassed Gemini in number of US paid subscribers, driven by successful product launches (like Claude artifact/design) and strong press momentum. Anthropic maintains a strict "no ads" policy and counts ~7.5% of its subscribers on the $100+/month max plan (compared to ~1% for ChatGPT and Gemini).
  * **Gemini:** Benefits from the natural distribution of Google's massive install base, though trailing Claude in paid consumer subscriptions.

### Creative Tools & Vertical Infrastructure
* **Audio and Music Specialists:** Labs have largely avoided audio due to IP headaches, allowing pure-play startups like **Suno** and **11 Labs** to run away with the top traffic and spend ranks.
* **Image & Video Dynamics:** 
  * OpenAI and Google have captured casual consumer traffic with advanced image/video generation.
  * However, power users still favor **Midjourney** for its distinct aesthetic taste.
  * Video generation remains a complex frontier where Chinese companies training on unconstrained data have gained an early edge.
* **The Software Experience Layer:** Startups like **Granola**, **Whisper Flow**, and **Superhum** succeed by building frictionless interfaces that improve automatically as underlying model capabilities get better, avoiding the innovator's dilemma faced by tech giants.

### Consumer White Space (The Unbuilt Frontier)
* **Time-Saving vs. Time-Spending:** Most consumer AI tools focus on *saving* time (productivity), whereas the largest internet companies thrive on helping people *spend* time (entertainment, social, connection).
* **Open Categories:** Major web 2 categories remain largely untouched by native AI startups, including **dating, recruiting, social AI, gaming, home buying, retail, and marketplaces**.

## How to Spot Exceptional Talent Before Everyone Else

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ywZlfznTa4
- Published: 2026-09-25
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260925.html#video--ywZlfznTa4
- Speakers: Erik Torenberg (Host, a16z); Josh Elman (Co-founder, Cosign); Olivia Moore (Co-founder, Cosign)

---

### TL;DR
**Cosign Introduction:** a16z introduced Cosign, a new professional reputation product designed to make early-stage tech talent, mentorship bonds, and high-conviction endorsements legible and durable across the startup community.

### The Problem with Existing Professional Networks
- **Friction in Talent Discovery:** **Olivia Moore** noted that information asymmetry makes discovering early-stage talent exceptionally difficult, citing how sorting through 2,000 applicant form submissions for portfolio hiring creates a massive filtering bottleneck.
- **Flawed Connection Binaries:** **Josh Elman** explained that legacy platforms like LinkedIn treat every connection equally (whether a brief party interaction or deep collaboration), diluting real professional signal.
- **Ephemeral Social Signals:** **Erik Torenberg** and **David Booth** observed that powerful endorsements on platforms like X (formerly Twitter) disappear into feeds rather than building lasting, searchable professional profiles.

### Core Features and Structure of Cosign
- **Granular Endorsements:** **Erik Torenberg** outlined that profiles feature three ordered categories: 
  - **Shaped My Career:** Highlighting the mentor economy and foundational influences.
  - **In the Trenches:** Identifying colleagues someone would "go to war with" or work with anywhere.
  - **Person to Watch:** Spotting emerging talent early (referencing a past experiment where a 22-year-old Tesla engineer named Russell Kaplan—now Cognition President—was identified early).
- **Private & Public Signals:** **David Booth** described how users can maintain private Pinterest-style lists of future collaborators alongside public endorsement lists, creating two-sided matching for job changes, acquisitions, and hires.
- **AI-Powered Profile Bootstrap:** **Erik Torenberg** noted that AI enables scraping public web sources and social firehoses (like fundraising and job transitions) to populate profiles automatically, bypassing the traditional cold-start problem.

### The Economics of Reputation and Long-Term Alpha
- **The Giver's Benefit:** **David Booth** and **Olivia Moore** emphasized that putting one's reputation behind emerging talent creates durable loyalty, ensuring early believers are remembered and prioritized when founders raise future rounds (comparing it to Brian Chesky shouting out YC as his first investor).
- **Beyond Founders:** **Josh Elman** and **Erik Torenberg** stressed that Cosign honors the broader village required to build companies—engineers, designers, marketers, and product managers—similar to the historic *Rise Awards* run a decade ago.
- **Inspiration and Precedents:** **Erik Torenberg** noted that Cosign draws inspiration from early AngelList (when it owned the startup/investor graph) and Wikipedia's aspirational, community-edited profile structures.
