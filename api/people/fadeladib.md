## Betazone: Sensing the Invisible

- Channel: World Economic Forum

**TL;DR:** Fadel Adib, professor at MIT and entrepreneur, explores how wireless and underwater signals—ranging from Wi-Fi to acoustic waves—can be harnessed to make the invisible world visible, unlocking transformative applications across healthcare, climate monitoring, retail, and agriculture without the need for cameras or batteries.

### Wireless Sensing Through Walls and Body Monitoring
* **Wi-Fi Through-Wall Tracking:** 
  * **Location Detection:** Wireless devices placed on the other side of a wall can track human positions and body movements (such as hand gestures to turn off appliances) in real-time without requiring individuals to wear any sensors.
  * **Respiration and Vital Sign Monitoring:** Systems can measure breathing (chest expansion and contraction during inhalation and exhalation) and detect heart rates by capturing micro-vibrations caused by blood flow forcing movement through the body.
* **Child and Healthcare Applications:** 
  * **Baby Monitoring:** Enhanced baby monitors can track an infant's breathing rate and pulse (e.g., detecting 123–126 beats per minute) entirely through wireless signal reflections.
  * **Disease Progression:** Integrated into 5G, Wi-Fi, and emerging 6G networks, the technology is used in home healthcare to monitor disease progression and provide early detection for neurodegenerative conditions like Alzheimer's and Parkinson's disease.
  * **Autonomous Navigation:** Utilized in self-driving cars to "see" around corners using wireless reflections.

### Sub-Aquatic Internet of Things (IoT)
* **The Ocean Climate Gap:** 
  * The ocean manages the global climate system, absorbing over **90%** of heat content and more than **93%** of excess carbon dioxide emitted into the atmosphere over the past few decades.
  * More than **95%** of the ocean remains unmeasured and unseen, creating a massive blind spot in climate models.
* **Battery-Free Underwater Cameras:** 
  * **High-Cost Maintenance:** Replacing traditional sensor batteries in the ocean requires research vessels costing **$50,000 to $70,000 per day**.
  * **Energy Harvesting:** MIT researchers built the world's first battery-free underwater wireless camera, which harvests energy from underwater sound waves to power light flashes and capture color images.
  * **Performance Scaling:** Newer prototypes operate **100x faster**, enabling prolonged monitoring of marine robotics, plastic pollution, sea stars, and plant growth over hours, days, months, or even years.
* **Real-World Deployments and Future Frontiers:** 
  * **Woods Hole Deployment:** Deployed in Woods Hole, Massachusetts earlier this year to measure ocean vitality (temperature, currents, and pressure), successfully detecting seasonal shifts and lunar tidal cycles within months.
  * **Space Exploration:** Discussions are underway with scientists to integrate these battery-free sensors into future space missions to search for life in subsurface oceans on Saturn's moon Enceladus and Jupiter's moon Europa.

### Commercial Inventory and Retail Solutions
* **Scale of Radio-Frequency Identification:** Over **100 billion** manufactured items are marked or will soon be marked with wireless tags annually.
* **Cartesian Foundation:** Fadel Adib co-founded Cartesian to deploy wireless models at scale across industries like retail.
* **Inventory Distortion:** Retail industry losses from stockouts and misplaced items—termed "inventory distortion"—cost the retail sector **$1.77 trillion**. Wireless tracking enables real-time location mapping of store inventory to automate restocking and generate analytics on top-selling products.

### Environmental and Sub-Surface Applications
* **Soil and Vegetation Sensing:** 
  * By placing battery-free tags beneath soil and deploying drones emitting wireless signals that penetrate the earth and reflect back, operators can analyze soil moisture and nutrient content.
  * Wireless signals can also bypass obstacles to sense beneath vegetation out of direct camera line-of-sight.
* **Demining and Defense Applications:** 
  * Underwater battery-free sensors can map and detect unexploded landmines and munitions (such as in the Strait of Hormuz).
  * Discussions with regulators explore incorporating these passive sensors into future Geneva Convention frameworks so deployed landmines can be automatically and immediately recovered post-conflict.

### Privacy, Security, and Scientific Frontiers
* **Addressing Privacy Risks:** 
  * **Technology Controls:** Integrating privacy safeguards directly into technology so devices cannot be used for sensing without informed consent.
  * **Public Awareness & Leadership:** Engaging with policymakers and keeping research in the public domain, while proactively steering initial applications toward healthcare and autonomous driving rather than intrusive surveillance.
* **Unified Spectral Intelligence:** 
  * Research over the past 15 years demonstrates a unifying thread across electromagnetic, cellular, millimeter wave, terahertz, visible light, X-ray, and mechanical/acoustic pressure signals.
  * The future points toward "full-spectrum intelligence"—systems capable of extracting meaning from all surrounding signals to unlock new capabilities.

### TL;DR
Global leaders and next-generation innovators at the World Economic Forum opening plenary in Dubai emphasized that while the world faces unprecedented geopolitical uncertainty, technological shifts, and climate disruption, young people retain immense optimism and agency to co-author a resilient future. The event launched key global initiatives—including a new Center for Global Future Opportunities and a $1 million AI challenge—to bridge the gap between foresight and practical action.

### Context, Geopolitics, and Global Challenges
* **Global Risks and Instability:** 
  * The World Risk Report underscores uncertainty as the defining condition of the current era.
  * In 2025, the world witnessed over 60 armed conflicts—the highest number since 1946.
  * Global military spending reached nearly $3 trillion, marking the largest annual increase since the end of the Cold War.
  * Global public debt approached 94% of global GDP in 2025, with the International Monetary Fund (IMF) expecting it to reach 100% by 2029, limiting government investment capacity.
* **The European Context:** 
  * Mathieu Flamini noted that Europe is operating outside its comfort zone without predictability.
  * The European chemical industry's global market share dropped from 25% five years ago to 13% today.
* **Resources and Climate Realities:** 
  * The world has experienced the 10 warmest years since modern records began, making climate and nature risks lived realities for 9 out of 10 young people.
  * Amid these challenges, renewable energy accounted for approximately 86% of global power capacity additions in 2025.

### Youth Sentiments and the 2026 Youth Pulse
* **Longitudinal Youth Pulse Insights:** Natalie Pierce presented preliminary insights from the World Economic Forum's longitudinal study (building on a 2016 baseline of 20,000 young people):
  * **Climate Concern:** Planetary resilience remains young people's top global concern, rising from 45% in 2016 to 52% today. 9 out of 10 young people state climate change affects their daily realities.
  * **Economic Anxiety:** When looking at personal futures, young people place economic uncertainty at the top of the list. Over the next decade, 1.2 billion young people in developing and emerging economies will reach working age, against an estimated 400 million new jobs—leaving an 800 million gap.
  * **Employment Disadvantage:** 1 in 5 young people globally are already outside of employment, education, and training.
  * **AI Adoption and Sentiment:** In 2016, 86% of youth believed AI would create jobs and 64% anticipated career impacts. By 2026, 91% have deeply embedded AI into their daily lives, though positive sentiment dropped slightly to 62%.
  * **Optimism vs. Geography:** 2 in 3 young people globally expect their economic prospects to surpass those of their parents. However, this optimism is geographically uneven: the overwhelming majority of youth in Sub-Saharan Africa expect better prospects, compared to just over half in Europe and Eurasia.

### Technology, AI, and Human Capability
* **Spatial and Physical AI:** Fidel Adib noted that AI is moving beyond software into the physical world through sensing, robotics, and automation (such as spatial AI developed in the US and adopted heavily in Europe).
* **The Data and Representation Gap:** Mariam Nurya Koné highlighted that women represent only 1 out of 5 data annotators globally, and current AI models are predominantly built outside of Africa, despite Africa housing the world's fastest-growing young population.
* **AI Inequities and Misinterpretation:** Suhani Jalota shared an example of an AI health chatbot in Mumbai misinterpreting a Hindi phrase about uterine evacuation as personal hygiene due to a lack of training data for marginalized populations.
* **Economic Inclusion via AI Training:** Mina Mahila Foundation's data training initiatives in India show that when women are paid to teach AI local dialects and health contexts, 25% to 50% of previously disengaged women enter the workforce, often using the role as a stepping stone to other businesses.

### Leadership Attributes and Institutional Transformation
* **Five Essential Qualities of Future Leaders (outlined by Mohammad Abdullah Al Gergawi):**
  1. *Anticipation:* Preparing for crises before they happen rather than merely reacting.
  2. *Adaptability:* Continually learning and unlearning yesterday's assumptions.
  3. *Wisdom:* Utilizing technology responsibly and ethically.
  4. *Partnership:* Building collaborative networks across sectors and geographies.
  5. *Execution:* Transforming vision into measurable impact on human lives.
* **Organizational Requirements for Scale:** Kiara Winona Sweeney identified three traits of future-ready organizations: willingness to make trade-offs and reallocate budgets, capacity to experiment without certainty through rapid piloting, and building deliberate pathways for scale.
