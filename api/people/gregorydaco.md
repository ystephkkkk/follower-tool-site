#### TL;DR
The global economy is exhibiting resilience alongside polarization, driven by geopolitical tensions, shifting tariff policies, and an AI-led tech revolution that is pressuring inflation and driving long-term government bond yields to historic highs.

### Economic Outlook & Sentiment Shift
* **Resilience vs. Polarisation:** The latest World Economic Forum Chief Economists Outlook survey indicates a shift toward stability, with over 50% of chief economists expecting global economic conditions to remain unchanged. 
  * *Context change:* In the prior survey 4 months ago—marked by a brand-new Middle East conflict and threats to key energy trade routes—approximately 90% of chief economists expected a weakening outlook.
* **Geopolitical and Trade Shocks:** The global economy continues to be impacted by negative supply shocks, including tensions in the Middle East and Ukraine, as well as renewed U.S. tariffs on trading partners.

### AI Revolution & Infrastructure Pressures
* **Capital-Intensive Phase:** The initial phase of the AI technological revolution requires intensive capital and infrastructure investment (capex, data centers, semiconductor facilities), straining resources and generating inflationary pressures.
* **Community Pushback:** About 79% of chief economists report likely pushback to data center expansion from local communities.
  * *Proactive management needed:* Governments and the private sector must collaborate to account for negative externalities such as energy tensions and labor market displacements while harnessing productivity gains.

### Inflation, Interest Rates, and Central Banks
* **Cost-of-Living Increases:** While overall inflation expectations for the year ahead have declined, the basics of the cost of living (food, electricity, and transportation) are expected to increase.
* **Monetary Policy Tightening:** Renewed supply chain disruptions, higher commodity prices, and rising oil prices are feeding into higher inflation, prompting central bank monetary policy tightening globally (including expected U.S. Federal Reserve and UK central bank rate actions).
* **Growth Constraints:** Higher interest rates and a higher cost of capital constrain private sector activity, creating a difficult duality alongside higher consumer prices.

### Bond Yield Dynamics & Government Debt
* **Understanding Bond Yields:** A government bond yield represents the cost of debt paid by governments to investors financing budget deficits; rising yields indicate reduced market confidence in government economic management.
* **The 5% Benchmark:** The yield on the U.S. 10-year Treasury hit the 5% mark—the highest level since before COVID and a key reference point not seen since the 2007–2009 great financial crisis.
* **Structural Drivers of Yields:** 
  * **Fiscal Deficits:** Post-COVID economies running larger budget deficits and accumulating higher debt loads.
  * **Inflation Volatility:** Makes it difficult for investors to price central bank moves.
  * **AI Capital Competition:** Private sector AI investments are drawing capital away from public sector safe-haven investments like U.S. Treasuries.
  * **Central Bank Balance Sheets:** Global central banks are cutting back on the size of their balance sheets, purchasing less government debt.
* **Real-World Impact:** U.S. auto loans, mortgages, and corporate bonds are priced off the 10-year and 30-year government bond yields, meaning rising yields rapidly translate into a higher cost of borrowing and potential pullbacks in private sector hiring and investment.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Global Economic Navigation:** "We have to monitor [the duality of higher inflation/costs and higher interest rates/cost of capital] very closely in the next few months as we navigate into 2027." — Gregory Daco
