## Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh News Conference

**TL;DR** 
In his first press conference as Federal Reserve Chair, Kevin Warsh announced the FOMC’s decision to hold interest rates at 3.5% to 3.75% while introducing five sweeping task forces to modernize the central bank’s communication, balance sheet, data analytics, inflation framework, and approach to productivity. Warsh signaled a major shift in monetary policy by removing forward guidance, emphasizing that the Fed will let markets react directly to incoming economic data.

***

### Interest Rate Decision & Economic Projections

*   **Policy Rate Held Steady:** The FOMC maintained the target range for the federal funds rate at 3.5% to 3.75% and reaffirmed its policy of maintaining ample reserves in the banking system.
*   **Median SEP Projections:** 
    *   **Real GDP Growth:** Projected at 2.2% for this year and 2.3% next year.
    *   **PCE Inflation:** Expected to run at 3.6% this year before declining to 2.3% next year.
    *   **Unemployment Rate:** Projected to remain stable at approximately 4.3%.
    *   **Target Interest Rate:** The median participant expects the appropriate federal funds rate to sit at 3.8% at the end of this year and 3.6% at the end of next year.
*   **Chair Abstention:** Consistent with his long-held skepticism of the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) framework, Chair Warsh did not submit his own economic projections.

### Overhauling the Fed: Five Key Task Forces

Warsh announced the appointment of five independent task forces comprising internal Fed experts and external academic, technology, and business minds. The groups are scheduled to begin work immediately, with most aiming to deliver final recommendations by the end of the year.

*   **1. Fed Communications:** Charged with reviewing press conferences, minutes, transcripts, and reforming the structure of the SEP and the "dot plot."
*   **2. Balance Sheet Policy:** Tasked with evaluating the risks and benefits of the current ample reserves regime, as well as testing alternative operational frameworks for monetary policy.
*   **3. Data and Methodology:** Tasked with integrating private-sector metrics and modern analytic techniques to reduce the Fed's reliance on lagging, survey-based historical data in favor of real-time, actionable information.
*   **4. Productivity and Jobs:** Aimed at assessing the speed, reach, and economic impact of transformative general-purpose technologies—specifically Artificial Intelligence (AI)—on the Fed’s employment and price stability mandates.
*   **5. Inflation Frameworks:** Designed to study the underlying structural drivers of inflation, though Warsh clarified that the Fed's long-held 2% inflation target remains non-negotiable and outside the scope of review.

### Restructuring Communication and Eliminating Forward Guidance

*   **Abandoning Forward Guidance:** The FOMC shortened and simplified its policy statement, entirely removing forward guidance. Warsh argued that forward guidance is poorly suited to the current economic landscape.
*   **Reducing Market Blinders:** By stopping the practice of guiding the market on future rate path decisions, Warsh wants financial markets to price asset risk based on real-world economic data rather than trying to guess how the Fed will react to data.
*   **"Pencil" Dot Plots:** Warsh observed that FOMC members submitted their rate projections in "pencil," stressing that these modal forecasts represent humility in a fast-moving economy rather than rigid policy commitments.

### The Real Economy: Restrictiveness, AI, and Fiscal Relations

*   **Uneven Policy Restrictiveness:** Warsh described the restrictiveness of current monetary policy as "uneven." While highly restrictive in sectors like housing, he noted it is difficult to characterize policy as restrictive when looking at broader, surging financial markets.
*   **AI as a Dual Force:** Warsh views AI as a generational technological shift representing "American ingenuity." 
    *   *Supply vs. Demand:* While the massive capital expenditures on AI infrastructure and data centers are easily measured on the GDP demand side, the supply-side productivity gains are harder to model and will take longer to materialize.
*   **Wide Lens, Narrow Remit:** Warsh emphasized that the Fed maintains absolute independence from fiscal authorities. However, he continues the tradition of weekly breakfast meetings with Treasury Secretary Bessent to keep a "wide lens" on fiscal policy and global events, such as conflicts in the Middle East.
*   **Building Renovations:** Addressing political scrutiny over the Fed's ongoing headquarters renovations, Warsh confirmed he has met with the Inspector General, who is scheduled to release a report on the project later this summer. Warsh pledged to ensure strict stewardship of taxpayer money once the report is issued.

### Notable Quotes

* **The Soft Landing Milestone:** Powell stated that the US economy achieved a soft landing by late 2024, defying predictions of a recession from nearly 100% of economists when the Fed aggressively raised rates in 2022.
    * **Key 2024 Indicators:** The economy grew at 2.5%, inflation fell to the "two plus a few tenths" range on a 12-month basis, and the labor market remained at essentially full employment.
* **Current Inflation Drivers:** While inflation has come down significantly from its 2022 peak, it sits around 3%, driven by unique supply-side factors:
    * **Tariffs:** Powell estimates that recent tariffs have added between 0.5% and 0.8% (or "half and a full percentage point") to inflation as a one-time price increase.
    * **Middle East Energy Shocks:** Rising geopolitical tensions threaten to impact gas prices. Because monetary policy operates with long and variable lags, Powell noted the Fed's tendency is to "look through" temporary energy shocks while closely monitoring inflation expectations.
* **Debunking Money Supply Theories:** Powell argued that pandemic-era inflation was not primarily driven by monetary expansion. Instead, it resulted from massive necessary stimulus colliding with a "vertical supply curve" (e.g., semiconductor shortages preventing auto manufacturing) when the global economy rapidly reopened.

### Financial Stability, Private Credit, and Systemic Resilience

* **Systemic Hardening Post-2008:** Thanks to Dodd-Frank and the Basel III accords, the US banking system has significantly higher capital and liquidity requirements, making the largest institutions highly resilient. 
* **The "Levee Building" Philosophy:** Rather than attempting to prevent every economic crisis, the Fed operates under a mandate of systemic resilience.
    * **Ongoing Monitoring:** The Fed established the Division of Financial Stability to maintain continuous oversight of systemic threats, moving away from the old reactive "crisis battle" model.
* **Private Credit and Emerging Risks:** 
    * **Private Credit Market:** Despite rising defaults among non-bank lenders, the Fed views the current situation as a market correction rather than a systemic risk. The Fed continues to closely monitor connections to the traditional banking system.
    * **Cybersecurity:** Powell identified a successful cyberattack on a major financial utility or institution as one of the most significant, untested threats to financial stability.

### Federal Reserve Governance, Independence, and Decision-Making

* **Valuing FOMC Dissent:** Powell welcomes thoughtful, non-unanimous votes among the 12 voting members of the FOMC. He noted that expecting unanimity during historically challenging periods would be misleading and that hearing all sides ultimately improves policy decisions.
* **The Role of Economic Models:** While the Fed utilizes sophisticated modeling, including its FRB/US model, Powell emphasized that models are merely illustrative. 
    * **Decision-Making Input:** Policy is ultimately driven by real-world feedback from the 12 Reserve Banks (summarized in the Beige Book), extensive public and private sector conversations, and policymaker intuition.
* **Monetary vs. Regulatory Independence:** 
    * **Monetary Policy:** There is broad bipartisan consensus that monetary policy must remain fully independent of political influence.
    * **Financial Regulation:** This area is more collaborative and statutorily led by the Vice Chair for Supervision, who sets the regulatory agenda and brings proposals to the seven-member Board of Governors. Powell acts as "another voter" to preserve the Chair's bipartisan appeal.
* **Sustainable Fiscal Policy:** Powell warned that the federal government's debt is growing substantially faster than the US economy, calling the current trajectory unsustainable. He urged a return to a primary balance, though he noted this is the responsibility of Congress, not the Fed.

### Technology, AI, and Career Advice for Graduates

* **Near-Term Job Market Hurdles:** Powell acknowledged that current college graduates face a challenging business cycle characterized by low job creation, which has been further impacted by immigration policy changes that reduced both the supply and demand of workers.
* **The Productivity Power of AI:** Powell advised students to deeply master generative AI and large language models, noting that these tools are already making workers and executives significantly more productive.
* **Long-Term Economic Optimism:** Powell expressed strong optimism for the US economy, pointing to its history of outperforming other mature economies. 
    * **Productivity Growth:** Since World War II, US productivity has grown at roughly twice the speed of Europe's. Powell believes that while AI may automate certain back-office and middle-management roles, it will ultimately raise living standards and create new industries.
