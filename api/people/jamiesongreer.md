U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer stated that recent trade talks with Canada collapsed because Canadian officials "wanted more" concessions despite the U.S. offering the world's most preferential market access. Greer emphasized that the U.S. is moving forward with targeted 5% tariffs, dismissing market concerns and political friction with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

### Background & Historical Trade Friction
* **Long-Standing Grievances:** Greer stated the U.S. initiated talks after Canada maintained restrictions for over a year, specifically noting:
  * **Wine & Spirits:** Canada prohibited the sale of American wine and spirits in their stores.
  * **Auto Limits:** Canada placed limits on the number of U.S. autos entering the country.
  * **Dairy Restrictions:** Long-standing restrictions on the sale of U.S. dairy into Canada.
* **The Auto Pact Context:** Greer explained that Canada’s auto industry originated from a 1960s agreement where Canada required companies to build locally if they wanted to sell into the Canadian market.
* **Current Tariffs:** The U.S. proposed tailored tariffs covering roughly **5%** of what Canada sends to the U.S., which amounts to **0.06%** of overall U.S. economic consumption.

### Breakdown of the U.S.-Canada Trade Negotiations
* **Canadian Outreach:** Greer noted the U.S. did not initially ask to negotiate, but agreed in good faith after Canadian officials approached them about a month prior.
* **Near-Agreement & Collapse:** Parties reached sufficient agreement by Tuesday night to announce a deal framework, but negotiations collapsed in the final hours when Canadian officials demanded broader concessions.
* **U.S. Concessions Offered:** To accommodate Canadian sensitivities, the U.S. offered:
  * **Steel and Aluminum:** Cutting tariffs in half.
  * **Automobiles & Lumber:** Extensively reducing tariffs on autos and softwood lumber.
* **The Sticking Point on Trucks:** Greer confirmed that negotiations stalled over expansions in scope, such as Canadian demands to ease tariffs on the heaviest medium- and heavy-duty trucks rather than just passenger cars, SUVs, and pickups.

### Internal Administration & Policy Clarifications
* **Relationship with Commerce:** Addressing reports of friction between his office and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Greer dismissed them as "garbage in, garbage out" from *Politico*. 
  * He stated he and Lutnick talk and work together every day under presidential direction.
  * He acknowledged it may be frustrating for Canadians that tariff authorities are split across two agencies (with Commerce holding authority over **Section 232**), but noted "that's how Congress set it up."
* **Trade Deficits & Oil:** Addressing the U.S. trade deficit with Canada, Greer noted that while oil imports skew the numbers, the U.S. wants natural resources and minerals, provided there is a balanced economic and security partnership.
* **Language and Content Disputes:** Greer debunked rumors regarding English-language mandates, clarifying:
  * The U.S. supports French language and cultural identity, noting he speaks French and has family ties to Paris.
  * The actual objection is to policies requiring American tech and streaming companies (like Netflix) to take earnings and pay a percentage (e.g., **5%**) to local content competitors, which the U.S. views as a discriminatory tax.

### Market Reaction & Broader Economic Indicators
* **Market Resilience:** Greer argued that markets are shrugging off the trade dispute because they understand it affects a very small percentage of trade.
* **Economic Trajectory:** Greer highlighted positive domestic indicators, pointing to falling inflation prints in June, flat recent trends, and rising real wages for manufacturing workers.

*   **Regulatory Tools:** While Section 122 tariffs face legal challenges, Greer confirmed that Section 338 (addressing discrimination against U.S. goods) remains "on the table."

### USMCA Renegotiations and Regional Strategy
*   **Bilateral Approach:** The administration is pursuing separate negotiations with Mexico and Canada rather than a trilateral approach.
    *   **Mexico Talks:** Formal negotiations are currently underway regarding "rules of origin."
    *   **Anti-Transshipment:** A primary goal is preventing Mexico from becoming a "hub" for Chinese or Vietnamese goods seeking duty-free entry into the U.S.
*   **Canada Status:** Greer noted that Canada is currently "behind" in the negotiation timeline compared to Mexico.

### Domestic Economic Policy and Industrial Base
*   **Manufacturing Shift:** Greer highlighted a shift in import profiles toward capital goods (factory equipment and machinery), which has contributed to rising manufacturing productivity and a 5% year-over-year wage increase for factory workers.
*   **Tariff Refund Controversy:** Greer criticized Democratic Attorneys General for suing to strike down Section 122 tariffs, arguing that if refunds are legally required, the money should be diconnected from "multinational corporations" and given directly to workers as bonuses.
*   **Defense Industrial Base:** The administration is using tariffs to protect domestic production of drones, jet engines, and robotics, framing these as critical national security issues.

### Institutional and Political Hurdles
*   **Federal Reserve Deadlock:** Greer expressed frustration over the "logjam" regarding Kevin Warsh’s confirmation as Fed Chairman, urging a quick resolution to ensure the Fed responds to current economic data.
*   **Government Shutdown Impact:** The USTR attributed a decline in recent GDP (stating it would have been over 3% otherwise) to the Department of Homeland Security shutdown, citing "economic fallout" from degraded border and travel operations.

**TL;DR**
The U.S. is executing a "whole-of-government" strategy to break China’s dominance in critical minerals while transitioning from multilateral trade agreements to high-stakes bilateral deals aimed at reducing the trade deficit and boosting domestic manufacturing.

### Critical Minerals Strategy and China Decoupling
* **Strategic Partnerships:** The U.S. is aligning with the EU, Japan, and Mexico to diversify supply chains for minerals vital to national security, such as rare earths, graphite, and neodymium.
* **Economic Safeguards:** The administration is negotiating mineral-specific **price floors** to protect domestic and allied producers from non-market Chinese subsidies.
* **Domestic Production:** Efforts are underway to accelerate environmental approvals and mining in the U.S.
    * **Utah:** A significant deposit of "heavy" rare earths—similar to those found in China—is being fast-tracked.
    * **South Carolina:** Production of permanent magnets (used in electric motors) has resumed for the first time in 25 years.
* **National Security:** Greer emphasized that these minerals are indispensable for missiles, satellites, and radiation-hardened semiconductors, making reliance on China an unacceptable risk.

### Shifting Global Trade Frameworks
* **Bilateral Focus:** The administration is abandoning broad multilateralism in favor of "America First" bilateral deals. Recent agreements include Argentina, Bangladesh, India, Guatemala, and El Salvador.
* **The India Pivot:** A major interim trade framework is in motion, with a broader deal expected by March.
    * **Energy Shift:** India is winding down Russian oil purchases in favor of U.S. energy.
    * **Economic Commitment:** India is expected to purchase up to $500 billion in U.S. energy, aircraft, and technology over the next five years.
* **Upcoming Indonesia Deal:** Greer identified Indonesia (250 million people) as the next major trade target, with a deal expected to be finalized within weeks to open the market to U.S. exports.

### North American Trade Resets (USMCA)
* **Bilateral Negotiations:** The U.S. is decoupling its approach to Mexico and Canada. Greer noted that Mexico is being "pragmatic," while negotiations with Canada remain "challenging."
* **Canada Friction:** Specific disputes are stalling a reset with Canada:
    * **Gordie Howe International Bridge:** President Trump is threatening to block the opening of this Canadian-owned bridge until the U.S. is compensated for revenue and American content is addressed.
    * **Trade Barriers:** Greer cited Canada’s refusal to sell U.S. wine and spirits as a persistent obstacle.
* **Review Process:** The administration is utilizing the built-in USMCA review period to adjust rules of origin and external tariff policies.

### Economic Impact and Tariff Leverage
* **Manufacturing Gains:** Greer reported that U.S. manufacturing productivity surged 5% higher than expectations last quarter, accompanied by rising real wages for workers.
* **Tariffs as a Tool:** Tariffs are being used as leverage to force supply chain shifts and reduce the trade deficit.
* **Supreme Court Ruling:** The administration is awaiting a SCOTUS decision (expected around Feb. 20) regarding tariff authority.
    * **Contingency Planning:** If the court rules against the administration, Greer stated they will use "other tools" to maintain continuity in tariff levels and address unfair trade surpluses.
