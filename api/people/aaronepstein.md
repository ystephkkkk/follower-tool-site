## This Is How You Should Actually Make Launch Videos

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v12aSIZIXUs
- Published: 2026-10-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261023.html#video-v12aSIZIXUs
- Speakers: Farza Majeed (Founder, Hey Clicky); Aaron Epstein (Host, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Launch videos in 2026 no longer require expensive $20,000 commercial productions; instead, founders should treat every product demo as a mini-story and consistently ship raw, process-driven content on algorithmic social platforms.

### The Modern Launch Playbook: Mini-Stories Over Major Events
- **Shift from Single Events to Continuous Shipping:** Founders should abandon the outdated meta of coordinating a single, high-stakes launch (like a TechCrunch article or costly agency video).
  - Farza Majeed notes that a simple, authentic video saying "hi" and showing a product performs far better than a $20,000 produced New York Times video.
  - Success comes from treating launches as "mini-stories" and constant demos rather than high-risk rocket launches.
- **Demo-Driven Development:** Work backwards from a demo at every stage of product creation.
  - If a feature sounds good on paper as a video hook before building begins, it is worth creating.
  - The internal excitement of shouting *"Yo, check this out!"* to a co-founder is the exact raw energy that should be packaged for the internet.

### The Content Creation Process: Scripting and Hook Mechanics
- **Religiously Scripting Off-the-Cuff Videos:** Despite appearing natural, high-performing videos are meticulously planned in advance.
  - Farza writes a script days ahead, memorizes and visualizes lines in the shower or on the way to work, and uses text editors like *Freewrite* (freewrite.io) to refine the copy.
  - The core rule is evaluating the text against oneself: *Did I stop scrolling to read this sentence?*
- **The First 10 Seconds & Visual Hooks:** Setup should comprise only 10-20% of a one-minute video.
  - Start with a clear hook defining who you are, what you are doing, and why it matters.
  - Utilize recognizable visual anchors in the first frame (such as *Xcode* for developers) and zoom in immediately to capture attention while users are scrolling.
- **Embracing the "Cringe" Phase:** Getting past initial discomfort requires sheer repetition.
  - Farza admits that many produced videos initially felt terrible or "cringe" to his team before going viral (e.g., a video he thought was awful hit 3 million views on Twitter).
  - Founders must push through publishing imperfect content because audiences forget quickly and the reps build necessary taste and skill.

### Channel Selection and Conversion Realities
- **Prioritizing Platforms by Down-Funnel Conversion:** Vanity metrics (like total views) matter far less than actual user downloads and retention.
  - **Twitter/X:** Historically yields massive view counts (e.g., 3 million views) but features the lowest conversion rates for actual app downloads (less than 1%).
  - **YouTube:** Generates lower initial views (e.g., 20,000 views) but acts as a long-term library with extremely high conversion rates (roughly 20% download the app).
  - **Instagram:** Drives the majority of traffic (around 70%) for Farza's current company, *Hey Clicky*.
- **Targeting Niche Audiences on General Platforms:** Mass-market algorithmic feeds can successfully capture B2B or specialized buyers if directly addressed.
  - By directly calling out specific personas (e.g., "Are you the founder of a design agency?"), Farza generated 50 targeted design agency leads through TikTok videos averaging 4,000 views.

### Lean Production Stack and Team Structure
- **Essential Hardware and Software:** High-end production is completely optional, but audio quality is non-negotiable.
  - *Screen Studio* ($30) is used for smooth screen and cursor recordings.
  - An iPhone selfie camera is sufficient for native, authentic updates.
  - For cinematic quality, Farza uses a *Sony FX3 / FX30* paired with a *DJI mic*.
  - **Audio Warning:** Clean audio is mandatory; viewers will tolerate 720p video in a basement, but poor audio causes immediate drop-off. Never use built-in MacBook or AirPod microphones.
- **Early Team Composition:** Hiring for creative leverage early on frees technical founders to build.
  - Farza's first hires at *Hey Clicky* and *Buildspace* prioritized a filmmaker alongside engineers.
  - A dedicated filmmaker handles the execution (sets, shots, motion graphics, and editing), allowing the founder to focus on product, hiring, and growth.

### Notable Quotes

> — Farza Majeed

> "In order to not be cringe, you must first be cringe, so... you got to take out your camera and start posting some cringe stuff."
> — Farza Majeed

## Magic Patterns CEO Reviews Startup Websites

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2tZT2-pS_pI
- Published: 2026-10-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261022.html#video-2tZT2-pS_pI
- Speakers: Alex Danilowicz (CEO, Magic Patterns); Aaron Epstein (Partner, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Magic Patterns co-founder and CEO Alex Danilowicz joined YC’s Aaron Epstein to demonstrate their AI design tool and review startup websites to help founders eliminate "vibe-coded" design flaws.

### Magic Patterns: Product & Origins
* **Company Background:** Founded in October 2023 by two non-designer front-end engineers, Alex Danilowicz and his co-founder launched as one of the very first prompt-to-design tools on the market alongside V0.
* **Pivot to Product-Market Fit:** Applied to YC with a text message analyzer before their YC partner advised them to focus on their core expertise: translating Figma mockups into code; after trying various pivots (component library editor, storybook on steroids, local host Chrome extension) over 9 months, they finally found traction when launching Magic Patterns.
* **Dogfooding Culture:** The team relentlessly dogfoods their own product internally, such as building their own loading indicators and using their own canvas tools before shipping.
* **Model and Feature Agnosticism:** Unlike competitors like Claude Design (which locks users into Anthropic models and Claude Code), Magic Patterns is model-agnostic and allows exports to Figma, cursor, cloud code, and any coding agent.
* **Team-Wide Adoption:** The tool democratizes design across non-designers, with everyone from the ops team to sales using Magic Patterns to create materials without outsourcing.

### The Magic OS Canvas & Workflow
* **Canvas 2.0 / Magic OS:** A newly released infinite canvas and operating system for context that allows product teams to bring together prompts, images, PRDs, and documentation.
* **Multi-Threaded Interaction:** Unlike traditional design tools or single-threaded AI chats where users must wait for an agent to finish, Magic OS supports multiple concurrent threads and artboards.
* **Model Context Protocol (MCP):** Initially released a year ago as a hackathon project, MCP usage has completely exploded and become a primary interaction method for enterprise users who want to connect context from Cursor, Claude Code, and PRDs directly to a visual canvas.
* **3D Game Experiences:** Demonstrates that modern code-based design is moving beyond flat 2D; users can now one-shot complex 3D WebGL experiences in the browser (such as a chocolate factory theme park in Roller Coaster Tycoon) by feeding component-based guardrails to the AI.

### Startup Website Design Reviews
* **Ego (Applied AI research lab building personal agents):**
  * *Critique:* Suffering from "vibe-coded" smells like unnecessary dark-mode red animations, blinking dots, an intimidating un-interactive eyeball, and hiding the core value proposition and waitlist CTA too far down the page.
  * *Insight:* Input forms or interactive elements should be immediately visible and expanded on the page rather than requiring hidden clicks or popups to maximize conversion rates.
* **Sentient OS (On-device LLM for Mac):**
  * *Critique:* Employs "scrolljacking" (altering normal trackpad/mouse scroll precision), which frustrates users and reduces conversion; uses trendy mid-2020s design defaults like Instrument Serif and Space Grotesque fonts and random bolding.
  * *Insight:* For local machine apps, trust, privacy, and security (such as emphasizing open-source, on-device processing) must be front and center on the hero section.
* **Saranovas (Institutional intelligence and decision infrastructure):**
  * *Critique:* Features excessive white space above the fold, vague enterprise copy, and forces users to click through target personas (enterprise, middle market, private equity) instead of immediately showing what problem the product solves.
  * *Insight:* Vibe-coded websites often read like investor pitch decks rather than customer-centric product pages.
* **Slashy (Intelligent inbox):**
  * *Critique:* The logo reads as "Sashy" due to the slash placement, and the demo video contains too much dense text for a user to process quickly.
  * *Strengths:* Clear value proposition above the fold, strong social proof from founders/investors, a trust and security navigation link, and high-quality footer spacing.

### Advice on Design Taste & Best Practices
* **Developer Design Taste:** Alex Danilowicz recommends reading *The Design of Everyday Things*, looking at beautiful art (like a Matisse exhibit), and realizing that design is not just pixel-perfect border radius, but putting the human and user experience first.
* **Cross-Functional Customer Support:** Every employee at Magic Patterns works in the customer support inbox daily to experience user frustrations firsthand and fix them immediately.
* **User Testing:** Founders must talk directly to users and ask for honest feedback without letting them know it is their own website to avoid overly polite bias.

### Notable Quotes
* > "Customers are our religion... talking to them every single day and then also adapting to new workflows." — Alex Danilowicz
* > "What's changed about software development in the last year especially is the end stage is so compressed now... engineers are just moving at the speed of light. There's too many PRs to review. And so, as a result, we can spend more time actually brainstorming, thinking about what to build and what not to build." — Alex Danilowicz
* > "There's this big misconception in Silicon Valley that design is pixel perfect border radius and it's not. It's thinking through the user experience and of course having high fidelity UI that's that's beautiful." — Alex Danilowicz


## How To Design In The Agent Era

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=P06RgnUKX_I
- Published: 2026-08-07
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260807.html#video-P06RgnUKX_I
- Speakers: Stephen Haney (Founder, Paper); Aaron Epstein (General Partner, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Stephen Haney, founder of AI-native design tool Paper, joins YC General Partner Aaron Epstein to demonstrate how Paper uses HTML/CSS rendering to bridge human design taste with agentic workflows, while warning founders against generic AI design "slop."

### The Genesis and Vision of Paper
* **Founding & Team Size:** Paper was started as a solo side project by Stephen Haney out of a love for creative tooling and founder-market fit, and has grown to a team of **12 designers and engineers**.
* **Agent-Native Rendering Engine:** **Stephen Haney** explains that Paper uses HTML and CSS as its actual rendering engine rather than a custom rendering engine.
    * **Developer & Agent HandOff:** This constraint-free system creates a seamless handoff for both human developers and AI agents, because agents natively understand HTML/CSS from their training data.
    * **Token Efficiency:** This approach saves tokens, runs faster, and produces fewer hallucinations.
* **The New Agent Stack:** **Stephen Haney** describes the modern builder setup as combining Cursor, Claude Code, and GitHub with Paper acting as the visual interface to communicate with agents.
* **Company Traction:** According to Ramp credit card subscription data cited by **Aaron Epstein**, Paper is the first company since Figma to make a measurable dent in the design tool market, growing rapidly and ranking alongside Sketch.

### Core Features and Demos
* **Paper Shaders:** Fully open-source WebGL animations designed by studying physical paper to model natural randomness. 
    * **Marketing Strategy:** **Stephen Haney** notes that Paper treats its shader library and custom fonts (like the upcoming **Paper Mono**) as its marketing budget instead of buying traditional ads.
    * **Real-World Usage:** **Aaron Epstein** notes that Y Combinator uses these shaders for acceptance tickets and Startup School branding.
* **Image Generation & Vectorization:** Paper features an image generation library using four different models simultaneously via a "variety pack," alongside color extraction and vectorization tools.
* **Desktop App & MCP Server:** Built following the December 2025 cloud code explosion, featuring an MCP server that allows agents like Cursor and Conductor to directly render and manipulate frames on the canvas.
* **Paper Snapshot:** A Chrome extension allowing users to pull live websites directly into the Paper canvas as editable layers instead of taking static screenshots.

### Deconstructing AI Design "Slop"
* **The Importance of Differentiation:** **Stephen Haney** and **Aaron Epstein** emphasize that using default, unedited AI output makes startups look generic, untrustworthy, and indistinguishable from millions of other projects.
* **Common AI Design Tells:**
    * **Excessive Font Weight & Sizes:** Models over-use bold weights and incorporate 5 to 8 different font sizes.
    * **The "Four Horsemen":** Side-swatches of color on every card, purple gradients, all-uppercase tiny headers with wide letter spacing, and badge pills with unnecessary small icons.
    * **Widgets for Widgets' Sake:** Pointless decorative numbers or small UI elements added merely to fill space.
    * **Default Dark Mode:** Forcing dark mode on every MVP regardless of whether it fits the product.
* **Quick Remediation Rules:** **Stephen Haney** advises designers to pull font weights back to regular or light, restrict type hierarchies to a maximum of **three font sizes**, and ruthlessly delete non-communicative decorative elements.

### Live Redesigns: Legion Health, Sciteex, and More
* **Legion Health Review:** 
    * **Aaron Epstein** notes the original design suffers from generic pill elements and weak alignment, lacking the trustworthy fit-and-finish required for mental health medication management.
    * **Live Fix:** Using Paper Snapshot and conductor, **Stephen Haney** generates multiple layout variations, cleans up font weights, and removes overlapping text elements.
* **Sciteex Review:**
    * **Aaron Epstein** points out that the field infrastructure platform's value proposition is buried below the fold, overshadowed by heavy purple glows and vibe-coded elements.
    * **Live Fix:** Simplifying the layout to three font sizes and removing bold weights immediately elevates the design's perceived trustworthiness.
* **The New Curation Workflow:** **Stephen Haney** explains that builders can leave comments on a design, let agents run overnight generating hundreds of variations, and curate the best ideas into a unified final product.

### Founder Lessons and Future Outlook
* **Previous Ventures:** **Stephen Haney** previously founded Modulz (focused on design-to-dev handoff) and created Radix UI, which was eventually sold to Work OS.
* **Operational Philosophy:** **Stephen Haney** stresses focusing on bottlenecks first, avoiding over-complicated strategies, and maintaining a strict rule of **talking to a user every single day** for the first year (a culture now instilled across Paper's entire 12-person team).
* **Roadmap:** Paper's upcoming focus for the next **6 months** includes building out table stakes features like components, comments, and prototype organization tools for designers using vibe-coding playgrounds.

### Notable Quotes
> *"If you want to be one of those really great companies, you have to put the car in on design and make sure that you stand out."* — **Aaron Epstein**

> *"It's like an insecure designer. The agents are very insecure. You want to like fill it out and there's like lots of little ones and twos... A lot of design is deleting."* — **Stephen Haney**

> *"We didn't have a product for the first year and we still got to about 25,000 Twitter followers just by talking about our values... If you're building design software, usually best product, most authentic product wins."* — **Stephen Haney**


## How Stripe Built Their New Website

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ypzNhwpmOD4
- Published: 2026-04-22
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260422.html#video-ypzNhwpmOD4
- Speakers: Aaron Epstein (Design Partner, Y Combinator); Katie Dill (Head of Design, Stripe)

---

**TL;DR**
Stripe’s homepage redesign highlights a shift from a payment-centric narrative to a comprehensive "financial manifesto," utilizing custom internal tools and AI to achieve a level of craft that signals technical reliability to global enterprises.

### Strategic Shift: From Feature-Adding to a Unified Narrative
* **Business Evolution:** Stripe’s previous site (launched in 2020) survived six years, but the business outgrew the "add-on" structure. The redesign moved away from simply stacking sections to a cohesive story targeting both micro-entrepreneurs and multinational enterprises.
* **The "Website as Manifesto":** Head of Design Katie Dill notes that a website serves as an indirect manifesto; the level of care in the UI signals the level of care Stripe takes in moving money and protecting sensitive data.
* **AI Market Dominance:** A primary goal was highlighting Stripe’s role in the AI economy—over 78% of the Forbes AI 50 currently use Stripe to monetize via usage-based billing and global scaling.

### New UI Paradigms: The "Bento" and Live Social Proof
* **Live GDP Counter:** Stripe utilizes a live counter of the global GDP processed through its systems. This is a "power move" unique to Stripe that immediately signals scale, dependability, and trust to large-scale users.
* **The "Bento" Box:** To solve the problem of product breadth, Stripe implemented a visual grid (the "bento") featuring:
    * **Progressive Disclosure:** Instead of navigating users away from the homepage, Stripe uses large modal overlays to provide product depth while keeping the user in a "lean-back" browsing mode.
    * **Purposeful Motion:** Animations (e.g., usage-based billing lines) are fine-tuned to feel "alive" without being distracting, serving as feedback that elements are interactive.
* **"Squeezy Boy" Section:** A flexible, dynamically sized area designed for changing content like the "Black Friday Cyber Monday" city or the "Cheeky Pint" podcast, maintaining visual interest regardless of window scale.

### The Design Process: Custom Tools and AI Integration
* **The Wave Tool:** Stripe engineers built a custom internal tool to allow designers to manipulate grain, texture, blur, and rotation of their signature "gradient wave." This allowed for "infinite variations" before settling on a version that felt timeless.
* **AI in Creative Production:**
    * **Iterative Imagery:** Stripe used AI to generate complex brand imagery (like the Stripe parallelogram integrated into customer brand photos). 
    * **The "Bubble" Critique:** Dill shared internal critiques showing that even AI-generated "realism" (like ice cube bubbles) required human intervention to fix anatomical errors and lighting nuances.
* **Raising the Floor:** Dill believes AI "raises the floor" for design, allowing teams to hit a "7 out of 10" quality level instantly. This frees designers to spend their time on new paradigms, such as "agent experiences" (AI interacting with AI).

### Culture of Quality: "Walking the Store"
* **Fighting Mediocrity:** Dill warns against the "gravitational pull to mediocrity," where teams accept "good enough" to save time. Stripe’s culture is built on "MVQP" (Minimum Viable Quality Product) rather than just MVP.
* **Essential Journeys:** Every Friday, Stripe founders and leaders "walk the store"—a practice of live-testing user journeys in front of the company to find "dead ends" and ensure cross-product cohesion.
* **Prototyping vs. Presenting:** The design team prioritizes high-fidelity prototypes over static presentations. This allows stakeholders to experience the product exactly as a user would, removing the need for explanatory "trade-off" narratives.

> "The gravitational pull is to mediocrity. It is so easy to accept 'good enough,' but you have to think about the cost. If you make that decision every day, what is that composite going to be?"
— **Katie Dill**, Stripe Head of Design

> "Don’t be wooed by just how easy that was to achieve with an AI tool... ask yourself: 'Is this really great? Does it feel well-crafted?' You should not accept slop."
— **Katie Dill**, Stripe Head of Design

> "What is the point of a website anyway? I remember being in a meeting where Patrick [Collison] asked that. It’s your manifesto... demonstrating who you are, what you are doing, and why you do it."
— **Katie Dill**, Stripe Head of Design
