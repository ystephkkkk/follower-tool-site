## Sovereign Stacks: Building Trusted AI on National Terms | RAISE Summit 2026

#### TL;DR
Sovereignty in AI is about maintaining practical control, choice, and a "Plan B" across the infrastructure stack rather than pursuing complete isolation or relying purely on regulatory compliance checkboxes. Addressing these challenges requires balancing massive infrastructure scale (such as $100 billion compute demands) with open ecosystems and pragmatic business realities.

---

#### Defining Sovereign AI: Control vs. Isolation
* **Pragmatic Goals over Paperwork:** Gleb Budman (Backblaze) noted that sovereignty is frequently confused with regulatory paperwork or mere residency, arguing that true sovereignty is the pragmatic ability for an organization to manage, move, and access its data and workflows without punitive taxes like egress fees.
* **Control, Not Autarchy:** Mohsen Moazami (DDN / ex-Groq) emphasized that sovereignty is about control and avoiding single-vendor hostage situations, not isolation, pointing out that Saudi Arabia achieved sovereign compute stack control while successfully exporting tokens.
* **Vendor Independence:** Thomas Di Giacomo (SUSE) defined sovereignty as the freedom to exit, pivot, and maintain choice, pointing to open-source software as a critical mandate supported by the European Commission to prevent vendor lock-in.
* **Business Enabler:** Simon Muskett (Digital Realty) described infrastructure platforms as the foundational enabler of sovereign decisions, where data location, security, and network architecture directly dictate business outcomes.

#### Navigating the AI Stack and Infrastructure Realities
* **Cross-Stack Interdependence:** Simon Muskett highlighted that all layers of the stack—from data centers and cooling (such as Digital Realty's Paris partnership with Mistral) to networking and models—are equally critical, requiring holistic design rather than focusing solely on frontier models.
* **Risk Mitigation and Plan B:** Thomas Di Giacomo argued that infrastructure layers (like hardware and cloud foundations) require a dual-vendor or backup strategy because failure at this layer halts mission-critical business operations.
* **Granular Enterprise Use Cases:** Corey Hawkvelt (Hyperstack) explained that enterprise AI sovereignty is not "carte blanche"; an enterprise may route low-sensitivity tasks (like marketing cat memes) to the cheapest global provider while keeping sensitive finance data under strict local compliance.
* **Global Scale Disconnect:** Mohsen Moazami contrasted European neo-clouds operating at scales of 10,000 GPUs due to 3-year depreciation risks with American model companies handling single-customer purchase orders worth **$100 billion** in compute.
* **Tokenomics and Efficiency:** Simon Muskett noted that tokenomics, latency, and liquid cooling requirements—costing hundreds of millions or billions of dollars—drive modern GPU placement and infrastructure profitability.

#### Geopolitical Pressures and Innovation Trade-Offs
* **Engineers Under Constraints:** Corey Hawkvelt argued that US foreign policy and export controls act as a positive catalyst for business and engineering innovation, forcing nations like China to build competitive indigenous models and hardware (such as Huawei GPUs).
* **The Rise of "Buy Europe":** Thomas Di Giacomo observed that while Europe has historically lagged and been shy about national procurement preferences, a "buy Europe" mindset is emerging, which can successfully revitalize European technology sectors.
* **Interdependence as a Pre-requisite:** Gleb Budman compared data infrastructure to airports: *“The only reason that any of those are valuable is because the planes fly between them,”* warning that hyper-restricted data residency without cross-border mobility renders data useless.

#### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Obsolescence of the Term:** Thomas Di Giacomo predicts that by 2030 or 2035, the specific word "sovereignty" will no longer be discussed because the underlying concepts of control and independence will have matured.
