## From deepfakes to DNA: the science of watermarking AI

- Channel: Google DeepMind
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HIUzrxQxTtw
- Published: 2026-10-01
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261001.html#video-HIUzrxQxTtw
- Speakers: Hannah Fry (Professor, UCL); Pushmeet Kohli (VP of Science, Google DeepMind); Jeremy Ratcliffe (Research Scientist, Google DeepMind)

---

### TL;DR
Google DeepMind's SynthID watermarking technology—developed over eight years for digital media like text, images, and video—has now expanded into biology as "SynthID Bio" to trace the provenance of AI-generated protein sequences and 3D structures without impacting their biological function.

### Core Concepts of AI Watermarking
* **Defining the Goal:** According to Pushmeet Kohli, watermarking provides a sense of provenance and lets users verify whether content originated from a human, nature, or an AI system.
* **Key Requirements:** **Imperceptibility** (invisible to users), **robustness** (survives attacks, crops, transformations, or noise), and **scalability** (easy to integrate and detect across systems).
* **Flaws of Traditional Classifiers:** **Declining Accuracy:** Machine learning classifiers that look for traditional tells (like overused words) lose accuracy over time as generative AI models improve.
* **The Text Watermarking Mechanism:** **Optionality & Secret Keys:** When a large language model generates text, it chooses from a distribution of valid words. A secret background key biases certain word choices, allowing detectors to identify AI generation through statistical patterns across a chain of words.
* **Limitations in Text:** **Short Snippets:** Very short text inputs (e.g., "What is the capital of France? It is Paris") cannot be effectively watermarked due to a lack of variable options.
* **The Image/Video Watermarking Mechanism:** **Co-Trained Networks:** A neural network modifies the unwatermarked image subtly to embed a signal. It is co-trained with a detector network and tested against an adversarial agent that crops, shrinks, rotates, or adds noise to ensure the watermark remains robust.

### SynthID Bio and Biosecurity Applications
* **Dual Biological Offerings:** Jeremy Ratcliff explains that SynthID Bio includes **SynthID Bio Structure** (predicting protein structures on top of AlphaFold 3 by shifting atom positions and angles) and **SynthID Bio Sequence** (introducing watermarks into amino acid strings).
* **The Biosecurity Threat:** **Bypassing Screening:** Bad actors can use AI to design custom protein sequences that look completely different in sequence space from restricted pathogens in DNA synthesis screening databases, but still fold into harmful structures.
* **The Solution for DNA Synthesis:** **Provenance Confidence:** When a DNA synthesis company receives an order, watermarking allows them to verify that a sequence originated from an AI system with built-in safety guardrails rather than an unknown source.
* **Protein MPNN Analogy:** **Sequence Generation:** SynthID Bio Sequence builds on protein MPNN (a third-party tool that goes from structure to sequence), taking advantage of allowable swaps between similar amino acids (like leucine and isoleucine) that maintain identical structure and function.
* **Wet Lab Validation:** **Hit Rates:** Laboratory tests on watermarked protein binders showed near-identical hit rates and quantitative binding measures compared to unwatermarked binders, proving functionality is preserved.

### Industry Adoption and Future Integration
* **Ecosystem Expansion:** SynthID is already utilized across the industry by companies like Nvidia and OpenAI, and detection is built directly into apps like Gemini.
* **Regulatory Alignment:** Pushmeet Kohli notes that generative content watermarking has been brought into law within the EU, with regulators recognizing it as a scalable solution for provenance.
* **Open-Source Biology Strategy:** SynthID Bio is being released as open-source code to catalyze conversations with model developers and DNA synthesis screening companies, allowing them to exchange keys and implement the technology without central coordination.


## 10 years of AlphaGo: The turning point for AI | Thore Graepel & Pushmeet Kohli

- Channel: Google DeepMind
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qoinGjj60Fo
- Published: 2026-03-10
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260310.html#video-qoinGjj60Fo
- Speakers: Thore Graepel (AlphaGo team & Distinguished Research Scientist, Google DeepMind); Pushmeet Kohli (VP, Science, Google DeepMind); Hannah Fry (Presenter and Mathematician)

---

**TL;DR**
The 2016 AlphaGo match against Lee Sedol marked the shift from AI as a tool for brute-force calculation to a system capable of creative intuition, establishing the reinforcement learning and search-space navigation techniques now used to solve grand scientific challenges like protein folding and mathematical proofs.

### The Technical Breakthrough: Intuition Meets Calculation
*   **The Complexity of Go:** Unlike Chess, which Deep Blue mastered via calculation, Go features a search space of $10^{170}$ possible positions, making it "mathematically impossible" to brute-force.
*   **Hybrid Architecture:** Thore Graepel explains that AlphaGo succeeded by mimicking the two modes of human thought:
    *   **Fast Thinking (Policy Networks):** Deep learning used to develop "intuition" to immediately recognize promising moves without analyzing every possibility.
    *   **Slow Thinking (Value Networks & Tree Search):** Rigorous look-ahead planning to calculate the long-term consequences of those intuitive choices.
*   **Reinforcement Learning:** The system was not merely a database of moves; it learned by playing against itself, eventually reaching a level where it could outperform professional players by "hallucinating" and then verifying superior strategies.

### Strategic Milestones: Moves 37 and 78
*   **Move 37 (The Creative Moment):** In Game 2, AlphaGo played a "shoulder hit" on the fifth line—a move human professionals were taught to avoid. 
    *   **Impact:** This move proved AlphaGo could go beyond human distribution and develop original, superior strategies that commentators initially dismissed as mistakes.
*   **Move 78 (The Divine Move):** In Game 4, Lee Sedol played a surprising "wedge" move that AlphaGo had not predicted, causing the AI to lose its grasp on the position and eventually resign.
    *   **Human Triumph:** Thore Graepel describes this as a "human triumph" where Sedol found a fragility in the machine, though AlphaGo ultimately won the match 4-1.
*   **Shifting Perspectives:** The Go community transitioned from shock to adoption; today, AI is the primary tool used by professional Go players for training and strategy analysis.

### AlphaZero: Discarding Human Knowledge
*   **Tabula Rasa Learning:** Following the initial match, DeepMind developed AlphaZero, which was provided only the rules of the game—no human data or professional game records.
*   **Beyond Human Logic:** AlphaZero eventually outperformed the original AlphaGo. Thore Graepel noted that its style appeared "alien" because it was not limited by human heuristics or traditional "Joseki" (opening patterns).
*   **Self-Refutation:** The AI rediscovered standard human openings only to later discard them after finding more efficient refutations that humans had missed for centuries.

### The Legacy: From Board Games to Global Science
*   **The AlphaFold Connection:** During the Seoul match, DeepMind leadership (Demis Hassabis and David Silver) realized that if AI could navigate the combinatorial search space of Go, it could solve the "protein folding" problem—a 50-year-old biological challenge.
*   **Algorithmic Discovery:** Pushmeet Kohli highlights how these techniques moved into other fields:
    *   **AlphaTensor:** Discovered a faster way to perform matrix multiplication, an algorithm that had remained stagnant for 50 years.
    *   **AlphaProof:** Now being used to solve complex mathematical theorems by coupling large language models with rigorous verifiers.
*   **The Role of the Human:** As AI moves toward "Move 37 moments" in science (discovering things humans cannot yet explain), the human role shifts from calculation to **Problem Specification**—defining the reward functions and asking the right questions.

### Notable Quotes

> "It’s just amazing seeing how quickly a problem that is seen as being impossible can change to being practically done. We can solve protein folding. That’s like... I mean, it’s just huge."

## AlphaGenome author roundtable

- Channel: Google DeepMind
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V8lhUqKqzUc
- Published: 2026-01-28
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260128.html#video-V8lhUqKqzUc
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (CEO, Google DeepMind); Pushmeet Kohli (VP of Research, Google DeepMind)

---

### TL;DR
AlphaGenome is Google DeepMind's new AI model, published in "Nature," that unifies DNA sequence-to-function prediction, representing a significant step towards deciphering the entire genome. It leverages novel architectural and data handling techniques to predict the functional impact of genetic variants across the vast non-coding regions, addressing critical unmet needs in understanding genetic diseases and fundamental biology.

### AlphaGenome's Mission and Impact
*   **Deciphering the Genome Source Code:** The team's overarching mission is to build an AI system to decipher the genome sequence, the "source code of life," with enormous potential benefits for health and other areas.
    *   Measuring progress involves assessing how well the system predicts the effects of mutations (small DNA changes) on cellular molecular mechanisms.
*   **Addressing Undiagnosed Genetic Diseases:** Improving genome understanding is crucial, as a "large fraction of rare genetic diseases remain undiagnosed today" due to poor genomic comprehension.
    *   AI is positioned as an "impactful application" for reading the genome and making meaningful predictions about genetic mutations.
*   **Expanding Genomic Coverage:** AlphaGenome significantly extends previous work (e.g., AlphaMissense, which covered only the 2% coding region) to focus on the **non-coding 98% of the genome**, illuminating its majority.

### Technical Breakthroughs and Unique Capabilities
*   **Unified Multi-Modal Prediction:** AlphaGenome uniquely models more modalities (molecular mechanisms) in one model than ever before, combining long input sequence length and finely detailed predictions.
    *   It inputs **megabases of DNA sequence** and outputs predictions at **single-base resolution** for many output types.
    *   This fills a gap left by previous models that either specialized (e.g., SpliceAI, Orca) or traded off resolution for context length (e.g., Informer, Borzoi).
*   **Overcoming Computational Hurdles:** The model addresses prior computational limits and high-memory usage associated with long-range, high-resolution, multi-modal predictions.
    *   **Distributed sequence processing:** Spliced sequences into shorter subsequences processed by multiple TPUs that communicate to maintain context, inspired by a breakthrough lunch discussion.
    *   **Optimized data loading:** Managed vast training data (up to 50 GB) by compressing sparse modalities (99% zeros) and rigorously quality-checking data for high quality and diversity.
*   **Integration of Complex Biological Modalities:** AlphaGenome successfully integrates predictions for diverse biological processes.
    *   **Splicing:** Models the process of bringing non-contiguous genetic information together for protein expression, a capability new to the team and the field, handling 2D sparse array predictions.
    *   **Contact Maps:** Captures 2D interactions between DNA regions vital for gene regulation (e.g., promoter-enhancer interactions) without degrading performance on other modalities.

### Comprehensive Evaluation and API Release
*   **Rigorous Two-Pronged Evaluation Strategy:** Model performance was assessed on both new DNA sequences and, critically, on variant effect prediction.
    *   **Variant effect prediction:** Compares predictions from reference vs. mutated DNA sequences to identify and summarize differences, overcoming initial challenges with **11 gigabyte outputs** via a new, fast variant scoring API.
    *   **Parallelized evaluation:** The team parallelized evaluation efforts, with individuals owning specific modalities or areas, often using pre-existing community benchmarks.
    *   **Multi-level evaluation:** Evaluations covered molecular-level recapitulation of experimental data and organism-level consequences like disease risk, including a surprising success in predicting cancer driver mutations.
*   **Accessible API for the Community:** AlphaGenome was released with a user-friendly API simultaneously with its preprint, allowing immediate interaction without specialized hardware (e.g., GPUs) or complex setup.
    *   Users can quickly make predictions and visualize results in a notebook environment.
    *   The team directly manages user support via a Discourse forum, actively soliciting feedback on case studies, gaps, and ideas.
*   **Efficient Development:** The entire process from initial prototype discussion to paper publication was completed in **under two years** through intensive team collaboration.

### Future Directions and Impact
*   **Enhanced User Experience and Summarization:** Plans include aggregating the tens of thousands of scores per variant into more digestible single scores, aiding scientists in prioritizing variants for deeper analysis.
*   **Enabling Large-Scale Genomic Analysis:** The team aims to pre-compute as many variants as possible to support large-scale analyses, such as genome-wide association studies, which require extensive predictions.
*   **Open-Sourcing Model Weights:** Releasing the model weights will empower researchers to experiment with embeddings, fine-tune the model, or extend it with new data, fostering diverse, unforeseen applications.
*   **Expanding Capabilities with Single-Cell Data:** Future improvements include leveraging new single-cell atlases to extend the model's applicability to individual cell types within tissues, enhancing its relevance for disease studies.
*   **Broadening Scientific Utility:** AlphaGenome is poised to be a foundational tool for pinpointing harmful mutations, understanding basic biology (e.g., functional genome parts, gene regulation, cell type activation), and maximizing research efficiency and funding returns.
