## AMI Founder & Chair Yann LeCun at Silicon Valley & The World | Semafor

- Channel: Semafor

- Speakers: Yann LeCun (Founder and Chair, Advanced Machine Intelligence); Shelly Banjo (Deputy Editor-in-Chief, Semafor)

---

### TL;DR
Yann LeCun, Founder and Chair of Advanced Machine Intelligence (AMI), explained why Silicon Valley is overly "LLM-pilled" and detailed his $1 billion seed-funded bet on "world models" and physical AI—funded entirely without Silicon Valley VCs. He also warned that the concentration of AI power among a few US and Chinese companies poses a major threat, advocating for open-source foundation models to ensure global AI diversity and sovereignty.

### The Limits of LLMs & Silicon Valley's Blind Spot
* **The "LLM-Pilled" Echo Chamber:** LeCun argued that while large language models (LLMs) are useful and commercially successful, they are not a path to human-like intelligence or superintelligence.
  * **Discrete Symbols Only:** LLMs rely on self-supervised learning to predict the next item in a sequence of discrete symbols (human language, computer code, mathematics), which leaves physical AI as a "complete blind spot."
  * **Pixel-Level Failure:** Generative models cannot accurately predict the physical world because most real-world information is unpredictable at the pixel level (e.g., predicting what an unseen part of a room looks like as a camera rotates).
* **Flawed Agentic Systems:** Current agentic systems apply trained recipes without being able to predict the consequences of their actions, which LeCun called an unreliable and unsafe way to build agents.

### AMI's $1 Billion Bet on World Models
* **Spinning Out from Meta:** AMI (Advanced Machine Intelligence) began as an internal project at Meta. While CEO Mark Zuckerberg and CTO Andrew Bosworth were supportive, Meta's broader AI organization increasingly focused on LLMs, and AMI's industrial applications fell outside Meta's core interests.
* **Record Seed Round ("A Coconut"):** AMI raised **$1 billion** in seed funding—likely the largest seed round outside the US—as a French company.
  * **Investor Breakdown:** **40%** from European sources, **33%** from the US, **27%** from Asia (including one fund from Dubai), and **$0 (0%)** from Silicon Valley VCs.
  * **Why No Silicon Valley VCs:** LeCun noted the round was oversubscribed, and Silicon Valley VCs wanted control they were not going to get.
* **Technology & Commercial Focus:** LeCun has worked on world models for **20 years** (advocating publicly for **10 years**, with real breakthroughs beginning **5 years ago** via self-supervised learning on video).
  * **No Language:** AMI is staying away from language entirely, focusing instead on video, audio, and sensor data.
  * **Industrial Applications:** Target use cases include physical AI, industrial process control, predictive maintenance, and anomaly detection.
  * **Robotics:** While robotics will be a big business, LeCun noted that current robotics companies do not know how to make robots smart enough to be useful, and domestic robots will require conceptual AI breakthroughs.

### AI Safety, Concentration of Power & Open Source
* **Cybersecurity Transition:** In response to moderator Shelly Banjo's questions on AI risks, LeCun said he is not worried about ad-supported chatbots, but expects a short period where AI favors cyber attackers over defenders before all future software is hardened with AI.
* **Concentration of Power:** LeCun's primary concern is that citizens' information diets are increasingly mediated by AI controlled by a handful of companies on the US West Coast or in China, which he called "extremely dangerous."
* **The Case for Open Source:** Open-source foundation models are essential for countries to achieve AI sovereignty and for organizations to build assistants that reflect diverse languages, cultures, and value systems.
  * **Meta's Open-Source History:** LeCun was influential in convincing Meta leadership to open-source **Llama 2**, which jump-started the global AI ecosystem. However, **Llama 4** was a disappointment, prompting Zuckerberg to reboot Meta's AI effort and briefly pull back from open-sourcing.
  * **China's Lead:** LeCun lamented that the most powerful open-source AI systems are currently coming out of China, though he noted that because they are open source, they can be unconditioned and fine-tuned for any purpose.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Cybersecurity & Software Safety:** There will be a short transition period where AI tools facilitate cyberattacks and favor attackers over defenders, followed by an era of "unprecedented safety of software systems" as all future software is hardened with AI. — *Yann LeCun*
* **Domestic Robotics Timeline:** Useful domestic robots are "not for tomorrow" and will not be a very short-term business, as they require several conceptual breakthroughs in AI. — *Yann LeCun*

---

**TL;DR**
Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch outlines the company's differentiation as a "sovereign AI" provider—offering portable, zero-trust, on-premise, and open-weight models for critical industrial and financial workflows—while positioning Europe as a vital hedge against US and Chinese AI dependencies ahead of an eventual European IPO.

---

### US Presence & Enterprise Strategy: "Sovereign AI"
* **Silicon Valley Footprint:** Mistral maintains its US headquarters in Palo Alto with a sizable team of researchers and go-to-market staff.
  * The company is increasingly partnering with cyber vendors to deploy its latest open-source release, which Mensch noted has strong cyber capabilities.
* **Defining "Sovereign AI":** Rather than competing on sheer scale with OpenAI/Anthropic or strictly on price with Chinese labs, Mistral targets mission-critical enterprise processes (such as R&D and manufacturing) with three guarantees: portability, business continuity, and affordability.
  * **Industrial Customers:** Mistral powers agentic engineering loops and physical simulations for major manufacturers, including **Airbus** (aircraft), **ASML** (lithography machines), and **BMW** (automobiles).
* **Horizontal vs. Vertical Model:** Mensch contrasted Mistral's horizontal, open-weight platform—which lets customers build custom systems—against competitors that go vertical to capture more of the value chain.

### Data Privacy, Portability & Competitive Positioning
* **Zero-Trust Architecture:** Addressing Shelly Banjo's question on whether OpenAI and Anthropic use customer data to build competing products, Mensch noted that enterprise clients in manufacturing and financial services do not want to rely on vendor promises.
  * Mistral proves it cannot see customer data either by deploying models **on-premises** (so data never flows back to Mistral) or through **confidential cloud guarantees**.
  * This ensures customers' IP and processes continue running even if Mistral were to disappear, and protects them if a vendor suffers a cyberattack.
* **Two-Tier Model Portfolio:** While Mistral's core platform is portable by design, it operates two distinct model categories:
  * **Open-source models:** Available for free.
  * **Proprietary models:** Specialized models deployed on-premises for customers rather than exposed for free.
* **Pricing:** Mensch emphasized that Mistral's models—as well as the third-party open-source models it serves—are "quite cheap."

### Geopolitics & Hedging Against US/China Dependencies
* **Global vs. Regional Drivers:** While Mistral's US growth is driven purely by technological differentiation, its growth in Europe and Asia is also fueled by geopolitical fragmentation.
* **Hedging Supply Chain Risk:** European and Asian enterprises are actively hedging against excessive reliance on US providers, fearing they could lose access to US models or compute.
  * Customers increasingly demand compute located in Europe and operated by European players.

### French Politics, Youth Employment & European IPO Plans
* **Youth & AI Anxiety:** Responding to Banjo's question on political unrest in France, Mensch attributed part of the youth anguish to companies freezing entry-level hiring in favor of senior staff due to AI, alongside unsustainable European pension systems.
  * Mistral actively hires young talent, arguing they adopt AI far better and create "much more value than people that are 10 years older than them."
  * Mensch criticized "Doomsday" AI narratives for fueling public anxiety.
* **Navigating French Politics:** Pushing back on the idea that Mistral is a "personal project" of President Emmanuel Macron, Mensch said sovereign AI is a bipartisan necessity to avoid trade imbalances and foreign dependency.
  * Ahead of France's **2027** presidential election, Mensch has been conducting **1-hour** education sessions with political candidates to prepare them for "five years of very technical discussions" on AI policy and data center capacity.
* **European IPO:** Europe represents Mistral's headquarters, the majority of its investor base, and "a little more than half" of its customer base, making it the natural listing venue.

---

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **European IPO Timeline:** Mistral is preparing to go public in Europe, though it will not happen this year — *Arthur Mensch* ("We're not going to do this this year, but we're definitely getting ready for it.")
* **AI Policymaking Horizon:** The next French administration will face five years of highly technical AI policy discussions — *Arthur Mensch* ("They need to get a little more technical because that's going to be five years of very technical discussions.")

**TL;DR:** Former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo criticized transactional US chip export policies while introducing RAISE, her $750M non-profit aimed at preventing an AI-driven labor crisis, as Hyundai CEO José Muñoz detailed his company's $26B US investment and pivot into "physical AI" with Waymo, Uber, and Boston Dynamics.

### US-China Export Controls & Chip Policy
* **National Security vs. Revenue:** Former Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo stated that export controls must prioritize national security without putting a price tag on it, criticizing the current administration's reported approach of allowing chip sales in exchange for a 25% cut.
  * During her tenure, she worked closely with chipmakers—including Jensen Huang, Lisa Su, and Cristiano Amon ("Christian")—to set clear "rules of the road" while minimizing lost revenue.
* **Cracking Down on Compute Rental:** Raimondo noted that the administration issued new rules to block China from renting compute (such as accessing Blackwell chips in foreign data centers).
  * She expressed frustration that it took two years to finalize, noting she had started that work before leaving office.
* **Geopolitical Stakes for South Korea:** Hyundai CEO José Muñoz emphasized that South Korea is the US's "number one ally" and heavily reliant on chip supply for companies like Samsung and SK.
  * Hyundai is a major GPU consumer for its robotics and autonomous driving initiatives and hopes Nvidia's work continues smoothly regardless of leadership changes.

### Hyundai’s Physical AI Pivot & US Investment
* **From Automaker to "Physical AI" Company:** Muñoz described Hyundai as a tech and physical AI company, leveraging Boston Dynamics for robotics and Motional for autonomous driving.
* **Robotaxi Rollouts:**
  * **Waymo:** Hyundai is beginning to deliver robotaxis to Waymo this month, with plans to export significant volumes, including to right-hand drive markets.
  * **Uber & Motional:** Hyundai is launching a robotaxi service on Uber's app using Motional vehicles produced in Savannah, Georgia.
* **Massive US Capital Commitment:** Muñoz stated Hyundai is the largest American OEM by investment, committing **$26 billion** in the US over the next two years—compared to **$20.4 billion** invested over the previous 40 years.
  * He noted that a strong automotive foundation funds these new technologies, giving Hyundai a competitive edge against Chinese rivals.

### Preparing the Workforce for the AI Economy (RAISE)
* **The Weakest Link in AI:** Raimondo argued that the biggest threat to AI's future is not compute, talent, or energy, but a messy labor transition that puts millions out of work and triggers a regulatory backlash.
  * She pointed to an election two weeks prior where more than 45 data center moratoria were on the ballot, and all but one passed due to public anxiety over jobs.
* **RAISE Mission & Funding:** Raimondo is full-time CEO, chair, and founder of RAISE, a non-partisan non-profit that has raised **$750 million** ("three quarters of a billion dollars") to modernize US workforce systems.
  * **Corporate Supporters:** Working with dozens of companies, including Apple, AT&T, and Amazon; tech company funding accounts for less than half of the total, and donors have no board governance.
  * **Policy Reform:** Partnering with both Democratic and Republican governors to fix unemployment insurance—currently, laid-off workers lose benefits if they start a business or go back to school—and to incentivize corporate redeployment over "fire-rehire."
* **Personal Motivation & 2028:** Raimondo dismissed running for president in 2028, committing to RAISE and citing her childhood experience when her father's jewelry factory job was shipped to China.

### Corporate Governance & Navigating the Labor Transition
* **Job Creation Over Displacement:** Muñoz argued that robotics and autonomous driving empower workers rather than replace them, comparing the shift to the expansion of TV channels and streaming services increasing overall volume.
  * Hyundai is actively hiring higher-qualified technicians and engineers in places like Savannah to train and maintain robots.
* **Protecting the Workforce:** Muñoz highlighted that Hyundai's 330,000-person "family" did not lay off a single worker during COVID-19, instead adapting operations to protect employees.
* **Board Independence:** Addressing questions on corporate oversight, Muñoz noted that Hyundai maintains strict governance with at least 7 out of 12 board members serving as independent directors.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **US Investment:** Hyundai will invest $26 billion in the United States over the next two years — *José Muñoz*
* **Waymo Deliveries:** Hyundai will begin delivering robotaxis to Waymo this month, expanding into significant export volumes including right-hand drive markets — *José Muñoz*
* **AI Backlash Risk:** If the US fails to ensure a prosperous job transition and puts millions out of work, it will trigger an economic recession and regulatory backlash that will stop AI — *Gina Raimondo*
