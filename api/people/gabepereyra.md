## How Harvey Built a Research Lab on a Budget | Gabe Pereyra

### TL;DR
Application layer companies can compete with well-funded frontier labs by leveraging the "frontier ecosystem" rather than building everything from scratch. Gabe Pereyra, co-founder and president of Harvey, shared how Harvey built its own research lab on a budget using open-source models, neo-lab partnerships, domain-expert synthetic data, and robust production evaluation infrastructure.

### Benchmarks & Synthetic Data Generation
* **Open-Sourced Legal Benchmarks:** Harvey built and released three major domain-specific datasets to evaluate models and gather community feedback:
  * **Legal Agent Bench:** A taxonomy of tasks associates do at large law firms, covering complex multi-practice work like fund formation drafting and case law research.
  * **Contracting Dataset:** Teaches agents to handle in-house negotiation tasks.
  * **Diligence Dataset:** One of the largest RL environments released, featuring data rooms up to **80 million tokens** and **over 1,000 unit tests** grading outputs via LLM-as-a-judge.
* **Domain-Expert Synthetic Data Generation:** Because client data is privileged and cannot be used for training, Harvey uses internal lawyers (led by Gabe's brother, Julio) to "vibe code" rubrics and plant specific issues into synthetic data rooms.
* **Data Scaling Partners:** Harvey partners with companies like **Mercor** and **Snorkel** to scale up synthetic data generation into larger training sets.
* **Ecosystem Feedback:** Open-sourcing datasets allowed external developers and labs (such as those prompted by Elon Musk retweeting them) to test against them and provide pull requests.

### Post-Training via the Frontier Ecosystem
* **Shift to Open-Source Base Models:** Open-source models (such as **Kimi 3**, **GLM 5.2**, **NeMo-Megatron**, and **Inkling**) have reached a level where post-training is viable for specific enterprise tasks.
* **Neo-Lab Partnerships:** Harvey works with multiple neo-labs to leverage existing infrastructure, training recipes, and diverse research bets:
  * **Fireworks:** Trained GLM 5.1 to use Fable or Opus 4.8 as an advisor model.
  * **Base 10:** Collaborated on KB compaction.
  * **N-gram:** Partnered on enterprise search and firm knowledge.
  * **Trajectory:** Trained NeMo-Megatron models.
  * **Applied Compute:** Worked on Harvey's Vault product.
* **Internal Post-Training:** Harvey increasingly conducts its own post-training using APIs like **Tinker** and infrastructure from partners like **Fireworks** and **Base 10**, with the goal of building their own version of Cursor's Composer 1.

### Production Serving & Evaluation Infrastructure
* **Multi-Model Scale:** Harvey operates in **60 countries** across multiple product surfaces, requiring complex serving matrices, model fallbacks to hit SLAs, and routing open-source models (like GLM 5.2) into tasks like citation generation.
* **Pre-Production Gates:** Before deploying any model (post-trained or otherwise), Harvey screens via:
  * Automated lab benchmarks
  * Human side-by-side testing
  * Critical user journey tests and human product testing per product surface
* **Production Monitoring:** Deployed models are evaluated using AB testing, engagement tracking, uptime monitoring, token efficiency metrics, and product feedback ("angry customer emails").

### Product Strategy: Individual vs. Organizational Productivity
* **Vertical vs. Horizontal:** While horizontal AI tools focus on individual user productivity, Harvey focuses on organizational and team workflows across law firms and Fortune 500 enterprises.
* **Complex Orchestration:** Enterprise legal work requires coordinating teams of 20–30 people across 6-month projects, managing thousands of clients, resource allocation, and billing—areas where hyper-vertical enterprise software outperforms generic productivity assistants.
