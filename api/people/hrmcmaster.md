## Trump’s Former Advisors Predict How His War with Iran Ends (w/ H.R. McMaster & Matt Pottinger)

**TL;DR**
Former National Security advisors H.R. McMaster and Matt Pottinger argue that the Iranian regime is fundamentally unstable and nearing collapse, advocating for a "counter-blockade" strategy while warning that a severely downsized National Security Council (NSC) risks losing the "peripheral vision" necessary for long-term competition with China.

### NSC Structure and Presidential Decision-Making
* **Staffing Reductions:** The current NSC policy staff has been reduced to approximately 36 people, down from a peak of nearly 200 during the Biden administration and 108 at the end of the first Trump term.
    * **Strategic Risk:** Matt Pottinger notes that while a small staff prevents territorial disputes, it makes "technically difficult strategy"—such as AI and semiconductor competition—nearly impossible to manage from the Oval Office due to sheer complexity.
* **The "Deep State" Narrative:** H.R. McMaster suggests that because President Trump was convinced his own staff was the "Deep State," he took a "cleaver" to the NSC, leading to inconsistencies in policy communication and integration.
* **Guarding Presidential Judgment:** McMaster emphasizes the National Security Advisor’s role as the "guardian of the president’s independence," which requires presenting multiple, distinct options rather than a single "shiny option" preferred by the bureaucracy.
    * **Problem Framing:** McMaster utilizes a "design thinking" approach: framing the challenge, identifying assumptions, and listing obstacles before ever discussing specific actions.

### Iran: Conflict, Nuclear Ambitions, and Regime Change
* **Regime Stability:** McMaster predicts a **75% chance** that the Iranian regime will not survive the next two years, citing the murder of 40,000 of its own citizens and a lack of organized internal opposition.
* **Counter-Blockade Strategy:** Pottinger defends the use of a counter-blockade against Iranian oil exports.
    * **Economic Pinch:** The goal is to ensure Iran does not benefit from oil exports while they attempt to close the Strait of Hormuz, forcing the regime to feel the same economic pain as its neighbors.
* **The Nuclear Divide:** A fundamental disagreement exists between the former Trump advisors and the Sullivan/Finer team regarding Iranian intent.
    * **The Trump View:** McMaster and Pottinger believe Iran has been actively pursuing a nuclear arsenal for decades.
    * **The Biden/Intel View:** Sullivan and Finer argue Iran uses the program as "leverage" and "option-buying" but has not had an active weapons program since 2003.
* **Ground Operations:** Pottinger counsels extreme caution regarding U.S. ground troops, noting the "character of warfare" has changed post-2022, with 90% of casualties in modern conflict (like Ukraine) coming from drones. McMaster suggests "limited missions" to take key terrain/islands might be necessary, but full regime change would likely come from the Iranian people or internal military splinters.

### China and the "Detente" Trap
* **Calibrated Confrontation vs. Detente:** Pottinger describes current U.S. policy as a "detente," which he views as a strategic mistake. He argues for a return to "calibrated confrontation" to prevent Beijing from using the time to catch up in AI and military strength.
* **Technological Offset:** Pottinger expresses concern over rethinking export controls on AI chips. He believes AI must remain a "third offset" that provides the U.S. an absolute—not just relative—technological advantage.
* **Regional Interconnectivity:** McMaster argues that China is a "huge loser" if the Iranian regime falls. China’s influence in the Middle East is currently predicated on its perceived ability to "constrain" Iran; if the regime changes, Gulf states will likely lose patience with Beijing’s previous support for Tehran.
