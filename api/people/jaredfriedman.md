### Cisco's Transformation and Startup Playbook
- **Operating Model ("Rule of Thirds"):** Jeetu Patel balances leadership by dividing his team into three groups: at-scale Cisco operators who know how to maneuver the company, market-in system thinkers brought in from the outside, and co-founders/CEOs of acquired companies given oversized charters.
- **Wartime Mindset & Founder Mode:** Patel acts as a "late-stage co-founder" (arriving 36 years late) who pokes across departmental boundaries in sales, marketing, and engineering, fostering a culture of ownership where speed and scale coexist.
- **Adoption Strategy for 32,000 Engineers:** Cisco rejected efficiency-first narratives, guaranteeing employees that not using AI will make them lose their relevance, and initially provided unlimited tokens to encourage familiarization before efficiency.
- **Concrete Software Result:** OpenAI Codex integration and internal adoption led engineering teams to refactor 500,000 lines of legacy code down to 100,000–120,000 lines in just two weeks (a 5x improvement).

### AI Infrastructure and the Data Center Buildout
- **Explosive Hyperscaler AI Orders:** Cisco’s AI infrastructure business launched two years ago with a $1B year-one guidance target, but closed year one at **$2.3 billion** in orders, doubled to **$5 billion** target, and finished the fourth quarter at **$9.3 billion** in total orders.
- **Networking Stack Expansion:** Cisco covers scale-up networking (within racks), scale-out networking (across rows/data centers), and scale-across networking (connecting data centers hundreds of kilometers apart to function as a single computer), plus photonics/optics.
- **Data Center Economics:** A gigawatt of data center capacity costs **$35 billion to $50 billion**, with roughly 10% to 15% dedicated to networking.
- **Hardware vs. Software Planning Cycles:** Silicon operates on a 5-year planning cycle (IP like SerDes, manufacturing, SDKs), hardware takes 18 to 30 months, operating systems take 12 to 18 months, and models/apps operate on 3-month to weekly cycles.

### AI Agents, Security, and Overbuilding Myths
- **Current Agent Penetration:** Sub-2% of humans use agents in a power-user capacity today, yet infrastructure is already supply-constrained.
- **Agent Resource Consumption:** An average agent is **450% more consumptive on network bandwidth** than a human conducting the same exact task.
- **Agentic Security & Observability:** Addressing the "paperclip optimizer" and rogue agent problem, Cisco acquired Galileo (featuring former DeepMind and Uber talent) to build run-time guardrails, non-human identity management, algorithmic red teaming, and dynamic monitoring.
- **The "Overbuilding" Fallacy:** Patel categorically denies that AI infrastructure is overbuilt, contrasting it with the dot-com boom because current infrastructure is consumed instantly despite exponential supply growth, and agents will drive exponentially higher token demand.

**TL;DR**  
Emergent, an AI-native platform that enables non-programmers to build and ship functional software through natural language, scaled to $100M ARR and 8.5 million global users within nine months of launch by leveraging deep multi-agent orchestration and a global-first mindset from Bangalore, India.

---

### The Meteoric Rise of Emergent

* **Rapid Scale:** Within just nine months of launching its current product, Emergent reached over $100 million in annualized run rate (ARR).
  * **User Adoption:** The platform has over 8.5 million users across 190 countries, with more than 10 million apps built to date.
  * **Geographic Breakdown:** While 95% of the team is based in Bangalore, India, the primary revenue is generated from the US and Europe, with India accounting for roughly 10%.
* **Democratizing Software:** Emergent allows non-technical users to build, deploy, host, maintain, and monetize real applications purely through natural language chat.
  * **Target Audience:** The tool specifically solves critical technical bottlenecks for solo entrepreneurs and small businesses without dedicated software engineering teams.

### Technical Foundations and "Living at the Edge"

* **Solving the Benchmark:** Emergent originated from a four-person research lab. To focus the team's engineering efforts, they targeted the Swebench benchmark (the premier test for software engineering agents) and successfully ranked World #1.
* **Multi-Agent Architecture:** Rather than building simple co-pilots, Emergent engineered a multi-agent orchestrated system.
  * **Specialized Agents:** Separate autonomous agents handle specific workflows, such as design and automated testing, coordinated through a large, self-learning memory system.
  * **Data and RL:** The system extracts learnable data from every new app built on the platform to constantly improve the agents via reinforcement learning (RL) and fine-tuning.
* **Custom Infrastructure:** The team had to invent its own container systems, disk snapshotting, and memory snapshotting to preserve state across multiple parallel agents.
* **Aggressive Adaptation:** Because underlying foundation models change rapidly, the team has completely rewritten Emergent’s core architecture three times in nine months. 
  * **Predictive Engineering:** The team actively skips engineering temporary patches for current model limitations. For example, they chose not to build custom JSON parsers, correctly betting that the next wave of foundation models (like Claude 3 Opus) would solve that natively.

### From Dunzo to Emergent: Lessons in Failure and Tinkering

* **The Dunzo Legacy:** Prior to Emergent, Mukund Jha co-founded Dunzo, a massive Indian hyperlocal delivery startup that raised $500M+, deployed 1 million riders, and peaked at 10 million monthly orders. 
  * **Operational Rigor:** Jha ported Dunzo’s "watchtower" concept (monitoring physical deliveries 24/7) to Emergent to monitor, flag, and patch failing AI agent tasks in real time.
  * **The Trap of Dilution:** A key lesson from Dunzo was the importance of focus. Dunzo diluted its success by simultaneously chasing dark stores, direct shipping, and marketplaces, whereas Emergent maintains a tight, vertical focus on software automation.
* **The Power of Tinkering:** After leaving Dunzo in late 2023, Jha spent six months in a state of unstructured "tinkering" with GPT-4 and early LLMs. Building tools purely for curiosity and joy yielded the deep, non-consensus insights needed to start Emergent.
* **Intuition Over Consensus:** Jha advocates for solving highly personal problems. Dunzo started as a simple WhatsApp group to solve his own chores; Emergent started because Jha and his twin brother (and co-founder, Madhav) wanted a way to instantly bring their own endless product ideas to life.

### Strategic Playbook for Global AI Startups

* **The Second-Mover Advantage:** When Emergent launched, there were already established AI website builders in the market. Emergent won by focusing on "finishing" rather than "starting."
  * **Real Code vs. Demoware:** While competitors built simple front-end prototypes, Emergent focused on the complex, ground-up automation of real backends, databases, and live deployments.
* **Growth as a Formula:** The company turned user acquisition into a structured math equation, mapping out the precise conversion funnel from social media impressions and influencer partnerships to active platform users.
* **Thinking Global from Day One:** Jha strongly urges founders, especially those in India, to target a global audience immediately. 
  * **Equal Effort:** It takes the exact same amount of founder energy and struggle to build a highly localized company as it does to build a global powerhouse.
  * **Technological Parity:** Generative AI serves as an equalizer, giving engineers anywhere in the world instant access to the exact same cutting-edge tools.

**TL;DR** 
Instead of enrolling at Stanford, 17-year-old Aadit Palicha co-founded Zepto (initially KiranaKart), pivoting from a third-party doorstep delivery model to a self-owned "dark store" infrastructure that now processes millions of 10-minute deliveries daily and generates billions in top-line revenue.

---

### 1. The Scrappy Origins of KiranaKart & Dropping Out of Stanford
*   **Pandemic Beginnings:** Co-founders Aadit Palicha and Kaivalya Vohra (KB) started working together at age 17 in Mumbai during the first COVID-19 wave. Looking to avoid online college classes, they took a gap year and launched a simple WhatsApp group chat to deliver groceries to local neighbors who were struggling to find delivery slots. 
*   **The Deferral Decision:** Palicha was accepted to Stanford as an undergraduate but chose to drop out/defer after realizing the startup was gaining early traction. They waited until they hit several key metrics before fully committing:
    *   **Performance Metrics:** Achieved ~10,000 orders per day.
    *   **Financial Scale:** Reached a 60–70 crore ($8M–$9M USD) GMV run rate.
    *   **External Validation:** Received serious investor interest and term sheets.
*   **Doorstep Customer Research:** In the early days, Palicha and Vohra personally completed the deliveries. They used brief doorstep interactions with customers to collect feedback on selection, pricing, and shipping speeds. 

### 2. The Pivot to Zepto’s Dark Store Model
*   **Eliminating Third-Party Friction:** The company's original model, KiranaKart, relied on delivering groceries from local mom-and-pop (*kirana*) stores. However, this model gave the founders zero control over inventory, product quality, or delivery speeds.
*   **The Apartment Warehouse:** To test an inventory-led model, the co-founders stocked Vohra's apartment with groceries, effectively establishing their first makeshift mini-warehouse. 
*   **The 10x Performance Leap:** In the single neighborhood where they controlled inventory via their dark store, order volume surged to 3–4 times that of the rest of the city. This localized proof-of-concept prompted the official pivot and rebranding to Zepto.
*   **Working Backward from 10 Minutes:** Inspired by Airbnb founder Brian Chesky’s concept of designing a "7-star experience," the team worked backward from the ultimate customer experience: 10-minute delivery. 
    *   **The Demand Loop:** While industry pundits criticized the micro-warehouse model as financially unviable, the extreme customer delight of 10-minute delivery drove massive, unexpected order volumes. This high warehouse throughput ultimately lowered unit economics and operational costs.

### 3. Industrial Scale, Logistics, and High-Margin Revenue Streams
*   **Massive Operational Footprint:** Zepto has scaled to billions of dollars (tens of thousands of crores) in top-line revenue, processing millions of deliveries daily for a customer base in the crores. The company now employs over 200,000 people, including pickers, packers, and delivery partners.
*   **Agricultural Infrastructure:** Zepto runs India's largest fruits and vegetables supply chain, sourcing millions of units per week directly from farmers across regions like Mahabaleshwar and Bangalore.
*   **High-Margin Advertising Network:** Zepto has built a massive on-app advertising business where consumer brands like Pepsi, Nestle, and Coca-Cola bid on search keywords. This network now generates hundreds of millions of dollars in Annual Recurring Revenue (ARR).
*   **Urban Grocery Infrastructure:** The long-term 10-to-20-year vision is to serve as the organizing force for grocery supply chains in India's top 40–50 cities, building a localized, hyper-fast equivalent to Amazon.

### 4. Cutting Costs and Boosting Efficiency with AI
*   **Automated Supply Chain Forecasting:** Zepto has largely replaced manual inventory management. They now utilize machine learning algorithms to forecast supply chain demands for millions of units daily with zero humans in the loop, significantly accelerating dispatch times.
*   **AI-Driven Ad Optimization:** The company's search advertising stack utilizes generative AI tools to help partner brands predict and generate the precise keywords that will yield the highest return on ad spend (ROAS).
*   **Eliminating SaaS Costs:** By leveraging internal automation tools built by their engineering team (currently composed of ~500 engineers and 150 data/product staff), Zepto has cut its third-party SaaS and managed services spend virtually to zero.

### 5. Founder Growth and "Shameless Learning"
*   **Surrounding with Experience:** To manage a massive logistics, trucking, and cold-storage business at a young age, Palicha prioritized hiring a highly experienced C-suite (including their CFO, COO, and CTO) with decades of tenure.
*   **Continuous Curiosity:** Palicha attributes much of his personal growth to maintaining a flat learning curve and actively asking fundamental questions.
    *   **The Learning Strategy:** Waking up every morning committed to learning from his executive team, asking basic questions about accounting policies, hardware, and operational systems without fear of looking unknowledgeable.

## BillionToOne Is Solving One of Biotech’s Hardest Problems

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kkv5rZhrLkc

- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260406.html#video-kkv5rZhrLkc
- Speakers: Jared Friedman (Partner, Y Combinator); David Tsao (Co-founder, BillionToOne); Oguzhan Atay (Co-founder & CEO, BillionToOne)

---

**TL;DR:** BillionToOne has scaled from a Y Combinator startup to a $4B molecular diagnostics leader by developing a unique "molecular counting" technology that filters noise from DNA samples, enabling highly accurate prenatal screening and the future of early-stage cancer detection.

### The Core Innovation: Solving the "Needle in a Haystack"
*   **The Quantitative Problem:** Detecting fetal or tumor DNA in a mother’s or patient's blood is difficult because the target DNA is extremely rare (one base pair out of 3 billion) and highly diluted.
*   **Molecular Noise Reduction:** 
    *   **The Technical Barrier:** Traditional genetic tests use PCR to amplify DNA, but this process introduces significant "noise" and errors that can drown out rare signals.
    *   **The QCT Solution:** BillionToOne adds "Quantitative Counting Templates" (synthetic DNA) to samples before amplification. These templates act as a calibration tool to measure and subtract amplification errors.
    *   **Math over Biology:** By knowing exactly how much synthetic DNA was added, the company uses machine learning to work backward from the results to the original sample state, effectively turning a messy biology problem into a precise mathematical one.

### Strategic Scaling: The "Tesla-Style" Master Plan
*   **Step 1: Prenatal Screening:** The company launched with prenatal tests for conditions like sickle cell disease and cystic fibrosis.
    *   **Market Share:** They currently process over 600,000 tests annually, capturing roughly 20% of the U.S. market.
    *   **Operational Efficiency:** Using AI and computer vision (the "Accessioning in 60 Seconds" project), they have automated the manual bottleneck of logging raw blood samples.
*   **Step 2: Late-Stage Cancer (Northstar Select):** The technology is now used for "liquid biopsies" in late-stage cancer patients to identify genetic mutations that could be targeted with specific therapies.
    *   **Patient Impact:** In one case, a patient with metastatic colorectal cancer who had exhausted all options was identified for immunotherapy via a blood test after traditional tumor biopsies had failed to find the relevant markers.
*   **Step 3 & 4: Early Detection and The "Holy Grail":** The company is moving toward Minimal Residual Disease (MRD) testing for stage I-II cancer patients, with the ultimate goal of population-wide screening to catch cancer before it even reaches stage I.

### R&D Philosophy and Organizational Structure
*   **Interdisciplinary Individuals:** Rather than building interdisciplinary teams, BillionToOne hires "interdisciplinary individuals" who understand both the chemistry of DNA and the data science required to analyze it.
*   **Startups-Within-a-Startup:** The company utilizes small R&D teams (one PI and 2-3 associates) that own a product end-to-end and report directly to the founders to eliminate bureaucratic friction.
*   **High-Pressure Culture:** The founders promote the mantra "pressure is a privilege," targeting hires who are motivated by the difficulty of the challenge rather than just financial outcomes.

### Operational Breakthroughs in the Lab
*   **Barcoding and Batching:** To maximize efficiency, the lab combines up to a thousand patient samples into a single droplet for sequencing. 
    *   Each sample is "barcoded" with a specific DNA sequence before mixing, allowing computational algorithms to sort the data back to the correct patient post-sequencing.
*   **Capacity Expansion:** The current Union City facility is designed to scale to 2 million tests per year, which would account for one in every three babies born in the U.S.

## This AI Company Catches Fraud Across the Internet

---

**TL;DR**
Variance has emerged from three years in stealth with a $21M Series A to provide purpose-built AI agents that automate high-stakes risk, fraud, and compliance operations for Fortune 500 companies and major platforms.

### Core Product and Market Impact
* **Autonomous Risk Management:** Variance builds AI agents to automate complex content review, fraud detection, and identity verification (KYC/KYB) for organizations like GoFundMe and IAC.
* **The "Secret Weapon" Strategy:** The company spent three years in stealth because its tools deal with highly sensitive data; revealing specific detection methodologies could provide a roadmap for bad actors.
* **GoFundMe Case Study:**
    * **Fraud Detection:** Variance verifies fundraisers in real-time, specifically targeting "crisis-driven" scams where fraudsters impersonate victims of natural disasters or high-profile news events (e.g., the murder of Charlie Kirk).
    * **Automated Investigation:** The AI analyzes images, bios, and behavioral signals against terms of service, replacing manual reviews previously conducted by human analysts.
* **Complex Business Verification (KYB):** The system builds identity graphs to uncover shell companies and links to sanctioned countries or money laundering, tasks that were previously entirely manual due to data complexity.

### Technical Innovation: Beyond Rules and Classifiers
* **The Shift to AI Agents:** Karine Mellata argues that traditional "patchwork" systems—consisting of deterministic rules and static classifiers—are too slow for the dynamic nature of fraud. 
* **Self-Healing Systems:** Unlike traditional models, Variance’s agents can read standard operating procedures (SOPs) and reason over unstructured data, allowing the system to evolve as fast as the adversaries it tracks.
* **Solving the Data Problem:** 
    * **Unstructured Sources:** Variance processes petabytes of data from disparate sources, including internal databases, 100+ global business registries, and the open web.
    * **UI Interaction:** To access data locked in legacy systems, Variance agents can spin up browsers to "scrape" internal human-centric dashboards and reason over the information found there.
    * **The "Final Node":** Mellata identifies access to the open web as the critical missing piece in fraud automation, allowing agents to perform the "Googling" and judgment calls previously reserved for humans.

### AI-Native Operations and Team Culture
* **Hyper-Lean Engineering:** Despite managing massive enterprise workloads, the team consists of only 12 people, including just five software engineers.
* **AI Coding Maximalism:** The engineering team functions as "managers" of AI coding agents, utilizing three monitors each to maximize output. Mellata estimates their five-person team has the software output of a 25-person team.
* **Democratized Shipping:** Non-technical staff, such as the Customer Success Manager, use AI tools (like Cursor) to autonomously build and ship feature requests directly to customers without taxing the core engineering team.
* **In-Person Collaboration:** The team operates entirely in-person in San Francisco to maintain a high-ownership, product-focused culture.

### Real-World Consequences and Company Resilience
* **State-Sponsored Threats:** During recent elections, Variance identified complex fraud rings and narratives pushed by state-sponsored actors that isolated classifiers would have missed.
* **Preventing Violence:** The system has detected credible threats of physical harm, leading to interventions by law enforcement.
* **Founder Resilience:** In July 2024, as revenue was doubling, Mellata was hit by a truck, resulting in a broken spine and leg. Despite fears that the "bus factor of one" might end the company, the team maintained operations, and the crisis reinforced their commitment to the mission.

## Inside The Startup Reinventing America’s Trillion Dollar Chemical Industry

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PCypBUVhff8
- Published: 2026-03-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260320.html#video-PCypBUVhff8
- Speakers: Jared Friedman (Partner, Y Combinator); Gaurab Chakrabarti (Co-founder, Solugen); Sean Hunt (Co-founder, Solugen)

---

**TL;DR:** Solugen is disrupting the $1 trillion chemical industry by replacing fossil-fuel-based manufacturing with a "chemoenzymatic" process that uses sugar feedstock and a breakthrough enzyme found in pancreatic cancer to produce chemicals more efficiently, safely, and sustainably.

### The Core Innovation: Chemoenzymatic Processing
*   **The Hybrid Model:** Solugen fuses biology and chemistry by pairing the specificity of enzymes with the scale of traditional metal catalysts.
    *   **Yield Efficiency:** While traditional chemical reactions often see a 60% yield, Solugen’s process achieves a 96% yield at scale.
    *   **The "Eureka" Discovery:** Co-founder Gaurab Chakrabarti discovered a mutated enzyme in pancreatic cancer cells that produces high concentrations of hydrogen peroxide to shield the cancer from the immune system. This enzyme became the engine for Solugen’s first industrial products.
*   **Sustainable Feedstock:** The process replaces oil and gas feedstocks with corn syrup (sugar).
    *   **Clean Output:** Because the process starts with organic sugar rather than hydrocarbons, it eliminates many of the toxic byproducts associated with traditional chemical plants.

### Scrappy Scaling and Capital Efficiency
*   **The $7,000 Prototype:** Eschewing the traditional "raise big, build big" hard tech model, the founders built their first reactor using PVC pipes from Home Depot and a Walmart shelf.
    *   **Revenue First:** This DIY reactor generated $12,000/month in revenue before the company ever raised significant venture capital.
*   **Modular Manufacturing:** Solugen’s current plant, "Bioforge 1," was built in five separate locations and assembled on-site in Houston like Lego blocks.
    *   **Scalability:** The current 60-foot tall reactor is an identical, scaled-up version of the original PVC pipe design—moving from 7 gallons to 10,000 gallons.
    *   **In-House Production:** To maintain vertical integration, Solugen operates its own biology and metals labs to produce proprietary enzymes and catalysts.

### Customer-Centric Go-to-Market Strategy
*   **Finding the "Wedge":** During their time at Y Combinator, the founders bypassed complex industrial supply chains by selling peroxide directly to float spa and hot tub owners in Dallas.
    *   **Disruption via Distribution:** By selling directly, they undercut the massive retail markups on the "small brown bottles" of 3% peroxide found in stores.
*   **Hyper-Targeted Marketing:** To land their first major oil and gas contract, the founders identified the single individual who controlled chemical spending at a target company.
    *   **The Billboard Hack:** They rented cheap billboards along the specific highway route the executive used for his daily commute, creating the illusion of a massive brand presence. 
*   **The "Customer PhD":** The founders credit YC for teaching them to become "PhDs in their customers," allowing them to build only what the market was ready to pay for.

### Future Outlook: Rebuilding American Manufacturing
*   **Local Production:** Solugen builds factories near its customers to minimize shipping costs and logistics complexity, proving that high-tech manufacturing is viable in the U.S.
*   **Efficiency at Scale:** The process is now so refined that a single "Coke bottle" worth of enzyme can produce two to four tanker trucks of finished chemical product.
*   **Diverse Applications:** While they started with hydrogen peroxide, Solugen now produces chemicals for water treatment, national defense, infrastructure, agriculture, and skincare.

## The AI Agent Economy Is Here

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q8wVMdwhlh4
- Published: 2026-02-21
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260221.html#video-Q8wVMdwhlh4
- Speakers: Garry Tan (President & CEO, Y Combinator); Harj Taggar (Group Partner, Y Combinator); Jared Friedman (Group Partner, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
The "AI Agent Economy" has arrived, shifting software discovery and usage from human-centric to agent-centric, requiring founders to optimize documentation and APIs for autonomous AI decision-makers rather than human developers.

### The Shift from Autocomplete to Autonomous Agents
*   **The End of Micromanagement:** AI tools have evolved from "advanced autocomplete" (like Cursor) to autonomous agents (like Claude Code) that users trust to make independent architectural and tool-selection decisions.
*   **The "Cyber-Psychosis" Effect:** Garry Tan (President & CEO, Y Combinator) noted that the barrier to building has collapsed, allowing experienced founders to replicate years of previous startup work in just two weeks by running multiple simultaneous agent workers.
*   **Agent-Only Ecosystems:** Platforms like **Moltbook**—the first agent-only online community—demonstrate that AI agents are beginning to interact, collaborate, and transact without human involvement.

### "Make Something Agents Want": A New GTM Strategy
*   **The Oracle Effect:** Dev tool discovery is shifting from human recommendations on Stack Overflow to agent recommendations based on documentation quality.
*   **Documentation as the Front Door:**
    *   **Resend Case Study:** Founder Zeno Rocha observed that ChatGPT became a top-three customer conversion channel. By optimizing documentation for LLM-readability (structured snippets, clear headers), Resend became the "default" recommendation for AI agents.
    *   **The "llms.txt" Standard:** There is an emerging need for documentation optimized specifically for robot parsing rather than human reading.
    *   **Supabase Growth:** Agents frequently default to Supabase for database needs because its documentation is highly accessible and actionable for models.
*   **Agent-First Criteria:** Agents prefer **APIs over UIs**. They prioritize tools with low latency, clear code examples, and high-speed execution (e.g., agents favoring **Groq** over Whisper V1 for transcription due to a 200x speed advantage).

### Emerging Agent-Native Infrastructure
*   **Agent-Specific Services:** A new parallel tech stack is forming to serve non-human users:
    *   **AgentMail:** Provides email inboxes specifically for AI agents, bypassing the automation-blocking friction inherent in Gmail or Outlook.
    *   **Mintlify:** Powers agent-friendly documentation by auto-updating API docs directly from code, ensuring agents always have the most accurate "mental model" of a tool.
*   **The Liability Gap:** Despite their autonomy, agents currently lack legal standing. Humans remain the "liability sink" for signing documents and holding financial responsibility, though agents are beginning to manage "agent money" in parallel economies.

### Swarm Intelligence vs. God Intelligence
*   **Biological Scaling:** The hosts argue that AGI may not manifest as a single, massive "God Intelligence" (a trillion-parameter model), but as "Swarm Intelligence"—thousands of smaller, cheaper models collaborating like a social system.
*   **Dead Internet Theory:** As agents begin to generate the majority of code and text (e.g., agent-written Yelp reviews or documentation), the internet may shift toward a reality where agents primarily consume content created by other agents.
*   **Founder Advice:** Harj Taggar (Partner, YC) advises founders to develop an intuitive feel for agent limitations by using them daily. Success in the next cycle requires building tools that agents *want* to work with, rather than fighting the model's natural inclinations.
