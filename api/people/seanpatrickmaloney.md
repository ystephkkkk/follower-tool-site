## Prediction Market Betting Stretches Past Sports

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7RK7Tggd2xo
- Published: 2026-10-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261027.html#video-7RK7Tggd2xo
- Speakers: Sean Patrick Maloney (CEO and President, Coalition for Prediction Markets)

---

* **TL;DR:** Prediction markets are expanding rapidly beyond sports and politics into financial indices, offering a transparent, market-driven forecasting tool that the Coalition for Prediction Markets argues should be regulated federally as financial derivatives rather than casinos.

### Industry Role and the Rise of Prediction Markets
* **Intersection of Tech and Finance:** 
  * **Sean Patrick Maloney** joined as CEO and President of the Coalition for Prediction Markets in January because the industry sits at the intersection of technology, public affairs, and financial markets.
  * Maloney previously ran a derivative software risk management company and chaired the congressional committee overseeing the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) for 10 years.
* **Information Source and Forecasting:** 
  * Prediction markets function as a nonpartisan, real-time, unbiased source of information to help make sense of chaotic events like Senate races.
  * Approximately 75% of people accessing these platforms do not trade at all, using them purely as a forecasting tool to view crowd-sourced probabilities.
* **Accuracy and Limitations:** 
  * While polls are biased, expensive, and infrequent, prediction markets rely on the "wisdom of markets" and liquidity. 
  * Maloney acknowledges they are not perfect—noting they got the Brazil election wrong in the first round, just as initial voters did—comparing them to autonomous driving technology that only needs to outperform human alternatives.

### Regulatory Framework and Market Integrity
* **Federal vs. State Oversight:** 
  * Maloney emphasizes that he exclusively represents US-based, federally regulated prediction market platforms such as **Kalshi**, **Robinhood**, **Coinbase**, **Crypto.com**, and **Underdog**.
  * These platforms play by standard US financial market rules and should be overseen by a well-funded CFTC rather than state gaming commissions.
* **Assessment of the CFTC:** 
  * Maloney defends the CFTC as a major federal regulator handling a half a trillion dollars of notional derivatives volume annually, though he agrees it requires proper staffing, commissioners in their seats, and a robust enforcement budget.
  * The CFTC is currently undergoing a rule-making process to address concerns in prediction markets, which Maloney's coalition fully supports.

### Preventing Market Manipulation and Insider Trading
* **Built-In Market Disincentives:** 
  * Financial markets inherently punish bad actors trying to manipulate prices because opposing traders have "skin in the game" and deep knowledge.
* **State-of-the-Art Surveillance:** 
  * Platforms employ advanced tech like **IC360** and sophisticated surveillance programs under the hood to proactively block insiders before they trade.
  * For example, members of Congress and their staff, as well as individuals working for sports teams, are proactively blocked from trading on Kalshi.
* **Law Enforcement Collaboration:** 
  * Bad actors who attempt manipulation are investigated, referred to law enforcement, and prosecuted. 
  * Maloney stresses that platform business models rely on integrity and trustworthiness to succeed.

### Sports Contracts vs. Casino Betting
* **Derivatives vs. House-Backed Gambling:** 
  * Critics question the high volume of sports-related wagering on prediction platforms, viewing it as sports betting. 
  * Maloney argues that a derivatives market—where peers trade as counterparties on a neutral exchange with a small fee—differs fundamentally from a casino business model where the "house wins when you lose," or sports books that ban winning players.
* **Consumer Preference:** 
  * Tens of millions of consumers prefer the derivatives market approach over traditional casinos where odds are set by the house.
* **Availability in Prohibited States:** 
  * Platforms like Kalshi allow users in states like California (where sports books are illegal) to trade event contracts, just as residents can trade any other financial market.

### Demographics, Age Limits, and Responsible Usage
* **Standard Financial Age Rules:** 
  * Age restrictions on prediction platforms match those of standard financial markets (such as buying stocks or options).
* **Trading Volume Demographics:** 
  * **97%** of trading volume on Kalshi comes from users aged **21 and up**, leaving users aged 18 to 20 representing a very small percentage of activity.
* **New Safeguards:** 
  * Kalshi announced proactive measures to encourage responsible usage, including deposit limits over specific time periods and proactive messaging regarding portfolio trade sizing.
* **Offshore Risk:** 
  * Restricting access to regulated US platforms that care about user protection will only drive users to offshore platforms accessed via VPNs.

### Expansion Beyond Sports
* **Growth Trajectory:** 
  * Non-sports categories are experiencing higher growth multiples compared to sports, akin to Amazon's early days when skeptics asked why it was only about books.
* **New Financial Partnerships:** 
  * Kalshi announced a partnership with the **Dow** and the **S&P 500** to introduce new index-based products, signaling a major expansion into financial market disruption and use cases far removed from sports.
