As public AI benchmarks saturate and self-reported model scores become increasingly unreliable, independent and continuously evolving evaluations are becoming critical for both enterprises measuring ROI and governments assessing safety risks.

### The Problem with Current AI Benchmarks
* **Saturation and Goodhart’s Law:** Public benchmarks are easily hacked and saturated as models optimize directly for the tests.
* **Flawed Self-Reporting:** Rayan Krishnan noted that when Meta released Llama 4, public open-source benchmarks showed incredible capabilities, but VALS's held-out private benchmarks revealed the model was actually underperforming.
* **The Need for Neutrality:** Rayan Krishnan explained that third-party evaluators must avoid conflicts of interest, drawing parallels to the Enron auditing scandal where mixing consulting with auditing corrupted incentives. ### Enterprise ROI and the Shift to Token Spend
* **Escalating Costs:** Ben Horowitz noted the challenge of measuring fluid frontier intelligence compared to traditional Gartner quadrants. Rayan Krishnan shared an example from a Fortune 10 company where an engineer's budget increased from $100 to $300 per day, causing peak productivity to shift to 4:00 PM–6:00 PM when rate limits reset.
* **Token Spend vs. Salaries:** Rayan Krishnan recounted a VALS internal experiment where a token-maxing test resulted in spending $1.5 million in a month on free tokens—which was 10x higher than employee salaries for that month.
* **Val Smith Enterprise Product:** Rayan Krishnan introduced Val Smith, a tool allowing companies to take their GitHub codebase and build internal coding benchmarks to determine the most performant and Pareto-optimal models for their specific repositories.

### Pre-Release Infrastructure and Evolving Testing
* **The 6-Hour Pre-Release Window:** Rayan Krishnan described how VALS went from co-founders pulling all-nighters to running distributed evaluations at maximum rate limits using an internal system called "Steve, the economic VALS employee."
* **Handling Complex Workflows:** Rayan Krishnan noted that as evaluations become more complex (testing agentic work over hours, days, or weeks), sample sizes shrink while rubrics and criteria grow significantly larger (e.g., testing 50 full-stack web applications rather than millions of static images).
* **Always a Higher Peak:** Rayan Krishnan outlined VALS's unofficial motto and practice of deprecating saturated benchmarks and perpetually constructing new mountains for foundation model labs to summit.

### Specialized Indexes and Frontier Metrics
* **Economic Applications:** Rayan Krishnan highlighted VALS's finance agent benchmark (used by major financial institutions) and VI codebench for full-stack web application generation.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** Rayan Krishnan discussed the newly released RSI Index, which creates an apples-to-apples way to benchmark pre-training, post-training, and harness-level engineering proxies across frontier labs.

### Policy, Government Coordination, and Sovereign AI
* **Division of Labor:** Ben Horowitz compared the AI policy challenge to the MPAA rating system ("I know it when I see it"), arguing that governments are well-suited to set and enforce rules, while competent private companies must evaluate whether those rules are broken.
* **Information Sharing:** Rayan Krishnan stated that VALS regularly briefs executive and legislative branches on model capabilities and risks, helping policymakers ground abstract conversations in empirical data.
* **Global Verification:** Rayan Krishnan discussed the paradox of sovereign AI investments and suggested that international coordination on catastrophic risks (like cyber, bio, and RSI) will eventually require a shared language of evaluations, comparing it to Reagan's "trust but verify" doctrine for nuclear stockpiles.

* **Exploding Token Consumption:** The volume of tokens consumed is scaling by orders of magnitude due to the transition from simple chat applications to reasoning models, long-running agents, and multi-agent workflows.

### Physical Limits and Rebuilding the Computing Stack
* **Power and Cooling Constraints:** Rack power requirements are surging from roughly 5–10 kilowatts to 100–150 kilowatts, making traditional AC power obsolete and forcing a rapid transition to liquid cooling and DC power.
* **Data Center Obsolescence:** Traditional data center designs are reaching physical limits regarding weight loads, acoustic output, water efficiency, and high-voltage electrical infrastructure (noting that only 2% of US electricians are certified on DC power).
* **First-Principles Innovation:** Existing infrastructure was built for a different computing era, requiring founders to break down compute, memory, networking, and power from first principles and rebuild the stack.

### The Shift in Venture Capital and Founder Dynamics
* **Surge in Hard Tech Founders:** The share of top-tier founders pursuing complex hardware and systems problems has risen dramatically from roughly 3% historically to north of 20%–30%.
* **Capital-Driven Scaling:** Unlike traditional software where throwing money at engineering fails due to the "mythical man-month," AI allows capital ($3B+ clusters) to directly drive intelligence and compress competitive leads.
* **Experienced Leadership:** Complex supply chains and manufacturing requirements are drawing more experienced founders and systems architects into the hardware space alongside younger software talent.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Token Demand Growth:** Demand for tokens will grow at close to 1,000% per year, far outpacing the physical rate at which supply can be expanded — Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, and Raghu Raghuram.
* **5-to-10 Year Vision:** In 5 to 10 years, America will win the infrastructure game, featuring an abundance of efficient, eco-friendly data centers, chips, memory, and power, securing technological leadership — Ben Horowitz.

### The Strategic Shift to Global Tech Leadership and Western Values

* **The Imperative of US Tech Leadership:** Ben Horowitz emphasizes that preserving America’s status as a land of opportunity requires dominant leadership across economic, cultural, military, and technological spheres.

---

### Technology as the Arena of National Power and Security

* **Deterrence Shift to Software Innovation:** Anne Neuberger notes that national deterrence is no longer defined strictly by the physical size of a military, but by the pace of software innovation. 
    * **Private Sector Dominance:** Critical defense capabilities—including autonomous systems, AI integration, and cyber defense—are now built in the private sector rather than inside government laboratories.
* **Geopolitical Chokepoints:** Technology has shifted from a tool of diplomacy to the actual arena of national power. Semiconductor supply chains, active pharmaceutical ingredients, and critical software vulnerabilities are now leveraged as foundational economic and national security tools.
* **The Evolution of Cybersecurity Threats:** Cybersecurity has transitioned through three distinct historical phases:
    1. *Vandalism:* Early desktop computer disruption.
    2. *Theft:* Financial crimes and ransomware enabled by the early commercial internet.
    3. *Warfare:* State-level attacks targeting critical physical infrastructure (e.g., rural healthcare networks, power grids, and water systems).
    * **Tackling Legacy Code:** A 20-year legacy of insecure, pre-AI code must be modernized, a task where defensive AI models are proving highly effective.

---

### A New Playbook for Startup Internationalization

* **Accelerated Global Footprints:** Raghu Raghuram explains that because digital products travel instantly via APIs, startups can no longer wait until they have hundreds of millions in revenue to go international; they must scale globally much earlier.
* **Targeting Top-Heavy Economies:** Rather than spending $5M to $10M upfront to establish localized legal entities and hire large regional teams, startups should leverage relationships with the small handful of top corporations and government entities that drive the majority of GDP in target countries.
    * **Case Study (TelevisaUnivision & ElevenLabs):** Mexican media giant TelevisaUnivision partnered with portfolio company ElevenLabs to dub its Spanish-language content into local dialects worldwide with authentic voice intonations, securing them a major distribution deal with Netflix.
    * **Case Study (Flow in Saudi Arabia):** a16z facilitated market entry for Flow (Adam Neumann’s real estate company) in Riyadh, directly connecting them to the government and business leaders driving Saudi Arabia’s modernization.
* **Key Geographies of Focus:** 
    * **Indo-Pacific (Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Australia):** Japan is rapidly modernizing its military posture to deter regional aggression, seeking private-sector partnerships in autonomous shipping and AI-driven battlespace integration.
    * **The Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Israel):** These regions are heavily investing in AI infrastructure and entrepreneurial ecosystems to transition from resource-based to innovation-driven economies.
    * **Mexico:** Strengthening Western Hemisphere supply chains and border tech integration is vital for both Mexican stability and US national security.

---

### The Myth of a "Distributed" Silicon Valley

* **Why Physical Ecosystems Still Matter:** Ben Horowitz disputes the notion that the internet has democratized innovation to the point of distributing Silicon Valley everywhere. He argues that local policies, cultures, and laws remain highly divergent and determinative of success.
* **The Three Non-Negotiable Ingredients for Innovation Hubs:**
    * **Technical Talent:** Proximity to elite technical universities that graduate skilled builders.
    * **Favorable Regulatory Frameworks:** Flexible labor laws (easy hiring/firing), streamlined company creation, and protective tax codes. Punitive tax models—such as Norway’s tax on unrealized capital gains—actively stifle local tech ecosystems.
    * **Cultural Status:** A society that structurally and culturally celebrates entrepreneurship, wealth creation, and high-risk ambition.
