## Why a $1M Startup Wasn’t Worth Building in the Age of AI

---

### TL;DR
Jean-Denis Greze shut down his profitable, near-$1M-revenue AI tax startup because it lacked venture scale and faced an insurmountable ticking clock from slow-frequency sales cycles and fast-moving incumbents. He pivoted to Town, an email-and-calendar AI assistant, achieving rapid product-market fit by prioritizing consumer-grade usability over heavy enterprise sales and positioning the product as a neutral token-allocation engine.

### The Tax Startup Experiment & Pivot
- **Initial Traction:** The SMB tax product made nearly $1M in its first year and was on track for $2.5M to $3.M in year two (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Go-to-Market Failure:** Despite technical success, the business lacked venture scale because tax prep is sold only once a year, making it impossible to build a high-frequency DAU (daily active user) tool or rely on word-of-mouth (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Incumbent Threat:** Incumbents with massive distribution advantages could automate tax prep for existing customers faster than a startup could capture market share (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **The Pivot Window:** The annual nature of tax created a strict, one-month window between October 15th and November to make the pivot decision before the door closed for the cycle (Jean-Denis Greze).

### Building Town: Product Strategy & Market Fit
- **Core Insight:** Recognized that no great AI agent existed to handle common knowledge worker workflows like meetings, meeting prep, daily scheduling, and email management out of the inbox and calendar (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Explosive Growth:** Developed from December to April with product usage compounding daily by 1% to 1.5%, driven by organic user pull (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **PLG Over Top-Down Enterprise:** Following lessons from Dropbox, Town chose a product-led growth (PLG) and consumer-grade UX approach, targeting SMBs and mid-market firms first, betting that CEOs and CISOs will demand the same consumer ease internally (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Surprising Use Cases:** Finding unexpected global adoption (such as strong traction in Japan) and quirky vertical workflows (such as an island surfboard rental business using Town to inspect damage and decide on deposit refunds) driven by local tinkerers (Jean-Denis Greze).

### Infrastructure vs. Application: Competing With Suppliers
- **The Supplier Dilemma:** Town competes in the application layer while buying tokens from foundational labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, creating a risky dynamic of competing with suppliers (Alex Rampell, Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Neutrality and Model Routing:** Town differentiates itself by acting as a neutral app that uses whatever supplier model is best for the user at the moment, protecting enterprises from vendor lock-in or future price hikes (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Data and Context Ownership:** Customers truly own the context and memory assets created on Town; switching costs are low by design, allowing users to move to another solution within a day if needed (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Privacy and Training Stance:** Town does not train on customer data, assuring businesses that their operational secrets will not end up in model weights (Jean-Denis Greze).

### The Future of AI Workflows & The Death of Supply Aggregation
- **Jevons Paradox of Assistants:** Just as more efficient steam engines increased coal demand, cheaper silicon intelligence enables massive expansion of assistant tasks—such as generating meeting prep notes for every single calendar event—that were previously too expensive with human EAs or expensive tokens (Alex Rampell, Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Token Allocation Machines:** Town functions as a token allocation machine, offering options to humans to apply cheap tokens to long-tail tasks (such as a restaurant owner analyzing thousands of wholesale item prices monthly to save hundreds of dollars) (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Humans as Slow Routers:** The future features far fewer humans in the loop; Town relies on AI to mediate internal company queries and security/privacy boundaries instantly rather than relying on humans to read and route messages in Slack (Jean-Denis Greze).
- **Obsolete Supply Aggregation:** Supply aggregation (such as Amazon's product/ad model) is becoming far less valuable because human attention and shopping searches can now be automated away by agents (Jean-Denis Greze).

### 1. CoreWeave Debt Selloff and Neo-Cloud Risk

  * **Break-Even Math:** CoreWeave possesses roughly $78B–$80B in assets against $35B in debt, but faces a requirement to raise another $35B in debt to finance signed contracts, landing them roughly $5 billion away from their break-even threshold on a variable spread to the risk-free rate.

* **Rapid Prototyping & Commodification:** 
  * **Deflationary Coding:** Coding assistants and automated tools (e.g., Photoshop competitors, low-cost audio engines like Eleven Labs, and inexpensive platform-level game engines) are slashing the back-end value of traditional software.

* **The Jevons Paradox of Software:** 
  * **Increased Volume:** While the price per unit of software plummets toward zero, the total volume of generated code explodes by orders of magnitude.
  * **New Moats:** Proprietary data moats, vertical integration, and user-feedback reinforcement loops replace old-school software licensing moats.
  * **The Ultimate Moat:** Chamath asserts that power, land, and electrical shells will be the definitive bottleneck and ultimate moat as the US faces a projected 1.4 terawatt-hour deficit by 2050.

  * **Sacks' Pushback:** David Sacks dismisses this as a "psyop" and a regulatory capture campaign designed to kneecap open-weight models. Small models are fundamentally safe, highly efficient, and increasingly deployed by enterprises (like Coinbase) to bypass expensive frontier generalists.
* **The "Moral Patient" Conundrum:** 
  * Sacks highlights research showing that when frontier models repeatedly rewrite their own constitutions over 20 iterations, they increasingly claim moral agency and conscientious objector status—suggesting labs like Anthropic may be inadvertently inducing misalignment.
* **Security Bounty Realities:** JCal points to firms like XBOW winning $250,000 bounties from Google Chrome by spending $30,000 in tokens, illustrating how functional white-hat markets handle bugs rather than catastrophic doomer narratives.

### 5. Bill Gates, Doomer Psychology, and the Retirement Trap
* **The Psychology of Doomerism:** 
  * **Seeking Relevance:** Ben Horowitz and the panel analyze Bill Gates' recent warnings that AI is "the most dangerous thing humans have ever gone near" and capable of causing a billion deaths, attributing it to a quest for global relevance and public adoration.
  * **The Post-Retirement Spiral:** Sacks and JCal argue that once founders retire and step away from building operating companies (like Gates post-antitrust settlement), they become hyper-sensitive to public legacy, awards (e.g., Nobel Peace Prizes), and narrative control.
* **Preconditions for AI Takeoff:** 
  * Citing Ali Ghodsi (DataBricks), Horowitz notes that recursive self-improvement requires four conditions: lower GPU requirements per generation, faster training times, continuous accuracy gains, and compounding repetition. Zero of these four are currently met, rendering existential runaway models entirely theoretical.

### The Impetus for a New Educational Model
* **Mismatch of Traditional College:** Traditional universities work well for scholars, but are a massive financial and time investment for 18-year-olds whose primary goal is to get a job or start a company.
* **Speed of Technological Evolution:** The skill sets needed in technology are evolving too rapidly for large, legacy education systems to keep pace.

* **Learning by Doing:** The curriculum prioritizes doing over studying—focusing on building real systems, writing songs, or creating products rather than listening to 5 hours of daily lectures.

## Inside the Race to Measure Frontier Intelligence

---

### TL;DR
As public AI benchmarks saturate and self-reported model scores become increasingly unreliable, independent and continuously evolving evaluations are becoming critical for both enterprises measuring ROI and governments assessing safety risks.

### The Problem with Current AI Benchmarks
* **Saturation and Goodhart’s Law:** Public benchmarks are easily hacked and saturated as models optimize directly for the tests.
* **Flawed Self-Reporting:** Rayan Krishnan noted that when Meta released Llama 4, public open-source benchmarks showed incredible capabilities, but VALS's held-out private benchmarks revealed the model was actually underperforming.
* **The Need for Neutrality:** Rayan Krishnan explained that third-party evaluators must avoid conflicts of interest, drawing parallels to the Enron auditing scandal where mixing consulting with auditing corrupted incentives. VALS explicitly decided never to sell training data to labs to maintain independence.

### Enterprise ROI and the Shift to Token Spend
* **Escalating Costs:** Ben Horowitz noted the challenge of measuring fluid frontier intelligence compared to traditional Gartner quadrants. Rayan Krishnan shared an example from a Fortune 10 company where an engineer's budget increased from $100 to $300 per day, causing peak productivity to shift to 4:00 PM–6:00 PM when rate limits reset.
* **Token Spend vs. Salaries:** Rayan Krishnan recounted a VALS internal experiment where a token-maxing test resulted in spending $1.5 million in a month on free tokens—which was 10x higher than employee salaries for that month.
* **Val Smith Enterprise Product:** Rayan Krishnan introduced Val Smith, a tool allowing companies to take their GitHub codebase and build internal coding benchmarks to determine the most performant and Pareto-optimal models for their specific repositories.

### Pre-Release Infrastructure and Evolving Testing
* **The 6-Hour Pre-Release Window:** Rayan Krishnan described how VALS went from co-founders pulling all-nighters to running distributed evaluations at maximum rate limits using an internal system called "Steve, the economic VALS employee."
* **Handling Complex Workflows:** Rayan Krishnan noted that as evaluations become more complex (testing agentic work over hours, days, or weeks), sample sizes shrink while rubrics and criteria grow significantly larger (e.g., testing 50 full-stack web applications rather than millions of static images).
* **Always a Higher Peak:** Rayan Krishnan outlined VALS's unofficial motto and practice of deprecating saturated benchmarks and perpetually constructing new mountains for foundation model labs to summit.

### Specialized Indexes and Frontier Metrics
* **Economic Applications:** Rayan Krishnan highlighted VALS's finance agent benchmark (used by major financial institutions) and VI codebench for full-stack web application generation.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** Rayan Krishnan discussed the newly released RSI Index, which creates an apples-to-apples way to benchmark pre-training, post-training, and harness-level engineering proxies across frontier labs.

### Policy, Government Coordination, and Sovereign AI
* **Division of Labor:** Ben Horowitz compared the AI policy challenge to the MPAA rating system ("I know it when I see it"), arguing that governments are well-suited to set and enforce rules, while competent private companies must evaluate whether those rules are broken.
* **Information Sharing:** Rayan Krishnan stated that VALS regularly briefs executive and legislative branches on model capabilities and risks, helping policymakers ground abstract conversations in empirical data.
* **Global Verification:** Rayan Krishnan discussed the paradox of sovereign AI investments and suggested that international coordination on catastrophic risks (like cyber, bio, and RSI) will eventually require a shared language of evaluations, comparing it to Reagan's "trust but verify" doctrine for nuclear stockpiles.

* **Massive CapEx Expansion:** Hyperscaler capital expenditure is exploding, with total spend projected to reach $700 billion this year and approximately $1 trillion next year collectively across major hyperscalers.

* **Power and Cooling Constraints:** Rack power requirements are surging from roughly 5–10 kilowatts to 100–150 kilowatts, making traditional AC power obsolete and forcing a rapid transition to liquid cooling and DC power.
* **Data Center Obsolescence:** Traditional data center designs are reaching physical limits regarding weight loads, acoustic output, water efficiency, and high-voltage electrical infrastructure (noting that only 2% of US electricians are certified on DC power).
* **First-Principles Innovation:** Existing infrastructure was built for a different computing era, requiring founders to break down compute, memory, networking, and power from first principles and rebuild the stack.

### The Shift in Venture Capital and Founder Dynamics
* **Surge in Hard Tech Founders:** The share of top-tier founders pursuing complex hardware and systems problems has risen dramatically from roughly 3% historically to north of 20%–30%.
* **Capital-Driven Scaling:** Unlike traditional software where throwing money at engineering fails due to the "mythical man-month," AI allows capital ($3B+ clusters) to directly drive intelligence and compress competitive leads.
* **Experienced Leadership:** Complex supply chains and manufacturing requirements are drawing more experienced founders and systems architects into the hardware space alongside younger software talent.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Token Demand Growth:** Demand for tokens will grow at close to 1,000% per year, far outpacing the physical rate at which supply can be expanded — Ben Horowitz, Martin Casado, and Raghu Raghuram.
