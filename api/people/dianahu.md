### TL;DR
Google Chief Scientist Jeff Dean and YC’s Diana Hu discuss AI progress, hardware breakthroughs, inference scaling, and why small teams can win by focusing on context engineering and niche problem domains.

### The State of AI & Junior Engineers
- **Agent-Based Coding Capability:** Models are now performing well on longer-running, agent-based coding tasks, effectively operating at the level of a junior engineer.
- **Underestimated Rate of Progress:** Complex task execution and agent application outside of coding have accelerated faster than initially predicted.
- **2027 Outlook — Automated ML Improvements:** Future systems will feature fully automated problem decomposition and experimentation loops, allowing ML systems (and other scientific fields) to continuously improve their own capabilities against measurable objectives.

### Hardware, Energy, and the "Inference Moment"
- **The 2001 Search Analogy:** In 2001, Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat realized Google's search index fit in RAM and shipped a RAM-based search version in days. The 2026 equivalent is high-performance, low-energy **inference hardware** that drastically cuts latency (e.g., 50x better).
- **The TPU Origin Story (2013 Napkin Math):** 
  - Realizing that 3 minutes of daily voice recognition per Google user would double the server fleet, Dean and Sanjay built the TPU—specialized for low-precision dense linear algebra.
  - The resulting chip was **30 to 80 times more energy-efficient** and **20 to 30 times lower latency** than contemporary CPUs and GPUs.
- **The Data Movement Energy Gap:** Moving data from accelerator memory (HBM) to the processor costs **1,000x more energy** than performing the actual computation. This hardware reality dictates why batching is necessary.

### Context Engineering & Long-Running Agents
- **Context vs. Training Data:** The model is only one piece of an overall system. Context engineering (retrieval, tool use, memory) provides clear information directly to the model, unlike trillions of training tokens stirred into a parameter soup.
- **Long-Running Agents:** Agents can now run for days or weeks on complex tasks (e.g., rewriting software in a new programming language for better safety or performance).
- **Overcoming Agent Rail Failures:** 
  - Multi-agent systems use inference-time compute to search through multiple potential solutions and evaluate the most promising paths.
  - Providing explicit guidelines, skills, and performance hints (such as Dean and Sanjay's published 30-page "Performance Hints" document) helps keep models on successful execution paths.

### Startup Opportunities & Where 2-3 People Can Win
- **Targeting 0% Success Rates:** Founders should look for problems where current general models succeed 0% to 1% of the time, rather than 20% (which indicates general capabilities are rapidly encroaching).
- **Privileged Data & Niche Models:** 
  - Build around personal/private data that general models cannot access.
  - Build specialized niche models (similar to AlphaFold for protein folding) for domains like material science or chip design.
- **The Power of Good Taste:** As AI automates code writing, the scarce, high-value skill is having great taste and wisdom regarding *what* problem to work on.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Jeff Dean (2027 Outlook):** ML systems will extensively automate their own capability improvements by running large-scale automated experimentation loops by 2027.

## Boris Cherny: Building Claude Code

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qyPCVqFUyDo
- Published: 2026-07-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260727.html#video-qyPCVqFUyDo
- Speakers: Boris Cherny (Creator, Claude Code); Diana Hu (Moderator, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Claude Code creator Boris Cherny announced the launch of Opus 5, highlighting its extended execution capabilities, near-zero prompt-injectability, and massive 80% system prompt reduction. Cherny emphasized that building with modern AI requires an empirical mindset—regularly "pressing delete" on legacy prompts and harnesses to unhobble models for complex, multi-week agentic workflows.

### Opus 5 Capabilities & Alignment
- **Extended Autonomous Execution:** Unlike previous models, Opus 5 can run continuously for days, weeks, or months without stopping, even without complex scaffolding like SLoG or Sloop.
- **Elimination of Prompt Injection:** By combining three layers—three years of alignment research, a prompt injection classifier based on Chris Olah's mechanistic interpretability work (detecting specific neurons lighting up), and an auto mode classifier—Opus 5 has become essentially prompt-injectable-free (a milestone achieved progressively since Opus 4.7/4.8 and Sonnet 5).
- **Vision and Computer Use:** Opus 5 represents a massive leap in vision and computer use, demonstrated by spontaneous capabilities such as drawing complex portraits, animals, and landscapes using OpenCV without explicit training.

### The "Press Delete" Product Strategy
- **System Prompt Ablation:** For every new model release, Anthropic deletes large portions of Claude Code's system prompt and tools, then rebuilds them line by line to determine what the new model actually requires.
- **Simple Mode Ablation:** Claude Code includes an undocumented feature (`CLAUDE_CODE_SIMPLE=1`) that deletes all system prompts from both the product and its tools; tests show the model is often slightly more intelligent without these legacy instructions.
- **Actionable Advice for Founders:** Cherny recommends that developers and founders using Claude Code delete their `CLAUDE.md`, skills, and hooks every six months to test what the model can do natively without legacy instructions.

### Unhobbling Models & Product Overhang
- **Product Overhang Defined:** The gap between what a model is currently capable of doing and what existing products allow it to do; scaffolding and rigid product design often "hobble" the model by getting in its way.
- **Giving Harder Tasks:** Users commonly make the mistake of giving overly specific, step-by-step instructions (e.g., "must do 1, then 2, then 3"); modern models perform better when given high-level tasks, guardrails, and exit criteria.
- **Real-World Examples of Scale:** 
  - The Bun team used Claude to rewrite an entire JavaScript runtime codebase from Zig to Rust in a single 11-day dynamic workflow prompt (saving over a year of engineering effort on a 100,000+ line codebase).
  - Cherny launched a personal experiment to rewrite the Electron desktop app in Swift inside a Mac virtual machine, having Claude screenshot and compare pixels pixel-by-pixel; the agent has been running autonomously for over two weeks.

### Dynamic Workflows & Autonomous Maintenance
- **Test-Time Compute Scaling:** Dynamic workflows function as an "algebra for agents" (running agents in sequence or parallel within a Bun runtime sandbox) to orchestrate dozens, hundreds, or thousands of agents for complex tasks.
- **Loops and Routines:** Local cron jobs (loops) and cloud-running tasks (routines) allow Claude to maintain Anthropic's own codebases across iOS, Android, desktop, and CLI apps.
- **Automated Codebase Maintenance:** Routine prompts run daily across Anthropic to handle dead code cleanup, feature flag removal, test coverage expansion, removal of obsolete tests, and unifying duplicated code abstractions via the "abstraction police" routine, operating hundreds to thousands of agentic tasks daily.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Boris Cherny** claims that routine agent maintenance will soon fully automate the maintenance of software applications, enabling teams to delegate thousands of daily agentic tasks.

### Notable Quotes
> "Every time that a new model comes out, we delete a bunch of the system prompt. Change a bunch of the system prompt. We change the set of tools all the time... The reason is every model is very different."
> — Boris Cherny

> "Forget all the things that you learn about past models. Forget everything that you learned about computer science theory in class. Look at the model, try to do a task, see where it struggles, and then based on that adjust."
> — Boris Cherny


## How To Build A Company With AI From The Ground Up

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EN7frwQIbKc
- Published: 2026-04-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260424.html#video-EN7frwQIbKc
- Speakers: Diana Hu (Partner, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
AI is evolving from a productivity tool into the fundamental operating system of the startup, enabling "closed-loop" organizations that replace human middle management with a queryable intelligence layer. By prioritizing "tokenmaxxing" (API spend) over headcount, AI-native founders can achieve 1,000x output compared to traditional competitors.

### AI as the Company Operating System
*   **From Productivity to Capability:** AI shouldn't be framed as a mere "copilot" for existing workflows. Instead, it represents a shift toward entirely new capabilities where a single person can build features that previously required an entire team.
*   **The Intelligence Layer:** Founders should treat AI as the central operating system. Every decision, workflow, and process must flow through an intelligent layer that constantly learns and self-improves.
*   **Closed-Loop Systems:** Unlike traditional "open-loop" companies—where decisions are made without systematic feedback—AI-native companies run as closed loops. These systems continuously monitor outputs and adjust internal processes to meet stated goals with higher correctness and stability.

### Creating a Queryable, Artifact-Rich Organization
*   **Total Legibility:** To function as a closed loop, the entire organization must be "queryable" or legible to AI. This requires capturing every important action as a digital artifact.
    *   **Tactical Implementation:** Use AI note-takers for all meetings, minimize private DMs and emails in favor of public channels, and embed agents across all communication streams.
*   **Automated Management:** When a company is queryable, agents can replace manual coordination.
    *   **Engineering Sprints:** AI agents with access to tickets (Linear), Slack, customer feedback (Pylon), and GitHub can analyze past performance and propose future plans more accurately than human managers.
    *   **Performance Gains:** Teams adopting these practices have reported cutting engineering sprint times in half while achieving 10x the output.

### The AI Software Factory and the 1,000x Engineer
*   **Evolution of Development:** The highest-velocity companies are moving toward "AI Software Factories," an evolution of Test-Driven Development (TDD). 
    *   **Human Role:** Humans define the spec and the success tests.
    *   **AI Role:** Agents generate the implementation and iterate until the tests pass.
*   **Removing Handwritten Code:** Some advanced teams, such as the AI group at **StrongDM**, have reached a point where repositories contain zero handwritten code—only specs and test harnesses. 
*   **The 1,000x Engineer:** By surrounding a single engineer with a system of agents, founders can finally realize the "1,000x engineer" (a concept popularized by Steve Yegge), enabling individuals to build products that were previously impossible.

### Structural Shifts: Eliminating Human Middleware
*   **Dissolving Hierarchies:** Traditional middle management exists to route information between layers. In an AI-native company, the intelligence layer handles this routing, making human middleware unnecessary and eliminating information loss.
*   **The Three New Archetypes:** Following the model discussed by **Jack Dorsey (Block)**, AI-native companies will consist of three specific roles:
    1.  **Individual Contributors (ICs):** Builders across all functions (Eng, Ops, Support) who use prototypes instead of pitch decks.
    2.  **Directly Responsible Individuals (DRIs):** Leaders focused solely on strategy and customer outcomes rather than people management.
    3.  **AI Founders:** Leaders who build and lead by example, refusing to delegate their AI strategy to subordinates.
*   **Tokenmaxxing vs. Headcount:** Success will be measured by maximizing token usage rather than hiring. Founders should be comfortable with "uncomfortably high" API bills because they replace the far higher costs of an inflated headcount.

### The Startup Advantage
*   **The Legacy Debt Trap:** Incumbents face "standard operating procedures" and entrenched hierarchies that are difficult to unwind. Every attempt to go AI-native risks breaking a working legacy product.
*   **The Clean Slate:** Early-stage founders have a massive edge because they can build queryable systems and agent-driven workflows from day one without the need for retraining or reorganization.
*   **Developing Conviction:** Founders cannot outsource their AI strategy. Diana Hu emphasizes that founders must personally spend time with coding agents and tools to break their own priors about what is possible.

> "The way to think about AI is that it should not be a tool your company just uses. It should be the operating system your company runs on." — **Diana Hu**

> "Maximizing token usage, not headcount, will be the critical shift. The best companies will be the ones that are tokenmaxxing." — **Diana Hu**

> "The company itself has to be rebuilt as an intelligence layer, with humans at the edge guiding it rather than routing information through it." — **Diana Hu (referencing Jack Dorsey)**
