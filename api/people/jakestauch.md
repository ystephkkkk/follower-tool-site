## Rebuilding IT From the Ground Up for the AI Age: Serval's Jake Stauch

**TL;DR** 
Serval is rebuilding enterprise service management for the AI era by using code-generation to automate workflows and database management. By utilizing a dual-agent architecture that balances AI autonomy with enterprise security, Serval aims to eliminate repetitive IT tasks and unlock meaningful work for employees.

---

### Redefining Enterprise Service Management
* **The Workflow Primitives:** Serval maintains the core abstraction popularized by legacy systems like ServiceNow—workflows on top of databases—but replaces manual, slow development with AI-driven building and maintenance.
* **Overcoming Automation Friction:** Building traditional enterprise workflows often takes weeks or months, meaning automation lags behind rapid business changes. 
* **The "Simpler to Automate" Rule:** To drive adoption, the process of building an automation must be simpler than performing the manual task. 
    * *Example:* If an IT admin is faced with manually resetting a Google Workspace password versus spending hours configuring a workflow builder, they will choose the manual task. Serval uses natural language code-generation to make workflow creation instant and effortless.
* **Managing "Slop Automation":** When creating automations becomes frictionless, organizations risk generating duplicate or redundant workflows. Serval manages this through a specialized agent with full contextual awareness that helps admins prune, categorize, and merge duplicate workflows.

### Dual-Agent Architecture and Model Strategy
* **The Enterprise Security Moat:** While foundation models offer nearly unlimited reasoning capabilities, enterprises require strict guardrails. Serval’s architecture separates capabilities into two distinct agents to balance autonomy with control:
    * **The Admin Agent:** Uses code-generation to spin up backend tools, permissions, and database syncs based on natural language instructions.
    * **The Help Desk Agent:** Interacts directly with end-users but can *only* execute tools explicitly approved and published by the Admin Agent.
* **Model Customization:** Serval splits workloads between leading foundation models based on performance strengths:
    * **OpenAI Models:** Power user-facing interactions, tool calling, and conversational logic.
    * **Anthropic Models (Sonnet/Opus):** Best suited for backend code-generation (TypeScript) and workflow scripting.
* **The Upgrade and Rollback Dilemma:** Upgrading to new model releases is rarely plug-and-play. Because Serval builds extensive prompt tuning and infrastructure around known model quirks, new model releases can break existing guardrails. Serval actively automates eval suites and has occasionally downgraded models in production to preserve reliability and predictability.
* **Highly Favorable Unit Economics:** Unlike simple "wrappers" that resell model tokens, Serval's code-gen architecture generates TypeScript code once. Subsequent requests execute the local code rather than calling expensive foundation model APIs, keeping margin profiles strong.

### Operating an AI-Native Enterprise
* **The "Fewer, Better" Hiring Mantra:** In an era where products may need to be entirely rebuilt every six months to keep pace with shifting AI paradigms, Jake Stauch argues that talent density is a startup's only durable moat. A small, elite team can pivot and adapt overnight, whereas a large organization cannot.
* **The AI Right of First Refusal:** Serval evaluates every internal operational role for automation before making a human hire. 
    * *No SDRs or SEs:* The company employs no Sales Development Representatives or Solutions Engineers. Sales representatives use Serval to instantly generate battle cards, custom decks, and technical comparisons on the fly during customer calls.
* **High-Empathy Customer Feedback Loops:** Rather than relying purely on quantitative metrics, Jake remains personally embedded in all 100 customer Slack channels. This rapid feedback loop allows forward-deployed engineers to push iterative product improvements daily, a process Jake calls "gradient descent for product development."
* **Flat Organizational Structure:** Serval operates with an incredibly flat hierarchy. The company deliberately eschews rigid career paths, traditional org charts, and formal corporate mentorship programs in favor of high-autonomy, day-one productivity.
