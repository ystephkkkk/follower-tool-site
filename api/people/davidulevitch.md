## How Founders Can Build for Law Enforcement and First Responders | The a16z Show

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ie07zcmbnhM
- Published: 2026-05-18
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260518.html#video-Ie07zcmbnhM
- Speakers: David Ulevitch (General Partner, a16z); Jeffrey Glover (Director, Arizona Department of Public Safety); Rahul Sidhu (Co-founder and CEO, CRADL)

---

**TL;DR**
Public safety is undergoing a paradigm shift toward a data-driven "ecosystem" where autonomous drones, AI-powered sensor networks, and officer wellness analytics are replacing traditional, reactive policing models to solve staffing shortages and improve de-escalation.

### The Shift to Autonomous First Response
The integration of drones and sensors is transforming how departments handle high-risk situations by providing immediate, low-cost situational awareness.

*   **Drone-as-First-Responder (DFR):** Rahul Sidhu (Flock Safety) notes that drones act as flying robots capable of automating tasks like chasing Amber Alert vehicles or pursuing shooters detected by gunshot sensors. 
    *   **Sustainability:** Replacing traditional helicopters with drones is more cost-effective; maintaining 24/7 aerial coverage with helicopters is financially impossible for most departments.
*   **De-escalation via Intelligence:** David Ulevitch cites an example where a drone identified a "man with a shotgun" as actually being a "janitor with a broom," preventing a potentially violent police confrontation through superior situational awareness.
*   **Layered Sensor Networks:** Flock Safety utilizes a combination of license plate readers (LPR), gunshot detection, and drone dispatch to create a proactive safety net.

### AI and Officer Wellness
Colonel Jeffrey Glover (Arizona DPS) highlights a shift toward using technology to support the "human" element of policing, specifically focusing on mental health and performance optimization.

*   **Brain Scan Diagnostics:** Arizona DPS utilizes **Vytania (Heal the Heroes)** to conduct brain scans as "temperature checks" for officers to assess mental readiness.
*   **Body-Worn Camera Analytics:** The department uses **Truleo** to run analytics on body-cam footage.
    *   **Behavioral Scorecards:** The tech tracks how troopers interact with the public and vice versa.
    *   **Burnout Detection:** The system flags signs of officer burnout or high-stress levels, allowing supervisors to intervene before a crisis occurs.
*   **Structural Support:** Based on tech-driven burnout data, the department has instituted well-checks and sabbaticals at the 15-year and 25-year marks to maintain job satisfaction.

### Global Intelligence and Large-Scale Events
As global events increasingly have local repercussions, law enforcement is expanding its intelligence apparatus beyond local borders.

*   **Fusion Centers:** The **Arizona Counter Terrorism Information Center (ACTIC)** collaborates with other U.S. fusion centers to share unclassified data on global crime trends.
*   **International Partnerships:** Colonel Glover is building intelligence-sharing bridges with nations including Mexico (Sonora), the UAE, and Liberia to stay ahead of international criminal trends.
*   **Major Event Readiness:** Departments are currently utilizing the **TRX program** to prepare for high-security upcoming events like the FIFA World Cup and the Olympics.

### Advice for Public Safety Founders
Building for law enforcement requires a high degree of empathy and a willingness to navigate a culture that is famously resistant to change.

*   **The "Dual Hate" Paradox:** Rahul Sidhu notes that "Cops hate two things: for things to change and for things to stay the same." Founders must navigate this narrow path by building tools that feel "inevitable."
*   **Immersion is Mandatory:** To build effective tools, founders must conduct ride-alongs or serve as reserve officers. Understanding the "language" and the feeling of being on the beat is critical for product-market fit.
*   **The 10-Year Outlook:** Colonel Glover predicts that within a decade, the officer skill set will shift from physical tactics (e.g., "kicking in doors") to more nuanced, investigative work focused on AI-driven fraud and technical video analysis.

---

**TL;DR**
Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) is positioning its record $15B fundraise as a strategic bet on American technological dominance, arguing that venture capital must actively support national interests and the U.S. government to ensure the next century of global progress.

### The Strategic Necessity of American Technology
*   **The AI Revolution:** Ben Horowitz emphasizes that just as the Industrial Revolution established American influence, winning the AI revolution is critical for maintaining economic, military, and cultural leadership.
*   **Irreplaceable Systems:** The U.S. system is unique because its foundational freedoms (e.g., free speech) are viewed as "self-evident" and not granted by the state, making them harder to revoke than in other nations.
*   **The Power of Opportunity:** A core tenet of "American Dynamism" is the country's unique ability to give individuals a "chance" to contribute, a trait Horowitz argues is rare globally but essential for human advancement.

### National Security and the Moral Role of Founders
*   **Technological Integration:** While China was initially faster at integrating AI into government and military bureaucracy, the U.S. is rapidly catching up due to a surge in entrepreneurial interest and government flexibility.
*   **The Ethics of Defense:** Horowitz rejects "dime-store morality" regarding military contracts, arguing that tech leaders have a moral obligation to provide the best tools to those risking their lives for the country. 
    *   **The Anthropic "Kerfuffle":** He suggests the recent high-profile deal collapse between Anthropic and the Department of War was likely a tactical business withdrawal rather than a deep ethical conflict.
*   **State Department Alignment:** Founders are cautioned against playing "vibe geopolitics." Horowitz argues that private companies should not presume to know better than the State Department or intelligence agencies regarding global security interests.

### The Evolutionary Shift in Venture Capital
*   **The Scale Problem:** The historical VC model was built to find ~15 companies per year. Because "software is eating the world," almost every interesting new company is now a tech company, requiring VC firms to scale significantly.
*   **Organizational Structure:** Scaling a firm to 600+ people requires centralized leadership rather than traditional "shared control" partnerships.
    *   **Reorganization:** Traditional firms struggle to adapt because individual partners with voting rights often block necessary structural changes.
*   **The Squeezed Middle:** Horowitz predicts the industry will bifurcate into "large-scale firms" (like a16z) that cover all sectors and "hyper-specialized firms" (e.g., AI infra, Bio, Crypto). Generalist firms in the middle are expected to struggle.

### Building a Global Allied Ecosystem
*   **Exporting Dynamism:** While the U.S. remains the primary hub for entrepreneurship, a16z is looking to leverage the specific strengths of allies:
    *   **Mexico:** High-quality manufacturing expertise to counter Chinese supply chains.
    *   **Japan:** Leading the world in robotics and industrial automation.
*   **Narrative and Optimism:** There is a concerning "optimism gap" where 70% of people in China are positive about AI, compared to only 30% in America. 
    *   **The Goal:** The U.S. needs to shift the conversation from "AI overlords" to practical outcomes like ending traffic deaths, curing cancer, and eliminating poverty.

> "When you're a leader in an industry, then the whole industry, the size of it, the ethics of it, the morality of it, kind of depends on you." — **Ben Horowitz**

> "You're going to decide that those people who are sacrificing their lives, putting their lives on the line to protect us, don't get the best technology? I'm not doing that." — **Ben Horowitz**

**TL;DR**
David Ulevitch, head of Andreessen Horowitz’s $1.776 billion "American Dynamism" fund, details a strategic shift in Silicon Valley toward "heavy" industries—defense, energy, and manufacturing—where advanced software can disrupt legacy government contractors. The core thesis posits that private venture capital can solve national security and infrastructure challenges more efficiently than traditional government-funded R&D by applying Silicon Valley’s high-speed, high-automation "bits meet atoms" approach.

### The "American Dynamism" Investment Thesis
*   **Target Sectors:** The fund focuses on categories serving the national interest, including defense, aerospace, energy resilience, supply chain, public safety, and domestic manufacturing.
*   **"Bits Meet Atoms":** Ulevitch defines the strategy as investing in the intersection of advanced software and physical hardware. 
    *   **Technological Shift:** The goal is to replace expensive, "exquisite" hardware with commodity hardware driven by sophisticated software (e.g., using phone optics and software to replace $25,000 vacuum-tube night vision systems).
*   **Market Durability:** While "capital-light" SaaS models are facing devaluation due to AI, Ulevitch argues that hardware-heavy businesses with high automation and verticalized supply chains offer high-margin, unconstrained upside.

### Disrupting the Defense Industrial Base
*   **Critique of Legacy Primes:** Ulevitch argues that major incumbents (the "Primes") are disincentivized to move fast or lower costs because their profit margins are often baked into "cost-plus" contracts where going over budget increases total profit.
*   **Economic Asymmetry:** Current military engagements highlight a cost crisis; the U.S. is using multi-million dollar Patriot missiles to intercept $250,000 Iranian-style drones. 
    *   **Startup Solution:** Startups like Anduril focus on high-rate, low-cost production using the "Elon philosophy" where the factory itself is the primary product.
*   **Procurement Shift:** Ulevitch advocates for a model where the government stops funding endless R&D and instead buys finished commercial products that meet specific capability specs, shifting the R&D risk to venture capitalists.

### AI, Ethics, and the "God Complex"
*   **The OpenAI/Anthropic Conflict:** Addressing the controversy regarding AI companies working with the Pentagon, Ulevitch views the choice as a "sorting algorithm" for talent.
*   **Opposition to "Extrajudicial" Decisions:** Ulevitch criticizes CEOs who refuse to sell to the government based on personal moral objections, calling it a "god complex." 
    *   **The Argument:** Private citizens lack the classified intelligence (satellite imagery, wiretaps) necessary to make better tactical decisions than the chain of command. 
    *   **Smart Weapons:** He argues AI will make weapons "smarter," allowing them to distinguish between a school bus and a tank more accurately than humans, thereby reducing collateral damage.

### Energy Abundance and Infrastructure
*   **Nuclear Renaissance:** The fund is heavily invested in nuclear energy (e.g., Radiant), viewing it as the cleanest and safest technology for meeting the "insatiable thirst" for power driven by AI data centers and EVs.
*   **Regulatory Acceleration:** AI is being used to drastically reduce the time needed to process complex regulatory and transport permits for nuclear facilities.
*   **The Venn Diagram of Security:** Ulevitch argues that energy, space, and defense are now inseparable; without domestic energy abundance and space-based communications, national security is impossible.

### Scaling Venture Capital to Trillion-Dollar Outcomes
*   **Outcome Potential:** Ulevitch pushes back against critics who say VC doesn't scale to heavy industry, citing SpaceX as a potential trillion-dollar outcome.
*   **The Capital Stack:** While VCs take the early equity risk, these companies eventually utilize private credit and real estate financing to build massive factories and data centers.
*   **Public Safety and Surveillance:** Defending investments like Flock Safety (license plate readers), Ulevitch argues that the benefit of solving crimes outweighs privacy concerns, noting that most privacy violations occur via data brokers and cell phones rather than transparent public safety tools.

> "The American Dynamism fund is a fund... focused on investing in companies that we say serve the national interest... it is where atoms meet bits, which historically has not been in vogue for the venture capital industry." — **David Ulevitch**

> "I actually think [the decision to work with government] is a sorting algorithm... there’s other people that say, 'we elect leaders, the leaders make laws, there’s lots of oversight... and I trust that democratic system.'" — **David Ulevitch**

> "If I had to bet on which end of the barbell will get to very high rates of production more quickly, is it the legacy incumbent primes who lack the institutional knowledge or appetite to move very, very fast, or the startups who have just survived an entire period of life of not dying? I would make that bet every single day of the week." — **David Ulevitch**

### The "American Dynamism" Investment Thesis
* **National Interest Focus:** Katherine Boyle and David Ulevitch lead a practice area dedicated to companies supporting the national interest, specifically in aerospace, defense, energy, transportation, and manufacturing.
* **Capitalism vs. Charity:** Boyle emphasizes that this is not "charity work." For a defense industrial base to be lasting and enduring, companies must be profitable, self-sufficient, and attract top-tier talent via the capitalist system.
* **The SpaceX Blueprint:** SpaceX is cited as the foundational success story that proved the private sector could build physical-world technology faster and more efficiently than traditional incumbents.
* **Strategic Fund Growth:** The practice recently raised a $1.176 billion fund, marking a transition from a contrarian "lone crew" philosophy to a cornerstone of the firm’s long-term strategy.

### A Generational and Cultural Pivot in Silicon Valley
* **The Shift from "Bits to Atoms":** Following COVID-19 and the Russian invasion of Ukraine, there has been a sea change in founder interest. Engineers are moving away from optimizing "ad clicks" toward solving physical reality problems.
* **The "Zoomer" Work Ethic:** Boyle identifies a distinct cultural shift in Gen Z founders compared to Millennials.
    * **Resilience:** She describes the younger generation as "not afraid of hard work" and motivated by the "struggle" of building difficult physical products.
    * **Inspiration:** Younger founders view figures like Elon Musk (SpaceX) and Alex Karp (Palantir) as the primary archetypes of success, rather than the "Harvard dorm room" app-builder model.
* **Apolitical Patriotism:** While many founders may not be explicitly political, there is a growing "bottom-up" desire to build things that make America functional and resilient.

### Manufacturing and the AI Renaissance
* **Reshoring Industry:** Ulevitch argues the U.S. is in a "transformation moment," reversing 40 years of offshoring. This is driven by the need for supply chain stability discovered during COVID-era interruptions.
* **AI as a Labor Multiplier:** AI and automation are not viewed as job killers but as solutions to the national talent crisis. 
    * **Lowering Barriers:** In companies like Hadrian, automation allows workers without years of apprenticeship to perform high-level machining.
    * **Efficiency:** Advanced software allows for more robust, high-margin, and high-output production that can compete globally without solely relying on protective tariffs.
* **Interdependency of Sectors:** The partners argue that national security, space dominance, and energy independence are now "linked at the hip"—one cannot succeed without the others.

### Operational Strategy and the Future of Work
* **Navigating Washington D.C.:** a16z maintains a massive presence in D.C. to assist portfolio companies with regulatory hurdles and government procurement.
* **Modernizing Government Procurement:** Ulevitch notes a shift in government leadership, citing tech-sector veterans like Emil Michael (CTO at the Department of Defense) who are pushing to let the best technology win rather than favoring legacy incumbents.
* **Remote Work Debate:**
    * **Builders:** Ulevitch believes founders and builders need to be in-person to brainstorm and iterate on physical products.
    * **Investors/Flexibility:** Boyle advocates for the broader benefits of remote work, noting its critical role in supporting family formation and increasing birth rates by eliminating commutes for parents.
