### Modernizing Space and Satellite Regulation
* **Shift to "Assembly Line" Processing:** The FCC is moving away from "individualized, bespoke" reviews of satellite applications toward a streamlined process based on objective criteria.
    * **Backlog Reduction:** The commission has already cut the satellite approval backlog by approximately 50%.

    * **Regulatory Role:** The FCC’s oversight includes spectrum access and managing orbital debris risks in conjunction with other agencies.
* **Direct-to-Cell Technology:** The FCC is establishing a framework for satellite-to-handset connectivity.
    * **Key Players:** Carr noted the agency is facilitating spectrum access for Starlink, Amazon, and AST SpaceMobile to ensure the U.S. leads globally in this sector.

### Spectrum Strategy and Wireless Competition
* **New "Super Band" for 5G/6G:** The FCC is voting this month to create 160MHz of new mid-band spectrum in the C-band.
    * **Goal:** This will create a "super band" 440MHz wide to support advanced wireless offerings.
* **Ending the "Four-Carrier" Mandate:** Carr dismissed the previous regulatory mindset that insisted on four standalone national wireless providers. 
    * **Market Stability:** He argued that despite Dish/EchoStar effectively exiting the mobile wireless market, consumer prices have not risen and speeds have increased.
* **Spectrum Acquisition:** Noted Amazon’s pursuit of Globalstar spectrum as evidence of a trend where satellite players seek exclusive rights to complement terrestrial connectivity.

* **Paramount-Skydance-Warner Deal:** Carr expressed skepticism regarding the reported antitrust lawsuit from several states.
    * **The CNN Factor:** He criticized the California Attorney General’s reported suggestion that the deal could proceed if CNN were spun off, stating it is "hard to conceive" of a legitimate antitrust theory based on owning or not owning a specific news outlet.

### Broadcaster Accountability and the Disney Inquiry
* **Disney License Renewals:** The FCC has called in all of Disney’s broadcast licenses for early renewal rather than standard administrative approval.
    * **DEI Investigation:** Carr is investigating whether Disney engaged in "invidious forms of DEI discrimination" regarding pay and workplace opportunities based on race or gender.
    * **"The View" Controversy:** The FCC is reviewing claims regarding whether *The View* qualifies as a "bona fide news program" under regulatory standards.
* **The Public Interest Standard:** Carr emphasized that broadcasters are not like cable or newspapers; they have a statutory obligation to act in the public interest in exchange for using public airwaves.

* **HBM and Supply Tightness:** Demand for High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) is currently outpacing supply. Chey expects this tightness to persist until the world reaches a settlement with Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
* **Capacity Expansion:**
    * **Doubling Output:** SK Hynix plans to double its total capacity within the next five years.
    * **Customer Feedback:** Despite aggressive expansion plans, Chey notes that major customers claim this doubling is "not enough" and are requesting five to six times more capacity.

* **Power Constraints:** Sites for data centers are being selected primarily based on the availability and cost of electricity, which Chey identifies as the main bottleneck for AI infrastructure.

### Future Tech: "Memory as a Service" and Token Costs
* **Memory as a Service (MaaS):** Chey introduced the concept of moving from a commodity-based "producer" model to a service-based model where SK provides specialized memory-software stacks tailored to specific AI inference needs.
* **Lowering AI Costs:** A primary goal is to reduce the "cost of AI" (token cost). Chey predicts token costs will drop to one-fifth or one-tenth of current levels within three years.
* **AI Bubble Concerns:** While acknowledging stock market "wobbles" and overshooting, Chey insists the underlying AI technology is real and currently in an "early stage," comparing it to a four-year-old child with significant room to mature.

**TL;DR**
Arm CEO Rene Haas reports record revenue and royalties, driven by an "exploding" data center business (100%+ YoY growth) that is on track to surpass handsets as Arm's largest segment sooner than anticipated due to pervasive global AI investments.

### Record Performance and Shifting Market Focus

*   **Exceptional Financial Results:** Arm achieved record revenue of $1.24 billion and record royalties of nearly $740 million, marking a 27% year-on-year increase.
*   **Data Center Business Explosion:** The data center segment grew over 100% year-on-year, significantly contributing to Arm's performance and is projected to become the company's largest business within a few years.
*   **Minimal Handset Market Impact:**
    *   Despite broader industry challenges like memory shortages affecting the smartphone market, Arm's diversified business across multiple sectors (including the rapidly growing data center) minimized direct impact.
    *   Arm's products are primarily used in higher-end devices, which are less affected by shortages that typically impact lower-royalty-rate products at the bottom end of the stack.
    *   The company raised its guidance for the next quarter.

### Hyperscaler Dominance and AI Integration

*   **Accelerated Data Center Inflection:** Arm anticipates its data center business will become its largest segment "sooner than we had thought," largely due to rapidly increasing demand for Arm CPUs.
*   **Dominant Hyperscaler Market Share:** Arm now holds over 50% market share among hyperscalers.
*   **Increasing CPU Core Count:**
    *   Next-generation chips are integrating significantly more Arm cores: e.g., Graviton processors moving from 96 to 192 cores, and NVIDIA's Vera (previously Grace) growing from 72 to 88 cores.
    *   More cores directly translate to higher royalties for Arm.
*   **CPU's Critical Role in AI:** The rapid growth in core count is driven by "agentic AI" workloads (moving, managing, and orchestrating workflows) which CPUs are uniquely suited to handle.

### Sustained AI Investment and Market Opportunity

*   **Unprecedented Capital Expenditure:** Major tech companies like Google (Alphabet), Meta, and Microsoft are announcing investments in data center infrastructure that significantly exceed previous expectations, indicating a long-term commitment to AI.
    *   Examples include Google's potential $185 billion CapEx and the "Stargate initiative" ($500 billion over several years), which previously seemed improbable but is now viewed as credible.
*   **AI as the "Final Frontier":** Rene Haas views AI as a transformative technology with a "long, long runway" for growth, having barely penetrated large enterprises, health systems, or drug discovery.
*   **Justifying Massive Investments:** Despite market nervousness due to the unprecedented scale of investment, the potential for AI to transform "how everything is done" necessitates continued heavy investment.

### Strategic Partnerships and Arm's Ubiquitous Role

*   **NVIDIA's Standalone CPUs:** Arm is "thrilled" by NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang's decision to sell Vera CPUs as standalone products.
    *   This initiative supports the trend toward a homogeneous Arm-based stack in data centers, simplifying maintenance, upgrades, and reducing costs while offering flexibility.
    *   The Vera Rubin platform, for example, uses six times the number of CPUs compared to Grace Blackwell for storage, DPU, and offload functions.
*   **Pervasive AI and Custom Chips:** Arm works with a vast array of industry players, including NVIDIA, OpenAI (reportedly with Broadcom for custom chips), and Meta (also exploring custom chips).
*   **AI Workloads "Everywhere":** Investment is not confined to data centers; the industry is focused on running AI workloads across all devices – wearables, smartphones, PCs, and physical AI applications like autonomous driving and robotics.
*   **Arm's Foundational Advantage:** In a rapidly evolving AI landscape where predicting future architectures is challenging, Arm's programmable, flexible, and low-power IP positions it strongly to drive AI workloads regardless of where chips land.
*   **SoftBank Stake Commitment:** Arm's largest shareholder, Masayoshi Son, remains "very long on ARM" with no intention to sell, demonstrating strong confidence in the company's future growth, which has exceeded initial IPO projections.

### TL;DR
Galaxy Space, a rapidly expanding Chinese commercial satellite company, is leveraging strong government support and innovative, cost-effective technologies to deploy its mega-constellations and explore global markets. The company is focused on integrating its satellite communication services with terrestrial 5G/6G networks to achieve ubiquitous connectivity, while also navigating the critical challenge of international spectrum coordination.

### Galaxy Space Overview & Strategic Context
- **Company Profile:** Galaxy Space is a Chinese commercial company specializing in communication and remote sensing satellites, developing key technologies in this area.
  - Has launched over 40 satellites to date and plans for further expansion.
  - Aims to produce innovative and efficient products for both domestic and global clients, securing more orders in the short and long term.
- **Favorable Policy Environment:** The company benefits from a supportive environment for private and commercial space ventures in China.
  - Chinese policy actively encourages the private sector to develop commercial space businesses.
  - Both central and provincial government levels are creating "good regulation structure" that companies like Galaxy Space are enjoying.
- **Role in China's Space Ambition:** Galaxy Space is a key player in China's fast-growing space sector, contributing to multiple mega-constellation projects.

### Technological Edge & Cost Leadership
- **Core Technology Focus:** Since its establishment as a private company in 2018, Galaxy Space has prioritized developing key technologies.
  - Achieved "very real technology breakthroughs" in satellite broadband communications and direct-to-cell technologies.
  - Also developed advanced massive solar panel systems for satellite energy.
- **Cost-Effective Manufacturing:** The company operates an in-house "flexible, smart factory" for satellite production.
  - This approach allows for cost reduction "without compromising any performances," meeting demand in a commercial and sustainable way.
- **Significant Cost Reduction (Antenna Example):**
  - **Innovation:** Successfully reduced the weight of a critical communication antenna from over 7 kilograms to less than half its original weight.
  - **Impact:** This single design optimization reduces launch costs by over 50% due to the heavy charges associated with launching weight into space.
  - Further technical innovations enhance antenna performance (throughput) while further reducing costs, making satellite communication services "more affordable than the original players."

### Market Expansion & Terrestrial Integration
- **Global Market Strategy:** Galaxy Space is actively exploring global markets, utilizing its "Constellation trial network" to validate technology and applications.
  - Collaborates with local telecom and space players to identify real-world use cases and understand "pain points" for commercialization.
  - Recently secured a contract with a Thai telco, crucial for gaining experience in the Asian market.
- **Priority Target Markets:** Based on economic growth, population density, and connectivity gaps, key regions of interest include:
  - Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Latin America.
- **Seamless Terrestrial-Space Integration (5G/6G):** A critical focus is integrating satellite communication networks with terrestrial networks to create "ubiquitous" connectivity.
  - This requires close collaboration with the telecom industry, especially operators.
  - **Key Challenge:** Ensuring frequency allocation and spectrum sharing to prevent interference with terrestrial networks.
  - **Ultimate Goal:** Provide smooth, uninterrupted services worldwide, with satellite communication becoming an integral part of future 5G and 6G infrastructure.

### Challenges & Future Outlook
- **Operational Headwinds:**
  - **Launcher Sourcing:** While manufacturing satellites in-house, the company sources launchers from various providers, considering different options.
  - **International Coordination:** The "biggest challenge" identified is international coordination for spectrum allocation, required for each spacecraft.
    - This necessitates more collaboration among different global players, including both sector-specific entities and government bodies.
- **Lunar Exploration Ambitions:** The company views lunar exploration as a "very big trend" globally, with significant progress being made by China.
  - The speaker expresses personal excitement for further deep lunar exploration and anticipated milestones, such as orbiting the moon.
