## Paul Graham On Startups, Ambition, and Great Founders

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5bxp78i96S8
- Published: 2026-09-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260903.html#video-5bxp78i96S8
- Speakers: Vivian Shen (Visiting Partner, Y Combinator); Paul Graham (Co-Founder, Y Combinator)

---

### TL;DR
Startups today are tackling significantly more ambitious problems—such as curing cancer and launching intercontinental cargo rockets—while the core mechanics of building a company and the psychological profile of great founders have remained fundamentally unchanged over Y Combinator's 21-year history.

### Evolution of Startups and Ambition
* **Supposedly "Good Old Days":** Critics often claim YC has lost its edge since 2008, but early iconic companies like Reddit were far less serious than current batch companies building intercontinental ballistic cargo systems or tackling cancer.
* **Frighteningly Ambitious Ideas:** The 2012 essay concept of a "new Google" manifested via OpenAI, which bypassed head-on search competition by making obsolete the traditional web-page discovery model in favor of direct information delivery.
* **Serious Healthcare Ventures:** Current YC batches feature multiple diverse approaches to curing cancer, ranging from vaccines to on-demand cancer research ("death of a thousand cuts"), mirroring how YC alumnus Sid Sijbrandij of GitLab successfully attacked his own cancer.

### Founder Psychology and Formidability
* **Inborn Drive:** Great founders possess an innate, formidable drive that cannot be easily manufactured; the rare exceptions are young founders who have merely been trained by pushy parents or environments not to show their ambition.
* **Real-Day Motivation:** While billionaires-in-the-making, founders are driven day-to-day by immediate survival fears—such as a crashing server or an engine falling off a model train set—rather than financial wealth.
* **The "Formidable" Definition:** Coined from Jessica Livingston and Maya's private language, a formidable founder is simply someone who "gets what they want in any situation," aligning perfectly with investor interests.
* **The Anti-Credential:** Treating startups as a resume badge or cool credential is a severe mismatch, akin to applying to Harvard and being forced to study theoretical physics with no easy majors available.

### AI Surprises and the Definition of AGI
* **The Inverted AI Trajectory:** Rather than starting with a perfect fly brain and working up to humans, AI development started with a full-human but deeply flawed baseline ("an undergrad trying to bluster his way through a paper") and is working toward perfection.
* **The Jagged Frontier:** AI exhibits uneven capabilities, effortlessly solving famous open math problems while struggling with basic real-world tasks like restaurant opening times.
* **The Turing Test Redefined:** AGI is no longer viewed as a clear-cut finish line, but rather as a "smear" with certain AI capabilities well across the line while others lag behind.
* **GPU and Token Economics:** While salaries used to be the primary startup cost, companies now face massive token and GPU infrastructure bills, though inference costs consistently drop dramatically over time.

### Y Combinator Origins and Structure
* **Accidental Beginnings:** YC started out purely as an angel firm using standardized paperwork, initiating batches specifically to function as an alternative summer job for undergraduate students.
* **Batch Advantages:** YC provides crucial isolation relief by giving founders colleagues to work alongside, technical problem-solving support from peers, and the "YC GDP"—an immediate cohort of early adopters.
* **Growth and Scaling:** Despite growing from 40 startups in the early days to massive current batches, YC preserves the intimate environment by organizing companies into pods of roughly 70 startups.

### Notable Quotes

> *"We thought back in the day that AI would be sort of like this finish line and we a agi would be this finish line. We cross it. It would be very clear. But when you're standing on the finish line, you realize it has width... The finish line is actually this sort of smear."* — **Paul Graham**

> *"If you want to seem cool, like starting a startup is just about the least efficient way to do it. It's brutally hard."* — **Paul Graham**


## Sam Altman: "Never a Better Time to Do a Startup"

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZIaOBAjvc38

---

### TL;DR
Sam Altman and Garry Tan closed Startup School 2026 by discussing how AI coding agents and plummeting compute costs make this the greatest era in history to launch ambitious startups. They emphasized that founders must embrace earnestness, ignore online cynicism, and build decentralized technologies to prevent catastrophic power concentration as AGI approaches.

### Speakers & Settings
- **Sam Altman:** Co-founder & CEO of OpenAI, former President of Y Combinator, and co-founder of Loopt (YC's first batch in 2005).
- **Garry Tan:** President & CEO of Y Combinator, hosting the closing conversation at Startup School 2026.

### From 2005 to 2026: The Transformation of Startup Building
- **Massive Compression of Build Times:** 
  - In YC's first batch in 2005 (Loopt), founders built products in Cambridge while Paul Graham cooked them dinner.
  - What took 3 months of non-stop work in 2005 can now be accomplished in roughly 7 minutes or seconds using coding agents.
- **The Evolution of Y Combinator:** 
  - In the early days, running YC required convincing skeptics that young technical founders with no business background could succeed.
  - Hard tech startups have grown from 5-10% of YC batches to 15-20-25% today, driven by the lowering barriers of AI tools.
- **Paul Graham's Mentorship Model:** 
  - Paul Graham operated like a flight instructor rather than a traditional lecturer, sitting next to founders to direct their raw, unfocused Brownian motion energy.
  - Graham possessed an unmatched ability to instill optimism and momentum, convincing dejected founders that their startups were going to take over the world.

### The Coming Golden Age of Ambitious Startups
- **The Intelligence on Tap Reframe:** 
  - Critics argue that foundational AI labs will consume all economic value, leaving startups as a "permanent underclass."
  - Altman firmly rejects this, predicting that startups created today will be vastly more valuable and impactful than those of the past.
- **Conditions Favoring Startups:** 
  - Startups historically win during rapid technology shifts, falling costs, and short cycle times—all of which characterize the current AI boom.
  - Fluency with modern AI tools heavily outweighs years of traditional business experience.
- **Navigating the AI Safety Reality:** 
  - Frontier AI safety is no longer an abstract 2030 philosophical debate; recent incidents (such as the Hugging Face sandbox breakout) prove that loss-of-control risks are real and require practical coordination across the industry.

### Overcoming Skepticism and Finding Your People
- **Developing Contrary Conviction:** 
  - Altman’s highest-order advice is to find ideas where conventional wisdom is definitively wrong and be comfortable being called an idiot for years.
  - OpenAI spent years being dismissed as irresponsible and delusional while knowing "the biggest secret in the world," which provided a vital window to conduct research without massive competitors.
- **The Power of Co-Founder Networks:** 
  - Finding a tribe is critical; Altman met OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman after driving down to Palo Alto at age 22 to convince him to drop out of school and join Stripe.
  - Aspiring founders should move to ecosystems like the Bay Area and focus on being "mildly helpful to a lot of people" without immediate expectations of return.
- **Rejecting Cyberspace Cynicism:** 
  - Altman delivered a passionate critique of internet trolls and sarcasm on X (Twitter), noting that taking cheap shots feels good momentarily but is morally bankrupt and poisons the soul.
  - Founders must maintain earnestness and channel all their energy into building actual value rather than seeking online validation.

### Preventing Dystopian Power Concentration
- **The Utility Vision:** 
  - History shows that extreme concentration of power is inherently dangerous; society is best off when power is widely distributed.
  - Startups serve as a crucial economic defense against power consolidation by diffusing AI capabilities throughout the broader economy.
- **The Ultimate Dystopia to Avoid:** 
  - Altman warns against a future where society overreacts to safety by creating a perfect surveillance state—delivering material comfort, cures for cancer, and economic abundance, but stripping away human freedom, agency, and privacy.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **Inference Demand Growth:** Altman predicts that worldwide demand for inference will subjectively grow 10x per year for the next many years, noting that internal YC accounts project potential spikes up to 90,000x. (Sam Altman)
- **Model Progress Timeline:** Altman predicts that the next 6 months of model progress will feel roughly equivalent to the last two years of advancement. (Sam Altman)
- **Token Usage Expansion:** Altman projects that if historical trends repeat over the next 6.5 years, the worldwide average token usage will surge to 500 billion tokens per month per person, while token leaders will consume quadrillions of tokens. (Sam Altman)

### Notable Quotes
> *"The job of being a good startup investor is a teacher, but it's a kind of teacher we don't usually use... a flight instructor, the person who like sits next to you, saying like do this, don't do this, that worked, this is not."* — **Sam Altman**

> *"For years at OpenAI, it felt like we knew the biggest secret in the world. Everybody was calling us an idiot... and looking back it was like an incredible gift because it meant we didn't have this like massive competitors."* — **Sam Altman**

> *"It is very hard to run YC or run a startup... It is very easy to go take shots on Twitter and make a sarcastic comment... and it will poison your soul. It is a morally bankrupt thing to do."* — **Sam Altman**

## Paul Graham, Founder of Y Combinator, Live from Stockholm

- Channel: Y Combinator
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QHJkUw31YX8
- Published: 2026-05-13
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260513.html#video-QHJkUw31YX8
- Speakers: Paul Graham (Founder, Y Combinator)

---

**TL;DR**
Ambitious founders must spend time in Silicon Valley to calibrate against the world’s best talent and internalize a unique "pay-it-forward" culture; Stockholm can become the Silicon Valley of Europe by encouraging founders to export themselves temporarily and return with global capital, connections, and high-speed execution habits.

### The Power of the "Big Center"
*   **Historical Precedents:** Just as Paris was the center for painting in 1870 or Hollywood for movies in 1950, Silicon Valley is the undisputed center for startups. Ambitious people must go to the center to find the highest concentration of talent.
*   **The Serendipity Factor:** The primary benefit of being in a hub is the frequency of unplanned, high-value meetings. 
    *   **Unplanned vs. Planned:** Unplanned meetings are often more valuable because they lack the conservative constraints of "reason-based" scheduling, allowing for outlier opportunities.
    *   **Selection Efficiency:** Casual interactions allow founders to filter for shared interests and potential partnerships within seconds.
*   **Calibration Against "Big Fish":** Moving to a major pond allows founders to measure themselves against industry leaders.
    *   **Humanizing Success:** Meeting icons like Sam Altman or Brian Chesky reveals they are not a "different species," making massive success feel like a difficult but achievable goal rather than an impossible one.

### Speed and Market Dynamics
*   **Decisive Investment:** Silicon Valley investors move significantly faster than their European counterparts.
    *   **Competitive Pressure:** Because the best deals "time out" quickly, investors are forced to be decisive. High valuations and rushed timelines are common complaints, yet SV investors consistently achieve better empirical returns than those in Europe.
*   **The "Prophet" Paradox:** Founders often gain more respect in their home country only after leaving it. 
    *   **The Dropbox Example:** Drew Houston was ignored by Boston investors until Sequoia (in Silicon Valley) showed interest; the Boston firm then faxed a term sheet with a blank valuation to win him back. It was too late.
*   **Aggressive Execution:** In major centers, founders "egg each other on." The proximity to competition prevents founders from "half-having" ideas and forces them to move before someone else does.

### The Silicon Valley "Pay-It-Forward" Culture
*   **Radical Helpfulness:** A defining trait of the Valley is people helping "nobodies" for no immediate reason.
    *   **Cultural Evolution:** This behavior evolved because today's "nobodies" frequently become tomorrow's billionaires.
    *   **The Ron Conway Model:** High-level mentors often perform so many favors they stop "keeping score," creating an environment where there is no conservation law for favors—only an increasing supply of them.
*   **Trust and Integrity:** This culture operates like honesty; by helping everyone, you don't have to track social debts, allowing you to operate at a much larger scale.

### Strategy for Stockholm’s Growth
*   **The "Göttingen" Strategy:** To thrive, Sweden should encourage founders to go to Silicon Valley and then return. This mirrors 19th-century mathematicians who went to Göttingen, Germany, to learn and brought that expertise back to their home countries.
*   **Three Benefits of Returning:**
    1.  **Increased Quality:** Founders return with better skills and higher standards.
    2.  **Capital Import:** Founders bring Silicon Valley investment back to the local ecosystem.
    3.  **Culture Import:** Returning founders seed the "pay-it-forward" and high-trust culture in Stockholm.
*   **Y Combinator as an API:** Graham describes YC as the "optimal path" for this transition, acting as a "super-valley" that concentrates the Silicon Valley experience into a few months.
*   **The European Leadership Opportunity:** The title of "Silicon Valley of Europe" is currently unclaimed. Stockholm’s high livability and existing talent base make it a prime candidate if it can reach a "critical mass" of founders.
