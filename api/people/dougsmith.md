## How to Face Uncertainty and Take Action: Lessons from a Blind Sailor's Pacific Crossing

### TL;DR
Ocean adventurer Hiro Iwamoto and crewmate Doug Smith share leadership lessons from sailing across the Pacific, proving that courage is moving forward despite uncertainty and that choosing the right partner is critical for impossible projects.

### The Pacific Crossings: Triumphs and Trials
* **First Attempt (2013):** Partnered with newscaster Mr. Shinbo, departing from Fukushima to encourage children impacted by the tsunami.
    * **The Whale Collision:** Six days into the voyage, the boat suffered a massive collision with a whale, forcing the crew into an inflatable life raft.
    * **Survival:** The duo floated in 5-meter waves for 11 hours with 15-foot storms before being rescued by a Japanese Maritime Self-Defense Force seaplane and rubber boat.
* **Second Attempt (2019):** Partnered with Doug Smith, a first-time sailor, to sail 5,000+ kilometers from San Diego back to Japan.
    * **The Route and Duration:** The journey took 55 days instead of the anticipated 60 to 75 days, though it included a frustrating 7-day stretch with zero wind at the start.
    * **Charity Impact:** The voyage raised funds for the Challenged Athletes Foundation, the Cure Blindness Project, and the Carter Center Trachoma Center.

### Leadership & Decision-Making Under Uncertainty
* **Defining Courage:** 
    * **Hiro Iwamoto:** "Courage is not absence of fear. It's your choice to move forward." Leaders make the mistake of waiting for 100% information before acting, which leads to stagnation.
* **Staying Present in Crisis:**
    * **Overcoming Panic:** During the life raft ordeal, Hiro remained calm by focusing strictly on the present moment ("living now") rather than worrying about future catastrophes. 
    * **Mental Resilience:** This calm mindset helped him manage his panic-stricken crewmate and maintain clear decision-making.
* **The Power of "Seeing the Unseen":** Developing internal vision and trusting intuition helps leaders navigate high-pressure situations when external indicators are missing or unclear.

### Building and Choosing the Right Partnership
* **Complementary Strengths:** 
    * **Hiro Iwamoto:** Chose Doug Smith despite Doug having zero sailing experience, valuing Doug's quick learning ability, positive spirit, and willingness to act as "seeing eye Doug."
    * **Doug Smith:** Brought sailing interest and a shared chemistry, stepping up to help facilitate Hiro's dream of inspiring others.
* **Avoiding "Dream Killers":** 
    * **Hiro Iwamoto:** Divided his network into "dream killers" (who told him blindness made sailing impossible) and "dream supporters" (who collaborated on how to make it happen). Leaders must surround themselves with problem-solvers rather than obstacle-pointers.

### Reflections on Modern Society and Isolation
* **Liberation from Noise:** Both sailors found the isolation on the open ocean—surrounded only by the sun and stars—to be deeply liberating, comparing the feeling of self-sufficiency to that of astronauts.
* **Consumer Shock:** 
    * **Hiro Iwamoto:** Upon returning to land, he experienced severe culture shock walking into supermarkets, realizing how overly materialistic modern society has become compared to the simple provisions needed to survive 55 days at sea.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Hiro Iwamoto's Solo Voyage (2027):** Hiro plans to sail completely single-handed from San Diego on **February 14, 2027**, targeting a 3-month journey to land in Japan by May before typhoon season.
* **Vessel and Tech Utilization:** Hiro will use a smaller boat of the same type that sank in his 2013 whale collision and will utilize a custom accessibility app called "Lina" (developed by Furuno) paired with Starlink and a shore crew providing "collective eyes" to read navigation data aloud.
