### TL;DR
At the HT Leadership Summit, former US Secretary of State Antony Blinken argued that the world is at a once-in-70-to-80-year inflection point driven by rapid technological change, generational inequality, and shifting power dynamics. He attributed recent Democratic gains in the US midterms to inflation, the Iran war, and perceived corruption, while maintaining that a two-state solution remains the only viable path to regional peace and Saudi-Israel normalization.

### The Global Inflection Point & Technological Disruption
* **Unprecedented Speed of Change:** Blinken noted that technological adoption is accelerating beyond governments' ability to manage, citing the time required to reach 50 million adopters:
  * Telephone: ~75 years
  * Radio: ~40 years
  * Television: ~20 years
  * Facebook: ~4 years
  * ChatGPT: 1 month
* **Private-Sector Dominance:** Unlike past technological shifts driven by governments, Blinken observed that AI is driven by the private sector, throwing Moore's Law "out the window" and leaving governments with limited expertise.
* **Generational Inequality:** Referencing economist Joe Stiglitz, Blinken noted that while inequality *between* countries has decreased over the last 50 years, inequality *within* countries has risen. 
  * In the US, 90% of people born in 1945 were doing better than their parents 30 years later; today, that figure is 50% and falling.
* **Diffused Power Dynamics:** Citing a *New York Times* column by Tom Friedman, Blinken explained that diplomacy is no longer just about calling four or five foreign counterparts; super-empowered actors in Silicon Valley, corporations, and NGOs now possess massive disruptive capacity.
* **Risk of Backlash:** Blinken warned that confusion and resentment create a "perfect storm" driving protectionism, isolationism, xenophobia, and demagogic leadership.

### US Midterm Elections & Domestic Drivers
* **Historic Electoral Shift:** Blinken acknowledged he did not foresee the scale of the Democratic sweep in the US midterms, which saw major gains in the Senate, House of Representatives, governorships, and state legislatures. 
  * Demographic and state-level shifts have temporarily altered the electoral college map, which previously looked like a growing "nightmare" for Democrats.
* **Economic Frustration & the Iran War:** Blinken attributed voter anger to President Trump's unfulfilled promises to lower prices, noting that continued inflation was driven in part by tariffs and the "war of choice" with Iran.
* **Revulsion at Corruption:** Polling showed that perceived public corruption was a major driver for voters—a pattern Blinken compared to global movements over the last 20 years, including Tunisia, Tahrir Square (Arab Spring), Maidan in Ukraine, and Operation Car Wash in Brazil.
* **Middle East Policy Impact:** While US perceptions of Israel have undergone a "sea change," Blinken stated that polling indicates this was not a primary driver in the midterm election results.

### West Asia, the Iran War, & Israel-Palestine
* **Pre-October 7 Diplomacy:** Blinken revealed he was scheduled to visit Saudi Arabia and Israel on October 10, 2023, to advance a normalization roadmap building on the first Trump administration's Abraham Accords. Documents discovered after October 7 showed Hamas attacked in part to disrupt this impending normalization.
* **Demographic Reality:** Blinken emphasized that neither side is leaving: there are 7 million Jews in Israel and 7 million Arabs and Palestinians across Israel proper, the West Bank, and Gaza.
* **The Two-State Imperative:** Despite extreme dehumanization on both sides, Blinken argued that a secure Israel and a sovereign Palestinian state remain the only viable solution to unlock normalization with Saudi Arabia, as well as countries like Indonesia, Qatar, and Lebanon.
* **Iran's Regional Standing:** Blinken noted that even after losing top leadership and being hit hard, Iran has paradoxically emerged from the war in a stronger regional position than when it started.

### The Future of India-US Ties
* **Bipartisan Foundation:** The moderator highlighted Blinken's past statement that strengthening the India-US partnership is a top US foreign policy priority, noting that the actions of both nations are shaping the 21st century.
* **Technology as the Next Phase:** Summing up the discussion, the moderator noted—and Blinken agreed—that the geopolitical context of India-US relations remains intact, with technology cooperation serving as the critical building block for the next phase of the partnership.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **AI Development:** Blinken stated that the world is "about to enter the age of recursive self-improvement" in artificial intelligence.
* **2028 US Election Dynamics:** Blinken predicted that if the incoming Democratic Congress (taking office in January) fails to deliver on the affordability crisis, it will hurt Democrats, but responsibility going into 2028 will still fundamentally fall on the sitting president.

### TL;DR
Former US Secretary of State Antony Blinken highlighted profound global inflection points—driven by technological velocity, generational economic inequality, and shifting power dynamics—while expressing long-term optimism alongside deep concern for navigating the transition intact. Speaking at the Hindustan Times Leadership Summit 2026, Blinken analyzed the severe November 3 midterm electoral defeat for the Trump administration, detailed the strategic missteps of the war on Iran, and strongly reaffirmed the enduring importance of the US-India strategic and technological partnership.

### US Midterm Elections & Domestic Fallout
* **Severe Republican Setbacks:**
  * **Electoral Shift:** Blinken noted that Republican losses in the November 3 midterm elections exceeded expectations, extending far beyond a typical midterm correction and flipping governorships, state legislatures, and historically safe red states.
  * **Demographic Realignment:** Democratic margins expanded significantly, notably among voters in the center and in key swing districts, shifting the electoral college map.
* **Voter Frustration & Affordability Crisis:**
  * **Unfulfilled Promises:** Voters expressed deep frustration over unfulfilled campaign promises by the Donald Trump administration to lower prices.
  * **Inflationary Drivers:** Blinken highlighted that inflation persisted due to presidential decisions regarding tariffs and an unnecessary war of choice with Iran.
* **Revulsion at Corruption:** 
  * **Public Backlash:** Polling indicated that a major driver of voter turnout was a strong revulsion against perceived government corruption, mirroring global anti-corruption movements seen in the Arab Spring and Ukraine.

### Geopolitical Realignment & West Asia
* **The War on Iran:**
  * **Policy Reversal:** Blinken criticized the Trump administration's decision to tear up the Obama-era Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), which had previously kept Iran's enriched uranium out of the country (97% shipped out) and maintained a breakout timeline of a year or more.
  * **Military Escapade:** The subsequent military action and pursuit of regime change was characterized as an avoidable war of choice that depleted US munitions, destabilized the Strait of Hormuz, and spiked global diesel and energy prices.
  * **Off-Ramp Probability:** Blinken estimated a 50% chance of the current status quo prevailing, a 20-25% chance of dangerous horizontal/vertical escalation, and a narrow path for an off-ramp requiring diplomatic resolution and implicit concessions to Iran.
* **The Emerging Global Order:**
  * **End of Unipolarity:** The 80-year post-WWII rules-based order built on enlightened self-interest is fading.
  * **Variable Geometry:** The future points toward a hybrid, bespoke world of variable geometry where shifting coalitions of nations, corporations, and non-state actors align on specific issues rather than rigid East-West or democracy-autocracy divides.

### US-India Relations & Strategic Convergence
* **Enduring Strategic Partnership:**
  * **Evolution:** The relationship has successfully transitioned over a 25-year period from "estranged democracies" to a unique partnership rooted in shared strategic convergence and profound people-to-people connections.
  * **Technological Cooperation:** Building on foundational civil-nuclear cooperation, the **Initiative on Critical and Emerging Technology (iSET)** serves as the new glue for bilateral ties, focusing on semiconductor manufacturing, AI supply chains, space, and quantum technology.
* **Managing Differences:**
  * **Strategic Autonomy:** The US acknowledges India's strategic autonomy and historic ties with Russia, focusing instead on diversification away from Russian energy and military dependency.
  * **Pakistan:** Blinken confirmed the complete de-hyphenation of US policy regarding India and Pakistan, categorizing the relationship with India as global and foundational, while treating ties with Pakistan as strictly regional and focused on counterterrorism.

### American Power & Institutional Foundations
* **The Engine of Innovation:**
  * **The R&D Alliance:** US global leadership was built on a unique partnership between the federal government, universities, national labs, and research institutions—scaling R&D spending from 1.5% post-WWII to roughly 3.5% of GDP under the Biden administration.
  * **Attracting Global Talent:** Immigrants drive US innovation; half of US billion-dollar venture-backed companies and top AI talent pools are fueled by foreign-born experts.
* **Self-Inflicted Vulnerabilities:**
  * **Internal Threats:** Blinken warned that the greatest risk to American superpower status is a self-inflicted wound: eroding the partnership between research institutions and government, and closing the welcoming mat to the world's best and brightest.

## The future of U.S. foreign policy: Competing with China, U.S. reliability and a new warfare era

- Channel: MS NOW
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c_Yjk8JHNKU
- Published: 2026-05-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260520.html#video-c_Yjk8JHNKU
- Speakers: Antony Blinken (Former Secretary of State); Jen Psaki (Host, MS NOW); Linda Thomas-Greenfield (US Ambassador to the United Nations)

---

**TL;DR** 
To successfully compete with systemic rivals like China and navigate a new era of high-tech warfare, the United States must abandon unilateralism, rebuild its damaged international credibility, and foster agile, issue-specific global alliances.

---

### China and the Power of Multilateral Alliances

*   **The Unilateral Deficit:** Former Secretary of State Antony Blinken warned that competing with China one-on-one is a losing proposition due to China's massive market, superior purchasing power parity, and manufacturing output, which is currently three times that of the U.S.
*   **The Power of Convergence:** Building allied coalitions is the primary strategic counterweight to China. 
    *   Aligning the U.S. with Europe, Japan, South Korea, India, Australia, and Canada shifts the coalition's economic weight from 20–25% of global GDP to 50–60%, making U.S.-led initiatives impossible for China to ignore.
*   **Declining Global Trust:** A poll of 130 countries revealed that China is currently viewed as a more credible international actor than the U.S. In Germany, a poll showed that 70% of respondents viewed the U.S. as an adversary.
*   **African Alignment:** Ambassador Linda Thomas-Greenfield noted that African nations are increasingly forced to choose sides, frequently favoring China because they currently view it as a more trustworthy and reliable partner than the U.S.

### Rebuilding U.S. Reliability and Diplomatic Credibility

*   **The Reliability Question:** Senator Andy Kim emphasized that the primary question foreign leaders ask about the U.S. is: *"Is America a reliable country?"* Rebuilding this baseline of consistency is a long-term challenge that will outlast any single presidential administration.
*   **Diplomatic Understaffing:** The U.S. is severely hamstringing its own foreign policy by failing to confirm diplomatic personnel. 
    *   The U.S. currently lacks Senate-confirmed ambassadors in over 100 countries, including 37 nations in Africa. This vacancy rate cripples the nation's ability to negotiate complex agreements or counter foreign influence.
*   **The Cost of Domestic Corruption:** Ambassador Thomas-Greenfield pointed out that reports of a past U.S. administration financially benefiting to the tune of $1.4 billion severely undercut American moral authority. This makes human rights and anti-corruption advocacy abroad appear hypocritical to foreign governments.

### Strategic Failures and Regional Fallout in the Middle East

*   **The Strategic Deficit in Iran:** Blinken characterized the U.S. conflict with Iran as a strategic failure. Despite any tactical successes, Iran is poised to retain its regime, its missile capabilities, vestiges of its nuclear program, and significant leverage over the Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes.
*   **Economic and Diplomatic Strain:**
    *   **Domestic Costs:** Senator Kim highlighted that the conflict has cost American consumers over $42 billion in increased gas and diesel prices.
    *   **Alienated Allies:** U.S. actions have alienated European allies (previously strained by disputes over NATO, Greenland, and Denmark) and frustrated Gulf partners who feel directly exposed to Iranian retaliation. Notably, Iran has fired more projectiles at the UAE than at Israel during this conflict.
*   **Diplomatic Off-Ramps:** Resolving the standoff will require highly skilled negotiation and compromise. Potential terms include down-blending Iran's highly enriched uranium, a temporary enrichment moratorium, and a Strait of Hormuz tolling system to fund regional reconstruction.

### The War in Ukraine and the Future of Warfare

*   **Resilience on the Battlefield:** Despite declining U.S. funding, Ukraine is holding its military and economic lines with European aid. Meanwhile, Russia’s war economy is beginning to cannibalize itself as defense spending fails to generate productive domestic infrastructure.
*   **Weapons of Mass Production:** Senator Kim warned that modern warfare has shifted from "weapons of mass destruction" to "weapons of mass production." The battlefield is now defined by the rapid, high-volume manufacturing of drones, AI tools (such as Mithos), and ballistic missiles.
*   **Rapid Tech Acceleration:** The pace of global change is unprecedented. Blinken noted that while the telephone took 75 years to reach 50 million users, Instagram took 4 years, and ChatGPT reached the milestone in just 4 months.
*   **Variable Geometry:** Future global governance will move away from rigid autocracy-versus-democracy blocs. Instead, the U.S. must lead via "variable geometry"—agile, issue-specific coalitions of countries, companies, and civil actors collaborating on targeted problems like climate change, AI governance, and food security.

### Strategic Competition and the Global Convergence with China

* **The Challenge of One-on-One Competition:** Former Secretary of State Antony Blinken warns that competing with China individually is a losing proposition for the United States.
    * **China's Structural Advantages:** China possesses a manufacturing capacity three times that of the US, a larger navy, greater purchasing power parity, and higher rates of patent filings.
    * **The Alliance Multiplier:** By aligning with key global partners—including Europe, Japan, South Korea, India, Australia, and Canada—the US and its allies represent 50% to 60% of global GDP, compared to just 25% for the US alone.

* **The Need for Reliability:** Senator Andy Kim (D-NJ) emphasizes that foreign leaders frequently question whether America remains a reliable partner. ### Strategic Failures and Diplomatic Deficits in the Middle East

* **The Iran Conflict as a Strategic Failure:** Antony Blinken argues that unilateral confrontational policies have left Iran with its regime intact, its missile capabilities preserved, and significant leverage over the Strait of Hormuz.
    * **Shipping and Economic Impacts:** Even minimal Iranian disruption in the Strait (e.g., through low-cost shoulder-fired missiles) threatens global shipping lanes, drives up maritime insurance costs, and forces the international community to include Iran in regional negotiations.
    * **Regional Destabilization:** The conflict has severely altered regional dynamics, evidenced by Iran launching more projectiles at the United Arab Emirates (UAE) than at Israel.
* **Hollowed-Out Diplomatic Infrastructure:** Linda Thomas-Greenfield highlights a critical vacancy crisis in US diplomacy that prevents effective negotiations.
    * The US currently lacks Senate-confirmed ambassadors in over 100 countries, including vacancies in 37 African nations.
* **Domestic and Constitutional Costs:** Sen. Andy Kim criticizes the normalization of executive-led, undeclared wars without congressional approval. 
    * He notes that the conflict has directly impacted American families, costing consumers over $42 billion in inflated gas and diesel prices.
    * **The Hypocrisy of Corruption:** Thomas-Greenfield points out that documented financial self-dealing in past US administrations severely undermines American diplomats' ability to advocate for anti-corruption and human rights abroad.

### The Evolution of Warfare and National Resilience in Ukraine

* **Ukraine's Battlefield Endurance:** Despite a reduction in direct US leadership, Ukraine continues to hold its military and economic lines against Russian aggression at a immense human cost.
    * **A Future Defense Hub:** Due to rapid innovation in drone technology and battlefield tactics, Ukraine is poised to become a central hub for Europe's future defense industry.
* **Russia's Cannibalistic War Economy:** Antony Blinken notes that while high oil prices temporarily sustained Russia, Vladimir Putin has constructed an unsustainable war economy. Resources poured into perishable military hardware are hollowing out productive infrastructure investments.
* **From WMDs to "Weapons of Mass Production":** Sen. Kim highlights a major paradigm shift in modern conflict, moving away from conventional weapons of mass destruction to high-volume, low-cost "weapons of mass production" like AI-assisted drones and ballistic missiles.
* **A Foreign Policy of Resilience:** Rather than simply opposing isolationist foreign policies, Kim argues the US must focus on domestic and supply-chain resilience—specifically securing critical minerals, pharmaceuticals, and energy production to safeguard national security.

### "Variable Geometry" and the Future of Global Governance

* **Unprecedented Pace of Technological Change:** Antony Blinken underscores the hyper-accelerated speed of modern development, pointing out the time it took various technologies to reach 50 million users:
    * **Telephone:** 75 years
    * **Radio:** 45 years
    * **Television:** 20 years
    * **Instagram:** 4 years
    * **ChatGPT:** 4 months
* **The Rise of "Variable Geometry":** Traditional rigid alliances (such as East-West or Democracy-Autocracy divides) are being replaced by fluid, issue-specific coalitions. Future governance will rely on "variable geometry"—dynamic alignments of nations, corporations, and non-state actors uniting to address distinct challenges like climate change, synthetic opioids, or AI governance.
* **Connecting Foreign Policy to Domestic Needs:** Linda Thomas-Greenfield advises future leaders to ground their platforms in the daily struggles of ordinary citizens. Rather than focusing on abstract foreign policy concepts, leaders must connect global stability directly to domestic pocketbook issues, education, and local economic opportunities.
