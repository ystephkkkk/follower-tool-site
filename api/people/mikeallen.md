Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent defended the administration's economic record amid public cost-of-living frustrations, outlined aggressive economic pressure campaigns against Iran, and discussed risks and regulation surrounding the U.S. AI boom.

### State of the U.S. Economy & Cost of Living

## Jensen Huang says the AI doomers have it wrong

- Channel: Axios
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fr1IQspixmM

- Speakers: Jensen Huang (CEO, NVIDIA); Mike Allen (Journalist, Axios)

---

### TL;DR
Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang dismissed extreme warnings about artificial intelligence as "complete nonsense," arguing that AI is driving unprecedented productivity and net job creation rather than eliminating human labor. During an exclusive interview with Axios' Mike Allen in Fort Worth, Texas, Huang also discussed U.S.-China technology competition, the rise of open-source models, and why Wall Street fundamentally misunderstands the current infrastructure buildout.

### Geopolitical Competition & Trade with China
* **State of China Sales:** Huang stated that Nvidia's sales to China are approximately zero and advised investors to expect zero sales unless the U.S. and Chinese governments permit a return to the market.
* **Chinese AI Models:** Praised models like DeepSeek, Kimi, and GLM as excellent tools that advance the global industry. Huang rejected the misconception that Chinese models contain backdoors, noting they operate safely within isolated "sandboxes" and harnesses.
* **Open vs. Closed Models:** Argued that while closed models (like Anthropic and OpenAI) are fantastic for everyday use, open-source models are foundational for science, cybersecurity, and national security because transparency allows the global community to inspect and harden them.
* **Government Regulation:** Emphasized that regulation should focus on specific dual-use applications (such as autonomous vehicles and medicine) rather than restricting the underlying technology itself.

### Market Misconceptions & Wall Street's "CAPEX" Blindspot
* **The Stock Market Drop:** Explained that Wall Street panicked over recent Chinese open-source releases (similar to the DeepSeek reaction), incorrectly assuming free AI hurts hardware demand. Huang maintained that "free AI is great for hardware, chips, and data centers" because better models drive higher usage.
* **The New CAPEX Reality:** Acknowledged that the new IT industry requires significantly heavier capital expenditure (CAPEX) to build out the intelligence layer (AI factories and data centers), but argued this infrastructure buildout is industrially driven rather than cyclical or seasonal.
* **Lack of Demand Bubble:** Stated that the semiconductor industry needs to be 5 to 10 times larger than it is today, with current shortages across memory, storage, optical interconnects, and TSMC foundries.
* **The AI Flywheel:** Noted that an inflection point has been reached where AI is both useful and profitable—evidenced by companies gladly paying hundreds of millions for coding agents that dramatically increase productivity.

### The Jobs Narrative & Economic Reality
* **Net Job Creation:** Refuted predictions that AI will eliminate half of American jobs, presenting evidence of rising headcounts:
  * **Radiologists:** Increased by roughly 20% due to automated scans allowing doctors to see significantly more patients.
  * **Paralegals:** Increased by roughly 10% as automated case handling allowed firms to clear massive backlogs.
  * **Manufacturing:** Increased by some 50% over the last several years to build AI data centers and infrastructure.
* **Closing the Technology Divide:** Highlighted AI as the first major technology that closes the technology divide, acting as a personal coach and tutor for anyone regardless of background.
* **Small Corporate Footprint:** Shared that despite Nvidia's massive valuation, it employs only 50,000 people and aims to keep headcount "as small as possible" (projecting roughly 75,000 in a decade) compared to tech giants with six-figure headcounts.

### Robotics, Agents, and Future Timelines
* **The Robotic ChatGPT Moment:** Declared that the "ChatGPT moment" for robotics has already arrived, noting that robots can now reason through complex, multi-step physical tasks when given natural language prompts.
* **The Agentic Era:** Predicted that the computing landscape is shifting from 100 million active human users to a future dominated by 100 billion to a trillion software agents running continuously and consuming vast amounts of computer resources.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Global Open Source Ecosystem:** Huang stated that open-source models will proliferate globally, driven by American, European, Chinese, Japanese, and Indian developers, making openness foundational for security.
* **IPO Milestones:** Predicted that OpenAI and Anthropic will grow to go public and become "the most successful IPOs in human history."
* **Bubble Timeline:** Asserted that a major infrastructure bubble is very unlikely to occur within the next 5 years, while the 5-to-10-year outlook depends entirely on physical building constraints.
* **Useful Robotics Timeline:** Estimated that robots will become practically useful in the everyday lives of ordinary people within **3 to 4 years**.
* **Corporate Headcount Projection:** Projected that Nvidia's workforce will grow from its current 50,000 to approximately 75,000 employees over the next **10 years**.

### Notable Quotes
> "The fact that this is going to be the end of humanity, it's complete nonsense. The fact that this is going to destroy half of the American jobs is complete nonsense and all of the facts, all of the evidence point exactly to the opposite."
> — Jensen Huang

> "We are laying the foundations, building the infrastructure, the largest industrial infrastructure buildout in human history, so that we can build the foundation of industries in the future for the next generation. And so this is just the very beginning."
> — Jensen Huang

## A new era of discovery: AI and the frontiers of science with Demis Hassabis

- Channel: Google for Developers
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dgBLVm2L1P4
- Published: 2026-05-21
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260521.html#video-dgBLVm2L1P4
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (Co-founder and CEO, Google DeepMind); Mike Allen (Co-founder, Axios)

---

**TL;DR**
Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis predicts that Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) will arrive between 2029 and 2031, ushering in a transformative "singularity" era marked by self-improving code, agentic systems, and a massive 100x acceleration of scientific and medical discovery.

---

### The Path and Timeline to AGI
* **AGI Arrival Horizon:** Hassabis projects AGI will be realized around 2030 (specifically within the 2029–2031 window). 
* **The "Einstein Test" for AGI:** While current models show "jagged intelligence," true AGI will require a leap of conceptual insight.
  * *The Benchmark:* Hassabis defines this as giving a model a knowledge cutoff of 1901 and evaluating whether it can independently formulate the conceptual leaps Albert Einstein did in 1905, such as special relativity.
* **Feeling the Shift:** Hassabis notes the approach of AGI is already palpable. He cites "vibe coding" game prototypes in his spare time in just 1–2 hours—tasks that previously required up to six months of manual engineering.
* **The Singularity:** Hassabis describes this impending era as the most transformative invention in human history, making long-term predictions beyond its arrival point nearly impossible.

### Google's Edge in the "Ferocious" AI Race
* **The Full-Stack Advantage:** Hassabis asserts Google is the only organization in the world operating a complete feedback loop across the entire AI pipeline:
  * Proprietary custom chips (TPUs) and specialized data centers.
  * Scaled cloud infrastructure.
  * A world-class frontier research bench (Google DeepMind).
  * Direct distribution channels reaching billions of consumers.
* **Rapid Scale and Adoption:** Google's streamlined, "agent-first" tech stack has allowed it to deploy powerful, efficient models directly to massive user bases.
  * *Milestones:* The Gemini App now boasts 900 million monthly active users, alongside massive deployments of AI Overviews in Search.
* **A Broad Research Bench:** Hassabis points to Google's diverse research capabilities—developing media generation models like Omni and highly efficient, performant models like Gemini 1.5 Flash and Pro—as its ultimate competitive edge.

### Societal Impact, "100x" Acceleration, and the Industry Backlash
* **The 100x Industrial Revolution:** Hassabis estimates AI's impact will be 10 times greater than the Industrial Revolution and occur 10 times faster (occurring over a decade rather than a century). 
* **Democratization of Opportunity:** AI tools lower the barrier to creation, allowing a single motivated individual or a 1-to-2-person startup to build what once required a team of 50 specialized software engineers.
* **Addressing Public Backlash:** Hassabis believes the AI industry must combat growing public anxiety and "tech backlash" by prioritizing and demonstrating unequivocal, concrete societal benefits. 
  * *Key Areas:* The main counterweight to public skepticism is applying AI to hard science challenges, such as drug discovery, climate mitigation, materials science, and clean energy.
* **A Cautious Optimist Philosophy:** Rejecting extreme positions of both "doomers" and pure "accelerationists," Hassabis argues for taking a measured approach to AGI. He emphasizes that society must give itself the time to implement safety, security, and robust guardrails over the next few years.

### AI for Science: From AlphaFold to World Models
* **The AlphaFold Paradigm Shift:** Hassabis recounts a pivotal decision filmed for the documentary *The Thinking Game*. Instead of setting up a standard web portal where scientists submitted individual genetic sequences for protein-folding predictions, DeepMind chose to run every known protein in existence (over 200 million) and release the entire database for free to the global scientific community.
* **Simulations and "Genie":** Simulations are critical for both teaching AI systems and generating safe training data.
  * *Real-World Testing:* Google is utilizing "Genie"—an interactive 3D world model—to safely simulate extreme, "one-in-a-billion" edge cases for Waymo autonomous vehicles (e.g., simulating forest fires or wild animals on the road).
  * *Complex Systems:* DeepMind's weather forecasting models can predict extreme events, like Hurricane Beryl, much faster and more accurately than traditional fluid dynamics equations.
* **The Next Technical Step-Changes:** Leading labs are heavily investing in several critical evolutionary steps for models:
  * *Self-Improvement:* Developing coding and mathematics agents where outputs are easily verifiable, creating a self-reinforcing flywheel.
  * *Continuous Learning:* Moving past static post-training cutoffs so models can adapt and learn dynamically after release.
  * *Consistency:* Eliminating "jagged" performance profiles so models do not fail on simple, out-of-distribution variations of tasks they otherwise excel at.

### Builder Advice: Direction over Velocity
* **The Warning on Freneticism:** While praising the unparalleled builder energy of Silicon Valley, Hassabis cautions that extreme "freneticism" can inhibit deep, long-term scientific thinking.
* **The Velocity Vector:** Hassabis warns developers and founders against favoring speed over direction.
  * *Reflective Thinking:* For deep-tech breakthroughs that take years to yield results, ensuring the correct strategic direction is far more critical than raw speed.

## OpenAI’s warning: Washington isn’t ready for what’s coming

- Channel: Axios

- Speakers: Mike Allen (Co-founder, Axios); Sam Altman (CEO, OpenAI)

---

**TL;DR:** OpenAI CEO Sam Altman warns that the next generation of AI models will represent a "significant step forward," potentially enabling career-defining scientific discoveries while simultaneously creating urgent, "world-shaking" cybersecurity and biosecurity risks that Washington is currently unprepared to manage.

### The Imminent Leap in Capability
* **Timeline to AGI:** Sam Altman states that the world is "far along" enough that precise definitions of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) matter less than the reality that AI is already discovering new scientific knowledge and performing high-scale economic work.
* **Knowledge Work Transformation:** The next class of models is expected to move beyond simple productivity gains to revolutionary outputs.
    * **Scientific Discovery:** While current models help with small discoveries, future models could help scientists make "career-defining" or "most important discovery of a decade" level breakthroughs.
    * **Software Engineering:** Altman predicts a shift where a single developer, paired with sufficient GPU power, can perform the work of an entire software team.
* **The "Next Generation" Release:** Altman suggests that as OpenAI prepares to launch its next generation of models, the gap between current capabilities and the next tier will be substantial.

### Urgent Security Threats & Resilience
* **Cybersecurity Risks:** Altman agrees that a "world-shaking cyberattack" is possible within the next year. 
    * **Mitigation:** He calls for a "resilience-style approach" involving deep cooperation between AI companies, cybersecurity firms, and the government to secure the open-source stack and infrastructure.
* **Biosecurity Concerns:** The same models that will cure diseases could be misused to create novel pathogens.
    * **The Open-Source Risk:** While frontier companies can align models and use safety stacks, Altman warns that society must prepare for a world where capable open-source models are accessible to terrorist groups.
* **Beyond Company Responsibility:** Altman emphasizes that AI safety cannot be managed by private companies alone; it requires a coordinated societal and governmental response.

### Washington’s Role and "The Blueprint"
* **Nationalization vs. Partnership:** Altman argues against nationalizing OpenAI or its competitors, suggesting the government is not currently equipped to build superintelligence successfully. 
    * Instead, he advocates for a "very, very deep partnership" between the state and developers to ensure AGI aligns with democratic values.
* **Policy Priorities:** OpenAI is pushing a new "blueprint" or industrial policy for debate.
    * **Low-Hanging Fruit:** There is significant bipartisan energy around building energy infrastructure and lowering electricity prices to support AI growth.
    * **The Overton Window:** Ideas regarding major shifts in the tax system or fundamental changes to capitalism (to rebalance leverage between labor and capital) are currently at the "edges" of political feasibility but are entering serious discussion.
* **Government Functionality:** Despite historical dysfunction in DC, Altman remains optimistic that the government will manage the "chaos" of the transition.

### The Future Economy: Intelligence as a Utility
* **The Utility Model:** Altman compares future AI to electricity—a background utility that people "plug into" for various services. 
    * Users will likely have a "personal super assistant" running in the cloud that performs background work, with billing based on the level of intelligence used.
* **Pricing Dynamics:**
    * **Falling Costs:** The "price per unit of intelligence" is dropping astronomically fast and will continue to do so unless there are extreme supply constraints.
    * **Frontier Premium:** While "last year's intelligence" becomes cheap, Altman expects a persistent premium for the "frontier" or most advanced models.
* **Human Essence:** Despite economic shifts, Altman argues that the core of the human experience—relationships, fulfillment, and connection—will not change, as people will always prioritize human-to-human interaction.
