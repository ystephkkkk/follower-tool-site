### TL;DR
The World Economic Forum’s Global Gender Gap Report 2026 shows the overall global gender gap index at 69.2%, meaning it will take 120 years at the current rate of change to reach full parity, with leadership parity stretched out to 90 years. Speakers Saadia Zahidi and Sue Duke highlighted that progress is uneven, stalling in leadership and political empowerment, and facing a "triple penalty" for women in the emerging AI economy.

---

### Global Progress & Regional Standings

* **Overall Global Parity:** 
  * The global aggregate index stands at **69.2%**, the highest in the 20-year history of the report (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * Across 97 economies tracked for the full 20-year arc, there has been a **5 percentage point increase**, with nearly all of those individual economies showing improvement (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * At the current rate of change, it will take **120 years** to reach full gender parity (**Saadia Zahidi**).
* **Top Rankings & Patterns:**
  * **Iceland** ranks #1 for multiple consecutive years, closing **93%** of its gap as the only economy crossing the 90% mark (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * Finland, Norway, and Sweden follow, consistently occupying top-10 positions over the 20-year span (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Namibia** climbs four places to rank as the highest country from Sub-Saharan Africa; **Australia** enters the top 10 for the first time alongside the UK, New Zealand, Germany, and Ireland (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Fastest 20-year ground gainers:** Bolivia, Chile, Ecuador, France, Guatemala, Iceland, Mexico, Namibia, and Nepal (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Economic Correlation:** There is no direct correlation between being rich and being gender-equal; countries that invest in human capital, education, health, and inclusive workplace policies grow faster and become more competitive (**Saadia Zahidi**).
* **Regional Trajectories:**
  * **Current Rankings (Highest to Lowest):** Europe $\rightarrow$ North America $\rightarrow$ Latin America $\rightarrow$ East Asia & Pacific $\rightarrow$ Central Asia $\rightarrow$ Sub-Saharan Africa $\rightarrow$ South Asia $\rightarrow$ Middle East & North Africa (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Projected Speed to Parity by Region:** 
    * Latin America: Fastest, in approx. **60 years** (**Saadia Zahidi**)
    * Europe: Approx. **70 years** (**Saadia Zahidi**)
    * Sub-Saharan Africa: Approx. **102 years** (**Saadia Zahidi**)
    * North America: Approx. **108 years** (**Saadia Zahidi**)
    * Central Asia: Approx. **202 years** (**Saadia Zahidi**)

### Leadership, Workforce, and Economic Stagnation

* **Labor Market Participation:**
  * While labor force participation has generally grown over the past 20 years, hiring is down **20% to 30%** from pandemic levels in large developed economies (**Sue Duke**).
  * Women face a 55% higher likelihood of taking a career break than men and are 4 times more likely to take one for full-time parenting (**Sue Duke**).
* **Leadership Stagnation:**
  * Over the past decade, female representation in leadership grew by roughly **2.5%** (from just over 27% to just under 30%) (**Sue Duke**).
  * For the past **4 years in a row**, the relative share of women hired into leadership roles has gone backwards, stretching the timeline to leadership parity from 50 years (pre-2020) to **90 years** (**Sue Duke**).
  * In the first year back from a parenting break, men are **40% more likely** than women to move into a more senior role, a 40% advantage that holds steady three years later (**Sue Duke**).
  * CFO and COO roles (key pathways to top jobs) are occupied by women only about **1 in 4** times (**Saadia Zahidi**). In contrast, women concentrate heavily in Chief HR, People, and Marketing Officer roles.

### The AI Economy & The "Triple Penalty"

* **AI Workforce Disparities:**
  * Women face a "triple penalty" in the AI economy: underrepresentation in AI roles, AI firms, and AI leadership (**Sue Duke**).
  * Women hold only **1 in 3** entry-level roles at AI firms, **1 in 5** AI engineering and machine learning roles, and **1 in 8** C-suite seats at AI firms (**Sue Duke**, **Saadia Zahidi**).
* **Drivers of the AI Gender Gap:**
  * **STEM Pipeline Deficits:** Longstanding underrepresentation of women in science, technology, engineering, and math talent pools (**Sue Duke**).
  * **Tech Sector Legacy:** Roots in male-dominated technology sectors like software engineering (**Sue Duke**).
  * **Career Interruption Impact:** Because the AI industry moves at an extremely fast pace, women taking career breaks (55% more likely than men to do so) face amplified penalties (**Sue Duke**).
* **AI Job Disruption:**
  * AI is disrupting white-collar administrative and paralegal roles where women have historically concentrated to build strong livelihoods (**Saadia Zahidi**).

### Political Empowerment & Legal Reforms

* **Political Empowerment Dynamics:**
  * Political empowerment carries the largest overall gap in the index, with only **22%** closed (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Ministerial Positions:** The ratio improved from 1 female minister for every 8 male ministers in 2006 to **1 for every 4** today; ministerial representation rose from 14% to over **22%** (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **Parliamentary Representation:** The ratio improved from 1 in 5.6 men to roughly **1 in 3** today (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * **The Reversal:** Political empowerment peaked a few years ago; progress was faster between 2006–2016 than between 2016–2026, marking a slight regression (**Saadia Zahidi**). Discrepancies persist, with women concentrated in portfolios like health and education rather than influential portfolios like finance, defense, foreign affairs, and trade (**Saadia Zahidi**).
* **Legal Reforms & Implementation:**
  * Partnering with the World Bank’s *Women, Business and the Law*, data shows **~2,000 legal reforms** since the 1970s, with **96%** expanding women’s legal rights (**Saadia Zahidi**).
  * While average implementation time dropped from 161 days to **96 days**, enforcement remains weak—particularly regarding workplace equality, entrepreneurship, and equal pay provisions (**Saadia Zahidi**).
