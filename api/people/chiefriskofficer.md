## The world really is more unpredictable than ever - two ‘chief risk officers’ explain why

### TL;DR
The world has entered an "age of unpredictability" where interconnected macro risks materialize simultaneously, requiring organizations to abandon rigid quarterly reports in favor of real-time signal monitoring, AI-assisted pattern recognition, and localized supply chain resilience.

### Risk Landscape & The Age of Unpredictability
* **Simultaneous Multi-Risk Shocks:**
  * Risks no longer occur sequentially in isolation; organizations face overlapping crises (e.g., Middle East geopolitical shocks constraining energy availability while natural disasters disrupt industrial supply chains).
  * **Global Fragmentation (Natasha Fields):** The most concerning macro risk is the fragmentation of the global economy into geopolitical and technological blocks, weakening the multilateral trading system.
  * **Automotive Market Pressures (Torben Oeder):** European car companies face compounded pressures from strict emission/safety regulations, increased material/energy costs, higher vehicle financing expenses, and currency depreciation in emerging markets.
* **Environmental Risk Underestimation:**
  * **El Nino & Cascading Impacts:** Natasha Fields warns that climate risks like El Nino are heavily underestimated due to a failure to account for cascading effects on geopolitics, food security, and water supply.
  * **Corporate Adaptation (Torben Oeder):** Volkswagen monitors weather extremes via early warning systems, collaborates with suppliers on inventory buffers, and actively works to reduce the massive water consumption required for vehicle manufacturing.

### AI, Data, and Human Judgement in Risk Management
* **Division of Labor:**
  * **AI & Quantitative Tools:** Used effectively for anomaly detection, data aggregation, signal monitoring, and pattern recognition to reduce noise amidst reduced industry headcounts.
  * **Human Leadership:** Human interpretation remains indispensable for providing context, making strategic decisions, ensuring ethical judgement, and taking ultimate board-level liability.
* **The Risk of Inaction:**
  * **Competitive Imperative (Natasha Fields):** Organizations must weigh the risks of using AI (such as data confidentiality risks at the WTO) against the greater risk of *not* using AI while competitors leverage it for efficiency and resilience.
  * **Overhyped Perceptions (Torben Oeder):** While AI helps identify cyber vulnerabilities, Torben Oeder views the broader AI panic as somewhat overhyped given that human strategic judgement cannot be replaced.

### Evolving Risk Management Strategies
* **From Efficiency to Localization:**
  * **End of Global Scale:** The days of developing a single car model and selling it worldwide are over; companies must adopt localized business models with regional sourcing, development, sales, and production.
  * **Efficiency vs. Resilience Trade-off:** While efficiency minimizes cost, true resilience—such as maintaining secondary suppliers—comes with a high price tag that boards only approve when presented with high-probability "hit rates."
* **Public vs. Private Sector Maturity:**
  * **Historical Gap:** Private sector organizations historically developed risk management frameworks ahead of multilateral and government organizations due to greater budgets and team sizes.
  * **Convergence:** International organisations are rapidly catching up by leveraging chief risk officer communities and cross-sector information sharing.
* **Shift to Ad-Hoc Agility:**
  * Risk functions are evolving away from traditional quarterly risk reports toward real-time, ad-hoc impact analysis capable of reacting instantly to overnight geopolitical or economic shocks.
