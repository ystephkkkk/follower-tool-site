## From 'polycrisis' to 'polytunity' - the future of geopolitics with Prof Yuen Yuen Ang

- Channel: World Economic Forum
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnBw9jxWtcI
- Published: 2026-07-29
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260729.html#video-AnBw9jxWtcI
- Speakers: Yuen Yuen Ang (Political Science Professor, Johns Hopkins University); Olivia Siong (Journalist, CNA)

---

**TL;DR:** The global "polycrisis" is actually a "polytunity"—a moment of systemic breakdown that forces structural transformation, particularly as power shifts from Western-led norms to the global majority. For China, navigating this means managing an economic paradox where advanced high-tech manufacturing coexists with weak domestic consumption and employment pressures.

### The Shift from "Polycrisis" to "Polytunity"
* **Defining Polycrisis:** **Professor Yuen Yuen Ang** notes that the term "polycrisis" (popularized by Adam Tooze at the WEF in 2023) captures fear-driven global anxiety over entangled threats like political polarization, conflict, and the breakdown of post-WWII trade norms.
* **The Polytunity Framework:** **Professor Ang** argues for flipping the term to "polytunity," reflecting a Singaporean realist perspective that global breakdown provides a rare, forced opportunity for deep reflection and transformation when normal business-as-usual is too risky to change.
* **Rise of the Global Majority:** **Professor Ang** points out that power has shifted away from the small number of Western countries that ruled for the last two centuries toward China, Africa, Asia, and Latin America, giving the rest of the world unprecedented agency.

### China's Economic Paradox and the "China Shock 2.0"
* **The Dual Reality:** **Professor Ang** identifies China's central economic paradox: impressive, high-tech advances (EVs, advanced materials, quantum computers) coexisting with a broad economic slowdown and falling retail sales (down 0.6% last month, the first drop in over three years).
* **The Global Manufacturing Shock:** **Professor Ang** and co-host **Olivia Siong** highlight that because China's new high-tech sectors lack sufficient domestic consumption power, highly competitive Chinese products flood overseas markets (such as Southeast Asia and South Africa), raising legitimate questions from other nations about their own manufacturing futures.
* **Beijing's Reframing:** **Co-host Robin Pomeroy** notes that the Chinese premier pushed back against the Western "China Shock 2.0" narrative at the opening plenary, recasting it as "China Opportunity 2.0."

### The Employment Challenge and the Innovation Divide
* **Robots vs. Jobs:** **Professor Ang** explains that China's new growth model (AI, quantum, EVs, aeronautics) is not mass-employment producing; automated factories employ robots rather than hundreds of millions of workers and incoming college graduates, directly undermining consumer confidence and spending.
* **Glamorous vs. Humble Innovation:** **Professor Ang** argues that too much capital is channeled into dazzling "0-to-1" cutting-edge technologies (which create few jobs and low participation) rather than "humble innovation" like modular manufacturing and e-commerce, which have low barriers to entry and drive massive employment.
* **Bureaucratic Incentives:** **Professor Ang** points out that local Chinese officials focus on glamorous projects (like flying taxis and dancing robots) because current political incentives do not reward the revival of traditional, employment-heavy sectors.

### The Evolution of China's 5-Year Plans
* **From Autopilot to Heavy Steering:** **Professor Ang** compares pre-14th 5-year plans to driving straight ahead on autopilot within defined paradigms (FDI, real estate, exports), whereas the 14th and 15th 5-year plans represent a heavy "right turn" by central leadership to structurally transform the growth driver from quantity to quality.
* **The 15th 5-Year Plan Addition:** **Professor Ang** observes that while the 14th plan focused singularly on "new productive forces" (cutting-edge tech), the 15th 5-year plan and recent economic work conferences explicitly add consumption as a necessary pillar to sustain production.

### Geopolitical Realities: Beyond the Cold War Mindset
* **Flawed US-China Framing:** **Professor Ang** criticizes Washington D.C. decision-makers for applying a Cold War, zero-sum mindset that expects a definitive winner and loser, arguing instead that the two superpowers are in a competition with no clear end game.
* **Agency of the Rest of the World:** **Professor Ang** stresses that middle powers like Singapore refuse to pick sides and will conduct business with both superpowers based on national interest, forming a truly multipolar world rather than a bipolar rivalry.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Professor Ang** predicts that the US-China competition will continue indefinitely with **no clear victor for a long time to come**.
