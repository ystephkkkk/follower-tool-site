### The Shift from Conversational Chat to Autonomous Agents

* **Earning Trust as a Product Feature:** Transitioning to fully autonomous execution requires building user trust. OpenAI is focusing on giving operators granular controls, allowing them to choose when an agent must draft and ask for permission versus when it can execute a task directly.
* **Fluid, Real-Time Voice Interaction:** Brockman notes that current "turn-taking" voice models feel unnatural. The next frontier is true bi-directional voice communication, allowing users and AIs to speak and process inputs simultaneously, enabling fluid, real-time feedback loops.

### Model Scaling Laws, Compute Constraints, and Market Economics

* **Unbroken Scaling Laws:** Despite persistent industry skepticism, Brockman emphasizes that there is still "no wall in sight" for model scaling. Increasing compute, refining data, and improving architectures continue to yield predictable, deterministic leaps in capability.
* **The Compute-Scarce Economy:** Brockman predicts the world is heading toward a compute-powered economy where demand will consistently outstrip supply. Even as more compute online, the depth of global agentic usage will keep compute as a highly valued, scarce resource.
* **Pricing Strategy and Value Progression:** 
    * **Declining Costs for Static Intelligence:** OpenAI will continue its historical trend of cutting prices for a fixed level of intelligence. What feels like "premier" intelligence today will become commoditized and inexpensive within a year.
    * **Premium Frontier Models:** True frontier models will remain high-value and premium-priced because their advanced reasoning capabilities can solve previously unsolvable scientific and mathematical problems.

### Navigating the Partner Ecosystem and the Microsoft Relationship

* **Cooperative Integration:** Rather than attempting to block or bypass existing software, OpenAI collaborates with software partners. Partners have welcomed OpenAI training its models to navigate their tools, viewing it as a massive driver of user engagement.
* **The "Operating System" Debate:** While some view a central agent as a threat to current operating systems (like iOS), Brockman views the agent as an ergonomic "interface to technology" rather than a traditional OS layer. 
* **The Microsoft Partnership Dynamics:** In response to Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella's comments that models are becoming commodities and the value lies in company-specific data loops, Brockman argues that no single layer of the stack (compute, models, or domain orchestration) will be removed from the value chain. Instead, they act as multipliers of one another.
* **Hardware Devices:** Brockman acknowledges that OpenAI is exploring physical devices. However, he views hardware not as the AI itself, but as an ergonomic interface designed to make the agent dynamically accessible in daily life.

* **Key Healthcare Milestones:**
    * **Solving Cold Cases:** Clinicians recently used OpenAI's early reasoning model, O3, to diagnose a patient who had suffered from a mysterious, undocumented ailment for 20 years.
    * **Mass Consumer Adoption:** Approximately 230 million people currently use ChatGPT every week for health-related queries, showcasing a major shift toward patient empowerment.

    *   He argues that neither "centralized control" nor "unfiltered release" is the perfect solution, but emphasizes "democratic access" as a core value.
*   **Cyber Safeguards:**
    *   OpenAI utilizes a **Preparedness Framework** to evaluate risks in cyber and bio-intelligence.
    *   The model is tested by "defenders" (security professionals) before release to ensure ecosystem resilience.
    *   Brockman asserts that GPT-5.5 is not "cyber permissive," meaning it has built-in safeguards to prevent it from being used for malicious attacks.

### The Future of Autonomous "Fleet" Agents
*   **The Super App:** OpenAI is evolving its "Codex" harness into a "Super App" for general computer work, shifting the user’s role from "worker" to "CEO" of a fleet of agents.
*   **Workspace Governance:** 
    *   OpenAI recently released "Workspace Agents," which allow enterprises to host Codex harnesses in the cloud.
    *   **Governance Features:** To manage the "law of large numbers" risk, IT organizations can now oversee all agent conversations and set specific guardrails within the enterprise environment.
*   **Compute Scarcity:** Brockman predicts a future world defined by "compute scarcity," where the scale of available hardware determines the speed at which humanity can solve massive problems, such as drug discovery or curing Alzheimer’s.

OpenAI is shifting focus from broad research demonstrations to real-world utility, prioritizing a unified "Superapp" over divergent projects like video generation.

*   **Product Unification:** The goal is to combine ChatGPT, Codex (coding), and web browsing into a single endpoint.
    *   **Personal Assistant:** The app is envisioned as a "personal AGI" that knows user context, has memory, and is aligned with specific goals.

*   **Deprioritizing Sora:** While Sora (video) is impressive, OpenAI is focusing compute on the GPT reasoning branch.
    *   **Branching Challenges:** Brockman noted that maintaining multiple distinct "tech trees" (like diffusion for video vs. transformers for reasoning) is unsustainable in a compute-constrained environment.

### Technical Roadmap: "Spud" and the Path to AGI
OpenAI is preparing to release a new series of models that represent years of accumulated research breakthroughs.

*   **The "Spud" Model:** A new base model (pre-train) is imminent, incorporating roughly two years of research. 
    *   **Qualitative Shift:** Brockman expects it to eliminate the "friction" of today’s models, solving much harder, open-ended problems with longer time horizons.
    *   **Raising the Ceiling:** The model has already shown the ability to solve unsolved physics problems in 12 hours that previously stumped human researchers.
*   **Defining AGI:** Brockman believes we are "70-80% there."

Compute is no longer viewed as a cost at OpenAI, but as the primary driver of revenue and capability.

*   **Compute as a Revenue Center:** Brockman compares buying GPUs to hiring salespeople; as long as the product is scalable, more compute directly equals more value and revenue.
*   **Investment Strategy:** Brockman admitted telling his team to buy "all of it" regarding compute. Even with massive investment, OpenAI remains "compute-strapped" because demand for reasoning models consistently outstrips supply.
*   **Infrastructure Realities:** Data center construction requires 18-24 month lead times. Brockman argues that those who didn't commit to massive infrastructure builds by late last year will be unable to compete.

### The Future of Work: The "CEO of Agents"
The paradigm of work is shifting from "doing" to "delegating" as AI agents become more autonomous.

*   **Management Paradigm:** Users will act as the "CEO of a fleet of hundreds of thousands of agents."
    *   **Accountability:** Brockman warns against "abdicating responsibility." Humans must remain the accountable party, providing vision and feedback even if they don't perform the mechanical tasks.
    *   **Losing the "Pulse":** There is a risk of losing one's "finger on the pulse" of a problem when delegating to AI, which requires users to build trust through oversight rather than blind reliance.
*   **Economic "Takeoff":** Brockman describes the current moment as the "takeoff phase," where AI moves from a "sideshow" to the main driver of economic growth and national security.

## Who’s Winning The AI Race? + Software’s Future — With Sridhar Ramaswamy

- Speakers: Sridhar Ramaswamy (CEO, Snowflake); Alex Kantrowitz (Host, Big Technology)

---

**TL;DR**
The AI race has shifted from an OpenAI monopoly to a fierce, multi-polar competition where technical leads shrink in months, forcing software companies to transition from static SaaS tools into "agentic platforms" that prioritize internal data integration over simple chat interfaces.

### The State of the AI Race: Shrinking Leads and High Stakes
* **Vanishing Leads:** Sridhar notes that a "lead" in the current market lasts about a year—an eternity in tech—and even that is shrinking. While OpenAI and Anthropic held early advantages, Google has leveraged its "bottomless well of money," distribution, and proprietary TPUs to close the gap with Gemini.
* **The Google "Code Red" Culture:** Drawing on his 15 years at Google, Sridhar explains that while the company may look placid from the outside, it is "well-calibrated for crises." He attributes their AI resurgence to the integration of DeepMind and the hands-on involvement of founders like Sergey Brin.
* **The Rise of Alternatives:** The aura of OpenAI’s invincibility is being challenged by open-source and international models like DeepSeek and Qwen2. Sridhar highlights that specialized models (like Anthropic’s coding model) can temporarily claim the "state of the art" title, forcing competitors to play a constant game of catch-up.

### The Shift to Agentic Enterprise AI
* **Snowflake Intelligence:** Snowflake’s agentic platform is its fastest-growing product, with over 2,000 customers using it within three months of General Availability (GA). 
* **Beyond the Chatbot:** Sridhar argues that the future of work isn't just a chatbot, but a system that connects "Past, Present, and Future":
    * **The Past:** Historical data stored in Snowflake.
    * **The Present:** Ambient information like emails, documents, and market trends.
    * **The Future:** Prediction models that recommend actions based on data.
* **Operational Gains:** 
    * **Support Efficiency:** Snowflake’s own support team saw a **10x reduction** in time spent debugging complex cases by using "Cortex Code," a data coding agent.
    * **Complex Scaling:** Sridhar cites a manufacturer with **5 million SKUs** using agents to analyze dynamic market pricing, potentially unlocking hundreds of millions in revenue that was previously impossible to manage manually.

### The Future of Software: Platforms vs. "Dumb Backends"
* **SaaS Multiple Contraction:** The market is currently devaluing traditional SaaS (multiples dropping from ~33x to ~23x) because of uncertainty over who wins the AI era. Sridhar suggests that "AI as a bolt-on" is not a winning strategy.
* **The "Dumb Backend" Risk:** Niche SaaS providers that rely on data lock-in are at risk. If they don't provide agentic value, they become mere data pipes for "master agents" like ChatGPT or Gemini. 
* **Platform Concentration:** Sridhar believes value will concentrate in platforms that own the "entirety of the experience"—the model, the critical enterprise data, and the integrations into operational systems.

### Geopolitics and the Open Source Ecosystem
* **The Academic Gap:** Sridhar expresses concern that leading US AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) have become "walled gardens" that publish very little. This has caused academia to diverge from cutting-edge research.
* **The Chinese Advantage:** Paradoxically, because Chinese companies (like Alibaba with Qwen or DeepSeek) are publishing their work to gain influence, they are becoming the primary resource for global students and professors to build upon.
* **Net Positive Competition:** Despite the geopolitical friction, Sridhar views the rise of high-quality open-source models as a "net positive" because it prevents a monopoly and forces US companies to innovate faster.

> "The AI race changes every month. We should all feel great about making predictions because one of them will come true and the world will change enough that we have to make new predictions." — **Sridhar Ramaswamy**

> "A lot of these [SaaS] players risk becoming dumb backends to the models, which is why Snowflake is leaning so far forward on agentic AI... that’s the place where value is going to get created." — **Sridhar Ramaswamy**

> "It’s a bit of a red pill moment where you’re like, 'Wait, you mean I can release new software products pretty much every day because releasing a new piece of functionality is as simple as writing a recipe in English?'" — **Sridhar Ramaswamy**
