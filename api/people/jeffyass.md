## Episode 58: Jeff Yass on Social Security privatization

### TL;DR
Jeff Yass, co-founder of Susquehanna International Group and vice chairman of the Cato Institute, argues that Social Security is an unsustainable "Ponzi scheme" set to run dry in 2032, and proposes privatizing it by routing payroll taxes into individual, low-cost index fund accounts ("Trump accounts"). Under Yass's plan, workers receive stock market returns with a government-backed guarantee—funded by a potential tax on the top 2%—ensuring they never receive less than traditional Treasury bill returns.

### The Current Social Security Crisis
* **No Actual Trust Fund:** Yass explains that despite political rhetoric—such as Al Gore's "lockbox" or Franklin D. Roosevelt's original framing—the Social Security trust fund has never existed as a vaulted savings account. 
  * Weeks after Social Security launched, Roosevelt's Treasury Secretary admitted the funds were spent immediately in exchange for a political promise.
  * In the 1960 or 1962 Supreme Court case *Fleming v. Nestor*, the court ruled that taxpayers have no legal ownership of Social Security money, only a promise that Congress can alter or revoke at any time.
* **The 2032 Cliff:** Yass notes that Social Security faces an annual shortfall of $400 billion to $500 billion and will run dry in 2032.
  * If Congress takes no action, benefits will automatically drop by 22%, leaving recipients with only 78% of their promised payouts, with further declines over time.
* **Political Gridlock:** Asked by host Amir why Washington has ignored these dates for decades, Yass states that the only traditional fixes—cutting benefits or raising taxes—are wildly unpopular.
  * Yass warns that the most politically plausible fix in 2032—eliminating the current $183,000 income cap on Social Security taxes—would raise about $250 billion a year (covering just over half the shortfall) while constituting the "biggest tax increase in American history" and turning Social Security into a welfare program.

### The Privatization Plan ("Trump Accounts")
* **Account Structure:** Yass proposes redirecting the 12.2% to 12.4% payroll tax (paid by individuals and employers) into private accounts with the worker's name on them, utilizing the "Trump account" infrastructure already developed with Brad Gerstner.
  * Funds would be invested in zero-cost or low-cost index funds.
  * At retirement (around age 65), the worker receives the money—which Yass favors distributing as a lump sum—and any remaining funds pass to their heirs upon death.
* **Overcoming Past Objections:** Yass notes that Bill Clinton nearly passed a 2% private account carve-out in 1998 before dropping it amid the Monica Lewinsky scandal and opposition from figures like Chuck Schumer, and George Bush's later attempt failed for similar reasons: high Wall Street fees and market volatility.
  * **Eliminating Fees:** While mutual funds charged around 100 basis points in 1998, modern index funds cost practically zero.
  * **Eliminating Downside Risk:** Yass points out that in U.S. history, no 30-to-40-year investment in the stock market—even starting on the worst day in 1929—has ever underperformed Treasury bills. To guarantee this, any shortfall below Treasury bill returns would be covered by an extra tax on the top 2% of earners, giving workers a "free roll" on the stock market.
* **Wealth Creation:** Yass contrasts a typical 67-year-old retiring today with roughly $300,000 in Social Security benefits against what they would have had—well over $1 million—if this plan had been enacted 45 years ago.

### Transition Costs and Opt-Out Savings
* **Honoring Current Promises:** When Amir asks who pays for current retirees during a transition, Yass states their benefits would be paid out of general Treasury funds without a 22% cut.
* **Reducing the $4–$5 Trillion Liability:** While honoring the existing system carries a $4 trillion to $5 trillion obligation, Yass cites studies showing that if 40-year-olds (who have already paid in $100,000 to $200,000) are given the option to forfeit their past benefits in exchange for putting all future contributions into the stock market, enough would opt out to save the Treasury $1 trillion to $2 trillion.

### Political and Philosophical Impact
* **Making Every Laborer a Capitalist:** Referencing Yass's 2019 writing, Amir asks how this changes the country. Yass argues it ends the 200-year ideological battle between Marxism/collectivism and free-market capitalism.
  * A Walmart worker making $60,000 to $80,000 a year would eventually generate as much income from their investments as from their labor, turning them into a hybrid "laborer-capitalist" aligned with economic growth.
* **Partisan Divide:** Yass anticipates a "war of misinformation," predicting that because Donald Trump supports the idea, the political left will oppose it. He argues only Marxists would oppose workers owning the means of production. Amir agrees that very few people could oppose the plan in good faith and expresses hope that listeners will support it to make the U.S. more prosperous over the next decade.

## Episode 55: Economic Myths with Jeff Yass - Co-Founder and Managing Director of Susquehanna

**TL;DR** 
Measuring the national debt against GDP misdiagnoses the US economy's health because it ignores America's massive $230 trillion in national wealth; the real threat to prosperity is not the debt itself, but policies that stifle the wealth-generating "golden goose" or reject market-driven voucher systems in education and welfare.

---

### Redefining the US Debt Crisis: Debt-to-Equity vs. Debt-to-GDP

* **The GDP Misconception:** Assessing US fiscal health purely by comparing the $31 trillion national debt to the $31 trillion GDP (a 100% ratio) is fundamentally flawed.
    * **The Wealth Factor:** Unlike other countries, US national wealth is roughly seven times its GDP, sitting at close to $230 trillion.
    * **Stock Market Disparity:** The US stock market is worth $80 trillion (more than 2.5 times GDP), whereas in Europe and China, the stock market-to-GDP ratio is closer to 1:1.
* **The Debt-to-Equity Reality:** When measured against total national assets—the equivalent of a corporation's debt-to-equity ratio—US debt is only about 13% to 14% of its wealth, which is near a historic low.
    * **The Earnings Analogy:** Judging the US by GDP is like refusing to lend to someone who earns $100,000 but has $5 million in the bank. Wealth, not immediate earnings, determines solvency.
* **The Real Economic Threat:** The danger is not the debt itself, but the potential destruction of the wealth-generating engine.
    * **Government Waste:** While the government wastes an estimated $2 trillion of its annual $7 trillion budget, the country will not go broke unless bad regulations and high taxes decimate the $230 trillion wealth base.

---

### The Underappreciated Power of Consumer Surplus

* **The Extraction Myth:** Critics often view wealth accumulation as a zero-sum game, operating on the assumption that billionaires actively extract wealth from the lower and middle classes.
* **The Concept of Consumer Surplus:** In reality, the vast majority of value created by entrepreneurs is gifted back to the public in the form of time and money saved. 
    * **The Amazon Example:** Jeff Bezos accumulated roughly $200 billion when Amazon was valued at $2 trillion. However, the consumer surplus Amazon generated—by saving consumers trillions of hours—is estimated to be worth at least $10 trillion. Bezos ultimately captured only about 2% of the total value he created.
    * **The Tylenol Example:** A consumer is willing to pay $100 to cure a severe headache but only pays $1 for Tylenol. The pharmaceutical company makes a tiny profit, while the consumer pockets $99 of "surplus" value.
    * **The Uber Example:** Uber provides immense societal utility and convenience, yet the company's actual profit is a tiny fraction of the overall value it has added to global transportation.

---

### Busting the "Tax the Rich" Accounting Myths

* **Corporate vs. Personal Tax Liabilities:** Political claims that ultra-wealthy founders pay "zero taxes" rely on deceptive accounting practices.
    * **Indirect Contributions:** While founders like Jeff Bezos may have low personal income tax liabilities because they hold unsold stock, they pay massive taxes through their corporations. If Amazon pays $30 billion in annual taxes, Bezos's personal share of that payment is effectively $6 billion.
* **The Life Cycle of Billionaire Wealth:** Critics overlook the ultimate destination of billionaire capital.
    * **The Buffett Example:** Warren Buffett legally minimizes his tax burden to maximize the compounding of his capital, only to pledge his entire fortune to philanthropic causes (such as eradicing disease in Africa via the Gates Foundation). Attempting to confiscate this wealth beforehand simply diverts it to government entities looking to buy votes.

---

### Solving Inefficiency with a Universal Voucher System

* **"Legalizing" the Education System:** The most effective lever to relieve societal suffering and eliminate government waste in America is introducing free-market competition to schools.
    * **The Cost Discrepancy:** New York City spends approximately $43,000 per student annually, while top-tier private and Catholic schools achieve better results for less than half that cost.
    * **The Voucher Solution:** If parents were given a voucher to spend at any school of their choice, the remaining balance could be saved. By graduation, a child could have $300,000 banked for their future.
* **Why Government Inefficiency Persists:** The government effectively hides the true costs of public systems. Because parents do not receive a direct invoice for $43,000 every school year, they remain unaware of the systemic waste.
* **A Voucher-Based Welfare State:** While returning to the strict limitations of the 9th and 10th Amendments is politically unfeasible, the country can drastically reduce corruption and waste by transitioning all welfare programs to voucher systems.
    * **The Universal Model:** Just as the government uses food stamps (vouchers) instead of running grocery stores, it should issue vouchers for education and healthcare rather than operating the schools and hospitals directly.
