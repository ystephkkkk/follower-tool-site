## Inside Salesforce’s Acquisition Strategy for the AI Era

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y4BdOtm40SA
- Published: 2026-11-06
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261106.html#video-y4BdOtm40SA
- Speakers: John Somorjai (Executive, Salesforce); Alex Immerman (General Partner, a16z)

---

#### TL;DR
Salesforce executive John Somorjai and a16z General Partner Alex Immerman analyze Salesforce's nearly $100 billion M&A strategy—highlighting major acquisitions like Slack, MuleSoft, and AI-native startups like Finn and Listen Labs—while examining venture capital valuations, AI-driven outcome pricing, and startup diligence pitfalls.

---

#### M&A Strategy & Acquisition Playbook
* **Core Acquisition Framework:** **Salesforce evaluates potential deals using a strict internal three-part criteria:**
  * **Strategic Fit & Customer Success:** Assessing customer demand, product gaps, and the value the asset can immediately drive.
  * **Business Acceleration & Stack Integration:** Determining how fast the technology can integrate into the existing stack, evaluate technical feasibility, and review cultural alignment and talent.
  * **Financial & Shareholder Value:** Balancing reasonable valuations and timelines for value accretion against current market conditions.
* **The Distribution Advantage:** **Leveraging Salesforce's massive sales engine:**
  * Salesforce maintains **15,000 sales reps** and an ecosystem of **8,000+ ISV partners**.
  * Acquisitions like Listen Labs gain instant access to global enterprise, mid-market, and small business distribution that would take a startup years to build independently.
* **Build-versus-Buy Calculus:** **Speed to market dictates the decision:**
  * Software development has never been faster or cheaper, but hiring scarce AI engineers remains extremely difficult.
  * End-to-end M&A integration typically takes **6 to 9 months**, serving as the benchmark for whether Salesforce can build solutions internally faster than buying them.

#### AI, Agents, & Outcome-Based Pricing
* **The Rise of Outcome-Based Pricing:** **Shifting from per-seat models to resolution-based value:**
  * Products like Finn pioneer pricing based on successful resolutions rather than software seats because time-to-value is exceptionally fast (**16 days average** externally, **14 days internal pilot**).
  * AI agents capture a larger share of the enterprise labor budget by automating entire workflows across customer support and sales.
* **High-Performance AI Deployments:** **Quantifiable metrics from recent acquisitions:**
  * **Finn (Service Cloud AI):** Handles over **40% of traffic** and delivers **65% resolutions without human escalation** on help.salesforce.com.
  * **Qualified (Piper Agent):** Replaces traditional sales development rep (SDR) pipelines by handling website traffic, answering product questions via lifelike avatars, and automatically booking meetings.

#### Major Platform Products & Cultural Impact
* **Slack's Evolution:** **Transforming from collaboration tool to enterprise AI interface:**
  * Acquired in **2020**, Slack revenue is now nearly **3x** what it was at acquisition and stands as Salesforce's fastest-growing product.
  * Serves as the ultimate conversational interface for AI agents and the central corpus mapping corporate decisions and organizational context.
* **Velocity and Enterprise Verticals:** **Injecting industry-specific go-to-market strategies:**
  * Led by David Schmire, the Velocity acquisition expanded Salesforce from a horizontal CRM into **13 dedicated industry verticals**.
  * Acquired founders have risen to top leadership internally, with former founders stepping into Chief Product Officer roles **four times** in company history.

#### Salesforce Ventures & Global Investment Mandate
* **Venture Portfolio Scale:** **Investing across 750+ companies with 29 total acquisitions:**
  * Early ecosystem investments include DocuSign, Eninova, Twilio, and Snowflake.
  * Created a **$1 billion AI fund** in early **2023** to back foundational model leaders like Anthropic (invested across **6 rounds**), Mistral, and Cohere.
* **Expansion into Frontier Technologies:** **Diverging beyond traditional enterprise software:**
  * Backing voice (11 Labs), video (Runway), and cybersecurity (Wiz).
  * Venturing aggressively into physical AI and robotics with investments in Figure AI, Physical Intelligence, Dina Robotics, Skilled, drone delivery via Zipline, and Japanese logistics robotics company Mugen.

#### Startup Diligence & Deal Killers
* **Common Diligence Red Flags:** **Why deals collapse during deep review:**
  * **Hidden Attrition:** Founders relying solely on ARR metrics often mask underlying customer churn, product quality issues, or unhappy users.
  * **Security Vulnerabilities:** Security is the single function that can issue an immediate deal-ending red flag; Salesforce penetration tests have occasionally exposed flaws allowing unauthorized access to source code and employee laptops.
  * **Financial Controls:** Lack of strong controls over financials and disorganized customer contract data slow down or kill otherwise promising transactions.

## AI, Infrastructure, and the Next Investment Cycle

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lr3hNhA0IfQ
- Published: 2026-09-30
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260930.html#video-lr3hNhA0IfQ
- Speakers: David George (General Partner, a16z); Sarah Wang (General Partner, a16z); Alex Immerman (General Partner, a16z)

---

### TL;DR
Driven heavily by tech (which accounts for ~55% of US capital spending and nearly 40% of aggregate stock market value), current market highs are supported by fundamental earnings rather than multiple expansion. Meanwhile, AI infrastructure buildout and agentic workflows are accelerating demand across compute, power, and physical supply chains.

### Macro, Markets, and the Infrastructure Boom
* **High-Tech Capital Concentration:**
  * High-tech equipment, software, and R&D account for roughly 55% of US capital spending (**David George**).
  * US tech companies represent 8 of the top 10 valued companies globally, with tech comprising almost 40% of the aggregate US stock market value (**David George**).
  * Model companies combined have raised over $350 billion (**David George**).
* **Earnings-Driven Market Highs:**
  * The market is up 90% (17% annualized) since JBT came out almost 4 years ago (**Alex Immerman**, **David George**).
  * Stocks are up ~20% while trading multiples are down ~20%, meaning performance is driven by fundamental earnings rather than multiple expansion (**David George**).
  * The S&P 500 earnings multiple is below 20x, while cyclical memory companies trade at 6x–7x forward earnings (**David George**).
* **Massive Hyperscaler CapEx & Infrastructure Scale:**
  * The AI infrastructure buildout has surpassed railroads as a percentage of GDP (**David George**).
  * CapEx for the five largest hyperscalers (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) is projected at $416 billion in 2025, $780 billion in 2026, and over $1 trillion annually from 2027 onward (**David George**).
  * Combined cloud backlog for Microsoft, Google, and Amazon sits at roughly $1.7 trillion (**David George**).
* **The "Age of Atoms" & Power Grids:**
  * Global infrastructure investment needs are estimated at $90 trillion through 2040 across power, water, roads, transit, and manufacturing (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * Portfolio examples include Anduril’s manufacturing facility (spanning 87 football fields), Waymo depot expansions, and SpaceX's $100 billion investment in Louisiana (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * Data centers can actually help lower residential electricity rates; a US study showed that for every 10% increase in data center capacity, residential electricity rates decreased by 40 basis points due to spreading fixed grid costs over more units of electricity (**Santiago Rodriguez**, **Alex Immerman**).

### Models, Applications, and Enterprise Adoption
* **Rapid Revenue Growth at the Model Layer:**
  * Combined annualized revenue for OpenAI and Anthropic has climbed significantly, well-surpassing net new revenue estimates for the greatest software companies in history (**Sarah Wang**).
* **Early Enterprise Diffusion:**
  * Live deployments at S&P 500 companies stand at 69%, but quantifiable impact sits at 30%, and tracked recurring impact is only at 2% (**Sarah Wang**).
  * Most enterprise exposure remains limited to Microsoft Copilot (**Sarah Wang**).
  * Forward-leaning large Fortune 500 companies allocate roughly 1% of headcount-equivalent spend to AI tools, whereas AI-native portfolio companies reach up to 10% (**David George**).
* **Power Users Pulling Away:**
  * Median AI vendor spending in the top 1% of users is roughly 8x the level of the top 10% (**Sarah Wang**).
  * In AI-native portfolio companies, top users spend $7,500 to $9,000 per month, compared to median users spending $200 to $400 monthly (**Sarah Wang**).
* **Measurable ROI and Cost Reductions:**
  * Chime reduced its cost-to-serve by over 10% annually for four years (compounding to nearly a 50% total reduction), supported by Decagon (**Alex Immerman**).
  * Shopify's AI sidekick helped increase the percentage of onboarded merchants reaching 5 orders within 15 days by 8% (**Alex Immerman**).
  * ServiceNow reported over $1 billion in AI annual contract value (ACV) and a 9x increase in agentic deployments (**Sarah Wang**).
  * Stripe's SAS customer data shows growth accelerating into 2026 across both young and mature businesses, a dynamic Stripe calls "the renaissance" (**Alex Immerman**, **Sarah Wang**).

### Agents, Costs, and Efficiency Gains
* **Plummeting Inference Costs & Agent Scaling:**
  * Agent token usage on Open Router experienced 14x growth (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * Context caching has drastically lowered costs (e.g., Hebia saw financial chat workloads become 10x cheaper to run) (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * Advanced routing (e.g., Data Bricks' smart router) solves more problems at 35% lower cost than the strongest individual model (**Sarah Wang**).
  * Fine-tuning smaller models (e.g., Elise AI fine-tuning a model to be 60% cheaper with lower latency) makes live audio use cases tenable (**Sarah Wang**).

### Consumer AI and Marketplaces
* **Consumer Adoption and Engagement:**
  * Just over 2% of US households have a paying subscription for AI (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * AI subscriptions exhibit "smiling retention curves" that flatline and actually rise as products improve (**Santiago Rodriguez**, **Alex Immerman**).
* **Marketplace Impact and Frictionless Commerce:**
  * While Amazon declined integration with Muse, Instacart integrated it, looking to capture incremental orders and GMV from automated agentic purchasing (**Santiago Rodriguez**).
  * Search engines like Google have remained resilient because high-monetizing ad terms (e.g., buying insurance, booking hotels) involve intent that AI has not fully automated on the user's behalf (**Alex Immerman**).

### Public and Private Software Markets
* **Public Software Biffurcation:**
  * Roughly 75% of public software companies are profitable, but only 30% are growing at 20% or more (**Sarah Wang**).
  * Cybersecurity (e.g., CrowdStrike), observability, and vertical software have held up significantly better than horizontal applications (**Sarah Wang**).
* **Late-Stage Private Champions:**
  * The top 6 private companies (Anthropic, OpenAI, Databricks, Stripe, Waymo, Revolut) have a combined last-round valuation of $2.4 trillion—larger than the combined market cap of all IPOs over the last 10 years excluding SpaceX ($1.7 trillion) (**Alex Immerman**).
* **Secondary Liquidity Dynamics:**
  * Employee participation in company-held tender offers tracked by Carta is only 58%, reflecting high employee conviction in business performance (**Alex Immerman**).
  * Discounts on secondary market transactions relative to last-round prices are essentially zero, contrasting with previous years (**Alex Immerman**).
