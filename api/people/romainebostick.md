## IMF MD on the Global Economic Outlook

- Channel: Bloomberg Live

- Speakers: Kristalina Georgieva (Managing Director, IMF); Romaine Bostick (Host, Bloomberg)

---

### TL;DR
Global economic growth is proving resilient at around 3%, supported by positive AI demand and emerging market strength, but faces persistent risks from sticky inflation, rising debt-servicing costs, and AI-driven financial stability concerns.

### Global Economic Outlook & Shocks
- **Growth Resiliency:** The global economy is hovering around 3% GDP growth, a massive achievement despite ongoing shocks, though projections will be revised to reflect high ongoing risks.
- **Opposing Forces:** Two main drivers are pushing in opposite directions:
  - A negative energy supply shock holding the economy back.
  - A positive AI investment demand shock lifting the economy up.
- **Emerging Market Strength:** Emerging markets and developing economies have built strong institutional buffers and monetary policy frameworks that match or exceed advanced economies.

### Inflation, Debt, and Fiscal Pressures
- **Persistent Inflation:** Inflation is stable but not resolving quickly, requiring central banks (including in Europe and the US) to maintain or increase tightening.
- **Climbing Debt-Servicing Costs:** Higher interest rates have increased debt-servicing burdens, making fiscal consolidation essential.
- **US Fiscal Deficits:** US debt levels have climbed to over $40 trillion, and while relying on growth to reduce debt is a strategy, the IMF advocates for gradually bringing deficits down.
- **Low-Income Country Debt:** The IMF is engaging in debt restructuring discussions—notably using a new playbook for Senegal—and utilizing the Global Sovereign Debt Roundtable to coordinate traditional, new, and private creditors.
- **Argentina's Transition:** Argentina has successfully moved from deficit to surplus and brought inflation down; the focus must now shift to translating macroeconomic stability into growth and employment.

### AI Risks and Energy Security
- **Financial Stability Front-Loading:** High expectations for AI create risks of front-loaded financial instability, circular financing, and leverage vulnerabilities if expectations are disappointed.
- **Global Value Chain Exposure:** US-centric AI concentration impacts integrated value chains globally, with visible stock market vulnerabilities in Korea and critical production in the Netherlands, Malaysia, Thailand, and Singapore.
- **Surging Energy Consumption:** AI is already consuming 3% of energy in Europe, and consumption is projected to triple by the end of the decade, making energy security critical.
- **Energy Shock Mitigations:** The 2025 energy shock was sustained through rapid responses:
  - Nigeria and India expanded refinery capacity quickly.
  - The US and Norway increased production.
  - Saudi Arabia and the UAE provided alternative routes around the Strait of Hormuz.
  - European renewables and reduced energy intensity helped absorb the shock.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **AI Energy Consumption Growth:** AI energy consumption will become three times higher by the end of the decade — Kristalina Georgieva.
- **Mid-October Forecast Revisions:** The IMF will release new global projections in mid-October that reflect continued high risks to the global economy — Kristalina Georgieva.

### Notable Quotes
> "The risks are high and uncertainty is the new normal."
> — Kristalina Georgieva

> "We always overestimate the positive impact of new technology in the short run. We underestimate it in the long run. What does it mean? It means that risks are front loaded."
> — Kristalina Georgieva

### Quantum Advantage and Practical Applications
* **Accelerated Timeline:** IBM remains on track to demonstrate quantum advantage using its hardware by the end of 2026 or mid-2026, supported by early progress from partners like the Cleveland Clinic and Oak Ridge National Laboratory.
* **Exponential Scale in Molecular Modeling:**
  * **Rapid Progression:** Krishna noted that IBM moved from modeling a 5-atom molecule in June 2025 to a 300-atom molecule by November. By April, they modeled a 12,000-atom molecule (half of the trypsin protein) and are currently pacing toward 30,000 atoms. 
  * **Computational Limits:** At the 12,000+ atom scale, molecular simulations can no longer be performed using classical supercomputers, marking a functional shift toward quantum reliance.
* **Target Industries:** Initial applications will focus on materials science and small-molecule drugs.
  * **Energy & Chemistry:** Key targets include magnetic materials, more efficient EV batteries, advanced lubricants, weather modeling, chemical plant flow simulations, and aerodynamics for aerospace.

### National Security and the US Quantum Foundry
* **Public-Private Funding:** The US federal government is investing $1 billion to establish a dedicated quantum chip foundry in upstate New York.
* **An Open Ecosystem:** IBM has committed to opening this quantum foundry to all US-approved allies and organizations (approximately three dozen companies).
* **The Geopolitical Race:** 
  * **US Leadership:** Krishna believes the US maintains a definitive lead over China in quantum technology, estimating the US is "ahead by a couple of years."
  * **Strategic Advantage:** Securing quantum supremacy is framed as a critical priority for both long-term economic competitiveness and national security.

### Driving Near-Term ROI through AI Integration
* **Beyond Personal Productivity:** Krishna argues that the real return on investment (ROI) for AI lies in changing corporate operating models rather than simple desktop productivity tools.
* **M&A Cost Synergies:** 
  * **Instant Profitability:** IBM utilizes AI to automate contract migration, sales automation, and revenue forecasting during mergers, yielding up to 10 percentage points of profit synergy on "day one" of an acquisition.
  * **Proven Case Study:** In its most recent acquisition, IBM successfully eliminated 60% of G&A (General and Administrative) expenses immediately upon closing.
* **Workforce Reallocation:** 
  * **Entry-Level Hiring Surge:** IBM tripled its college and entry-level hiring in 2026 compared to 2025, betting that AI-enabled junior employees will scale output rapidly.
  * **Structural Shifts:** Total corporate headcount is expected to remain stable, but the workforce will shift away from classic back-office roles and double down on value-driving areas like R&D, sales, marketing, and creative development.

### Project Lightwell and IBM’s Financial Outlook
* **Defensive Cyber Investments:** IBM has committed $5 billion toward "Project Lightwell," a security initiative designed to proactively identify and patch critical vulnerabilities in open-source software and the AI supply chain.
* **Market Outlook:** Krishna compares the current commercial runway of quantum processing units (QPUs) and cyber tech to where GPUs stood in 2015–2016. He expects to see significant revenue and margin returns within the next two to three years.
* **Capital Adequacy:** IBM remains highly active in debt markets, securing favorable rates. Krishna asserts that IBM possesses the internal capital and wherewithal to fund its strategic roadmap over the next five years.
