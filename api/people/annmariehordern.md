### TL;DR
QatarEnergy is navigating an unprecedented total shutdown of its oil and gas facilities due to disruptions at the Strait of Hormuz, cutting 17% of its LNG capacity. Despite project delays and force majeure declarations, the company remains committed to regional diplomacy, long-term expansion, and cementing its status as the world's leading LNG trader.

### Impact of the Strait of Hormuz Crisis on Production and Exports
- **Damage Assessment:** Hit to three facilities at Ras Laffan—two LNG trains and one GTL train.
- **Export Limitations:** Only very sparse shipments are exiting through the Strait (some via an Iranian route to Pakistan and a US route), yielding volumes so low they are statistically unmeasurable as a percentage point.
- **Supply Chain Bottlenecks:** Large prefabricated industrial modules (some 50m by 50m) manufactured in Asia cannot be tugged through the Strait, causing delays for expansion projects.

### Pipeline Alternatives and Regional Relations
- **Pipeline Rejection:** Economic and technical studies evaluated alternative pipeline routes through Syria, Yanbu (Saudi Arabia), Fujairah (UAE), and Oman. QatarEnergy decided against them because transporting natural gas via pipeline would require building redundant liquefaction terminals outside Qatar.
- **Long-term Peace:** Minister Al-Kaabi emphasized the necessity of peaceful coexistence with neighbors like Iran for future generations, aiming to build lasting economic ties that outlast hostilities.
- **Strait Longevity:** Al-Kaabi disputed suggestions by US officials that the Strait of Hormuz could become an obsolete waterway within two years, noting it is essential for the trade and survival of numerous Gulf nations.

### Ras Laffan Repairs and Global Market Impacts
- **Repair Timeline:** GTL train repairs are expected to finish by **Q1 2027**; the two LNG trains are targeted for return within **the next three years** (accelerated from an initial 3-to-5-year estimate by working directly with sole closed-box manufacturer FCI). Operations can return to normal within a couple of weeks once the Strait reopens.
- **Contractual Status:** All long-term Sales and Purchase Agreements (SPAs) are tied to force majeure clauses linked to specific trains, meaning QatarEnergy has no obligation to make up lost volumes from alternative global locations.
- **Global Energy Demand:** Unseasonably hot European summers have driven up cooling demand and power usage, depleting European reserves and threatening higher winter prices if the weather is harsh.

### International Projects and Trading Ambitions
- **Global Trading Expansion:** QatarEnergy aims to become **the largest LNG trader in the world by far** in the very near future, building upon the massive trading organization established in 2024.
- **US and Global Assets:** 
  - **Golden Pass (US):** First train has started shipments; both second and third trains are expected in full operation next year.
  - **Golden Triangle (US):** The world's largest ethane cracker (a joint venture with Chevron Phillips, where QatarEnergy owns 49%) is beginning commissioning and will start up in the coming weeks.
  - **Exploration:** Active in global exploration blocks including Namibia, Cyprus, Angola, Brazil, Argentina, and the US, with serious evaluations underway regarding potential entry into Venezuela.
- **Domestic Expansion Status:** North Field East (NFE) construction is proceeding well, with the first train expected to come online in the **first half of next year**, followed by North Field South (NFS) trains, though ongoing Strait access issues could cause localized delays.

## Chevron CEO Wirth on Energy  Market Volatility

- Channel: Bloomberg Live

- Speakers: Michael K. Wirth (Chairman & CEO, Chevron Corporation); Annmarie Hordern (Journalist, Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR** 
While tactical supply-and-demand responses have temporarily buffered the global energy market from geopolitical conflicts, Chevron CEO Mike Wirth warns that declining inventories could trigger severe market tightness by July or August. To mitigate geopolitical choke points, Chevron is expanding its Western Hemisphere advantages while selectively pursuing highly favorable new investments in the Middle East and developing off-grid gas-fired power solutions for AI data centers.

***

### Geopolitics and the Strait of Hormuz Bottleneck
*   **Strait of Hormuz Flows:** Chevron estimates that current oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz are closer to 3 million barrels per day (bpd), lower than the 7 million bpd cited by Energy Secretary Chris Wright and the 5 million bpd estimated by J.P. Morgan. 
    *   Flows are being maintained via high-risk nighttime transits with transponders off, supported by the U.S. military.
*   **The July/August Inventory Cliff:** While initial fears of $150-a-barrel oil have subsided due to demand destruction in Asia and strategic petroleum reserve (SPR) releases, global inventories are drawing down to "uncomfortable" levels. Wirth predicts the current market equilibrium can only hold until July or August 2026.
*   **Unstable "Ceasefire" Dynamics:** Wirth characterizes the current geopolitical state in the Middle East as "less shooting" rather than a true ceasefire, noting unreported military clashes and fatalities continue to occur at sea. 
*   **Long-Term Systemic Recovery:** Rebuilding global inventories, repositioning displaced tankers, and restoring shut-in production to reach normal flows of 20 million bpd will take "weeks and months, not days."

### Regional Portfolio Shifts & Middle East Strategy
*   **Insulated Geopolitical Footprint:** Chevron has significantly lower exposure to Middle East risks than its peers. Only 5% of Chevron’s total production is located in the Middle East (primarily in the Partition Zone between Kuwait and Saudi Arabia, petrochemicals in Saudi Arabia and Qatar, and natural gas in Israel), compared to up to 20% for industry peers.
*   **Opportunistic Middle East Expansion:** Despite current tensions, Chevron is actively negotiating new deals in the Middle East. Wirth notes that host nations are offering much more favorable commercial terms and balanced returns to attract foreign capital than they have historically.
*   **The Western Hemisphere Advantage:** The crisis underscores the structural security of the Americas. Blessed with abundant resources and deepwater ports, the U.S., Canada, and Latin America are poised to become increasingly vital to global energy security due to their lack of geographic choke points.

### Power Generation and the AI Data Center Boom
*   **No Merchant Grid Entry:** Chevron will not enter the power sector as a merchant generator or distributor to sell electricity to the public grid.
*   **Off-Grid Data Center Solutions:** Chevron is leveraging its extensive experience of generating 5 gigawatts (GW) of self-use power daily at its remote global facilities to partner with technology hyperscalers.
    *   **Monetizing Stranded Gas:** The company plans to construct dedicated, off-grid, gas-fired power plants to supply AI data centers in West Texas. This strategy utilizes abundant "stranded gas"—which sometimes trades at negative prices due to pipeline capacity constraints—and avoids raising electricity rates for public ratepayers.

### Political Engagement and Energy Policy
*   **Engaging with Trump 2.0:** Wirth notes a highly collaborative relationship with the current administration, citing open communication channels with Secretaries Chris Wright (Energy), Doug Burgum (Interior), Scott Bessent (Treasury), and Marco Rubio (State). This contrasts sharply with the minimal dialogue under the previous administration.
*   **Windfall Tax Opposition:** Wirth strongly opposes calls from some members of Congress for a windfall profit tax, pointing to the 1970s and 1980s U.S. case studies and the current investment decline in the U.K. as proof that such taxes reduce domestic supply and fail to generate projected revenues.
*   **Pragmatic Shifts in Energy Politics:** Wirth observes a growing bipartisan recognition of energy affordability and reliability over purely climate-centric messaging, noting that even some California Democrats are shifting toward more pragmatic, pro-drilling stances.
