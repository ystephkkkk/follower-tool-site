**TL;DR**
In a wide-ranging interview with Andrew Ross Sorkin at the 2026 DealBook Summit, Anduril Industries founder Palmer Luckey argued that the U.S. must overhaul its military and technological posture—from renaming the Pentagon to the Department of War and restarting nuclear EMP testing to building offshore industrial platforms—while warning that rapid AI-driven cyber exploits are outpacing legacy defenses.

### Cyberwarfare, AI, and National Preparedness
*   **AI-Driven Cyber Exploits:** Discussing ongoing infrastructure outages (including PlayStation) and Vice President J.D. Vance's emergency meetings in Washington, Luckey attributed the disruptions to advanced AI tools enabling rapid exploit creation. He noted that organizations, particularly in North Korea, are exploiting the mismatch between fast-moving attackers and outdated defense systems for financial gain.
*   **Personal Preparedness:** Luckey, who keeps the world's largest video game collection 200 feet underground in a decommissioned Air Force missile base, advised that every citizen should maintain three days of water, three days of dry food, and refill prescription medications 72 hours before running out to weather short-term grid or supply disruptions.
*   **AI Controllability & Autonomous Weapons:** Responding to Sorkin's questions on Anthropic and OpenAI, Luckey stated empirically that no major AI model is controllable at the model-use level, as every major model has been jailbroken within months. However, he distinguished general-purpose LLMs from Anduril's autonomous weapons AI, which analyzes radar, thermal imagery, and electromagnetic emanations, arguing smart weapons are far more reliable and reduce civilian casualties compared to "dumb" analog systems.

### Geopolitics, the "Department of War," and Project Meridian
*   **Renaming the Pentagon:** Luckey took credit for pushing the Trump administration (including transition head Howard Lutnick) to rename the Department of Defense to the Department of War. He cited five reasons: clearer deterrence, fiscal transparency (forcing explicit trade-offs between hospitals and war), clarity of purpose, morale ("the War Department won every war"), and acting as an "objector-repellant" to weed out ideologically misaligned employees.
*   **China, Iran, and Stockpiles:** Sorkin pressed Luckey on whether U.S. struggles in Iran and depleted munitions stockpiles invite Chinese aggression toward Taiwan. Luckey noted that Xi Jinping recently fired all his top generals, giving the U.S. breathing room, and defended President Trump's rhetoric on "indefinite" stockpiles as strategic deterrence akin to Nixon's "madman theory."
*   **Project Meridian (2050 Warfare):** Luckey co-leads Project Meridian, a 120-day Pentagon study run by the Mitre Corporation alongside Elon Musk, Newt Gingrich, and over 20 military and industry leaders reporting to Pete Hegseth. The public report focuses on neglected concepts for warfare in 2050 that lack existing champions, such as weather control (e.g., altering ocean precipitation to clear visibility).
*   **Defending Industry Ties:** Addressing criticism from retired Air Force Master Sergeant Wes Bryon—who called Meridian indicative of an "oligarchy"—Luckey dismissed the attack as partisan, noting Sorkin would never have leveled the same critique at Eric Schmidt serving on Joe Biden's Defense Innovation Board.

### Corporate Power, Starlink, and U.S.-China Tech Competition
*   **Starlink vs. Starshield in Ukraine:** Luckey argued corporate executives should not dictate foreign policy ("I'm a dog on a chain"). Defending Elon Musk's limits on Starlink in Ukraine, Luckey noted Russia threatened to destroy the entire global Starlink constellation if used for offensive strikes. He pointed out that the U.S. government refuses to give Ukraine access to Starshield (the DoD-owned equivalent) because it fears Russia will destroy it—making it untenable to expect a private company to take that risk.
*   **Ukraine & Europe:** Luckey stated he fully supports Ukraine and has been there personally, but argued no one in the West actually wants Ukraine to win—only "to not lose for as long as possible." He noted that since the war began, Germany has sent more money to Russia than to Ukraine, and smart thermostat data shows German indoor temperatures actually rose by one degree in winter.
*   **Nvidia and China:** Explaining his comment that "China wants Jensen [Huang] out, so I want him in," Luckey noted that 600,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs are currently sitting in a port because Beijing refuses import permits in favor of building a domestic silicon industry. He argued that ousting Huang—which knocked $1 trillion off Nvidia's market cap—weakens America.

### Nuclear Testing, Offshore Platforms, and Silicon Valley History
*   **Restarting Nuclear Testing:** Luckey advocated resuming nuclear tests not to measure blast yields, but to test broadband electromagnetic pulse (EMP) effects on modern electronics. Current military hardening guidelines are based on simulations for transistors thousands of times larger than those in modern smartphones, leaving U.S. forces vulnerable to a high-altitude nuclear air burst.
*   **Offshore Industrial Platforms:** Luckey revealed he has spent 10 years developing floating offshore platforms for industries blocked by state and local regulations, including metal ore processing, chemical engineering, oil refining, desalination, and potentially data centers.
*   **Public Executions:** Luckey defended his support for televised capital punishment as a libertarian check on state power, arguing that medicalizing and hiding executions enables government overuse, whereas public brutality forces accountability.
*   **Facebook Firing & Re-Partnering with Zuckerberg:** Luckey recounted being fired from Facebook three years after selling Oculus for $2 billion in 2014, attributing it to a $9,000 donation to a pro-Trump group despite receiving a "greatly exceeded expectations" performance review in H1 2016. While he still resents the Oculus lieutenants who pushed him out, he holds no grudge against Mark Zuckerberg (whom he only met about a dozen times) and recently partnered with Meta to integrate their tech into Anduril's IBAS (infantry vision augmentation systems) program for the U.S. Army.

**TL;DR:** In a wide-ranging conversation with Walter Isaacson, Anduril founder Palmer Luckey explains how getting fired from Facebook led him to build a product-first defense tech giant, why he believes autonomous weapons make warfare more humane, and why Steve Jobs’ iPhone design set human-computer interaction on a backwards course.

### Early Roots, Homeschooling & The $2B Oculus Sale
* **Homeschooling & Mod Retro:** Luckey credits homeschooling for allowing him to take high school and college credits simultaneously at ages 14–15, freeing up time to build VR prototypes in a camper trailer.
  * He recruited Oculus’s early hardware engineers—including first employee Chris Dicus—from *Mod Retro*, an online electronics forum he founded.
  * He was heavily influenced by tech-centric sci-fi like Jules Verne’s *20,000 Leagues Under the Sea* and *Star Trek*, noting he was born at the exact right moment to turn VR from sci-fi into reality.
* **Why Facebook Over Google or Microsoft:** When Luckey sold Oculus for roughly $2 billion in 2014 at age 21, he chose Facebook because it lacked its own OS or hardware platform and *needed* VR to succeed.
  * Facebook guaranteed $1 billion per year in R&D for 10 years ($10 billion total), but ultimately spent roughly $70 billion over the decade.
  * By contrast, Google shut down its VR program ~1.5 years after the Oculus acquisition, restarted it, and killed it again.
* **Meta’s Reality Labs Layoffs:** Luckey defended Meta’s recent 10% layoff at Reality Labs, noting the remaining 90% is still 10x larger than the next biggest VR competitor.
  * While calling *Meta Horizons* a "terrible product," he argued that cutting first-party gaming apps is healthy for the ecosystem, as Meta will no longer compete directly with third-party developers like Ubisoft, Rockstar, and Capcom.
* **Getting Fired & Side Hustles:** Fired from Facebook at 24 after donating $9,000–$10,000 to a Trump-supporting PAC, Luckey chose not to sue and spend five years as a "professional full-time victim," opting instead to start Anduril.
  * Anduril takes up 99% of his time, with 1% spent on side projects: manufacturing a high-end Nintendo Gameboy tribute via *Mod Retro* (featuring a sapphire screen lens and magnesium aluminum alloy shell) and co-owning a niche ice cream burrito company.

### How Steve Jobs "Ruined Everything"
* **The Low Skill Ceiling:** Responding to Walter Isaacson’s theory that human-computer interfaces drive technological progress, Luckey argued the industry has gone in the wrong direction since the iPhone.
  * The iPhone optimized for a 1-minute learning curve and 10-minute mastery, eliminating incentives for users to spend 1,000 to 50,000 hours mastering a tool for superhuman output.
  * Luckey admitted his own Oculus Rift followed this same low-barrier-to-entry model and argued that brain-computer interfaces like Neuralink are making the same mistake.

### Anduril, Autonomous Weapons & Deterrence
* **A Product-First Defense Model:** Luckey founded Anduril with Trae Stephens of Founders Fund (who had previously met with ~2,000 defense startups and made zero investments).
  * Unlike traditional contractors, Anduril operates like SpaceX or Palantir—investing billions of its own capital upfront (e.g., developing aircraft for 5 years before receiving a dime from the government).
  * Anduril’s AI-powered fighter jet, *Fury* (Air Force designation *FQ-44*), recently beat Northrop Grumman, Boeing, and Lockheed Martin for the U.S. Air Force’s Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) Increment One contract.
* **The Lattice Platform:** *Lattice* is the AI software brain powering Anduril’s 25+ hardware products (missiles, submarines, fighter jets, sensor towers), allowing every sensor to act as an eye for every other system.
  * Anduril employs as many people on Lattice as it does on all of its hardware products combined.
* **The Morality of Autonomous Weapons:** Luckey argued that autonomous weapons are the only rational moral choice because they reduce collateral damage and civilian deaths compared to "dumb" weapons.
  * Challenging U.S. military doctrine’s "human-in-the-loop" requirement (such as remotely piloted Predator drones), he noted that radio links create cyber vulnerabilities and that the U.S. has used autonomous radiation-seeking missiles since Vietnam.
  * He equated AI targeting to thousands-of-years-old autonomous traps (like pit traps designed to let foot soldiers pass while collapsing under horses/officers) and landmines, stressing that humans must still be held accountable for how force is deployed.
* **Deterrence Over Credit:** Luckey believes modern precision warfare has already reduced casualties by ~90% compared to World War II. Anduril’s ultimate goal is deterrence—meaning if the U.S. avoids a war with China over the next 20 years, Anduril succeeded, even if it receives little credit.

### AI’s Greatest Risk & Future Bets
* **Bioweapons & AI:** While generally anti-censorship, Luckey identified AI-enabled biological and chemical weapons as his #1 fear.
  * AI can already guide lab technicians ("meat robots") through pharmacological experiments without requiring PhDs, raising the risk of bad actors designing targeted bioweapons.
  * He advocated tampering with or restricting dangerous biological data outside approved institutions to prevent this.
* **Vertical Integration & Seasteading:** Asked by Isaacson to pick the next big breakthroughs, Luckey highlighted two areas:
  * **Ultra-cheap manufacturing:** Vertically integrating power generation, manufacturing, processing, and recycling to mirror the textile industry (which made fabric ~1,000x cheaper).
  * **Settling the oceans:** Using new material science to build maintenance-free mega-structures at sea that last for thousands of years.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Anduril Contract Wins:** "You're going to see us winning major programs I think on a regular cadence because investments that we've made over the last eight years are finally bearing out." — Palmer Luckey
* **The Future of War Casualties:** "We're going to get to a point where most of the casualties are going to be robots and systems and hardware rather than flesh and bone." — Palmer Luckey
* **$1,000 Cars:** "I think a world where you can buy a car for a thousand dollars is something that... is actually within our lifetime." — Palmer Luckey
* **Seasteading Comeback:** "We're going to get to the point where we're able to build mega structures at sea that persist for thousands of years without any significant maintenance... seasteading... will come back." — Palmer Luckey

## Palmer Luckey on Threats, Autonomy, and the Future of American Power

- Channel: West Point - The U.S. Military Academy
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2THslsOYzY
- Published: 2026-05-26

- Speakers: Palmer Luckey (Founder, Anduril Industries)

---

**TL;DR** 
To counter global threats like China, the U.S. must pivot from handcrafted, low-volume "exquisite" weaponry to high-volume, mass-producible autonomous systems. This transformation requires utilizing commercial manufacturing lines, adopting open software standards, deploying onboard drone autonomy, and developing reusable kinetic interceptors.

---

### 1. Redefining Geopolitical Threats: China vs. Ukraine
*   **The China Threat:** China is the primary strategic threat to the United States. Chinese President Xi Jinping is actively establishing national narratives to claim territories beyond Taiwan, specifically targeting the Ryukyu Islands (Okinawa), parts of the Philippines, and Vietnam.
*   **Avoiding the "Ukraine Overfit":** While the Russia-Ukraine conflict offers valuable lessons, the U.S. military must not design its future force structure solely around it. 
    *   Ukraine’s conflict is a unique hybrid of trench warfare and localized modern technology occurring between two adjacent nations with deeply interconnected borders.
    *   A conflict with China in the Pacific will look entirely different and will not be fought in trenches.
*   **Asymmetric Maritime Deterrence:** The U.S. does not need to match China’s manufacturing capacity one-for-one to protect Taiwan. By maintaining high-volume coastal denial systems, the U.S. and its allies can make any Chinese amphibious landing or occupation logistically infeasible.

### 2. Re-Industrializing Defense with Mass-Producible Design
*   **The World War II Manufacturing Blueprint:** During WWII, the U.S. succeeded because it designed weapons specifically to be built on existing automotive and agricultural assembly lines. 
    *   Military hardware was designed around the tooling, welding limits, and sheet-metal capabilities of car factories rather than requiring highly specialized, handcrafted defense manufacturing facilities.
*   **The Modern Procurement Crisis:** The U.S. currently manufactures high-cost, handcrafted weapons at an unsustainable rate, burning through critical missile stockpiles thousands of times faster than they can be replaced.
*   **The Factory Standard Test:** Future weapon systems must be designed for rapid, distributed manufacturing. Luckey argues that any critical deterrent weapon should be capable of being produced on existing automotive or industrial lines (such as Ford, GM, John Deere, or Caterpillar) with minimal worker retraining.

### 3. The Future of Autonomous Warfare and Counter-UAS
*   **Onboard Autonomy vs. Radio Links:** Radio-frequency links and fiber-optic cables on drones will soon be obsolete due to jamming and cost. 
    *   **Moore's Law Advantage:** Within three to four years, the cost of advanced onboard computer chips will drop below the cost of physical fiber-optic reels or radio systems.
    *   **Visual Navigation:** Autonomous drones will navigate and target visually, completely independent of GPS or vulnerable communication links.
*   **Networked Counter-UAS (C-UAS):** To defeat massive, simultaneous drone attacks, the military must integrate all sensors and effectors.
    *   **Interoperable Standards:** Proprietary, "stovepiped" defense systems must be replaced by open data standards, similar to commercial web technologies, allowing different hardware systems to share target tracks seamlessly.
*   **The Necessity of Reusable Kinetics:** The current practice of firing multi-million dollar or hundred-thousand-dollar single-use missiles to destroy cheap, mass-produced drones is economically unsustainable.
    *   The military must scale down the "fighter interceptor" model to small, high-speed, turbine- or rocket-powered interceptors.
    *   These interceptors must be capable of destroying targets and returning to base to be refueled, rearmed, and reused, preserving the expensive onboard seekers and airframes.

### 4. Leadership, Talent, and Emerging Warfighting Domains
*   **The Subterranean Domain:** Luckey identifies the Earth’s crust as the next major warfighting domain. 
    *   **Autonomous Tunneling:** While past U.S. and Soviet manned underground vehicle programs failed, modern onboard autonomy allows for small-diameter, unmanned digging vehicles ("snake trains") to transport explosives and materials underground without high excavation costs.
*   **Evaluating Talent and Leadership:** When recruiting and identifying leaders, Luckey emphasizes personal initiative over formal metrics:
    *   *The Crucial Question:* Luckey asks candidates, "What have you done that you didn't need to do?" to identify those who build and create outside of mandatory school or work requirements.
    *   *Decentralized Trust:* In fast-growing organizations, top executives must trust talented leaders to execute autonomously without micromanagement.
