### TL;DR
Pershing Square founder and CEO Bill Ackman discussed his strategy for Howard Hughes Corp., the capitalization of insurance venture Vantage, Pershing Square USA's market discount, AI frontier models, and a new brain-care institute inspired by his daughter's recovery.

### Howard Hughes & Vantage Insurance Strategy
* **Taking a Page from Buffett:** Pershing Square is replicating Berkshire Hathaway's playbook by utilizing Vantage—a specialty insurer and reinsurer closed in June—as a cash-flow-generating vehicle with low leverage.
* **Leadership & Asset Allocation:** Mark Risan (former CEO of Arch Capital Group) serves as executive chair of Vantage, alongside David Gansburg and Lucy Vetto. 
* **Treasury & Equity Split:** Fixed income assets were immediately sold upon closing; approximately 65% of Vantage assets are in short-term treasuries and 35% in a managed common stock portfolio, with common stock exposure ultimately targeted at 40% to 45%.
* **Howard Hughes Real Estate Restructuring:** 
  * Howard Hughes is shifting from 100% equity financing to bringing in third-party partners (such as Brookfield or Blackstone) to reduce equity commitment in real estate by up to 80%.
  * The restructuring aims to free up **$2 billion to $3 billion** in equity by the end of next year to redeploy into Vantage.

### Pershing Square USA (PSUS) & Market Dynamics
* **Fund Structure & Cost:** PSUS is a liquid public closed-end fund charging no incentive fee, offering retail investors access to Pershing Square's strategy at the lowest cost globally.
* **Performance & Deployment:** Net Asset Value (NAV) is down about 3% from the IPO (with ~240 basis points driven by IPO costs); capital has been fully deployed into "super durable growth companies."
* **Discount and Valuation Realities:** PSUS shares trade at a 25% discount to NAV; Ackman attributes this to a marketing and awareness challenge rather than underlying portfolio weakness, pointing to discounted entry prices on holdings like Meta, S&P Global (bought at 21x earnings vs. typical 30x), and Intercontinental Exchange (bought at 17x vs. 24x).

### AI, Frontier Models, and IPOs
* **Anthropic and OpenAI:** Ackman praised Anthropic's rapid revenue ramp and product quality (noting internal use of Claude), questioning whether frontier model companies can maintain market presence against open-source and open-weight models.
* **Capital Intensity vs. Durability:** Pershing Square avoids fast-growing, capital-consuming AI companies where investors bet on future line crossings, preferring predictable businesses like Microsoft, Visa, and Mastercard.
* **Catastrophic Risk & Regulation:** Ackman is less concerned about regulated labs like Anthropic or OpenAI causing systemic harm—arguing that massive financial and legal disincentives provide self-regulation—and more concerned about rogue actors exploiting open-source models without guardrails.

### Medical Innovation & New Brain Institute
* **Daughter Lucy’s Recovery:** Ackman detailed the severe brain hemorrhage suffered by his daughter Lucy, who lost the ability to speak, walk, and see light, but is making progress through aggressive care, nutrition, and experimental mitochondrial therapy (which restored light perception to 22,000 in one eye).
* **New Brain Institute:** Inspired by Lucy's journey, Ackman and his wife are building a new institute focused on delivering direct clinical care, leveraging data generated by emerging brain-interface technologies (such as Neuralink, Precision Neuroscience, and Synchron) combined with AI data-processing capabilities.

#### TL;DR
Bill Ackman breaks down his investment philosophy, the future of AI and market bubbles, and how a family medical emergency inspired him to build a world-class brain institute. He also discusses scaling Pershing Square, transforming Howard Hughes into a modern Berkshire Hathaway, and shifting away from traditional activism toward friendly dialogue and large-scale permanent capital.

### Personal Crisis & The Brain Institute
- **Daughter's Medical Emergency:** Bill Ackman’s daughter, Lucy, suffered a sudden brain hemorrhage due to an undiagnosed arteriovenous malformation (AVM) that caused 19 hours of brain pressure.
- **Resilience and Recovery:** Despite grim initial prognoses, Lucy has made remarkable progress in regaining cognition, speech, and mobility through round-the-clock family support, friends visiting daily, and aggressive rehabilitation.
- **Ackman-Oaksman Institute (AIOI):** 
  - Ackman and his wife Neri closed on a vacant 3.4-acre biotechnology building at 65th and 11th Avenue 60 days after discovering it, intending to build a world-class brain institute focused on rehab, recovery, and longevity.
  - The institute aims to solve systemic inadequacies in hospital nutrition, short insurance-funded rehab windows, and medical nihilism surrounding brain injury recovery.
  - Leveraging technology and AI (including future bionic vision glasses and AI speech therapy) to make advanced neurological recovery accessible to all patients.

### Investment Process & Public Markets
- **Research Methodology:** A two-person team conducts deep dives starting with SEC filings (10-Ks, 10-Qs), earnings transcripts, expert networks, and competitor analysis, followed by an independent peer review before committing capital.
- **Criteria for "Super Durable" Companies:** Focuses on simple, predictable, high free-cash-flow businesses with wide moats, strong balance sheets, and low risk of disruption.
- **The Checklist & Risk Management:** Following a major loss in 2015–2016, Ackman engraved investment principles on stone tablets and instituted strict checklists, an aversion to short selling, and a focus on asymmetric macro bets (e.g., predicting COVID-19 shutdowns and inflation).
- **Netflix Case Study:** Pershing Square initially bought Netflix on a subscriber dip, sold three weeks later when management abruptly shifted strategy to an ad-supported model (breaking the certainty thesis), and rebought the stock years later when it was cut in half after Netflix successfully won the streaming wars and executed on ads.

### AI, Bubbles, and the Venture Landscape
- **Assessing AI Disruption:** AI represents the fastest industrial revolution and pace of improvement ever seen, dramatically increasing the risk of disruption for dominant businesses.
- **AI Use Cases:** Ackman views AI as a powerful research tool (not used for financial modeling to avoid groupthink) and used it to help vet medical decisions during his daughter's trauma.
- **Venture Capital Bubbles:** Points to extreme private market froth—such as Series A rounds commanding $5B pre-money valuations and companies raising $50M at $400M valuations only to jump to $1B weeks later—driven by FOMO.
- **Advice to Founders:** Raise capital now while available, but treat every dollar as your own and maintain years of runway rather than burning cash expecting perpetual liquidity; a high-profile blow-up will eventually reset the market.

### Building a Modern-Day Berkshire Hathaway
- **Howard Hughes Transformation:** Transforming Howard Hughes from a real estate developer of master-planned communities (MPCs like The Woodlands in Houston and communities in Texas, Las Vegas, and Hawaii) into a conglomerate.
- **The Berkshire Playbook:** 
  - Modeled after Warren Buffett, Ackman and CIO Ryan are shifting cash generated from land sales ($300M+ NOI and $4B in Hawaii condos under contract) into a growing P&C specialty insurance and reinsurance business (Bantage Holdings).
  - Pershing Square manages the assets of the insurance float while the company maintains a permanent capital base with zero stock dilution.
- **Ownership Alignment:** Ackman owns ~47% of Howard Hughes, ensuring a long-term, unpressured strategic vision.

### Activism, Leadership, and Public Life
- **Shift Away from Activism:** Pershing Square has not made an activist investment since 2016; Ackman believes he no longer needs to run proxy contests because 3 million Twitter followers and a strong long-term track record grant sufficient influence to dialogue directly with CEOs.
- **Criteria for CEO Success:** Prefers leaders who have "done it before" (e.g., recruiting Brian Nicols to Chipotle) and assesses candidates based on passion, high energy, capability, and unyielding character.
- **Personal Reflections:** Reaches age 60 with a perspective that every bad experience yields a silver lining, viewing his career skills as providentially aligned to save his daughter and build a transformative brain institute.

## Bill Ackman: Here's What the Market is MISSING

- Channel: All-In Podcast
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_TJFqEhxQg4
- Published: 2026-06-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260603.html#video-_TJFqEhxQg4
- Speakers: Bill Ackman (CEO, Pershing Square Capital Management)

---

**TL;DR** 
Pershing Square CEO Bill Ackman discusses his shift toward backing durable, non-disruptable, founder-led businesses in the AI era, while outlining his strategy to transform the Howard Hughes Corporation into a long-term compounding machine modeled after Warren Buffett’s Berkshire Hathaway.

---

### Evolution of Activism and Investment Philosophy

*   **Prioritizing Business Quality:** Ackman’s core philosophy has evolved to focus intensely on long-term, durable, and non-disruptable growth. As Pershing Square's capital base grew, the necessity of holding concentrated, long-term positions eclipsed short-term liquid plays.
*   **Constructive Activism:** In its early days, Pershing Square had to wage public campaigns just to get corporate attention. 
    *   *The Wendy’s/Tim Hortons Precedent:* Early on, Ackman couldn't get a phone call back from the Wendy’s CEO. He partnered with Steve Schwarzman at Blackstone to write a public fairness opinion advocating for a Tim Hortons spin-off, which forced the spin-off and doubled their investment.
    *   *Modern Relationship Capital:* Today, Ackman’s reputation allows him to collaborate directly with S&P 500 CEOs constructively without aggressive public proxy battles. 
*   **The Public Market Shield:** Ackman views large, concentrated shareholders as crucial buffers for public companies. They provide management the coverage to make multi-year, long-term decisions that may hurt short-term quarterly earnings but build massive enterprise value.

### Navigating AI Disruption and the "SaaS-pocalypse"

*   **Undervalued Tech Giants:** While short-term capital chases chips, energy, and start-ups, legacy giants like Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon are being valued as "old-fashioned" businesses and are currently undervalued relative to their massive AI advantages.
*   **The "SaaS-pocalypse" Risk Profile:** Independent software companies relying on high-margin, niche software (e.g., charging $30,000 per year per seat) are at immense risk of AI-driven disruption. Conversely, platforms like Microsoft, which charge very low per-seat prices, have a highly defensible moat.
*   **Venture Underwriting in Late-Stage Companies:** Highly valued, late-stage private companies like SpaceX, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Palantir must be underwritten using venture frameworks rather than traditional discounted cash flow (DCF) models.
    *   *The SpaceX Moat:* SpaceX benefits from a near-monopoly on low-cost space launch systems, making it a critical infrastructure provider even for competitors like Amazon. Ackman invested in X, xAI, and SpaceX (encouraged by Ron Baron) via SPVs.
*   **Slow Enterprise AI Adoption:** Despite the massive hype, actual enterprise implementation of AI remains in the very early, pilot-heavy stages. At Pershing Square, AI is currently used strictly for legal, compliance, and back-office workflows.

### The Market "Rubber Band" and the Founder Premium

*   **The Rubber Band Effect:** Stock valuation serves as a tether. When valuations stretch too far from the fundamental present value of future cash flows—whether in a bubble or a panic—the "rubber band" inevitably snaps back. Recognizing this allowed Ackman to successfully call the market bottoms during the March 2020 COVID panic and again in late 2023.
*   **The Founder Advantage:** Founder-led companies possess a massive structural advantage over hired CEOs in navigating tech disruption. 
    *   *Long-Term Alignment:* The average S&P 500 CEO has a tenure of 3 to 4 years and is incentivized to avoid mistakes. Founders, having their entire wealth and reputation tied to the firm, have the internal authority to make massive, risky, but necessary pivots (e.g., Mark Zuckerberg acquiring Instagram and WhatsApp).

### Replicating Berkshire Hathaway via Howard Hughes Corp

*   **The Insurance Float Engine:** Warren Buffett built Berkshire Hathaway by using insurance float (premiums collected upfront) to invest in compounding common stocks. Because traditional investment talent rarely works for insurance companies, Buffett's compounding model is rarely replicated successfully.
*   **The Howard Hughes Pivot:** Born out of the Chapter 11 restructuring of General Growth, the Howard Hughes Corporation owns massive, master-planned cities (such as Summerlin in Las Vegas, comprising 26,000 acres). 
    *   *The Strategy:* Rather than reinvesting all real estate cash flows back into development, Ackman is converting the business into a compounding machine. Over the next week, Pershing Square will begin redirecting Howard Hughes' cash flow into building a brand-new insurance operation to compound assets over the next 50 years.
*   **Three Paths to Invest with Pershing Square:**
    *   *Pershing Square Management Company:* An asset-light, high-margin royalty on the compounding of the underlying funds.
    *   *Pershing Square Holdings (PSH):* The public fund holding Ackman's core stock ideas, currently trading at an 18% discount to NAV.
    *   *Howard Hughes Corporation:* A play on the next Berkshire Hathaway compounding model, currently trading at a steep discount to its liquidation value.
