## Vertically Integrated, Horizontally Open | AI Factory Insider Ep 5

- Channel: NVIDIA
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mFv4VRoFj-k

- Speakers: Kaushik Shirhatti (VP AI Factory, NVIDIA); Pradeep Gupta (VP Industries Solutions Architecture, NVIDIA); Stephen Jones (CUDA Architect, NVIDIA)

---

### TL;DR
NVIDIA's accelerated computing platform combines vertical integration for maximum hardware-software performance with a horizontally open ecosystem across 17 industry verticals, all powered by the 20-year-old CUDA platform and its 900+ CUDA-X libraries. 

### Architectural Philosophy: Vertically Integrated & Horizontally Open
* **Vertical Integration (Accelerated Performance):** Designed as a full-stack computing problem to drive maximum performance through extreme hardware-software co-design.
    * *Hardware-Software Continuity:* Hardware and software co-evolve across generations (from Hopper to Blackwell to Vera Rubin, while maintaining support back to Ampere and Turing).
    * *Layered Architecture:* Core foundational layers (like `cuDNN`, `cuBLAS`, and `CUTLASS`) remain common across workloads, minimizing reinvention.
* **Horizontal Openness (Ecosystem Flexibility):** Open at every layer to hardware vendors, middleware, orchestration software, model builders, and domain-specific library developers.
    * *Definition of "Open":* Freely available to use without asking permission from NVIDIA; permits building proprietary IP and businesses on top.
    * *Open Standards & Open Source:* Follows open standards (e.g., C++) and contributes actively to open-source projects (submitting code to PyTorch; open-sourcing libraries, Nemotron models, and datasets).

### Industry Applications & Workloads
* **Multi-Industry Breadth:** A single toolset with emergent behaviors powers diverse applications across 17 industry verticals.
* **Concrete Industry Use Cases:**
    * *Healthcare & Biopharma:* Automated data processing in hospitals, image reconstruction for MRI/CT machines, and AI-driven drug discovery (reducing timelines from 10 years to under 2 years via libraries like `BioNeMo` and `cuEquivariance`).
    * *Retail:* Recommendation systems, search, and indexing (`RecSys` engine).
    * *Manufacturing:* Physics simulations.
    * *Finance:* Fraud detection, alpha signal discovery, portfolio optimization (`cuDF` for data processing, `cuGraph` for contextual relationship mapping).
    * *Smart Cities:* Intelligent infrastructure solutions (`Metropolis`).

### CUDA & CUDA-X Libraries: The Foundation
* **Role of CUDA:** The magic connecting applications to GPU hardware, functioning as a deep platform stack from top-level frameworks down to bottom-level hardware controllers.
* **Evolution & History:** 
    * Celebrates its 20th anniversary (version 1.0 launched 18+ years ago).
    * Early debugging limitations led architect Stephen Jones to write the universal `printf` function for CUDA in his first week.
    * Performance debugging began via a manual Excel spreadsheet tracking counters, which ultimately led to the creation of NVIDIA's visual profiler.
* **CUDA vs. CUDA-X Libraries:** 
    * *CUDA:* The underlying platform/machine.
    * *CUDA-X Libraries:* Reusable pieces of IP, data patterns, and optimized software (900+ libraries total, built by both NVIDIA and partners, including `cuBLAS`, `cuFFT`, `cuRAND`, `Riva`, and `MONAI`).
    * *Interoperability:* Lower-level and higher-level libraries interlock (e.g., `cuDNN` depends on `cuBLAS`, which depends on `CUTLASS`), allowing classical scientific computing mechanisms to augment seamlessly with AI disciplines.

### Agentic AI & CUDA Skills
* **Agentic Software Development:** Agents operate with a brain-like harness (context, observation, reasoning, action) and rely on tools and sub-agents.
* **CUDA-X Libraries as Agent Skills:** Every CUDA-X library is being exposed as a "skill" for AI agents (e.g., exposing `cuDF` data-processing pipelines or visual profiler debugging tools to agents).
* **Impact on Developer Jobs:** 
    * Software engineers are shifting from writing manual code to writing prompts and acting as "agents managers."
    * Agents serve as a force multiplier, enabling developers to accomplish significantly more by pulling in domain-specific libraries automatically.
    * Deep domain expertise and full-stack system design skills remain critical for guiding agents and deploying systems efficiently.

## Tokenomics 101: What Are Tokens & Why They Matter | AI Factory Insider Ep. 4

- Channel: NVIDIA
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MkbepPfaeMU
- Published: 2026-09-01
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260901.html#video-MkbepPfaeMU
- Speakers: Kaushik Shirhatti (VP, AI Factory, NVIDIA); Shruti Koparkar (Sr. Manager, Product Marketing, NVIDIA); Tue Tran (GTM Strategic Ops, NVIDIA)

---

### TL;DR
Tokens are the fundamental unit of AI output and the bridge between AI factory infrastructure and business ROI, requiring organizations to balance intelligence, speed, and unit economics across different use cases.

### Defining Tokens and Tokenomics
* **What is a Token:** Defined by Shruti Koparkar as the most basic unit of AI, representing a set of numbers corresponding to four characters (for language), a pixel (for images), or molecular structures (for biology models).
* **What is Tokenomics:** Described by Tue Tran as the economics of generating a token—examining the cost, demand, and value of tokens to determine the business spread between cost and value.
* **Token Inequality:** Shruti Koparkar noted that tokens are not all equal; their value depends on intelligence (model complexity and context window) and the rate of arrival (interactivity/speed).

### Matching Tokens to Workloads and Use Cases
* **High-Frequency Trading (Financial Services):** Tue Tran explained that capital markets require extremely fast, simple tokens (machine-grade speed with minimal latency) where speed outweighs deep reasoning.
* **Long-Term Investing (Financial Services):** Tue Tran contrasted high-frequency trading with 5-to-10-year investment theses, which require hours of processing time, heavy context windows, hundreds of reports, and sub-agents reasoning together.
* **Retail Video Monitoring:** Tue Tran cited a Metropolis blueprint where a model monitors streaming video to generate a single-token "yes/no" answer for theft detection.
* **Retail Video Captioning:** Tue Tran noted that the same retail chain generating advertising or customer video captions produces 100 to 200 tokens per chunk as an overnight batch workload.
* **Telco Model Right-Sizing:** Shruti Koparkar shared that a telco customer discovered only **8% to 16%** of tasks required a premium model, with the rest transitioned to post-trained open models or APIs.

### Sizing Demand and Supply
* **Layered Demand Math:** Shruti Koparkar outlined a framework starting with back-of-the-napkin math (users, requests per day, tokens per request), factoring in multipliers like reasoning models, agentic loops, and cache hit rates.
* **KV Cache and Prefix Matching:** Shruti Koparkar explained that KV cache acts as short-term memory, while prefix matching avoids recomputing prompts that the model has already seen (leading to high cache hit rates in enterprise search).
* **The AI Factory Engine (Supply):** Tue Tran used a car engine analogy, where efficiency depends on the model layer (dense models vs. mixture-of-experts requiring high-speed GPU networking), the hardware engine, and the orchestration software layer.
* **Infrastructure SKUs:** Shruti Koparkar highlighted NVIDIA's NVL72 flagship systems for Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models, alongside HGX systems and RTX PRO Servers to match different enterprise footprints.
* **Software Tooling:** Shruti Koparkar and Kaushik Shirhatti mentioned open-source tools including **NeMo Switchyard** (for intelligent routing based on cost and model fit), **NeMo Relay** (for observability), and **Dynamo** (the operating system for the AI factory).

### Monetization and Strategic Implementation
* **Monetization Avenues:** Shruti Koparkar identified three main monetization paths: selling tokens directly (cloud model), building AI-native products, and infusing AI into existing products or internal operations.
* **Internal Tooling and Getting Started:** Tue Tran emphasized building internal estimation tools and advised enterprises to start small by involving both business teams (defining use cases and utility) and IT teams (selecting infrastructure).
* **Avoiding Token Tsunamis:** Shruti Koparkar advised focusing on outcomes and ROI rather than arbitrary token limits, utilizing efficient models (like Nemotron 3 Ultra) to minimize tokens per task.

### Notable Quotes
> "The only thing they care about is when the business outcome lands, and that comes when the first token lands. And so if you want to tie the infrastructure and AI factory investment to business return on investment, the bridge is the output, and the outputs are the tokens."
> — **Kaushik Shirhatti**

> "A token is the most fundamental, the most basic unit of artificial intelligence."
> — **Shruti Koparkar**

> "At this rate, like we might see the word being the word of the year for 2026... it is a combination of token and economics, and really it is a review, an examination of, um, the, the economics of generating a token."
> — **Tue Tran**


## Securely Deploy Autonomous Agents in Your AI Factory | AI Factory Insider Ep. 3

- Channel: NVIDIA
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8TQYkjViaBQ
- Published: 2026-08-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260803.html#video-8TQYkjViaBQ
- Speakers: Kaushik Shirhatti (VP, AI Factory, NVIDIA); Anne Hecht (Sr. Director of Product Marketing, NVIDIA)

---

### TL;DR
Autonomous agents that are persistent, autonomous, and self-evolving are driving a massive shift from simple gen AI chatbots to agentic AI, with 28% of enterprises now seeing sustainable ROI. NVIDIA and its ecosystem are supporting this growth through secure open architectures, custom models like Nemotron, and a blend of open and frontier models.

### Agentic AI: Evolution and Key Characteristics
- **The Shift from Gen AI:** Technological advancements, particularly harnesses from partners like OpenClaw, have unlocked "useful AI" that can learn, evolve, and use skills without requiring users to be engineers.
- **Enterprise ROI Breakthrough:** **28% of enterprises** now see immediate, sustainable, and consistent ROI on AI projects, a major jump from previous pilot phases.
- **Three Defining Traits of Agents:**
  - **Persistent:** Agents maintain context and remember prior user interactions and preferences.
  - **Autonomous:** Agents can take action independently on a user's behalf; they receive projects rather than just handling back-and-forth prompt responses and can even critique their own results.
  - **Self-Evolving:** Agents can discover and ingest new skills (such as PowerPoint generation or NVIDIA CUDA-X libraries) to teach themselves capabilities they lack.
- **NVIDIA Internal Metrics:** NVIDIA runs **4 trillion tokens a day** across its operations.

### The Financial and IT Perspectives
- **CFO and Tokenomics:** 
  - CFOs track token utilization and marginal costs.
  - Infrastructure investments are heterogeneous, spanning on-prem, local compute/inference (e.g., GB10, DGX Station), data centers, colocation, and the cloud.
- **IT and CISO Governance:**
  - **Managing Agents:** Agents must be managed like employees—assigned specific roles, limited network and information access, and controlled permissions.
  - **NVIDIA's Approach:** The company started conservatively and gradually expanded digital assistant permissions under CISO and IT guidance.
  - **The Power of Connections:** An agent is only as good as its connections (e.g., email, calendars, CRM, SharePoint). NVIDIA mandates strict boundary rules, such as an agent viewing a calendar for openings but requiring human approval to schedule against another person's calendar.
  - **Shadow AI:** IT teams must establish adoption strategies to combat Shadow AI (employees uploading company roadmaps to public models) which has become the new shadow IT.
- **End-User Experience:**
  - Users benefit from enterprise agreements that protect data privacy and confidentiality (e.g., uploading lab work to corporate AI without fear of data exposure to public models).
  - Designing a seamless user experience is exceptionally hard under the hood ("easy is hard"), allowing non-technical users to focus on innovation while infrastructure teams handle guardrails.

### Anatomy of an Agentic System
- **The Four Building Blocks:** According to NVIDIA’s GTC presentation by Jensen, an agent equals a **harness + model + skills**.
- **The Harness:** Acts as the orchestrator of the workflow and agent lifecycle, imposing governance, observability, context, and skill utilization (e.g., LangChain, Hermes, OpenClaw). Typically runs on CPU infrastructure.
- **The Brain:** The underlying AI model handling context, observation, planning, reasoning, and action. Runs on GPU infrastructure.
- **Skills:** Domain-specific tools and knowledge bases (such as PowerPoint creation or Biopharma CUDA-X / cuVS vector search skills) that agents can ingest.
- **Models:** A mix of frontier and open models; open models can be customized, fine-tuned, and post-trained for specific tasks, lowering costs and improving accuracy.

### Security Architectures and Confidential Computing
- **OpenShell:** 
  - An open-source project initiated in March by NVIDIA with contributions from Red Hat, SAP, Google, and other enterprise platform companies.
  - Operates as a sandboxed environment with governance and policy capabilities kept *outside* the sandbox so agents cannot alter their own operational policies.
- **Secure Agent Workspace:** A reference design showing how to deploy OpenShell agents and confidential computing based on NVIDIA's internal AI digital assistants.
- **Confidential Computing:** 
  - Protects models in use (protecting model weights) and data in use, spanning data at rest, in motion, and in use.
  - NVIDIA pioneered bringing confidential computing to accelerated GPU computing.
  - Adopted by partners like Google (bringing Gemini on-prem via Dell using confidential computing) and Apple (protecting consumer data).

### Frontier vs. Open Models and Ecosystem Collaboration
- **Dual Strategy:** Enterprises need a combination of open models and proprietary frontier models (an "and," not an "or").
- **Open Source Parallels:** Open source in AI mirrors the trajectory of Linux in the 1980s, serving as the foundational operating system for compute.
- **Nemotron:** NVIDIA's own proprietary/open model seeing phenomenal global adoption, enabling partners to post-train for higher accuracy, greater efficiency, and lowered costs.
- **Open Secure AI Alliance:** Formed recently with over 50 members (following Jensen joining Satya Nadella's open letter on transparency), aiming to drive open, transparent research for AI security, safety, and harness/model optimizations.

> — Anne Hecht

> "Think about your chatbot as a very young in-career employee that you have to coach along the way. Agentic agents are agents that have that same domain expertise, but now they know how to work within your enterprise and they have judgment and they are able to take more action."
> — Anne Hecht

> "You're not just prompting a model when you write into that context window, you are prompting an entire system. That includes the harness, the skills, the model, the governance layer."

## How NVIDIA Runs Its Own AI Factory | AI Factory Insider Ep. 2

- Channel: NVIDIA
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jpsq_-1kJTo
- Published: 2026-07-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260716.html#video-Jpsq_-1kJTo
- Speakers: Kaushik Shirhatti (VP, AI Factory, NVIDIA); Nic Borensztein (Distinguished Solution Architect, NVIDIA); Jon Fernandez (Director, Enterprise AI Compute, NVIDIA)

---

**TL;DR**
Scaling enterprise AI requires moving beyond hardware to Enterprise Validated Designs (EVDs) that integrate the full software stack; NVIDIA’s internal AI factory demonstrates this at scale, processing 4 trillion tokens monthly and 200 million inference requests daily.

### The Software Layer: Enterprise Validated Designs (EVDs)
*   **The "Five-Layer Cake" Analogy:** While Enterprise Reference Architectures (ERAs) cover the bottom three layers—energy, chips, and infrastructure—the EVD covers the top layers: models and applications.
    *   **Reliable Foundation:** ERAs establish the networking, compute, and Kubernetes layers.
    *   **Application Management:** EVDs define how agents, inference engines, and knowledge retrieval systems scale on Kubernetes.
*   **The Data Flywheel Lifecycle:** EVDs facilitate the full lifecycle of AI development:
    *   **Development:** Data curation, training, and model tuning.
    *   **Deployment:** Managing agent skills, harnesses, and production monitoring.
    *   **Continuous Improvement:** Capturing production traces and payloads to feed back into the next development cycle.
*   **Ecosystem Integration:** NVIDIA partners with Independent Software Vendors (ISVs) to create an "App Store" experience, providing "opinionated" full stacks where all components are pre-tested for compatibility.

### Scaling NVIDIA’s Internal AI Factory
*   **Massive Internal Adoption:** NVIDIA operates its own AI factory using EVDs to support 40,000 employees.
    *   **Volume:** The factory serves 4 trillion tokens per month with 99.9% availability.
    *   **Growth:** Internal demand for inference is growing at 40% month-over-month, totaling 200 million requests per day.
*   **Key Internal Use Cases:**
    *   **Chip Nemo:** A specialized agentic system used by 5,000+ hardware engineers for chip design. It handles proprietary file formats and non-public vocabulary.
    *   **IT Ticket Deflection:** Agents empower employees to self-service technical problems, moving from traditional support tickets to autonomous conversation-based resolution.
    *   **Knowledge Worker Productivity:** The "everyone is a developer" trend allows non-technical staff to automate workflows using AI agents.

### Security and the "Secure Agent Workspace"
*   **The Perceive-Reason-Act Loop:** NVIDIA defines the agentic workflow as a loop where the system perceives context (retrieval/memory), reasons via the LLM, and acts through tool calls.
*   **Secure Agent Workspace (SAW):** A reference architecture designed to run agents autonomously but safely.
    *   **Isolation:** Uses Virtual Machine (VM) isolation to create a "sandbox" for agents.
    *   **Network Perimeter:** Establishes strict policy enforcement around where agents run to prevent them from "running wild" or damaging adjacent systems.
*   **Confidential Computing:** A future-looking shift toward "zero trust" AI.
    *   **IP Protection:** Allows frontier AI labs to deploy models on-premises in an encrypted state that is "provably impossible to crack."
    *   **Data Custody:** Ensures the enterprise maintains full custody of inference payloads while the model provider’s intellectual property remains protected.

### ROI, Tokenomics, and the Move to On-Prem
*   **Drivers for On-Prem AI:**
    *   **Regulatory Compliance:** Critical for healthcare and finance where data mobility is restricted.
    *   **Tokenomics:** As AI projects move from Proof of Concept (POC) to scale, the cost of SaaS/cloud token budgets often makes owning the full stack on-prem more cost-effective.
*   **Measuring Impact:** NVIDIA prioritizes use cases with "clean statements of ROI," such as calculating engineering hours saved through automated customer support and ticket deflection.
*   **The Adoption "S-Curve":** Internal adoption typically starts slow during the pilot phase but spikes rapidly once tools are integrated directly into existing workflows.
