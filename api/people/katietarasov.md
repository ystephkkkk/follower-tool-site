### TL;DR
SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won explains that AI's explosive growth is driving massive demand for memory, prompting SK Hynix to invest heavily in fab expansions—including a $720 billion buildout plan—despite severe near-term supply constraints and high "chipflation."

### AI Demand and the Memory Super-Cycle
* **AI as a Growing Child:** **Chey Tae-won** likens current AI to a four-year-old child that will require exponentially more memory, knowledge, and experience as it matures into an adult.
* **Explosive Volume Forecasts:** **Chey Tae-won** projects that AI agents will scale 77-fold within five years, driving total memory production capacity demands up nearly five times within ten years.
* **Balanced Conglomerate Portfolio:** **Chey Tae-won** notes that while SK Hynix is currently the most important subsidiary, SK Group's broader portfolio strategically incorporates energy and telecommunication companies to support future AI data centers.

### Supply Bottlenecks, "Chipflation," and Customers
* **Lead Times and Shortages:** **Chey Tae-won** states that expanding manufacturing capacity requires a 4-to-5-year lead time, leading to a severe market crunch where customers are requesting double their previous volume.
* **The Hardest Year Ahead:** **Chey Tae-won** warns that next year (following the interview) will be the worst year for memory shortages because memory companies are not yet prepared to match surging demand.
* **Rising Prices (Chipflation):** **Chey Tae-won** acknowledges that memory prices have jumped 40% to 50%, forcing downstream consumer electronics, PC manufacturers, automakers, and companies like Apple to raise prices for society.
* **Key Partnerships and Customers:** **Chey Tae-won** identifies Nvidia and Jensen Huang as their most important customer and partner in building the AI ecosystem, while also maintaining major relationships with hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Meta) and TSMC CEO C.C. Wei.

### Managing Expansion Risk and Supply Constraints
* **Mitigating Overbuilding Risks:** **Chey Tae-won** explains that SK Hynix avoids historical memory-bust overbuilding cycles by strictly verifying that demand exists before expanding, utilizing long-term agreements, joint venture fabs, and "memory as a service" risk-sharing models.
* **HBM Production Trade-offs:** **Chey Tae-won** notes that producing High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) consumes more than four times the wafer space of normal DRAM, heavily eating into total capacity.
* **Token Cost Target:** **Chey Tae-won** states that SK Hynix's ultimate target is lowering the token cost for AI, which he currently views as too high.

### Global Footprint and Fab Expansion Strategy
* **Prerequisites for Fab Locations:** **Chey Tae-won** stresses that building a memory fab requires water, power, land, and an underlying ecosystem of 600 to 700 supporting material, chemical, and service companies.
* **Global Footprint Evaluation:** **Chey Tae-won** confirms that study teams are actively evaluating potential locations globally—including the U.S. (such as their Indiana project) and other regions—to address memory shortages based on where the right resources and ecosystem align.
* **Customer vs. Government Pressure:** **Chey Tae-won** notes that while U.S. customers strongly desire fabs built on U.S. soil, he has not yet spoken directly with the government, driving decisions primarily through business demand.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Chey Tae-won** predicts that AI agent usage will grow 77-fold within five years compared to 2025.
* **Chey Tae-won** predicts that total memory volume demand will increase to nearly five times current production capacity within ten years.
