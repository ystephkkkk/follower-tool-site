## Sam Altman: ‘It’s Hard To Imagine the AI Revolution Without Jensen’ | Silicon Valley & The World

- Channel: Semafor
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oi-E6xB4wSU
- Published: 2026-11-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261116.html#video-oi-E6xB4wSU
- Speakers: Sam Altman (CEO, OpenAI); Jensen Huang (Former CEO, Nvidia); Reed Albergotti (San Francisco Bureau Chief, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR:** Sam Altman addressed the ouster of Nvidia's Jensen Huang, defended the massive debt- and capex-backed buildout of AI infrastructure despite voter backlash and memory shortages, and argued that the AI industry must commit to keeping humans in control of the future rather than rushing into unmonitored recursive self-improvement.

### Jensen Huang, Nvidia, and Market Dynamics
* **Jensen Huang's Ouster:** Altman expressed strong personal empathy for Huang, referencing his own board ouster roughly three years ago. He called Huang a "hero" and noted that while heroes have flaws, "it's hard to imagine the AI revolution without Jensen."
* **Nvidia's Financial Guarantees:** Nvidia guaranteed $105 billion for OpenAI's data center in Ohio. Altman affirmed that Nvidia is a "behemoth" whose guarantees people still trust, and stated that AI demand and revenues are growing strongly enough that the broader industry will be fine.
* **OpenAI IPO & Investor Demand:** Responding to Anthropic going public this week, Altman said OpenAI will go public "at some point."
  * **Trade-offs:** Being private makes it easier to navigate high-stakes, consequential decisions around safety, though the public as a whole deserves exposure to AI's value creation.
  * **Market Appetite:** Altman dismissed concerns about OpenAI being the "Lyft to [Anthropic's] Uber," noting that over the past year there has been "way too much investor demand and not enough supply."

### Supply Chain, Memory Shortages, and Data Center Opposition
* **Chip "Double Counting":** Altman clarified his closed-door comments to Samsung executives, explaining that when there was only one main chip provider, wafer demand was easy to gauge. Now, multiple chipmakers are talking to the silicon supply chain assuming OpenAI, Anthropic, and others will buy *all* of their chips.
* **Shifting Bottlenecks:** OpenAI's supply chain constraints constantly shift between memory, rack assembly, data center capacity, and random components like PCB laminate.
* **The Memory (DRAM) Squeeze:** Addressing backlash over his comments on home computer RAM, Altman said he wants people to have RAM for home tinkering, but noted that years ago he warned the world needed $7 trillion in capex for the chip and memory supply chain.
  * Because the industry didn't build enough—due to decades of boom-and-bust cycles where memory makers "invest $20 billion and make $19 billion"—DRAM prices have jumped roughly 5x to 7x.
* **Data Center Backlash & Debt:** Following a "blue wave" where all but one data center referendum bill passed, Altman attributed voter opposition to a terrible industry sales pitch ("you are going to live like a Labrador") and deep-seated fears over jobs and human agency.
  * He defended debt-funded data center buildouts by pointing to unprecedented actual revenue growth and great margins, noting that businesses are actively coming to OpenAI after seeing what AI can do for their products.

### AI Safety, Recursive Self-Improvement, and Politics
* **"Pragmatic Centrist" Security:** Addressing corporate espionage—including a hidden microphone placed in his office and foreign agents targeting engineers—Altman described OpenAI's approach to security and safety as "pragmatic centrists." They neither build in total secret nor treat AI like a normal tech rollout.
* **Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI):** OpenAI is using its current models to develop new models faster, but is *not* pushing a button for unmonitored RSI. Altman stated OpenAI is holding back on pushing further into RSI as a tactic to ensure humans stay in control of the future.
* **External Evaluators:** OpenAI is working with embedded external evaluators, including METR (referenced as "Meter" in the transcript), to get outside opinions on system safety and robustness.
* **Political Engagement:** Altman reiterated his July 2025 comment about feeling "politically homeless," saying he hopes both parties move toward a culture of innovation. He noted he last spoke with Donald Trump at the China State Dinner.

### Products, Data Lock-In, and AI for Science
* **Dots & Data Portability:** When asked by moderator Reed Albergotti about data lock-in for "Dots" (a cloud-based memory product), Altman agreed in principle that users should be able to view, modify, and export their data (as they can with ChatGPT memory). However, he noted that Dots' nuanced understanding of a user's life is harder to export than an iPhone camera roll or iMessages.
* **AI for Science:** OpenAI announced an expansion of free compute for academic researchers. Altman noted that after chatbots and coding agents, AI is now driving breakthroughs in math theorems, physics, chemistry, material science, and chip design.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **OpenAI IPO:** OpenAI will go public "at some point." — *Sam Altman*
* **Memory Production:** Memory (DRAM) production will ramp up significantly. — *Sam Altman*
* **Data Center Buildout:** Data centers will still get built despite current voter opposition (potentially further out in the desert), and public sentiment will change over time. — *Sam Altman*
* **Scientific Acceleration:** The next couple of generations of AI models will "surprise people on the upside" as an engine of scientific discovery, potentially allowing humanity to compress 10 to 100 years of science into a single year. — *Sam Altman*
* **Quality of Life:** People in the not-too-distant future will look back at 2026 and think "they had it really rough" compared to the quality of life enabled by AI-driven scientific discovery and robotics. — *Sam Altman*

## DeepMind Chief Demis Hassabis Says Google’s Still Winning AI Talent | Semafor Tech

- Channel: Semafor
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hb9JPW_DkpQ
- Published: 2026-06-24
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260624.html#video-hb9JPW_DkpQ
- Speakers: Demis Hassabis (CEO, Google DeepMind); Reed Albergotti (Tech Editor, Semafor)

---

**TL;DR** 
Despite escalating industry competition and high-profile departures, Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis remains confident in Google's leading research bench and its multi-pronged, simulation-based path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).

---

### The AI Talent War and the Path to AGI
* **Industry Talent Dynamics:** While rival labs are aggressively poaching top minds, Hassabis emphasizes that talent movement between leading entities is normal in a "ferociously competitive" market. 
    * Google DeepMind still maintains the largest and deepest research bench in the world.
    * Historically, over 90% of the foundational breakthroughs powering the modern AI industry—including Transformers and reinforcement learning systems like AlphaGo—originated from Google Brain or DeepMind.
* **A Multi-Pronged AGI Strategy:** Google is not relying solely on scaling text-based models to achieve AGI. 
    * The company is betting on a diverse ecosystem of models, including Gemini (multimodal foundation), Omni, and Veo (generative media).
    * True AGI requires an understanding of the physical world of "atoms," which is essential for advanced robotics and smart-glass assistants.

### AI's Impact on Creativity and the Creative Industries
* **Evolution of Creative Tools:** Creative tools have evolved from simple generation to highly granular, iterative editing.
    * **Live Editing:** Users of Google's media models (like Imagen and Omni) can now edit specific sections of an output using natural language commands rather than regenerating an entire piece from scratch.
* **Democratization vs. Professional Leverage:** AI lowers the barrier to entry, allowing new creators to break into industries with fewer gatekeepers. 
    * For professionals, these tools allow creators to iterate "10x faster," enabling them to test complex, expensive ideas cheaply.
    * Hassabis warns that "lazy" usage of AI dilutes the creative process, but innovative usage elevates it.
* **The Need for New Economic Models:** Just as Spotify and YouTube's Content ID created new financial models for the music industry, the tech and creative sectors must develop fresh business models to manage IP attribution and payouts in the AI era.

### Deepfakes, Safety, and Global Governance
* **Standardizing Provenance with SynthID:** To combat misinformation and deepfakes, Google DeepMind developed **SynthID**, an imperceptible, unhackable digital watermarking tool.
    * SynthID is embedded into all Google-generated music, images, and videos.
    * Google has open-sourced the technology, and major competitors like OpenAI and NVIDIA have adopted it. Hassabis advocates for provenance watermark detection to become a regulatory requirement.
* **Escalating Global Risks:** As the world approaches "the foothills of the singularity," the threat landscape will expand beyond cybersecurity.
    * Hassabis warns of biological, nuclear, and systemic risks arising within the next two years.
    * He calls for an international standards body to evaluate and test the guardrails of frontier AI systems before they are deployed.

### The Neuroscience of AGI: Memory, Imagination, and Simulation
* **Reconstructing Reality:** Hassabis's landmark 2007 neuroscience research established that memory is not a recording, but a reconstructive process sharing the same brain mechanisms as imagination.
    * AI video models (like Veo) mirror this by reconstructing 3D physical worlds based on prompts, representing a system-level parallel to human cognitive processing.
* **The Einstein Test:** To prove an AI is genuinely creative, it must pass the "Einstein Test"—formulating a completely novel, mathematically sound hypothesis about physical reality (such as the theory of relativity) from a set of limited, historical data.
* **Simulations as the Ultimate Goal:** The core value of AGI lies in its ability to run thousands of complex simulations to find optimal pathways.
    * This methodology powered AlphaGo and is currently driving breakthroughs in scientific fields like drug discovery (via Isomorphic Labs) and protein folding (AlphaFold).
    * Hassabis envisions using learned simulations to model highly complex, historically unquantifiable systems like global economics and weather patterns.
