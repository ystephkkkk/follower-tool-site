## Learn 90% of charisma in under 60 minutes

**TL;DR**
Charisma is a learnable skill—not an innate trait—rooted in balancing warmth and competence (which together drive 82% of first impressions) to trigger oxytocin and dopamine in others. By using purposeful body language, vocal confidence, playful conversational hooks, and strategic mirroring, leaders and professionals can build immediate trust and influence without faking authenticity.

---

### The Science of Charisma: Warmth, Competence, and Brain Chemistry
* **Warmth as a Lubricant for Competence:** Vanessa Van Edwards notes that warmth and competence make up 82% of our impressions of people. Smart professionals often over-index on competence, which backfires because competence without warmth breeds suspicion.
  * Warmth is not "bubbly extroversion" or fake smiling; it shows up as vulnerability, humor, sarcasm, or even stumbling with words (e.g., Elon Musk’s unpolished vulnerability or Mark Zuckerberg’s everyday-guy demeanor).
* **Rejecting "Just Be Yourself":** Both Sam Parr and Vanessa agree that "authenticity" and "just be yourself" are unhelpful advice for people trying to improve. Instead, charisma is about honing your own distinct flavor of communication.
* **The First-Minute Rule:** First impressions are nearly irreversible; your first minute matters more than your first 30 minutes because the primitive brain is assessing friend vs. foe.
  * **Oxytocin (Connection):** Triggered in the first 3–5 seconds of eye contact, visible open hands (signaling no hidden weapons), and physical touch. A handshake produces roughly 3 hours' worth of eye-contact oxytocin.
  * **Dopamine (Motivation & Energy):** Triggered verbally by breaking social scripts and creating "information gaps" (e.g., Sam opening the show with a magic trick his daughter taught him, or Howard Stern asking, "Want to hear the weirdest thing that ever happened to me?"). Vanessa advises using information hooks every 1–2 minutes when speaking.

### Mastering Conversation: Hooks, Small Talk, and Playfulness
* **Entertainers vs. Appreciators:** Vanessa challenges Dale Carnegie’s advice to just ask lots of follow-up questions. While *Entertainers* love being the center of attention and answering questions, most VIPs are *Appreciators* who spend all day talking and want to be entertained or hear your stories.
  * Sam shares a story of dining with a ~$30 billion AI pioneer who spent the entire dinner asking Sam about his basic AI use cases because he was tired of being interviewed himself.
* **Dr. Dan McAdams’s Three Levels of Connection:**
  * **Level 1 (General Traits):** Basic facts ("What do you do?"). Vanessa recommends swapping this for, *"Working on anything exciting these days?"*—a question she honed over 20 years.
  * **Level 2 (Personal Concerns):** Motivations, values, and goals. Transition here quickly by asking about goals tied to time boxes (quarters, New Year's, birthdays) or what someone is learning (e.g., books).
  * **Level 3 (Self-Narrative):** The story someone tells themselves about themselves.
* **Playfulness and Trigger Words:** Keep a mental list of 20–50 "trigger words" (topics you enjoy) to steer conversations. Halting boring small talk with playful guessing games (e.g., guessing where someone is going on vacation or joking about baby names) triggers dopamine for both parties without requiring loud extroversion.
* **Being "Chalant":** Sam and Vanessa emphasize that enthusiasm—the physical display of passion—is contagious. Citing a dating study by Monica Moore, Vanessa notes that availability signals make people more attractive than physical looks alone, both romantically and professionally.

### Vocal Confidence, Language, and Leadership
* **Contagious Emotions & "Calm Assertive" Leadership:** Vanessa cites an fMRI study where participants smelled sweat from skydivers and "caught" their fear in their amygdala. Sam connects this to his experience at age 21 training an 80-pound pit bull ("Jake's dog"), where he learned that leaders must be *calm assertive* rather than high-energy reactive.
* **Eliminating Uptalk:** Using an accidental question inflection at the end of statements (e.g., stating a $10,000 price like a question) signals nervousness and deception, switching the listener's brain from listening to scrutinizing.
  * Men often do this in the first 10 seconds of a call/meeting while trying to sound warm; women often do it during the most nerve-wracking parts of a pitch.
* **Maximum Resonance Point:** Every voice has a 10-to-12-note range. Speaking on the out-breath at the lower, relaxed end of your range projects confidence—without overcorrecting to an unnatural extreme.
* **Purposeful Language (Behavior Cues):** In a prisoner’s dilemma study, groups told they were playing the "Wall Street game" shared an average of 1/3 of their profits, while groups told they were playing the "Community game" shared 2/3. Leaders should audit their sent emails and intentionally use achievement words (*win, succeed, master*) for performance days and collaborative words for brainstorming days.

### Reading People, Hiring, and Mirroring VIPs
* **Hiring "Ambitious Heroes":** Vanessa looks for candidates whose self-narrative is overcoming hurdles through grit.
  * **Behavioral & Hypothetical Questions:** Ask about the *most recent* hurdle they overcame (so it’s unrehearsed) or give them a real-time logistical hypothetical.
  * **The Luck Test:** Sam asks candidates, *"Do you feel lucky?"* Vanessa confirms research shows people who feel unlucky feel out of control and literally spot fewer opportunities.
  * **The Character Question:** From 10 years of research (Question #7 of 9 in her book), Vanessa asks: *"What book, movie, or TV character is most like you?"*—and warns never to argue with their answer, as it reveals how they predictably operate.
* **Finding Your Flavor:** Rather than mimicking one celebrity, watch the top 10–20 TED Talks to see which delivery style resonates with you.
* **Mirroring VIPs:** To charm high-status individuals without seeming like a clown (and with good, non-manipulative intent), mirror them on three planes:
  * **Non-verbally:** Match their animation, gestures, and pause lengths (e.g., matching a "2-second pauser").
  * **Verbally:** Adopt their exact lexicon (e.g., saying "clients" instead of "customers" if they do).
  * **Tonally:** Match their volume and speaking speed.

### TL;DR
High-performance coach Joe Hudson breaks down the mental, emotional, and nervous system patterns of elite tech founders with hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr, explaining how to untangle self-reliance, conflict avoidance, and negative self-talk through counter-intuitive emotional acceptance and weekly experimentation.

### Mind, Heart, and Nervous System: The Tri-Level Framework
* **The Three Levels of Transformation:** Real personal or professional change requires addressing three distinct systems rather than just intellectual understanding:
  * **The Mind (Prefrontal Cortex):** The easiest level for high-achievers to grasp intellectually, though knowing what to do rarely translates to doing it.
  * **The Heart (Mammalian/Emotional):** The emotional center where underlying fears and avoided feelings must be processed.
  * **The Nervous System (Reptilian):** The primal, physical layer (triggering a physical startle response to a Slack message or a late arrival) which changes not through a-has, but via repetition and expanding baseline pleasure.
* **The Self-Reliance Pattern:** Traced back to childhood where individuals learned they had to fend for themselves and others (e.g., dealing with critical or addicted parents), creating adults who:
  * Struggle to ask for help, own their desires, or announce when they are hurt.
  * Harbor a complicated relationship with authority—both rebelling against external authorities and refusing to fully step into their own.
* **The Golden Algorithm of Emotion:** The emotion you are trying to avoid is the exact emotion you end up inviting into your life by the way you try to avoid it.
  * *Example:* Avoiding the feeling of helplessness by over-grinding leads to burnout, poor decisions, and ultimately, a deeper state of helplessness.
  * *Solution:* Fall in love with the emotions you are avoiding; treat them as messengers and signals rather than enemies.

### Rewiring Self-Talk and Negative Thought Loops
* **Abusive Self-Talk Patterns:** High-achievers are frequently their own worst abusers, trapped in loops of overt criticism ("Why did you do that?"), endless pressure ("You've got to do this now"), or subtle undermining ("You can do it, Mike").
* **Reaction Over Content:** Trying to change the self-talk directly is inefficient; instead, change your *reaction* to the self-talk by treating it playfully or as bad management (e.g., responding to an internal critique with, "Oh, I see how scared you are. Prove it.").
* **Debunking the Narrative:** List the top 20 things the negative voice in your head says and find at least 7 concrete ways each is false (or ask friends to help prove it wrong).

### The Psychology of Founders and Conflict Avoidance
* **Conflict as Progress:** Avoiding conflict in a marriage or business is equivalent to ignoring a blinking warning light on a factory machine; conflict is the exact mechanism through which efficiency and alignment are gained.
* **Organizational Health Metrics:** Joe Hudson evaluates corporate health using just two metrics: how quickly an organization has hard conversations, and how quickly it resolves them.
* **Conducting Hard Conversations:** 
  * Do not solve the problem for the person (which is just a manifestation of CEO self-reliance); instead, ask them to present a plan to solve it by tomorrow.
  * Prioritize understanding their context via open-ended questions before delivering feedback.
  * Express your own vulnerability (e.g., "I feel like we've lost trust and that sucks for me") rather than making it entirely about attacking the other person.
* **The Psyche of Top 1% Performers:**
  * Exceptional fast-paced CEOs rarely second-guess themselves because the velocity of decision-making demands decisiveness.
  * Approximately 60% to 70% of high performers harbor an underlying paradox: they simultaneously feel superior to everyone else (conscious mind) and deeply broken (underneath), using achievement to prove they are not broken.

### Practical Weekly Experiments for Growth
* **The 10% Enjoyment Experiment:** Take what you are already doing and find a way to enjoy it 5% to 10% more without changing your actual actions, doing this roughly 5 times a day for a week.
* **The Do-Only-What-You-Enjoy Week:** Commit to doing only what you enjoy for a week; when forced to confront an unenviable task (like taking out the trash), figure out how to genuinely enjoy the process rather than enduring it.
* **The Gratitude Shift:** Consciously look around every day and thank 5 people for what they did for you to actively dismantle the self-reliance pattern.
* **Visualization of the Worst-Case Scenario:** Like samurai or stoics visualizing their own death or the destruction of their company, thoroughly feel the worst-case outcomes (such as homelessness or corporate collapse) so that fear no longer dictates your business decisions.
