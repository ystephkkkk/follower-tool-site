### TL;DR
High-performance coach Joe Hudson breaks down the mental, emotional, and nervous system patterns of elite tech founders with hosts Shaan Puri and Sam Parr, explaining how to untangle self-reliance, conflict avoidance, and negative self-talk through counter-intuitive emotional acceptance and weekly experimentation.

### Mind, Heart, and Nervous System: The Tri-Level Framework
* **The Three Levels of Transformation:** Real personal or professional change requires addressing three distinct systems rather than just intellectual understanding:
  * **The Mind (Prefrontal Cortex):** The easiest level for high-achievers to grasp intellectually, though knowing what to do rarely translates to doing it.
  * **The Heart (Mammalian/Emotional):** The emotional center where underlying fears and avoided feelings must be processed.
  * **The Nervous System (Reptilian):** The primal, physical layer (triggering a physical startle response to a Slack message or a late arrival) which changes not through a-has, but via repetition and expanding baseline pleasure.
* **The Self-Reliance Pattern:** Traced back to childhood where individuals learned they had to fend for themselves and others (e.g., dealing with critical or addicted parents), creating adults who:
  * Struggle to ask for help, own their desires, or announce when they are hurt.
  * Harbor a complicated relationship with authority—both rebelling against external authorities and refusing to fully step into their own.
* **The Golden Algorithm of Emotion:** The emotion you are trying to avoid is the exact emotion you end up inviting into your life by the way you try to avoid it.
  * *Example:* Avoiding the feeling of helplessness by over-grinding leads to burnout, poor decisions, and ultimately, a deeper state of helplessness.
  * *Solution:* Fall in love with the emotions you are avoiding; treat them as messengers and signals rather than enemies.

### Rewiring Self-Talk and Negative Thought Loops
* **Abusive Self-Talk Patterns:** High-achievers are frequently their own worst abusers, trapped in loops of overt criticism ("Why did you do that?"), endless pressure ("You've got to do this now"), or subtle undermining ("You can do it, Mike").
* **Reaction Over Content:** Trying to change the self-talk directly is inefficient; instead, change your *reaction* to the self-talk by treating it playfully or as bad management (e.g., responding to an internal critique with, "Oh, I see how scared you are. Prove it.").
* **Debunking the Narrative:** List the top 20 things the negative voice in your head says and find at least 7 concrete ways each is false (or ask friends to help prove it wrong).

### The Psychology of Founders and Conflict Avoidance
* **Conflict as Progress:** Avoiding conflict in a marriage or business is equivalent to ignoring a blinking warning light on a factory machine; conflict is the exact mechanism through which efficiency and alignment are gained.
* **Organizational Health Metrics:** Joe Hudson evaluates corporate health using just two metrics: how quickly an organization has hard conversations, and how quickly it resolves them.
* **Conducting Hard Conversations:** 
  * Do not solve the problem for the person (which is just a manifestation of CEO self-reliance); instead, ask them to present a plan to solve it by tomorrow.
  * Prioritize understanding their context via open-ended questions before delivering feedback.
  * Express your own vulnerability (e.g., "I feel like we've lost trust and that sucks for me") rather than making it entirely about attacking the other person.
* **The Psyche of Top 1% Performers:**
  * Exceptional fast-paced CEOs rarely second-guess themselves because the velocity of decision-making demands decisiveness.
  * Approximately 60% to 70% of high performers harbor an underlying paradox: they simultaneously feel superior to everyone else (conscious mind) and deeply broken (underneath), using achievement to prove they are not broken.

### Practical Weekly Experiments for Growth
* **The 10% Enjoyment Experiment:** Take what you are already doing and find a way to enjoy it 5% to 10% more without changing your actual actions, doing this roughly 5 times a day for a week.
* **The Do-Only-What-You-Enjoy Week:** Commit to doing only what you enjoy for a week; when forced to confront an unenviable task (like taking out the trash), figure out how to genuinely enjoy the process rather than enduring it.
* **The Gratitude Shift:** Consciously look around every day and thank 5 people for what they did for you to actively dismantle the self-reliance pattern.
* **Visualization of the Worst-Case Scenario:** Like samurai or stoics visualizing their own death or the destruction of their company, thoroughly feel the worst-case outcomes (such as homelessness or corporate collapse) so that fear no longer dictates your business decisions.

### Philanthropy & Skate Park Infrastructure
- **Tony Hawk Foundation:** 
  - **Founding Context:** Started in **2002** with his brother and sister to support kids in underserved areas who lacked safe, legal places to skate. 
  - **Scope:** Has contributed to close to **1,000 skate parks** to date.

### The Price of Greatness & The 900
- **Obsession over Accolades:** Hawk notes his childhood was defined by a relentless, singular focus on learning new tricks rather than chasing contest wins or magazine covers.
- **Financial Reality Check:** During his early professional career, Hawk had **$640 in the bank** and bought a Honda scooter to get to the skate park independently.
- **The 1991 Slump:** Following a popularity boom driven by *Back to the Future* (1985), Hawk's income dropped by **half every month** in 1991; his Airwalk shoe sponsor cut his monthly payments from **$1,500 to $1,000, then $500, and eventually $300** within a single year, forcing him to pay mortgages by doing odd jobs and Hollywood stunt consulting.
- **The 900 Landmark (1999):** 
  - Landed the historic 900 on live ESPN during a best-trick event after **20 minutes** of attempts.

### Video Game Empire: Tony Hawk’s Pro Skater (THPS)

- **The Buyout Offer:** Right before the game's release, Activision offered Hawk a **$500,000 buyout** to surrender future royalties, which he declined to "let it ride."
- **Explosive Success:** 
  - By the release of the fourth game, **four out of the top 10 best-selling video games** were *Tony Hawk's Pro Skater* titles.
  - Culminated in a single royalty payout meeting where an Activision executive slid a check across the table for **$4.5 million**.

### Current Business Ventures & Investments
- **Birdhouse Skateboards:** Founded in **1992** with partner Perry Wheeler; survived bleak early years, hit peak profitability and cultural mainstreaming between **1999 and 2005**, and maintains its classic **$60 deck price point** despite low profit margins.
- **900 Films:** Video production company handling media projects, commercials, and work with brands like Liquid Death.

- **Angel Investments:** Invested alongside Silicon Valley figures in notable companies including **Nest**, **Blue Bottle**, **DocuSign**, and **Events.com** (nearing an IPO).

### Aging, Culture, and Skateboarding Longevity
- **Skate Culture & Media:** Recalls making DIY zines in **1982** (selling them for **$50 cents** at Del Mar Skate Ranch) before the era of skate magazines like *Thrasher* and *Transworld*.
- **Aging and Stretching:** Hawk shares specific pre-skate warm-up routines (such as deep kneeling and joint rotations) to combat the physical toll of skating approaching age **58**.
- **Skate Park Etiquette:** Identifies prime skate park hours for older skaters as **8:00 AM to 11:00 AM**, avoiding peak crowds and high-impact street skating runs that risk police intervention (such as a **$150 ticket** he received in Oceanside in **1990** for skating the boardwalk).

### The CEO Hardcore Playbook: Incentives & Operations
*   **Option Drop:** Shared by Martin Basiri (Founder of Applyboard & Passage). 
    *   *Mechanism:* Rather than relying on static company values, Basiri uses instant equity rewards ("option drops") on the spot—awarding 50,000 options for great ideas or impromptu late-night work—to reinforce desired behaviors in real time.
*   **Friction Removal Service:** Used by OpenAI.

### Communication & Focus Hacks
*   **One-Bit Communication:** Utilized by the Siemens CEO and Jeff Bezos.
    *   *Siemens Approach:* The CEO responds to emails with exclusively "okay" or "no," holding zero recurring calendar meetings; if more detail is needed, employees must talk to him immediately in person.
    *   *Bezos Approach:* Bezos famously forwarded random customer complaints to executives with a single question mark ("?"), signaling the executive team to investigate potential systemic machine failures rather than just addressing isolated anecdotes.
*   **Single-Tasking Enforcement:** Utilized by Peter Thiel at PayPal.
    *   *Mechanism:* Thiel forced extreme focus by physically walking out of the room if employees attempted to bring up any topic outside of their single assigned primary problem.
    *   *The Output Law:* Thiel's philosophy is that "focus is convex"—moving from 80% to 90% focus doesn't just add 10%, it doubles your impact and separates top performers from the rest.

### Resource Culling & Talent Cloning
*   **Continuous Culling:** Utilized by Jack Welch at GE.
    *   *Rules:* Every business division had to be #1 or #2 in its market within one year, or it was fixed, sold, or closed; additionally, Welch fired the bottom 10% of company performers every single year.
*   **Employee Cloning:** Utilized by Jimmy Donaldson (MrBeast).
    *   *Mechanism:* In Beast Industries' early days, new key hires shadowed Donaldson from the moment he woke up until he went to sleep for six months straight, turning them into exact mental proxies who knew how he would make every decision.

### The Cost of Greatness vs. "Just Being Great"
*   **The Kevin Kelly Framework:** As discussed via David Perell's interview with Wired co-founder Kevin Kelly, achieving historical greatness requires an extreme, unbalanced life. Kelly consciously surrendered the path of greatness to live a flexible, exploratory life as a "gadfly."
*   **The Mother Teresa & Historical Legacy Reality:** True long-term legacy is rarely bought through money or corporate success; centuries later, people forget billionaires, while remembering inventors, artists, and humanitarians.
*   **The Shift to Daily Character:** Shaan Puri and Sam Parr conclude that instead of chasing abstract, multi-generational corporate greatness (which carries a brutal personal price tag, such as missing out on young children's lives), the better target is "active greatness"—showing up with character, kindness, and excellence in everyday, micro-moments.

### The Cargill Empire: Silent Dominance at Planetary Scale
* **Scale and Scope:** Cargill is the largest private company in America, 88% owned by the Cargill family, generating $150B a year in annual revenue.
    * The company has produced more billionaires in a single family than any other entity in history.
    * Their operations touch nearly every part of the modern food supply: seeds, fertilizer, grain elevators, shipping barges, meat packing, salt, and corn syrup.
* **Logistics Monopoly:** The company began 160 years ago (pre-Civil War) by building physical grain elevators next to railroads, evolving from a grain storage middleman into a planetary-scale logistics titan.
* **Operating Systems:** The family enforces an 80/20 rule (reinvesting 80% of profits, paying 20% as dividends) and employs professional CEOs rather than family managers, maintaining a secretive, low-profile corporate culture ("wealth whispers").

* **Family Meetings:** Shaan Puri highlights the importance of intentional family meetings—treating household culture and financial strategy like formal board meetings rather than keeping children in the dark about money.
* **Legacy Structures:** Both hosts discuss how dynastic families (such as the Hearsts and Rockefellers) utilize ironclad legal trusts, clear values, and mutual respect to survive across generations.
* **The Project vs. Empire Dichotomy:** Sam Parr notes a fundamental temperament divide between founders who treat businesses as temporary "projects" to build and sell versus those laying brick-by-brick foundations for multi-generational "empires."

### Jevons' Paradox and Technological Disruption
* **The Economic Principle:** Named after James Jevons, the paradox states that as technology makes resource use more efficient, total consumption and demand increase rather than decrease (e.g., steam engines increasing total coal usage, or the cotton gin dramatically increasing slavery and cotton output).
* **AI and Code:** Sam Parr argues that AI will make code creation significantly cheaper, driving a massive, mind-boggling explosion in total software demand rather than a net-negative reduction in jobs.
* **Historical Parallels:** The hosts compare current anti-AI sentiment and worker anxiety to historical Luddites smashing mechanical looms during the Industrial Revolution, arguing that human desire to expand and fill space always generates new, unforeseen industries.

* **Frederick Tudor ("The Ice King"):** Shaan Puri shares the story of Tudor, who shipped blocks of winter ice from Boston to South America before refrigeration existed, creating demand by giving local bartenders free ice to popularize cold drinks.
* **The Trap of Over-Architecting:** Shaan Puri reflects on advice from former Twitch boss Emmett Shear, noting that founders frequently waste time building elaborate, over-engineered solutions (like Trello-board bookshelves) instead of "simply solving the problem."

### Predictions & Forward Calls

## I'm 80 and I wasted 25 years of my life. Don't make my mistake. - Howard Marks

- Channel: My First Million
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kp__G0eUyIc
- Published: 2026-07-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260715.html#video-Kp__G0eUyIc
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-Chairman, Oaktree Capital Management); Sam Parr (Host, My First Million); Shaan Puri (Host, My First Million)

---

**TL;DR:** Legendary investor Howard Marks emphasizes that superior results come from "second-level thinking" and the courage to act despite fear, noting that true success is defined by the autonomy to live life on your own terms.

### AI and the Future of Investment Judgment
Howard Marks recently updated his stance on AI, moving from skepticism to a recognition of its unprecedented potential, specifically regarding its **autonomy**.

*   **Distinctive Capabilities:** Unlike previous technological shifts (railroad, internet) which were merely tools for speed, AI possesses autonomy—the ability to figure out *how* to do a job without being told.
*   **The "Defrocking" of Mediocrity:** AI will likely "unfrock" investors who lack true talent, much like indexation exposed active managers who couldn't outperform averages.
*   **The Human Edge:** Marks believes experienced investors remain essential for two reasons:
    *   **The "Hair on the Neck" Test:** The ability to sense "bad people" or "wrong" deals through undefinable intuition and judgment.
    *   **Lack of Historical Data:** AI relies on pattern recognition; humans are still required to navigate unprecedented events where no training data exists.

### Investing Through Crisis and Fear
Marks recounts raising $11 billion for a distressed debt fund in 2008, highlighting that the best opportunities often feel the most terrifying.

*   **Building the Ark:** The best time to raise money is *before* a flood. Oaktree raised their massive fund in 2007–2008 because they saw market discipline disappearing, not because they knew exactly when the crash would happen.
*   **Acting Without Conviction:** Marks admits that during the Lehman Brothers collapse, he was not 100% confident. He argues that acting without trepidation in a crisis suggests something is wrong with the investor.
*   **The Rationality of Trepidation:** If you wait until there is nothing to fear, the opportunity has already passed. Success requires moving forward despite uncertainty.
*   **Quantitative Safety:** In 2008, Oaktree invested $500M/week for 15 weeks based on the logic that even if the companies they bought ended up being worth only 25% of their purchase price, the debt would still break even.

### The Architecture of Successful Partnerships
Marks and his co-founder, Bruce Karsh, have worked together for 39 years without a single fight. Marks attributes this to two core pillars:

*   **Shared Values:** A partnership fails if one person is a "cowboy" and the other is a "chicken." Disparate ethics or risk tolerances create friction during market shifts.
*   **Complementary Skills:** Marks and Karsh avoid overlap; Marks handles the external communication and "memos," while Karsh manages the money and stays out of the spotlight.
*   **Mutual Respect:** Acknowledging that the partner is better at certain tasks removes the "financial maximizer" ego that often destroys professional relationships.

### Success and Intentionality
Despite his legendary status, Marks admits he spent his first 25 years in the industry "unintentionally," making haphazard decisions based on luck and convenience.

*   **The Only Real Success:** Quoting Christopher Morley, Marks defines success as "living your life your own way." 
*   **The Late Start to Intentionality:** Marks did not start Oaktree until he was 49. He encourages younger people to find work that plays to their strengths and avoids their weaknesses, rather than following societal or parental pressure.
*   **Parenting via Autonomy:** Marks advocates for letting children make choices early—even incorrect ones—to build their own decision-making muscles, rather than asserting parental superiority.

### Insights on Warren Buffett and Reading
Marks shares observations from his long-standing relationship with Warren Buffett, particularly regarding the late Charlie Munger.

*   **The Buffett-Munger Dynamic:** Munger’s greatest contribution was moving Buffett away from "cigar butt" investing (buying cheap, low-quality companies for a final "puff" of value) toward buying "great companies at fair prices."
*   **Intellectual Humility:** Marks stresses that the most dangerous phrase in investing is "I am 100% convinced."
*   **Recommended Reading:**
    *   ***A Short History of Financial Euphoria*** **by John Kenneth Galbraith:** Essential for understanding the psychology behind market cycles.
    *   ***Fooled by Randomness*** **by Nassim Nicholas Taleb:** A guide to understanding how much of "success" is actually attributable to luck.
