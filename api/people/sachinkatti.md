## The Infrastructure of Intelligence: NVIDIA and OpenAI on a Decade of Building Frontier AI

- Channel: NVIDIA
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KT89PEe9d7U
- Published: 2026-09-16
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260916.html#video-KT89PEe9d7U
- Speakers: Ian Buck (VP of Hyperscale and High-Performance Computing, NVIDIA); Sachin Katti (VP of Infrastructure, OpenAI); Dina Bass (Reporter, Bloomberg)

---

#### TL;DR
AI infrastructure has evolved rapidly from single-GPU nodes to gigawatt-scale data centers, driven by continuous scaling laws and deep co-design partnerships like the decade-long collaboration between NVIDIA and OpenAI.

---

### 1. Evolution of AI Scale and Architecture
* **The Shift to Data Center-Scale Compute:** 
  * **Broader Footprint:** Models no longer fit on a single GPU or server; compute has expanded from GPU and rack scale to row and data center scale.
  * **Holistic System Design:** Ian Buck noted that modern architecture requires end-to-end design encompassing power distribution, cooling systems, warm shells, CPUs, storage, and networking.
* **Historical Collaboration Milestones:** 
  * **The Hopper-to-NVLink Transition:** Moving from single-box Hopper nodes (eight GPUs per server) to disaggregated rack architectures (such as NVLink 72-GPU systems) was an existential engineering moment required to reach trillion-parameter models.
  * **Astra on Vera Rubin:** Sachin Katti shared that OpenAI tested an Astra model checkpoint on a Vera Rubin rack over a weekend; out-of-the-box hardware yielded a 3x throughput improvement, and 72 hours of agent-driven recursive loop optimization produced an additional 2x throughput gain.

### 2. Infrastructure Innovations and the Ohio Data Center Project
* **Greenfield Optimization at Gigawatt Scale:** 
  * **Massive Footprint:** The Ohio data center project (a partnership between OpenAI, NVIDIA, and SB Energy) is designed to reach an 8-gigawatt capacity when fully built out, representing a substantial portion of the local grid.
  * **Dynamic Power Scheduling:** Workloads are categorized by power and latency sensitivity (e.g., maximizing user token rates versus running offline/batch pre-fill jobs). Scheduling jobs across different nodes and rows enables dynamic load balancing.
  * **Hardware-Level Power Management:** The design relies on smart GPUs that understand and manage their own power, racks built to handle transients and electrical spikes, and intelligent power controllers (PDUs) to achieve upwards of 30–40% more GPU density.
* **Grid Stabilization:** 
  * **Adaptive Load Sharing:** By co-designing workloads with the power system, the data center can scale down usage during grid spikes and scale up during gaps. 
  * **Economic Benefit:** This adaptive load balancing helps stabilize the local grid, modernize infrastructure, and reduce electricity bills.

### 3. AI in Chip Design and Software Engineering
* **Internal AI Deployment at NVIDIA:** 
  * **Bug Analysis and Yields:** NVIDIA utilizes open-source models (including its own NeMo-Tron models) and third-party options (like GPT) to analyze historical bug databases dating back decades, understand hardware issues, and optimize silicon yields.
  * **Code Review Agents:** An agentic pre-check tool runs on Cuda driver check-ins to flag common bugs and known issues. Developers settled on a **15-minute** processing window as the acceptable threshold for pre-checks.
* **Token Efficiency:** GPT models are leveraged for their strong token efficiency, which speeds up pre-check tests and keeps developer friction low.

### 4. Software, Heterogeneity, and Safety Compute
* **Heterogeneous Orchestration:** 
  * **Complex Ecosystems:** Modern data centers require stitching together GPUs, CPUs, networking, and storage. 
  * **Agentic Optimization:** With OpenAI operating as a relatively small team, they rely on AI models and tools like Astra to handle end-to-end system optimization and inference serving across diverse hardware.
* **Compute Impact of Safety and Alignment:** 
  * **Increased Demand:** Discussions around safety pauses do not slow down buildouts; instead, Sachin Katti emphasized that achieving safety and alignment for capable models requires *more* compute investment dedicated specifically to safety and alignment models.

## Advancing AI 2026 | Build What's Next with @AMD

- Channel: AMD
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=crEztVjfAPM
- Published: 2026-07-23
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260723.html#video-crEztVjfAPM
- Speakers: Lisa Su (Chair and CEO, AMD); Tom Brown (Co-founder & Chief Compute Officer, Anthropic); Sachin Katti (Head of Infrastructure, OpenAI)

---

#### TL;DR
AMD announced its 2026 AI roadmap at the Advancing AI event, anchored by the launch of the Helios AI rack, the Venice (6th Gen EPYC) CPU family, the ROCm.ai agentic software platform, and localized hardware like Ryzen AI Halo and Kria robotics modules. Joined by executive partners from Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Cerebras, AT&T, and Cisco, AMD detailed hardware-software co-design strategies to capture a projected $2 trillion high-performance and adaptive computing market by 2030.

---

### 1. Data Center AI Infrastructure & Helios Launch
- **Helios AI Rack System:** Announced by Dr. Lisa Su as the industry's highest-performing AI rack, featuring integrated AMD Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs, and Pensando DPUs/NICs.
  - **Hardware specs:** Each rack integrates over 18,000 CDNA5 GPU compute units, over 4,600 Zen 6 CPU cores, and 31 terabytes of HBM4 memory.
  - **Performance vs. competition:** Delivers 15% more compute, 50% more HBM4 memory capacity and bandwidth, 50% more scale-out bandwidth, and up to 30% more tokens per dollar within a fixed 100kW rack power envelope.
  - **Production timeline:** Fully in production with shipments starting at the end of Q3 and ramping into Q4 2026.
- **MI455 Accelerator:** Built on TSMC's 2nm and 3nm process nodes with 320 billion transistors, combining 12 compute/IO chiplets and 432 GB of memory via 3D chip-stack packaging.
- **Enterprise Air-Cooled GPU (MI350P):** Introduced by Dan McNamara; an air-cooled GPU supporting up to 260 billion parameters on a single GPU to bring LLM-scale inference to enterprise data centers without facility upgrades, delivering 4x higher tokens/sec/dollar than competitors.
- **Sovereign & HPC Compute (MI430X):** Highlighted by Vamsi Boppana; features native hardware double precision (FP64) delivering 288 teraflops of FP64 compute (9x the competition) with leadership memory capacity, shipping in H1 2027 to power exascale AI factories at Oak Ridge National Labs and in Europe.
- **Partner Commitments:**
  - **Anthropic:** Tom Brown announced plans to deploy up to two gigawatts of Helios infrastructure.
  - **OpenAI:** Sachin Katti confirmed OpenAI was the first to receive MI455/Helios racks three months prior, with massive-scale deployments slated for late 2026 through 2027, building on their commitment to deploy up to 6 gigawatts of AMD infrastructure.
  - **Meta:** Santosh Janardhan noted Meta's ongoing multi-generation server and GPU deployments (spanning MI300, MI350, and upcoming MI450/450X clusters tied to their 6-gigawatt strategic agreement).

### 2. Server CPUs & The Agentic Era
- **Market Projections:** Dr. Lisa Su updated the 2030 server CPU market projection from $60 billion to over $200 billion (a 50%+ surge) driven by the rapid enterprise adoption of agentic AI.
- **Venice (6th Gen EPYC Family):** Unveiled by Dr. Su as the CPU family designed specifically for agentic AI workloads, leveraging TSMC's 2nm process and Zen 6 cores (203 billion transistors).
  - **Venice HF (AI Host Nodes):** Features 8 compute chiplets (96 cores total) running up to 5 GHz to keep GPUs fully fed; delivers 1.8x more tokens/second than previous-generation Turin.
  - **Venice 256-Core (Agent Sandboxes):** Features up to 512 threads per socket for industry-leading compute density, delivering over 2x more agents per watt compared to competitive X86 and ARM processors.
  - **Venice 128-Core (General Purpose):** Optimized for low-power, cost-effective enterprise workloads.
  - **Future Roadmap:** Verano (next-gen AI host nodes with low-power memory and faster CPU-GPU interconnects) and Venice X (featuring 3D V-Cache memory stacking beneath compute triplets for technical computing) arriving next year.

### 3. Disaggregated Inference with Cerebras Systems
- **Partnership with Cerebras:** Andrew Feldman joined Dr. Su to introduce a joint disaggregated inference solution combining AMD Helios racks (leadership throughput and memory capacity) with Cerebras wafer-scale engines (leadership SRAM and memory bandwidth).
- **Performance & Availability:** Delivers 5x higher throughput while maintaining ultra-low latency, available later in 2026 via the Cerebras Cloud and on-premise deployments.

### 4. Software & AI-Assisted Development (ROCm.ai)
- **ROCm Evolution:** Vamsi Boppana reported release cycles shortened to every six weeks with Day Zero support now standard across Hugging Face, PyTorch, JAX, vLLM, and SGLang.
- **ROCm.ai Platform:** An agentic AI development platform turning coding agents (Cursor, Claude, Codex) into ROCm superusers to generate, profile, and optimize GPU kernels automatically.
  - **HyperLoom:** An AI-assisted optimization layer that analyzes workloads, tunes configurations, and iterates toward performance goals (tested across a suite of 14,000 models).
  - **Performance Gains:** ROCm.ai delivers up to 3.3x speedups on DeepSeek models and a 2.4x boost in training performance via fused flash attention and checkpointing.
- **OpenAI Software Contributions:** Philippe Tillet discussed OpenAI's work with AMD on open-source compiler stacks (LLM code generation) and deep low-level code generation down to assembly scheduling via Triton and Glueon.

### 5. Client, Personal, and Physical AI (Edge & Robotics)
- **Personal AI & Ryzen AI Halo:** Jack Huynh detailed the shift toward personal AI agents running locally on PCs.
  - **Ryzen AI Halo:** Features 128 GB of unified memory supporting models up to 200 billion parameters.
  - **Gorgon Halo:** Expands unified memory to 192 GB and supports models up to 300 billion parameters.
  - **Hugging Face Partnership:** Includes a full year of Hugging Face Pro for every Halo box to provide optimized open models and agentic toolkits.
- **Enterprise Edge Management with Cisco:** Jeetu Patel joined to announce an integrated enterprise solution combining Ryzen AI Halo desk-side computers with Cisco Cloud Control for unified tokenomics monitoring, security guardrails, and device quarantine capabilities (Generally Available in early fall).
- **Physical AI & Kria Robotics:** Introduced the AMD Kria AI System on Module (powered by Ryzen AI Embedded X100) and the Kria AI Robotics Developer Platform (running ROS/ROS 2). In third-party testing against NVIDIA Jetson Thor, Kria delivers 3.4x better real-time results, 2.3x more concurrent agents, and 1.6x more CPU capacity.
