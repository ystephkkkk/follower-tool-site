### TL;DR
SpaceX has evolved from a launch provider into a dominant AI hyperscaler and vertically integrated conglomerate, while firms like 137 Ventures are investing in multi-product tech giants and co-founding platforms like Onodrim to scale European aerospace and defense manufacturing.

### SpaceX's Evolution: From Launch Provider to AI Hyperscaler
- **Financial and Operational Growth:** 
  - 137 Ventures has invested in SpaceX for 16 years, making it their largest position.
  - SpaceX recently posted top-line growth of ~160%, $4.4 billion in net income, and $8.8 to $9.10 billion of EBITDA in Q3.
  - The business is valued at $2 trillion, trading at roughly 20 to 30 times consensus EBITDA based on Q3 performance.
  - More than 50% of SpaceX's revenue is now attributed to AI in some capacity.
- **Hyperscaler Strategy & Compute Infrastructure:** 
  - SpaceX is building out software, services, and game infrastructure around its compute stack, functioning as a true hyperscaler.
  - Reiterated earnings calls indicate sub-12-month payback periods, signaling massive unsatisfied demand where supply cannot match demand.
  - Vertical integration allows SpaceX to monetize from the app layer and model layer down to compute, providing risk-adjusted investment durability against shifts between open-source and closed-source models.
  - If outer-year demand slows, SpaceX can shift capacity to its own products, making its capex risk nominal and shorter-duration.
- **Starship & Starlink Milestones:** 
  - Starship is successfully flying, delivering payloads to space—including compute payloads on V3 satellites for Starlink.
  - A single launch with a handful of Starlink V3s delivered roughly 1% of the world's capacity, with tens of thousands more planned.
  - Starlink is expanding rapidly into Direct-to-Cell (DDC).
  - Starship is lowering launch costs by orders of magnitude, enabling downstream businesses like Varta (founded by Delian, Will Brewy) to serve pharmaceutical and defense industries.
- **Orbital Data Centers & Space Compute:** 
  - Starcloud (led by CEO Philip in Seattle) is developing Starcloud 2 and early iterations of Starcloud 3 to be hosted on Starship.
  - Orbital compute satellites are targeted for launch in the back half of next year.
  - Orbital data centers solve terrestrial constraints around power, land, and energy.

### Portfolio Strategy: Growth, Valuations, and Power Law Dynamics
- **Long-Term Duration Over Single-Year Rates:** 
  - 137 Ventures focuses on the duration of compounding revenue growth rather than chasing single-year spikes (e.g., SpaceX compounding at ~40% for 20 years).
  - As minority growth-stage investors, the firm wants conviction that a portfolio company can compound for decades.

### The Return of the Conglomerate & Dual-Use Defense
- **Modern vs. Historical Conglomerates:** 
  - Traditional conglomerates of the 1980s suffered from disparate subsidiaries with no shared operating systems or synergies.
  - Modern tech conglomerates (like Apple, SpaceX, and Anduril) utilize a common operating tech platform and vertical integration to manage multiple high-growth business lines.
- **The Dual-Use Imperative:** 
  - True resilience requires "capital C" dual-use conglomerates that sell to both the commercial private sector and government/defense.
  - Palantir and SpaceX are prime examples where the majority of revenue stems from the commercial sector, subsidizing and maintaining surge capacity for the defense sector without relying solely on industrial policy.
  - China's defense primes derive 68% of revenue from the commercial sector, mirroring the commercial dominance needed in the West (e.g., commercial shipping driving naval manufacturing).

### Onodrim Industries: Re-industrializing European Defense
- **Founding & Scale:** 
  - Co-founded by Christian Garrett alongside ISIS and Alex (former early Palantir leaders).
  - Onodrim is an aerospace and defense scale-up platform that recently raised a **$300 million Series A at a $1.3 billion valuation**.
- **The European Bottleneck:** 
  - While thousands of European drone companies and neo-prime startups ("Start Quantum," Cambridge-backed firms) are heavily funded, the critical supplier layer underneath—components, engines, precision manufacturing—is fragmented and under-capitalized.
  - Unlike the US, Europe lacks supplier consolidators like Transdigm, Teledyne, Hico, or Honeywell.
- **Strategy & Execution:** 
  - Onodrim acquires and partners with multigenerational European aerospace, defense, and industrial manufacturers.
  - The platform deploys capital (capex), engineering resources, and modern manufacturing technology (including AI software to modernize operations) to scale these businesses into pan-European and global champions.
  - The team spans 9 nationalities, combining backgrounds from private equity, traditional defense, McKinsey, and tech firms like Palantir and SpaceX.

### Industry Culture, Zero-Sum Mindsets, and Political Weaponization
- **Preserving Non-Zero-Sum Culture:** 
  - Unlike legacy industries, the technology sector has historically thrived on a non-zero-sum, high-growth culture.
  - Petty infighting, internal attacks, and tribalism threaten to erode the collaborative spirit that drives global economic share.
- **The Danger of Political Weaponization:** 
  - Tech companies and founders are increasingly facing political targeting and boycotts based on personal political views.
  - This trend is misguided because these companies are nonpartisan engines of job creation, GDP growth, and national security.
  - Consumer choices should rely on the merits of products and markets, rather than political litmus tests that could severely backfire on Western competitiveness.
