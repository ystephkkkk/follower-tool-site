## Draghi: France is Far From Becoming a Euro-Area Crisis

- Channel: Bloomberg Live
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=49UYb5Q81xw
- Published: 2026-10-15
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261015.html#video-49UYb5Q81xw
- Speakers: Mario Draghi (Former Prime Minister, Italy); John Micklethwait (Editor-in-Chief, Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR:** Former ECB President Mario Draghi dismissed fears of a French debt-driven euro area crisis while warning that global trade fragmentation requires Europe to rapidly forge new economic coalitions with partners like India and Canada.

### France and Euro-Area Stability
* **Fiscal Profligacy:** **Mario Draghi** stated that France is the victim of its own fiscal profligacy, coinciding with a worldwide rise in interest rates.
    * The ideal remedy is a medium-term budgetary retrenchment path agreed upon during ongoing parliamentary discussions and future presidential debates.
* **Euro Crisis Comparison:** **Mario Draghi** argued that France is a long way from becoming a euro-area crisis, contrasting the current situation with the 2011 Greek debt revelation.
    * **Market Safeguards:** The European Central Bank (ECB) now possesses established tools (such as the TPP) and governing council protocols to address credit market contagion and transmission mechanism impairments.
* **ECB Intervention Hurdles:** **Mario Draghi** noted that the bar for ECB intervention on French debt is currently high due to high and rising inflation—the central bank's primary price-stability mandate—alongside France's excessive deficit procedure and domestic political infighting.

### The Draghi Report and the AI Landscape
* **AI Integration:** **Mario Draghi** noted that artificial intelligence has accelerated dramatically over the last two years, becoming pervasive across society and requiring central integration into industrial policy.
* **Global Positioning:** **Mario Draghi** acknowledged that Europe unquestionably trails the US and China in developing frontier models.
    * **European Industrial Advantage:** Europe does not need to lose the broader competition; its well-developed manufacturing and automated sector positions it well for model diffusion.
    * **Market Baseline:** Europe historically accounts for **20%** of the global market for industrial automation, providing an ideal base to combine robots with open-source AI models.
* **Changing Political Reception:** **Mario Draghi** observed that while the Draghi Report initially met with European complacency, awareness of the deep structural challenges has completely changed, shifting the debate toward action.

### Trade Reorientation and New Global Coalitions
* **Vulnerability of Open Trade:** **Mario Draghi** pointed out that Europe derives more than **50%** of its GDP from trade (compared to **~27%** in the US and **31–32%** in China), making it highly vulnerable to dismissed multilateral rules.
* **Leveraging Market Size:** Europe's primary tool is its market of **450 million** relatively wealthy citizens.
* **New Strategic Coalitions:** To de-risk supply chains predominantly owned by China, Europe must build new trade channels with key partners:
    * **India:** A major prospective partner highlighted by a significant pending trade agreement involving the abolition of **96%** of tariffs for European exports and vice versa.
    * **Other Coalitions:** Canada, Mercosur, Chile, Southeast Asia, and Australia.
* **Partnership Openness:** **Mario Draghi** expressed personal support for open arrangements, new associate memberships, and rapprochement with the UK, while maintaining that Ukraine's EU or NATO membership depends on post-peace defense guarantee discussions.

### US Fiscal Deficits and Economic Comparison
* **US Debt Trajectory:** **John Micklethwait** noted that total US debt is projected to hit **$40 trillion**, with the debt-to-GDP ratio exceeding a record **106% by 2030**.
* **Fiscal Divergence:** **Mario Draghi** warned that while US productivity growth previously made stabilization feasible without extraordinary efforts, the US deficit has continued to rise substantially under both political parties without medium-term retrenchment plans.
* **Structural Differences:** **Mario Draghi** highlighted that the US is a net exporter of energy (unlike energy-importing Europe) and has driven the current wave of technology innovation, making its challenges more circumscribed primarily to budgetary policy.

### Bottom-Up European Integration
* **Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up:** **Mario Draghi** contrasted slow top-down European decision-making (which spans governments, the EU, and the European Parliament) with a dynamic private sector.
* **Private Sector Dynamism:** European consolidation, mergers, and acquisitions are gradually overcoming industrial fragmentation through "integration from the bottom rather than from the top down."

### Notable Quotes

> "Europe is the most open of all the other continents. Europe draws more than 50% of its GDP from trade, while the United States is about the same figure is about 27% and China 31–32%." — **Mario Draghi**

> "We should be very open to new arrangements. So new partnerships, new associate memberships and more integration with the rest of the world is only for the benefit of Europe." — **Mario Draghi**


## Apollo's Rowan Puts Current Credit Cycle Into Context

- Channel: Bloomberg Live
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BiQg5c8h16k
- Published: 2026-03-03
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260303.html#video-BiQg5c8h16k
- Speakers: Marc Rowan (CEO, Apollo Global Management); John Micklethwait (Bloomberg)

---

**TL;DR:** Apollo is pivoting from its "buccaneering" roots toward investment-grade private credit to fund a "global industrial renaissance," arguing that moving credit risk from bank balance sheets to private investors has made the global financial system more resilient.

### Macroeconomic Outlook and Geopolitical Risks
* **The 70/30 Risk Split:** Marc Rowan notes that while 70% of the economic picture is positive (strong employment, high capital spending, open markets), the remaining 30% consists of unpredictable "disruptors" including geopolitics, government borrowing, and rapid technological change.
* **Fiscal Accountability:** Rowan warns that Western governments are spending beyond their means. While Japan sustained this for decades, he points to the UK’s "Liz Truss moment" as a template for how markets may eventually hold governments accountable for fiscal excesses.
* **Inflationary Pressures:** 
    * **Watch Items:** The restriction of labor and goods is fundamentally inflationary, though markets haven't fully priced this in yet.
    * **The "Skunk" at the Party:** Persistent inflation would handicap the Federal Reserve's ability to lower rates, potentially ending a long accommodative cycle.

### The "Global Industrial Renaissance" and Sector Strategy
* **Massive Capital Demand:** Rowan highlights a "global industrial renaissance" driven by energy transition, next-gen manufacturing, defense ramping, and AI infrastructure. 
    * **Scale of Spending:** He describes the current investment surge in data centers and energy as the world "spending every dollar since the creation of fire... all at once."
* **Software Caution:** Apollo holds **zero software** in its private equity portfolio and has minimal exposure in credit. 
    * **AI Threat:** Rowan argues that AI's impact on software is a fundamental risk that many leveraged lenders ignored until recently. 
    * **Market Concentration:** Software represents 30% of the leveraged buyout market, making it over-represented and vulnerable to technological disruption.
* **Infrastructure Bullishness:** Apollo prefers lending to diversified tech giants (Amazon, Google, Microsoft) for digital infrastructure rather than "equity-story" startups, provided they offer sensible underwriting.

### The Evolution of Private Credit
* **De-risking the Economy:** Rowan argues that the migration of credit from bank balance sheets to private markets is a successful post-2008 regulatory outcome. 
    * **Socialized Risk:** By moving credit into BDCs and insurance portfolios, the risk is distributed among investors who can price it, rather than sitting on government-guaranteed bank balance sheets.
* **The Investment Grade Shift:** While media focuses on the $1.5 trillion "leveraged lending" market, Rowan emphasizes that the vast majority of private credit is actually **investment grade.**
    * **Apollo’s Scale:** Out of $300 billion in originations last year, 80% was investment grade.
    * **Creativity in Credit:** Rowan rejects the idea that investment-grade lending is boring, noting it requires more creativity and scale than traditional "buccaneering" deal-making.

### Firm Strategy and Industry Consolidation
* **Growth Constraints:** Rowan asserts that Apollo's growth is limited by its capacity to **originate good risk**, not by its ability to raise capital. 
    * Over-expansion leads to "commoditizing" the business and taking on bad risks.
* **The Coming Shakeout:** A "reckoning" is expected for firms that failed to manage risk or over-concentrated in sectors like software. 
    * **The Winners:** Firms with "fortress balance sheets" and disciplined underwriting will gain market share, similar to how dominant banks emerged after the GFC.
* **Solving the Retirement Crisis:** Rowan positions Apollo as a bridge between the global need for retirement income and the massive capital requirements of industrial companies. 
    * **Critique of 401ks:** He notes that the current retirement system is overly concentrated, joking that the US has "levered the entirety of the retirement system... to Nvidia."

> "We're essentially spending every dollar since the creation of fire and we're doing it all at once... We will see the largest need for capital ever." — Marc Rowan

> "The formation of credit particularly in these private BDCs is a de-risking activity for the economy... it has moved it out of the levered government-guaranteed portion of our economy." — Marc Rowan

> "Public is not safe or risky, private is not safe or risky, they're both safe or risky—they're just different degrees of liquidity." — Marc Rowan
