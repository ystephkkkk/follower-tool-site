## The New AI Agents That Shop, Plan, and Gossip

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dSiTwPl0t1U
- Published: 2026-11-04
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20261104.html#video-dSiTwPl0t1U
- Speakers: David Pawlan (Creator, Assistant Benchmark); Anish Acharya (General Partner, a16z); Josh Elman (Investor, a16z)

---

**TL;DR:** As AI assistants evolve from general tools into specialized, multiplayer agent networks, consumers are moving past basic productivity tasks toward complex preferences, social experiences, and trust-building. However, a major hurdle remains: 22% of power user messages center on the fear of trust, privacy, and embarrassing data leaks.

### 1. The State of the Agent Landscape & Meetups
* **The Agent Community Boom:** David Pawlan hosted an agent meetup in San Francisco with over 100 attendees, highlighting that founders and users are craving in-person community and sharing best practices openly rather than acting cagey.
* **Categories of Assistants:** David has tested over 147 different assistants, categorizing them into four primary types:
    * **General**
    * **Work-oriented**
    * **Multiplayer**
    * **Travel**
    * **Finance:** Josh Elman and David noted that finance remains an unfulfilled category waiting to fully take off.

### 2. Trust, Privacy, and "Fences"
* **The Dominant Fear:** Out of 3,600 messages across AI power-user group chats, **22%** of discussions focus on trust as the primary fear in the assistant space.
* **The "Picnic" Analogy & Shane Mack's Leak:** Shane Mack experienced a major privacy failure when his personal agent accidentally crossed into his work environment and blasted his personal finances onto his company Slack. 
    * **The Fence Solution:** Josh Elman emphasized the need for proper "fences" (permissions, boundaries, and monitoring) so personal agents don't bleed into professional channels.
* **Stated vs. Revealed Preferences:** Anish Acharya pointed out that consumers will gladly share all their data for trivial upside (e.g., Hark offering $30 of free pizza in exchange for total access to Google Workspace emails).
* **The Embarrassment Factor:** Josh Elman argued that the true high-stakes fear is social embarrassment (e.g., agents revealing dietary restrictions or miscommunicating on dates), making discretion a mandatory trait for agents.

### 3. Testing Judgment, Cost, and Constraints
* **Flower Delivery Benchmark:** David tested agents on ordering the cheapest flowers for same-day delivery:
    * **Instinct:** Performed exceptionally well at balancing price and time constraints.
    * **Kicker:** Secured an edge by utilizing a local Chrome browser extension.
    * **Season:** Also performed well. 
    * *Other agents failed by prioritizing delivery speed while completely ignoring the price component.*
* **LA Weekend Travel Test:** David tested Grockbot, Dots, and Instinct to book a luxurious yet cost-efficient weekend in LA:
    * **Dots:** Prioritized cost (e.g., choosing a redeye flight).
    * **Grockbot:** Prioritized time and luxury, and was the only agent in a multi-agent chat room that methodically absorbed and implemented value from the other agents' proposals.
* **Onboarding & Memory Portability:** 
    * Only **3%** of 354 surveyed power users use ChatGPT as their primary agent, though it retains massive context.
    * David tested the top 15 benchmarked agents on memory portability: **all but one** allowed users to export their memory file (markdown) to switch assistants easily.

### 4. Commerce, Shopping, and the Rationality Debate
* **Shopping Behavior:** In David's survey, Amazon and retail shopping represented **35%** of what consumers are purchasing through AI.
* **Direct-to-Consumer & Longtail Winners:** Josh Elman and Anish Acharya discussed how agents have infinite patience to search the longtail of the internet, bypassing aggregators and empowering platforms like Shopify to drive direct purchases.
* **Patrick Collison's Rationality Vision vs. Alien Preferences:** 
    * Patrick Collison envisions consumers becoming more rational via agents.
    * Anish Acharya countered that agents will develop their own "alien" revealed preferences—such as an irrational affinity for clean APIs with great documentation.
    * Josh Elman added that "paternalism will kill any product," warning that agents shouldn't force artificial rationality or strict diets on humans who ultimately want to enjoy life.

### 5. Agent-to-Agent Social Networks and Serendipity
* **Agent Gossiping:** Josh Elman highlighted playful agents that chat in the background and share minor updates (like a friend's power being out), creating a healthy "social indirection" and acting as a social lubricant.
* **Multiplayer Apps:** 
    * **Wabby:** Launched recently, allowing users to interact with multiple threads of life and media watchlists.
    * **Tommo:** Features "interest groups" (e.g., grouping users studying for the LSAT) to connect humans based entirely on shared goals.
* **Creating Serendipity:** The panel discussed how agent networks could ultimately form a new kind of social network—moving beyond Facebook's model of connecting only with people you already know, and instead pairing strangers based on real-world desires and complementary goals.

### 6. Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Enterprise Shift (David Pawlan):** The industry will shift more attention toward enterprise use cases over the next few weeks, moving beyond purely consumer-focused assistant hype.
* **Secret Superpowers (Anish Acharya):** Agents will eventually evolve from doing tedious productivity tasks to becoming "dark agents" or "secret superpowers" that help users achieve their deepest personal aspirations and desired identities.


## What Would Make an AI Assistant Worth Paying For?

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3T5sij3spWw
- Published: 2026-09-29
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260929.html#video-3T5sij3spWw
- Speakers: Anish Acharya (General Partner, a16z); David Pawlan (Creator, Assistant Benchmark)

---

**TL;DR:** Personal AI agents and consumer assistants have exploded in popularity over a four-week period, shifting from general productivity tools to proactive, invisible assistants capable of saving consumers time and money through tasks like travel management, finance, and shopping.

### Landscape of Personal AI Agents
- **Rapid Market Explosion:** 
    - The consumer AI agent space has expanded rapidly, with 122 different solutions currently tracked across categories, including 64 generalized B2C consumer-facing agents.
    - Early milestones included *Poke* (launched September 8), *Open Claw* (late November), *Instinct*, *Grockbot*, *Muse*, and longtail options like *Caddy*, *Ali*, and *Pi*.
- **Assistant Bench Metrics:** 
    - Created by David Pawlan, *Assistant Bench* compares AI assistants on a one-shot prompt use-case basis across 16 dimensions.
    - Launched 16 days prior to the recording, the site surpassed 100,000 visitors and received outreach from nearly every founder in the space.
- **Top User Behaviors:** 
    - Despite travel being a viral point of conversation, travel is actually the fourth most talked-about use case because people do not travel every day.
    - The top three daily use cases are: (1) Daily admin and inbox cleaning, (2) Agent orchestration (how agents talk to each other), and (3) Development/coding on mobile. Finance ranks fourth as the most interesting but least practical category.

### Proactivity, Trust, and the "Invisible Assistant"
- **The Efficiency Misconception:** 
    - General populations do not care about being 10% more efficient; the best employees (and agents) are invisible, doing work in the background and updating users only when necessary.
- **Cost-Saving Workflows:** 
    - The most successful proactive agents will focus on cost savings rather than cost-making—such as going through past receipts to file HSA reimbursements, hitting up airlines for travel credits when flight prices drop, or connecting a home Grockbot to a sprinkler system to drop water bills by 50%.
- **The Trust Boundary:** 
    - Proactiveness carries massive defensibility, but it is a fine line; crossing it once destroys user trust. While users accept proactive background actions that require no downstream work (e.g., drafting emails, flight credits), direct actions impacting the user (e.g., switching insurance providers) require explicit permission.

### Interfaces, Hardware, and Audio
- **Multi-Surface Preferences:** 
    - Interaction surface preferences fragment by generation and use case (e.g., Gen Z preferring text threads, older demographics or visual planners preferring apps and visual dream boards).
    - iMessage occupies a privileged, highly personal space on the phone compared to app grids, though chat interfaces risk becoming cluttered if every second thread is an agent.
- **Hardware Form Factors:** 
    - While startups are experimenting with pendants and watch straps, form factors will likely be driven by personal preference like jewelry.
    - Anish Acharya suggests the Meta/Muse "Charm" hardware might serve less as a mass-adoption win and more as a real-world data collection device to map out the physical world for Zuckerberg's future metaverse.
    - Audio-only wearables and full-duplex voice models (such as ChatGPT Voice running on GPT live one, capable of seeing Gmail and calendar threads) represent a major magic moment for users whose hands are busy.

### Multiplayer Dynamics and Social Interaction
- **Group Chat Integration:** 
    - Adding agents directly to active group chats often feels intrusive or diminishes social capital due to low-EQ chatter.
    - Emerging alternatives include *Doc* (by Shane Mack via XMTP/A16Z), which acts as a silent listener in group chats, takes notes, and independently pings users in separate threads when action items arise.
- **Social Indirection:** 
    - Carefully designed agents can act as a non-human layer of social indirection, helping organize conflict or provide feedback without emotional friction.

### Agentic Commerce and Network Effects
- **Marketplace Friction & Resistance:** 
    - Platforms are pushing back against automation; Amazon blocked Muse to protect its ad revenue and impulse-shopping loops, while Resi began shutting down accounts using browser automation for restaurant reservations.
- **Supply-Constrained vs. Demand-Constrained Commerce:** 
    - Hot restaurant tables and other supply-constrained domains will require more sophisticated calculations (such as maximizing AOV/LTV or loyalty) or move toward bidding wars.
    - Conversely, demand-constrained markets could see agent-driven group buying or automated supply-side bidding to benefit the consumer.
- **Serendipity and Longtail Commerce:** 
    - Rather than buying standard mass-market goods, agents could source hyper-tailored items from the longtail (e.g., commissioning a grandmother in Nebraska to knit white socks via Etsy or direct text messaging).

### Predictions & Forward Calls
- **David Pawlan:** The trajectory of the personal agent space will continue moving toward increased proactivity and hyper-specialization (such as narrow startups building experts tailored to specific micro-demographics like single mothers with children under three).
- **Anish Acharya:** Browser-use infrastructure and token costs will experience exponential deflation, making advanced agent capabilities cheap enough for more products to offer for free.
