## Howard Marks Flags the Uncertainty in AI Investing

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ZdgxiRWuS0
- Published: 2026-09-20
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260920.html#video--ZdgxiRWuS0
- Speakers: Howard Marks (Co-founder & Co-chair, Oaktree Capital Management); David Gura (Host, Bloomberg); Christina Ruffini (Host, Bloomberg)

---

### TL;DR
Markets have been dominated by optimists driven by strong economic aggregates, the TINA trade, and AI enthusiasm, though this requires heightened investor caution. While AI's transformative potential is undeniable, extreme uncertainty surrounding future profitability and societal impact makes it impossible to definitively label current market exuberance as irrational.

### The Macro Environment & Market Psychology
- **Federal Reserve Communication:** Howard Marks argues that less central bank communication is preferable, as it forces businesses and portfolios to become antifragile rather than relying entirely on Fed projections.
  - Expresses a personal preference for a less activist central bank that lets the free market determine natural interest rates through borrower-lender negotiations unless the economy is heading off the rails.
- **Optimism-Driven Markets:** Markets have been ruled by optimists since roughly October 1, 2022, with the S&P 500 more than doubling in that period.
  - The S&P surge is supported by positive underlying truths, including a US economy that performs as the best-developed world economy, the "TINA" (there is no alternative) trade, and AI excitement.
  - Marks attributes persistent optimism partly to the absence of a truly severe downturn since the global financial crisis market bottom in March 2009, leading people to extrapolate good times.
  - When optimists are in charge, they slough off bad news and react positively to settlement rumors without getting depressed when deals fall through, creating a positive market bias that calls for built-in defense and caution.

### AI Investment & Valuation Challenges
- **The Definition of Value Investing:** Marks defines his approach as a "small v" value investor whose goal is to calculate an asset's intrinsic value and buy it at a fair price relative to that value.
- **The "Too Hard" Pile:** Evaluating pure-play AI companies is extremely challenging because they may not have produced profits yet, making it difficult to quantify earnings potential 5 years out.
  - Hyperscalers like Microsoft and Amazon have supporting legacy businesses, but companies all-in on AI present an unusual degree of uncertainty for intrinsic valuation, fitting Warren Buffett's description of the "too hard pile."
- **Exuberance vs. Irrationality:** Alan Greenspan's 1997 phrase "irrational exuberance" defines a bubble, and while AI exuberance is undeniable, Marks hesitates to label it definitively as irrational.
  - Because nobody can definitively scope what AI will accomplish, its profitability, or its broader effects, nobody can prove the exuberance is irrational.
  - The presence of uncertainty does not mean investors should do nothing, but it must be acknowledged to properly inform investment sizing and strategy.

### Societal Impact & Government Policy
- **Existential Economic Concerns:** Marks previously warned about the acute societal ramifications of AI displacing workers in Middle America and across the country.
  - Reiterates that sentient human beings must acknowledge these risks, though he notes his wife defines worrying as "bleeding before you're shot" since individuals cannot personally stop the trend.
- **Policy Vacuum:** Marks criticizes the lack of government foresight, stating there should be a dedicated task force examining the coming effects of AI.
  - Poses a critical unanswered fiscal question for policymakers: If AI replaces human workers, will it replace them as taxpayers, and what will happen to government revenues when people lose their jobs?

### Corporate Integration & Critical Thinking
- **Enterprise Usage at Oaktree:** Oaktree Capital Management uses AI internally to marshal data and perform early analysis on tasks that used to take a long time, but has not turned business or investment decisions over to algorithms.
  - Exceptional investing does not come from computers, but from a superior ability to assess the import of data—a superiority AI does not yet possess.
- **Preserving Cognitive Muscles:** Oaktree does not encourage younger hires to outsource critical thinking to AI.
  - Stopping critical thinking causes individuals to "lose the muscle," and the ultimate edge in investing remains fundamentally human.
  - Marks compares AI's disruptive potential to indexation in equity investing—putting many active investors out of business, but not the best.

### Notable Quotes
> *"When society thinks—when everybody in society thinks they know exactly what the Fed's gonna do and prepares for that, then the society is dependent on that outcome. And if if if something else happens, then, you know, bad things can happen."* — **Howard Marks**

> *"My wife defines worrying as bleeding before you're shot. And in this case, there's nothing we can do about it. Nothing certainly nothing I can do about it personally. So, you roll with it."* — **Howard Marks**

> *"If people are put out of work, they won't pay taxes. If AI takes their place as as workers, will it take their place as taxpayers and how? So what will happen to government revenues when when and if people lose their jobs?"* — **Howard Marks**

### TL;DR
Despite global uncertainties and regional instability, Qatar continues to maintain strong GDP growth and attract record foreign investment, while partnerships like Brookfield and the Qatar Investment Authority are scaling up local AI infrastructure and energy-to-compute projects.

### Economic Resilience and Stability in Qatar
* **Long-Term Regional Growth:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal bin Thani bin Faisal Al-Thani noted that Qatar has achieved 6% year-over-year GDP growth across the past decade despite facing multiple crises including Covid-19, oil price crashes, and regional instability.
  * Bruce Flatt described the development in the Middle East and Qatar over the past 25 years as "almost a miracle," noting that capital availability and a push for system resilience will drive significant investments over the next 15 years.
* **Ground-Level Business Activity:** 
  * In the three months following the onset of the recent conflict, over 5,000 new foreign-owned companies were established in Qatar, representing a 60% increase compared to the same period last year.
  * Local Qatari manufacturers increased their exports by 12% over the same timeframe.
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal noted that active discussions are ongoing with international firms to relocate regional offices or expand operations, representing at least $15 billion of foreign direct investment (FDI) with plans remaining unchanged.

### $20B AI Infrastructure and Compute Partnership
* **Energy-to-Compute Transition:** 
  * Brookfield and the Qatar Investment Authority (QIA / Chi-I) formed a $20 billion joint venture in December to focus on artificial intelligence infrastructure.
  * Bruce Flatt highlighted that Qatar's abundant natural gas allows the country to convert local energy directly into compute services, creating high-margin economic value.
* **Global Demand for Localized Compute:** 
  * Data centers are being commissioned and construction will start shortly to sell compute both domestically—as every country requires local AI capacity—and internationally.
  * Bruce Flatt noted that global data storage and intelligence are shifting away from being exclusively centralized in the United States toward local country build-outs due to domestic capacity constraints.
* **Agnostic Supplier Strategy:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal stated that Qatar and Brookfield remain agnostic regarding technology suppliers, utilizing both Chinese and American technology based on customer demand (such as building identical neighboring data centers in the Maldives and Indonesia using different national technologies).
  * Bruce Flatt observed that the global market is developing into parallel systems—a Chinese-led system and a Western/US-led system—operating independently.

### Investment Strategy and Public-Private Collaboration
* **Core Investment Rules:** 
  * Bruce Flatt outlined Brookfield’s 40-year investment philosophy: pick the right countries (operating in roughly 30 nations including the US, UK, France, Australia, Canada, Brazil, and Colombia), partner with good people, and buy or build good backbone infrastructure.
  * Brookfield manages approximately $700 billion of investments in the United States and is a major infrastructure investor across the globe.
* **Domestic Policy and KPIs:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal explained that while QIA is a financial institution prioritizing financial returns, resilience metrics (such as food, medical, and manufacturing security) serve as positive considerations under Qatar's National Development Strategy (which features eight key pillars, including manufacturing).

### Value of Global Institutions and Dialogue
* **Continued Relevancy:** 
  * H.E. Sheikh Faisal emphasized that international gatherings like the UN General Assembly and G20 remain essential because they maintain channels for dialogue, even among opponents.
  * Bruce Flatt agreed on the value of global meetings, noting that despite event fatigue, human commonalities make regular dialogue a positive force for stability.
