## AI Revolution: Winners & Losers w/ Dan Ives & Gil Luria | The Real Eisman Playbook Ep 70

- Speakers: Steve Eisman (Host, The Real Eisman Playbook); Dan Ives (Analyst); Gil Luria (Analyst)

---

#### TL;DR
The AI trade has evolved from an uncritical consensus into a complex debate over return on investment (ROI), capital intensity, and software disruption, with Dan Ives remaining firmly bullish on the structural buildout while Gil Luria advocates for a nuanced, stock-by-stock approach to identify emerging winners and losers.

---

### 1. The Current State of the AI Debate & The Macro Buildout
* **Two Core Market Debates:** Gil Luria identifies the primary debates as: (1) whether massive capital expenditures on data centers will yield a sufficient ROI, and (2) how generative AI will impact traditional software businesses (the so-called "SAS Apocalypse").
* **Current Stage of the Revolution:** **Dan Ives:** Views the market as being in **Year 3 of an 8 to 10-year buildout**, comparing the current data center expansion to building the Las Vegas strip in 1955.
* **Geographic & Chip Demand:** **Dan Ives:** Reports a **15-to-1 demand-to-supply ratio** for chips observed during a recent Asia trip, noting that the technological disparity between the US and Asia has narrowed and it is now "the US's game to lose."
* **Economic Traction & Revenue Run Rates:** **Gil Luria:** Points out that OpenAI and Anthropic have a combined run-rate revenue **clearly above $75 billion** (and over $100 billion when including Gemini, Meta, and XAI), up from zero just two years ago, proving real economic activity despite trillions in infrastructure spending.

### 2. Market Counterarguments: Moes, Capital Intensity, & Pricing
* **Capital Intensity:** **Steve Eisman:** Highlights that tech giants like Google, Microsoft, and Meta historically never raised equity capital since inception, but AI has transformed them into capital-intensive businesses.
* **Lack of Moes & Open Source Threat:** **Gil Luria:** Notes the threat of open-source models (such as Chinese models like Kimmy K3 charging a fraction of US closed-source prices). 
    * *Definition:* Closed-source models (OpenAI, Anthropic) control parameters and code entirely. Open-source models share weights and code, allowing offline usage and drastically lower costs.
    * *Ecosystem Shift:* **Gil Luria:** Notes that players like NVIDIA (via the Neotron model), Microsoft, and Palantir are embracing open-source to prevent Chinese model dominance while still driving GPU and memory consumption.
* **Palantir & Model Agnosticism:** **Dan Ives & Gil Luria:** Explain Alex Karp's philosophy that businesses should remain model-agnostic; feeding proprietary business data directly into Anthropic or OpenAI risks proprietary data exposure and leaves companies vulnerable if a specific model provider hits regulatory or operational roadblocks.

### 3. Company-Specific Deep Dives: Google, Apple, & Oracle
* **Alphabet / Google:** **Gil Luria:** Notes Google flipped from being labeled an "AI loser" and antitrust breakup target a year ago to an AI winner with accelerated cloud growth (into the **60s**) and accelerating search advertising growth—though they currently lag in state-of-the-art consumer chat and enterprise AI. **Dan Ives:** Considers Google the best-positioned hyperscaler from an end-to-end perspective.
* **Apple:** **Dan Ives:** Calls Apple the "Easy Pass" on the consumer AI highway, with an opportunity to monetize its **2.5 billion iOS devices** and **1.5 billion iPhones** via a flexible AI strategy (New Siri) that lets others fight out model wars.
* **Oracle & The OpenAI Backlog:** **Gil Luria:** Recalls accurately taking a cautious stance in September 2025 when Oracle stock surged to $330 after announcing a massive OpenAI backlog, which the market later questioned when OpenAI faced capital and commitment scrutiny. **Gil Luria:** Notes that with OpenAI's subsequent **$122 billion fundraising round** (the largest in history) and code-red focus on compute, Oracle's **$630 billion compute backlog** is essentially being valued at zero by the market, presenting significant upside.

### 4. Winners vs. Losers Across Hardware and Software
* **Hardware & Memory Dislocations:** **Gil Luria:** Points out a market inconsistency where speculative stocks (Intel, Cerebras) trade as if the hardware cycle extends through 2030, while **Micron** trades at **6x earnings** as if the cycle is already over, despite memory chips being in a superior market position to CPUs.
* **Software Disparities:** 
    * **Microsoft:** Highlighted by **Gil Luria** as a software winner benefiting from AI tailwinds in Azure and Office products.
    * **Salesforce & Adobe:** **Gil Luria & Dan Ives:** Identify Salesforce and Adobe as legacy software companies struggling because they failed to innovate while continuously raising prices, making them prime targets for budget cuts.
    * **Private Equity Software Debt:** **Gil Luria:** Emphasizes that financial distress in software is concentrated in private equity-owned small vendors that stopped investing in product refreshes, whereas publicly traded software giants carry net cash positions.
* **Cybersecurity:** **Dan Ives:** Highlights cybersecurity as an underappreciated sector where budgets will double over the next 2-3 years because AI agent deployment exponentially increases enterprise digital surface areas.

### 5. Data Centers, Energy, & Political Pressures
* **The Degrowth & PR Problem:** **Dan Ives & Gil Luria:** Argue that AI suffers from a self-created PR problem driven by tech leaders warning of widespread job losses and rising electricity bills. 
* **The China Threat:** **Dan Ives:** Warns that local moratoriums and political pushback against data centers (such as projects in New York) risk handing technological dominance directly to China.
* **Productivity & Labor:** **Gil Luria:** Reassures that increased productivity driven by technology structurally increases capital returns and net job creation rather than destroying long-term labor markets.

**TL;DR:** Palantir and industry analysts characterize the current AI era as a "Fourth Industrial Revolution" that is rapidly transforming commercial sectors, accelerating software deployment to near-zero latency, and necessitating a radical shift in labor skills to survive significant white-collar job displacement.

### The AI Market Outlook and "Golden Child" Status
* **Palantir’s Market Position:** Dan Ives (Managing Director, Wedbush Securities) identifies Palantir as the "golden child" and "poster child" of the AI revolution.
    * **Financial Growth:** The commercial business is the primary driver of Wall Street interest; Ives predicts the combined government and commercial sectors could lead Palantir to a trillion-dollar valuation within two to three years.
    * **The "Ghost Trade":** Ives describes the current software market as a "ghost trade" where legacy companies are struggling to compete against new AI-native stacks and install bases.
* **Broader Tech Predictions:** 
    * **NVIDIA:** Labeled the "godfather of AI," NVIDIA is expected to reach a $6 trillion market cap by 2027 as they move toward owning end-to-end platforms.
    * **Tesla:** Positioned as a premier "physical AI" play; autonomous driving capabilities are estimated to be worth $1 trillion alone.
* **Investment Strategy:** Despite geopolitical tensions, Ives argues that selling is historically the wrong move during "white-knuckle moments." He maintains that tech stocks will likely hit new all-time highs this year.

### Transforming Infrastructure and Vertical Markets
* **Defense and Drones:** Eric Brock (Chairman & CEO, Ondas) highlights the shift from drones as commodities to drones as autonomous systems.
    * **Counter-Drone Infrastructure:** Protecting global locations from drone threats is viewed as a decade-long infrastructure build-out rather than a short-term military trend.
    * **Integration:** Ondas is focused on a "system of systems" approach, integrating air and ground robots via platforms like Palantir.
* **Western Sports Analytics:** Casey Lane (Teton Ridge) explains the application of Palantir’s technology to rodeo and bull riding.
    * **"Moneyball" for Rodeo:** Teton Ridge is building a video analytics platform to track animals and riders, creating predictive models for performance and scoring.
    * **Viewer Engagement:** Using data to explain complex scoring to fans, similar to "Next Gen Stats" in the NFL.

### The Evolution of Software Engineering (FDE Model)
* **Accelerated Deployment:** Ted Mabrey (Palantir, 16-year veteran) reports that the pace of software deployment is "accelerating to zero." Applications that used to take years to develop are now reaching MVP (Minimum Viable Product) status in days or weeks.
* **The "Iron Man Suit" for Operations:** The role of the Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE) has shifted from standardizing management tools to empowering "the guy at the edge" (e.g., a plant manager).
    * **Operational Experts:** Software is now designed to act as an "Iron Man suit" for subject matter experts, allowing them to dictate how the system runs based on real-world tribal knowledge.
* **Infrastructure Strategy:** Palantir’s core philosophy is building adaptive infrastructure that presumes models will constantly improve, rather than building rigid, legacy-limited systems.

### Geopolitics, Job Loss, and Civil Liberties
* **White-Collar Displacement:** Dr. Alex Karp (CEO, Palantir) issues a stark warning regarding AI-driven job loss, particularly for "Lawyer 14506"—the average, non-specialized white-collar worker.
    * **The "Dyslexic" Advantage:** Karp argues that "normal-shaped skills" are becoming less valuable. He suggests that "neuro-divergent" individuals and those with "artistry" who look at things from unique directions will thrive in the new economy.
* **National Security and Sovereignty:**
    * **US vs. China:** The world is viewed as a "have/have-not" binary between American and Chinese AI capabilities. Karp argues that America must win on the battlefield to protect domestic order.
    * **Nationalization Risks:** Karp predicts a future political movement toward nationalizing AI technology as displacement causes social unrest.
* **Privacy and Rights:** Karp positions himself as a defender of Fourth Amendment (privacy) rights at the executive table, arguing that while AI must be lethal on the battlefield, its domestic use must be strictly litigated to prevent government overreach.
