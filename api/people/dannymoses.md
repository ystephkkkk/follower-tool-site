#### TL;DR
Former "Big Short" partners Steve Eisman and Danny Moses discuss current market risks, drawing parallels between today's massive AI debt issuance/securitization and the 2008 subprime mortgage warehouse lines, while also analyzing the federal deficit, the housing market, and private equity's growing monetization challenges.

### Fed Policy, Treasury Management & The Housing Market
* **Higher Rates for Longer:** Both speakers agree that underlying interest rates will remain elevated over a secular cycle rather than a cyclical one.
* **Treasury Limits:** **Danny Moses** notes that government efforts to control the long end of the curve (such as Treasury bond-swapping) are largely symbolic, stating that "nobody's bigger than the market itself."
* **The Housing Squeeze:** **Steve Eisman** highlights mortgage rates sitting at approximately 7.5%, effectively stalling home purchases for average buyers. 
* **Wealth Shift:** **Danny Moses** points out a major structural shift where household wealth tied to the stock market now exceeds real estate, making equity market downturns a greater economic risk than housing.

### The AI Trade, CapEx & Concentration Risk
* **Valuation Parallels:** **Danny Moses** compares the AI craze to the 1999–2000 telecom and fiber-optic bubble, noting that while the technology is real, the sheer scale of CapEx, token economics, and cash burn by players like OpenAI and Anthropic requires strict mathematical scrutiny.
* **Balance Sheet Liabilities:** **Danny Moses** notes that AI infrastructure provider CoreWeave carries **$51 billion** in debt (including leases).
* **Nvidia Concentration:** **Steve Eisman** expresses concern over extreme concentration risk, citing Nvidia's recent 10-Q filing showing that **70%** of its accounts receivable come from just five customers.
* **Securitization Parallels:** **Danny Moses** draws a direct parallel between today's Wall Street-Nvidia securitization partnerships and the 2008 subprime era, pointing out how Wall Street warehouse lines initially fueled the mortgage boom before being abruptly pulled.

### Private Equity and Private Credit Pressures
* **Monetization Lag:** **Steve Eisman** highlights that the typical private equity monetization timeline has extended from 3-4 years to 7 (and potentially 10) years, creating cash flow friction.
* **The AUM Machine:** **Danny Moses** explains that private equity built its massive footprint as an AUM-gathering game to replace traditional bank lending post-GFC, but the model has not been severely tested by a prolonged shutdown cycle outside of COVID.
* **Retail Stuffed:** **Danny Moses** warns retail investors to be wary of private market products and gated funds being pushed into their inboxes, noting that institutional investors pass on these deals when the terms fail to favor them.

### The U.S. Debt, Deficit & Global Reserve Status
* **Political Paralysis:** Both speakers agree that neither political party shows any appetite to make the painful spending cuts required to address the growing national deficit.
* **Global Standing:** **Steve Eisman** acknowledges similarities between U.S. debt-to-GDP and France's struggles, but argues that the U.S. remains insulated because Treasuries serve as the undisputed foundation of the global financial system.
* **De-Dollarization Risks:** **Danny Moses** counters that the U.S. is "playing with fire," noting that the dollar's share of global reserves has dropped from **71%** to **57%** over the past two decades, while global central banks now hold more gold (**27%**) than U.S. Treasuries (**22%**).

## "Big Short" Legend Danny Moses on Dimon, Private Credit, Prediction Markets | Bloomberg Businessweek

- Channel: Bloomberg Podcasts
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TmUB10Qsz6M
- Published: 2026-03-03

- Speakers: Danny Moses (Money Manager)

---

**TL;DR**
Danny Moses warns that the current expansion of private credit into retail products mirrors the pre-2008 subprime mortgage crisis, cautioning that while the risk isn't yet systemic, a liquidity crunch could eventually necessitate a federal bailout.

### The Resurgence of GFC-Era Credit Patterns
* **Private Credit Warning:** Moses argues that private credit and private equity firms' recent push into retail products "rhymes" with the years preceding the 2008 Great Financial Crisis (GFC).
    * **Warehouse Lines:** Banks are currently lending to private equity and private capital firms, much like they provided warehouse lines to mortgage companies (e.g., New Century, Countrywide) in 2004–2006.
    * **The Liquidity Trap:** The danger arises when Wall Street sees credit turn and pulls back lines; firms that cannot sell their debt products get "stuck" with them on their balance sheets, exposing hidden leverage.
* **Retail "Democratization":** Moses expresses skepticism toward "democratizing" private credit for everyday investors.
    * **Investment Strategy:** He advises retail investors to avoid individual private credit funds and instead buy the parent companies of large PE firms—such as **Blackstone, KKR, and Apollo**—which offer liquidity and permanent capital.
    * **Caveat Emptor:** He suggests that if a retail broker recommends a private credit fund for a 401(k), it is a red flag regarding the broker's incentives.

### AI and the Impending Employment Shift
* **White-Collar Displacement:** While AI increases efficiency and expands margins for corporations, Moses views it as a major threat to the labor market.
    * **The Block Example:** He cites Jack Dorsey’s 40% staff reduction at Block as a template for other CEOs looking to maximize margins through AI-driven "efficiency."
    * **The "Think Piece" Logic:** Moses highlights the "Citrine memo," which outlines a potential AI-driven economic apocalypse by 2028, noting that while the logic is sound, it remains a "logic piece" that CEOs are already acting upon.
* **Market Impact:** Moses believes the stock market is currently propping up the economy through a "wealth effect," but a sell-off in stocks—triggered by rising unemployment—could be the ultimate catalyst for a downturn.

### Macro Concerns: US Debt and Inflation
* **US Debt Sustainability:** Moses expresses significant concern over the rising US debt, currently hovering near $35 trillion.
    * **The $50 Trillion Forecast:** He monitors prediction markets for the possibility of US debt exceeding $50 trillion by 2028, noting that hitting that number would likely imply another pandemic-scale crisis or a private credit bailout.
    * **Treasury Issuance Risk:** He criticizes the government's shift toward issuing more T-bills instead of 10-year notes to hide pressure on long-term yields, which creates massive refinancing risk if inflation stays high.
* **The Gold Trade:** Moses remains "long" on gold and gold miners as a hedge against central bank "incompetence" and currency debasement.
    * **Commodity Outlook:** He believes the commodity trade is "here to stay" and views gold as the ultimate insurance against the eventual endgame of bailing out the over-leveraged private credit sector.

### The Rise of Prediction Markets
* **Sentiment Indicators:** Moses uses prediction platforms like **Kalshi** not just for trading, but as a news-gathering tool to gauge the probability of geopolitical events and economic shifts.
    * **Market Utility:** These platforms allow for direct expressions of macro views (e.g., "Will the Fed cut in March?" or "Will Gold outperform Bitcoin?") without the complexity of trading Fed fund futures.
    * **Institutional Adoption:** He notes that major firms like Goldman Sachs and JP Morgan are increasingly citing these markets in research notes, signaling their evolution into legitimate sentiment indicators.
