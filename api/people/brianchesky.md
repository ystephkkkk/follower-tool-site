## How Brian Chesky Is Redesigning Airbnb for the AI Era

**TL;DR** 
Airbnb CEO Brian Chesky outlines a critical leadership shift from hands-off corporate delegation ("CEO mode") back to a deeply detailed, design-driven, and hands-on approach ("founder mode"). By rebuilding Airbnb around small, elite teams, flat hierarchies, and a relentless focus on the end-user experience, Chesky is positioning the company to lead an upcoming "consumer AI renaissance" where the atomic unit of the product shifts from the home to the individual.

---

### The Industrial Design Philosophy of Business

* **The Influence of RISD & Classic Design:** Chesky's background in industrial design at the Rhode Island School of Design (RISD)—studying legends like Raymond Loewy, Charles and Ray Eames, and Apple's Jony Ive—deeply shaped his approach to leadership and company building.
* **The Fusion of Design and Product:** Unlike other design fields where aesthetic awards are detached from business success, an industrial design is only successful if it sells. 
    * Because there are no traditional "product managers" in industrial design, the designer naturally functions as the PM, marrying empathy, user journeys, mechanical engineering, and commercial viability.
* **Designing the Complete User Journey:** Great products require looking at a problem through the eyes of every stakeholder.
    * *The Ventilator Example:* When designing a child's medical ventilator at RISD, Chesky had to design not just the machine, but the psychological journey of a scared six-year-old patient, the anxious parents looking for reassurance, and the technician who took pride in operating a complex device. 

### Transitioning from "CEO Mode" to "AI Founder Mode"

* **The Fallacy of Over-Delegation:** Chesky argues that founders are often pushed to become traditional "CEOs" by over-delegating to professional managers. By 2019, Airbnb had grown to 7,000 employees, and Chesky felt he had lost control of the company’s direction, describing it as "a car without a steering wheel."
* **Wartime Leadership in the Pandemic:** When the COVID-19 pandemic struck, Airbnb lost 80% of its business in eight weeks. Chesky pivoted from peacetime delegation to wartime "founder mode"—working 100-hour weeks, removing management layers, and personally auditing every detail of the business for nearly three years.
* **The Blueprints for AI Founder Mode:** As companies adopt generative AI, Chesky is actively redesigning Airbnb around a more intense, flat structure based on a few core principles:
    * **Eliminating Pure People Managers:** Managers who only manage people and do not produce actual work will not survive the AI transition. Leaders must stay close to reality and manage people *through the work*.
    * **Flat Hierarchies:** Chesky points to the Catholic Church, which has operated for 2,000 years with only four layers of management, suggesting tech companies must aggressively flatten their organizational charts.
    * **Universal "Coding":** Every team member must "code" within their respective field. If you are a lawyer, you must actively read case law; if you are a design leader, you must actively design. 

### Project Hawaii: Scaling via Small, Elite Teams

* **The "Hawaii" Model:** To replicate early-stage startup velocity within a multi-billion-dollar company, Chesky created "Project Hawaii"—a lean, cross-functional team of 10 to 12 designers, engineers, and data scientists tasked with optimizing Airbnb's guest booking conversion funnel.
    * **Financial Leverage:** This single lean team delivered $200–$300 million in incremental revenue in year one, $400–$500 million in year two, and now runs at an incremental run rate of over 600 basis points of a $13–$14 billion revenue base.
* **The "One to Ten to Many" Playbook:** Airbnb has institutionalized the Hawaii model to launch new verticals. Instead of trying to scale globally from day one, they pilot intensely in one market, refine the model in 10, and then "industrialize" the product to scale.
    * **Focus on a Niche:** This strategy relies on Peter Thiel’s advice to dominate a tiny monopoly before scaling, and Paul Graham’s YC principle: *"It is better to have 100 people love you than a million people sort of like you."*
* **The 11-Star Experience:** An exercise where teams design a user journey from a standard 5-star check-in up to an absurd 11-star experience (e.g., being greeted by Elon Musk and taken to space). By pushing the boundaries of imagination to the absurd, a highly differentiated 6- or 7-star experience (like a personalized local welcome and airport pickup) becomes realistic, highly achievable, and scalable.

### A High-Leverage Approach to Pipeline Hiring

* **The Hiring vs. Managing Trade-off:** Chesky views hiring as a direct lever for management. Spending more time recruiting highly autonomous, elite talent directly minimizes the time required for day-to-day management. 
* **The Problem with Traditional Searches:** Standard search firms collect resumes and narrow down candidates to a few choices, which often results in mediocre hires who then build their own bloated empires.
* **Pipeline Recruiting Strategy:**
    * **Proactive Networking:** Chesky continuously meets the best talent in Silicon Valley informally, building deep pipelines years before an active search is opened.
    * **Working Backwards from Results:** Instead of looking at resumes, Chesky identifies exceptional creative outputs (e.g., an incredible ad campaign or product feature) and traces the work back to the specific individuals who built it.
    * **CEOs as Co-Hiring Managers:** Chesky remains the co-hiring manager for the top 200 roles at Airbnb. He spends two to three hours every single day on recruiting, viewing it as his most important job.

### Shifting to Intrinsic Motivation and Consumer AI

* **Rejecting the "Adulation" Trap:** Chesky reflects on Airbnb's $100 billion IPO during the pandemic, realizing that achieving external status and public adulation felt incredibly empty. He refocused on the intrinsic, artistic joy of making things.
* **The Impending Consumer AI Renaissance:** While Silicon Valley is currently saturated with enterprise AI software, Chesky predicts a massive consumer AI renaissance over the next 12 to 24 months as interfaces become simpler and more intuitive.
* **Redesigning the Atomic Unit of Airbnb:** Airbnb is preparing to shift its core "atomic unit" from a *home* to a *person*. 
    * By building the most robust authenticated identity, preference library, and real-world social graph on the internet, Airbnb aims to transition from a single-product company to an integrated ecosystem offering dozens of travel and lifestyle services.
