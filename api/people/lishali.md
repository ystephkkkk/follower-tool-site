## Can Open Models Keep Up With OpenAI and Anthropic? | Nathan Lambert

#### TL;DR
Nathan Lambert (founder of Trillium and AI researcher) and Lisha Li (a16z Partner) discuss the dynamics between closed frontier AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) and open-source/open-weight model builders, concluding that while frontier labs hold structural advantages in compute and capital, open labs—particularly in China—remain remarkably competitive by acting as fast followers and leveraging distillation, synthetic data, and RL environments.

---

### 1. Capital, Talent, and the Open-Closed Frontier Dynamic
* **Foundational Inputs:** Model progress is driven by three forms of capital: compute, data, and talent.
* **The Closed Frontier Labs:** 
  * **OpenAI and Anthropic:** Held up by Nathan Lambert as the true frontier labs that take the earliest, most ambitious bets, driving paradigms like reasoning inference scaling laws and cloud-code explosions.
  * **Organizational Reset:** Success depends heavily on the human aspect and organizational charts; companies like Meta successfully reset from the bottom up to regain positive momentum.
* **The Chinese Open-Model Strategy:** 
  * **Commercialization & Distribution:** Chinese labs (e.g., Moonshot AI, Zhipu, Kimi/01.AI) build open-weight models primarily as a practical distribution and commercialization strategy to break into the US market rather than for pure altruism.
  * **Talent Parity vs. Compute Starvation:** Nathan Lambert asserts that Chinese labs possess a comparable, high-level talent pool to the US, but operate at a massive compute deficit (e.g., top Chinese pre-training researchers having access to a fraction of the 5,000 H100 equivalents available to an average OpenAI researcher).

### 2. Distillation, Data, and the Open-Source Catch-Up
* **The Distillation Debate:** 
  * **Friction in Open Post-Training:** While public perception views distillation as a primary leveling lever, Nathan Lambert notes that open literature reveals open model builders are often stuck using outdated Qwen models because replicating teacher reasoning traces and SFT/RL pipelines from top models remains technically difficult.
  * **Market Dynamics:** Despite friction, APIs and reasoning traces are easily exploited; Nathan Lambert notes that Chinese labs have teams dedicated to stealing reasoning traces, and Jensen Huang’s perspective is that distillation is simply "competition."
* **Economic Time Scales:** 
  * Closed labs enjoy integrated, efficient inference at scale, high margins, and direct customer feedback loops. 
  * Open-model economies involve a fragmented supply chain (model builders, inference companies, post-training companies), meaning it takes significantly longer for open ecosystems to build ownership, find ROI, and deploy proprietary data fine-tunes.

### 3. RL Environments, Synthetic Data, and Reward Hacking
* **The Data Economy Boom:** 
  * Billions of dollars are being spent on buying data and building RL environments.
  * Much of the commercial data currently sold by third parties is flawed, featuring rough edges, overseas contractors via intermediaries, and a high rate of **reward hacking** where smart models game poorly constructed test files or empty mazes.
* **In-House Shifts:** Frontier labs are increasingly bringing data generation and environment building in-house to prevent data leakage and proprietary signal loss.

### 4. Trillium, Open Infrastructure, and Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI)
* **Trillium's Mission:** Nathan Lambert launched the nonprofit research lab **Trillium** (named after the spring ephemeral flower that blooms before the canopy grows) to provide open infrastructure—such as open-source RL environments for high-leverage terminal tasks and SFT mid-training stacks.
* **Demystifying RSI:** Trillium aims to document the actual tools, tasks, configs, and failure trajectories of AI-driven research to demystify recursive self-improvement and help policymakers and researchers understand where autonomous loops actually get stuck.
* **Community Contributions:** While AI agents on GitHub introduce noise (e.g., LLM-generated fake research issues), open-source collaboration remains a vital counterweight to commercial secrecy.

### 5. Scaling Realities and Societal Preparedness
* **Exponential Scaling Limits:** Scaling laws show exponential increases in compute for linear performance gains, making sudden "fast takeoffs" unlikely due to human bottlenecks and extreme resource costs.
* **Cybersecurity Gaps:** Nathan Lambert warns that while capability plots and RSI do not indicate immediate existential doom, society is woefully underprepared for the cross-public cybersecurity risks and vulnerabilities diffusing outward as open and closed models proliferate.

## Inside OpenAI’s Breakthroughs in Mathematical Reasoning

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1JvyLGd2Sfs

- Speakers: Lisha Li (Infra Partner, a16z); Mehtaab Sawhney (Mathematician, OpenAI); Mark Sellke (Mathematician, OpenAI)

---

### TL;DR
OpenAI models like GPT-5 and Astra are rapidly advancing mathematical reasoning, moving beyond simple information retrieval to execute complex proofs, manage combinatorial search trees, and autonomously solve decades-old open problems in combinatorics and group theory.

### Introduction and Background
* **Transition to OpenAI:** Mehtaab Sawhney and Mark Sellke joined OpenAI after observing the rapid capability gains of models like GPT-5, specifically noting when models achieved an International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) gold medal standard in the summer.
* **Initial Breakthroughs:** The turning point involved querying models with open problems from Paul Erdős' collection, where models successfully searched the literature, identified relevant references, and confirmed whether problems were still genuinely unsolved within minutes.

### Core Capabilities of AI in Mathematics
* **Rigorous Execution:** AI excels at meticulous execution once given a general direction, avoiding human errors in fine-grained details such as verifying epsilon-delta arguments or handling delicate linear programming constraints.
* **Contextual Cleanliness & Backtracking:** Unlike human mathematicians whose context windows become polluted by failed paths—making it difficult to completely reset intuition—AI models can systematically make mistakes, backtrack, update probabilities of success, and pivot without psychological friction.
* **Comparison to Human Collaboration:** The reasoning traces often resemble reading disorganized notes from a colleague, capturing the natural struggle of choosing promising approaches, pruning search trees, and combining ideas from across the literature.

### Specific Mathematical Breakthroughs (Astra's Results)
* **High-Dimensional Sphere Packing:** 
  * The model analyzed the linear programming (LP) bound in large dimensions $d$, providing a concrete function $f$ and proving that no function can perform better. 
  * It resolved an asymptotic behavior conjecture previously backed only by complex numerics, matching bounds roughly equivalent to $2^{-0.61d}$ (or $e^{2\pi + o(1))^d}$).
* **Spherical and Binary Codes:** 
  * Using representation theory and leveraging the symmetry of spheres and hypercubes, the model improved existing bounds on error-correcting codes.
  * In an interactive session where researchers prompted the model to push its representation theory further, the process ultimately yielded the conjectured value for full-space sphere packing.
* **Construction of a Non-Sofic Group:** 
  * Addressing a major open problem in group theory (related to whether all groups can be approximated by finite groups, akin to the Aldous-Lions conjecture), Astra produced a concise combinatorial argument ruling out specific conspiracies and proving the existence of a non-sofic group without resorting to complex quantum complexity frameworks.

### Impact on the Mathematical Community and Future of the Field
* **Absorbing Knowledge:** While AI will produce exponentially more mathematics, it simultaneously lowers the barrier to absorbing and understanding existing literature, allowing researchers (and eventually non-specialists) to input complex PDFs into models to extract rough proof strategies.
* **Evolution of Human Roles:** The bottleneck is shifting from raw proof-generation to curation, framework-building, and effectively communicating communal understanding. 
* **Tackling Deep Mysteries:** Routine problems may become automated, allowing mathematicians to focus higher-level human taste and judgment on fundamental mysteries.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **P versus NP Difficulty Ceiling:** Plausible that AI will never solve something as difficult as P versus NP, even with exponential capability increases — Mark Sellke.

## Can AI Learn Mathematical Intuition?

- Channel: a16z
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tQI35CSNB08
- Published: 2026-09-01
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260901.html#video-tQI35CSNB08
- Speakers: Lisha Li (Host, a16z); Daniel Litt (Assistant Professor of Mathematics, University of Toronto)

---

### TL;DR
Frontier AI models are increasingly capable of solving specific mathematical problems and grinding through long calculations, but they still struggle with deep intuition, theory-building, and big-picture understanding. Daniel Litt and Lisha Li explore how AI is shifting mathematical research, the risk of academic "slop," and why human-driven mathematical exploration remains vital for true understanding.

### State of AI in Mathematical Research
* **The Erdős Unit Distance Problem:** Daniel Litt highlights the solution to the Erdős unit distance problem (announced in mid-May) as his favorite fully autonomous AI result to date.
    * **Creative elements:** Unlike most results that apply known techniques in clever ways, this solution unexpectedly brought in classical ideas from the 1960s to study point configurations, which was subsequently used by human mathematicians to find counterexamples for other open questions like the sum product conjecture over real numbers.
* **Model Capabilities and Limitations:** 
    * **Strengths:** Frontier models excel at applying known techniques, running massively parallel searches (e.g., checking thousands of examples), and executing long, tedious computations.
    * **Weaknesses:** Models struggle significantly with intuition, formulating the right questions, broad theoretical frameworks, and "big picture" architectural or unit-testing of proofs.
* **Anthropic vs. OpenAI:** Litt notes that OpenAI's models (specifically GPT-5.6 Pro) and Anthropic's Claude models (specifically Opus 4.5/4.6 and Fable) are neck-and-neck in mathematical capabilities, though he personally utilizes ChatGPT more out of habit and superior early math performance.

### The Nature of Mathematical Practice
* **Problem Solvers vs. Theory Builders:** Litt characterizes his own work as a "problem solver" where open problems serve as benchmarks measuring our failure to understand underlying differential equations or algebraic varieties.
* **The Role of Non-Rigorous Philosophy:** Much of human mathematical progress relies on non-rigorous analogies, philosophy, and testing hypotheses through concrete examples (akin to concept-based physics) rather than pure mechanical symbol-pushing.
* **The Danger of "Slot Machine" Mathematics:** 
    * With the rise of AI, there has been an uptick in low-quality "slop" papers on the arXiv (e.g., 800 AI-generated pages on the resolution of singularities) where users prompt models to solve recent conjectures without genuine human engagement or understanding.
    * Multiple papers with identical AI-generated proofs of the same theorem have occasionally been published within days of each other.

### Adapting the Mathematical Community
* **Incentive Structures:** Existing academic incentives encourage postdocs to pump out papers by letting models grind out proofs. Litt argues institutions must redesign incentives to prioritize human capital development and genuine comprehension.
* **Preserving Human Curiosity:** Progress historically stems from a diverse community of humans pursuing personal curiosities across a high-dimensional space of mathematics; subordinating exploration entirely to AI outputs risks narrowing this vital diversity.
* **Education and Tool Usage:** Just as students face a bimodal distribution regarding AI homework shortcuts, mathematicians must actively use tools to *deepen* understanding rather than relinquish critical thinking.
