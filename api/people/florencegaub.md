### TL;DR
Global trade is experiencing its most severe disruption in 80 years of the WTO, driven by geopolitical conflict and protectionism, yet it continues to demonstrate unexpected resilience by rewiring supply chains and forging regional corridors. Maintaining economic connectivity now depends on building integrated multi-model infrastructure, modernizing 20th-century multilateral institutions, and prioritizing strategic flexibility over isolation.

### Geopolitical Shifts and the Reshaping of Global Trade
* **WTO Disruption Levels:** The head of the WTO reported that current global trade disruption is the worst seen in 80 years of the organization, though global trade has shown surprising resilience against these pressures.
* **Security and Trade Convergence:** **Florence Gaub** noted that trade and security are increasingly intertwined, pointing to maritime routes like the North Sea and the Arctic—where Russia holds 51% of the territory and has increased maritime exports by 13-15% over the last 10 years—as future focal points of geopolitical and economic competition.
* **Emerging Economic Demand:** **Karen E. Young** highlighted that global demand for openness is increasingly driven by emerging markets and developing economies, noting that China's exports of AI-related technical manufacturing increased 36% in September compared to the previous year despite major trade actor tensions.

### Supply Chain Resilience and Infrastructure Adaptations
* **Multi-Model Preparedness in the UAE:** **Fahad Al Gergawi** explained that the UAE adopted a multi-model supply chain logistics system starting in the early 1980s, combining ports, airports (such as Emirates cargo/passenger operations), and services to maintain an outward-facing economy.
* **Eastern Corridors and Connectivity:** The UAE officially designated its eastern corridor ports of Khor Fakkan and Fujairah as key trade routes to bypass the Strait of Hormuz, complemented by a rail system linking to Sohar in Oman.
* **East-to-West Regional Solutions:** **Farid Belbouab** described how Gulf Tainer utilized the UAE's East Coast optionality during recent Strait of Hormuz disruptions by discharging cargo at Khor Fakkan, trucking it to Mina Khalid on the West Coast in under 3 to 4 days, and reloading it onto feeder ships for upper Gulf countries.
* **Energy Flow Volatility:** **Karen E. Young** noted that the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea disruptions dramatically increased shipping costs, raising a VLCC journey between the Gulf and East Asia from $40,000 per day to $1.3 million per day, and forcing reliance on older tanker ships and shuttle traffic.

### Economic Sovereignty and Small State Strategies
* **Resilient Bridges over Walls:** **Timcho Mucunski** stated that small nations succeed not by building the largest walls, but the most resilient bridges, emphasizing that North Macedonia's major capital investments target pan-European transport corridors (Corridor 8 and Corridor 10) and energy diversification.
* **Modernizing Institutional Architecture:** **Timcho Mucunski** argued that the global trading system operates on a 20th-century institutional architecture that must be reformed and modernized for the 21st century.
* **Economic Sovereignty via Connectivity:** **Timcho Mucunski** emphasized that a nation's economic sovereignty is deeply connected to its physical and energy connectivity.

### Proactive Trade Diplomacy and Regional Partnerships
* **Bypassing the Wait:** **Fahad Al Gergawi** emphasized the UAE's proactive philosophy: within the first month of regional conflicts, ministers traveled to Japan to conclude a Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA), rejecting the instinct to wait for dust to settle.
* **CEPA and FIT P Frameworks:** The UAE launched its CEPA program in 2021 to eliminate tariff barriers and established the Future Investment and Trade Partnership (FIT P)—alongside Singapore, Switzerland, and New Zealand—as a platform for like-minded medium-size economies to navigate rough economic seas.
* **Investment and Trade Volumes:** The UAE recorded $48.3 billion in inbound foreign direct investment last year while ranking as the sixth-largest global investor at $63 billion, deploying capital significantly into global supply chain infrastructure.
