## 'WINNER & LOSERS': AI FEARS spread, stocks slide on disruption anxiety

- Channel: Fox Business Clips
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCnxiWZUYlo
- Published: 2026-02-12
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260212.html#video-BCnxiWZUYlo

**TL;DR**
While AI-driven "scare trades" have recently pressured financial stocks, industry leaders view AI as a significant productivity tailwind rather than a disruptive threat, as traditional banks aggressively outspend fintech rivals to integrate automation.

### AI Disruption and the "Scare Trade"
* **Market Volatility:** Bank and wealth management stocks have faced recent sell-offs following the launch of AI tools—such as Altruist’s automated personalized tax strategy—which investors fear will displace human advisors and shift market share.
* **CEO Sentiment:** At the KBW Winter Financial Services conference, Tom Michaud reported that among 100+ participating banks, AI is the most frequent topic of discussion.
    * **Tailwind vs. Headwind:** Bank CEOs largely view AI as a tool for efficiency and productivity rather than a threat. 
    * **Operational Integration:** Traditional firms are moving "all in" on AI to remove friction in client communications and improve backend productivity.
* **Adaptability:** Michaud argues the disruption risk is overstated, noting the banking industry has historically been highly adaptable to technological shifts and currently maintains strong balance sheets to fund necessary investments.

### The "Giant Collision": Banks vs. Fintech and Crypto
* **Converging Sectors:** A "giant collision" is occurring as fintech firms seek bank charters while traditional banks invest heavily to function like fintechs. 
* **Technology Spending Power:** Large incumbents are outspending pure-tech rivals to maintain their edge.
    * **JPMorgan Chase:** Spends approximately $18 billion annually on technology, a figure Michaud notes is surpassed by only a handful of "hyperscaler" companies in the U.S.
* **Market Share Competition:** While firms like Coinbase and Robinhood are building "real businesses," traditional banking tools are fighting back successfully; for example, the bank-owned Zelle is seeing strong growth compared to competitors like PayPal-owned Venmo.

### Capital Discipline and Sector Rotation
* **Investor Priorities:** Mark Tepper notes that the market is currently punishing companies that take on heavy debt to fund AI expansion (e.g., Oracle) while rewarding those that fund innovation through cash flow.
* **Financial Fundamentals:** Banks are currently benefiting from a sector rotation because they lack the massive capital expenditure (CapEx) demands of tech hardware companies, yet they possess the earnings power to adopt the software.
* **Strong Performance:** For the first time in years, many banks are outperforming the broader market and trading at or near all-time highs despite the recent AI-related volatility.

### Regulatory Landscape and Future Stability
* **Leveling the Playing Field:** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) recently approved several bank charters for fintechs, signaling that "non-banks" must now adhere to the same regulatory frameworks as traditional institutions.
* **AI Backlash in Washington:** There is growing political friction regarding the rapid expansion of AI data centers, with Senators like Bernie Sanders and Richard Blumenthal raising concerns over massive power consumption.
* **Deposit Insurance Reform:** Michaud identifies deposit insurance reform as the "remaining piece" of the 2023 banking crisis that has yet to be resolved.
    * **Soundness:** In a digital world where money moves instantly, Michaud argues that the safety of the banking framework remains a primary competitive advantage over crypto and fintech "stablecoins" that lack similar protections.

> "Investors want to see a disciplined allocation of capital right now and anytime they envision that a company is potentially being reckless with that [AI] spending, they're getting punished." — **Mark Tepper**

> "It's a giant collision... you've got fintechs that want to become banks and you've got banks that are investing to become fintechs. I believe there'll be winners and losers on both sides." — **Tom Michaud**
