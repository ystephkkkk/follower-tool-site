## The AI Gold Rush: Models Are Shovels, Data Is the Gold | RAISE Summit 2026

- Channel: RAISE Summit
- Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NITnsKtHDu4
- Published: 2026-07-27
- Briefing: https://ystephkkkk.github.io/follower-tool-site/20260727.html#video-NITnsKtHDu4
- Speakers: Gareth Davies (CPO, Okta); Will Bryk (Co-Founder & CEO, Exa); Dmitry Panenkov (Founder, emma)

---

**TL;DR:** While AI models are commoditizing into a base layer like the Linux kernel, the true strategic value and bottleneck lie in accessing, governing, and securing proprietary data. Enterprises must navigate a landscape of shifting economics (multi-step agent workflows), data trust, and security challenges to successfully move AI from sandboxes to production.

### The Commoditization of Models vs. the Value of Data
* **Models as Commoditized Infrastructure:**
  * **Will Bryk (Exa):** Open-source and base models are already crossing a threshold of intelligence where they are "good enough" for most knowledge work, shifting real differentiation to the data products have access to.
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Compared foundation models to the Linux kernel—a trusted, shared commodity that everyone relies on, with true value created by building specialized layers on top.
* **The Scale of Available Data:**
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Noted that the web's data is only a fraction of the world's total data.
  * **Will Bryk (Exa):** Illustrated the scale gap by noting web data sits in the exabyte range (mostly video), while the world's total data is in zetabytes (roughly 1,000x more), representing an enormous, mostly locked pool of value.
* **Data Enrichment and Trust Barriers:**
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Compared raw enterprise data to unenriched uranium—it requires "reactors" (preparation, governance, and infrastructure) before it can safely be utilized.
  * **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Emphasized that the biggest barrier to unlocking enterprise and consumer data is **trust**. People fear exposing their data and memory to external AI labs without choice, control, or fair compensation.

### Enterprise Adoption: Production vs. Sandbox Reality
* **Current Enterprise Maturity:**
  * **Alex Bouzari (DDN):** Argued that enterprise adoption is actively accelerating, citing real-world deployments where DDN serves as the data engine for Salesforce (increasing training productivity), RUI (in pharmaceuticals/genomics for drug discovery), financial services compliance, and sovereign government frameworks spanning departments from commerce to defense.
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Countered that broad enterprise adoption is still limited because much of enterprise data remains unstructured, unlabeled, and restricted by strict regulatory frameworks (especially in Europe).
* **The Shift to Agentic Workflows:**
  * **Gareth Davies (Okta):** Stressed that the industry is transitioning from simple chatbots to multi-step agents. While a standard chatbot makes one call, an agentic workflow makes dozens of calls, driving a massive increase in compute, data usage, and cost.

### Security, Governance, and Identity
* **The Security Gap:**
  * **Gareth Davies (Okta):** Highlighted that **48% of enterprises deploying AI in production are not securing their agents**, often leading developers to hard-code API keys and grant overprivileged static credentials. 
  * **Identity as Foundation:** Agentic security requires identity management to enforce granular access policies, govern reasoning chains, and monitor tool calls to internal and third-party MCP servers.
* **Sovereignty and Compliance:**
  * **Alex Bouzari (DDN):** Noted that sovereign AI implementations require air-gapping, multi-tenancy controls, and strict data localization to satisfy national and enterprise security requirements.
  * **Dmitry Panenkov (emma):** Emphasized that legal compliance, data residency regulations, and secure data movement are non-negotiable pillars for enterprise and government AI architectures.

### Predictions & Forward Calls
* **Will Bryk (Exa):** Predicts that the emerging agent economy will **"thousand-x over the next few years,"** enabling individuals and companies to monetize their proprietary data via agent-consumption APIs like ExaConnect.
* **Mark Surman (Mozilla):** Predicts that Privacy-Enhanced Technologies—specifically **federated learning**—will reach maturity within the **next 5 years**, allowing organizations to collaborate and train models on distributed data without exposing or pooling base datasets.
