TL;DR

Vitalik Buterin and Balaji Srinivasan discuss how AI is accelerating formal verification, cryptography roadmaps, and the expansion of network schools into startup cities like Kazakhstan.

AI, Cryptography, and Formal Verification

  • Public Key Cryptography Resilience: Vitalik Buterin notes that while public key cryptography will continue, it may take hits to concrete security. Just as factoring methods evolved from brute force to quadratic sieves and GNFS, elliptic curves and lattices could harbor undiscovered shortcuts that AI bots are uniquely equipped to find.
  • Learning via AI Interaction: Vitalik Buterin recommends an active-learning loop with AI models—asking questions, re-explaining concepts in one's own words, and iterating until the AI stops suggesting improvements—noting that even open local models like Qwen 3.8 Flash suffice for most day-to-day tasks.
  • Blockchain Node Integration: Balaji Srinivasan proposes bundling open-weight models directly into blockchain nodes, using a percentage of mined tokens to continuously audit codebase assumptions, similar to a defense department budget. Vitalik Buterin refines this into a "Proof of Excellence" issuance mechanism where useful proof-of-work tasks optimize the blockchain's own algorithms.
  • The Lean Ethereum Roadmap: Vitalik Buterin states that the ambitious Lean Ethereum roadmap—utilizing Lean 4 for EVM, recursive proving, and consensus—would be impossible without AI accelerating research and coding speedups by decades (potentially saving 15–25 years of work).
  • End-to-End Formal Verification: Vitalik Buterin predicts that the future of formal verification will stretch across the entire operating system and hardware, resulting in formally verified computers where the only underlying assumption is the laws of physics.
  • Pure Mathematicians and AI Prompts: Balaji Srinivasan argues that pure mathematicians' salaries will surge 10x because they are best equipped to prompt, verify, and navigate verifiable domains where AI output does not asymptote.

AI Writing and Social Networks

  • Structuring vs. Prose: Vitalik Buterin defends AI-generated formatting elements (like emojis and syntax highlighting) as a structured form of writing that separates writing effort from reading effort, comparing it to the visual efficiency of math formulas and code syntax. Balaji Srinivasan counters that such formatting currently signals bot spam rather than care.
  • Proof of Human Networks: Balaji Srinivasan predicts the rise of "Proof of Human Social Networks" where AI writing is strictly prohibited, positioning human-generated networks as upstream of AI models because they generate novel information.

Network Schools, Zuzalu, and Startup Cities

  • Network School Milestone: Balaji Srinivasan reflects on two years of Network School, highlighting that the initiative has successfully secured bids from roughly 10 countries, with Kazakhstan emerging as a primary hub.
  • Kazakhstan Node & Startup Cities: Balaji Srinivasan highlights Kazakhstan as an ideal location due to its vast land mass (3,000 times the space of Singapore with only 3 times the population), enabling the incubation of startup cities like Ethereum City, Coinbase City, and Stripe City.
  • Evolution of Educational Institutions: Balaji Srinivasan compares the evolution of educational/societal infrastructure across eras: Harvard represented pure offline/fiat (1.0), Stanford represented the fiat-internet hybrid (2.0), and the Network School represents the post-internet order (3.0).
  • The Leadership Dilemma: Vitalik Buterin and Balaji Srinivasan agree that physical-world coordination still requires leadership ("the only thing worse than having a leader is having no leaders"), necessitating a transition from a single leader to decentralized leadership across 100 network school equivalents.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Pure Mathematicians' Salaries: Balaji Srinivasan claims that pure mathematicians' salaries will go up 10x in the years to come because they are best positioned to prompt and verify verifiable AI domains.
  • End-to-End Formal Verification: Vitalik Buterin predicts the realization of fully formally verified computers where security proofs extend across the entire operating system and hardware, relying solely on the laws of physics.
  • Network School Expansion: Balaji Srinivasan targets scaling the Network School model to 100 equivalents around the world, each with their own leaders ("decentralized democracy").
