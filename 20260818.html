TL;DR

Flock CEO Garret Langley addresses privacy backlash, police data abuse, and safety technology, emphasizing that local communities must democratically control data retention windows (recently reduced to a 7-day default) and adopt mandatory audit tools to catch rogue law enforcement officers.

Company Footprint & Core Product Architecture

  • Founding & Mission: Started 9 years ago by Garret Langley with an initial $8 neighborhood pitch to live in a safer community; now operating in roughly 6,000+ cities across the U.S.
  • Core Technology Function:
    • License plate reading (ALPR) cameras capture still images of vehicles and read license plates.
    • System can detect vehicle features such as roof racks, bumper stickers, dents, and damage.
    • Explicit limitations: Does not do facial recognition, does not capture video, does not look inside cars, and does not allow searching for individuals via license plate cameras.
  • Impact Metrics (Annual/Monthly):
    • North of 1 million crimes solved using Flock last year.
    • North of 10,000 missing people found (e.g., grandparents with dementia, children).
    • July data points: ~1,125 missing people and over 20,000 stolen cars recovered.

Data Retention, Privacy Limits, and Democratic Choice

  • Data Retention Policy:
    • Default retention has recently been shifted down from 30 days to 7 days.
    • Flock estimates 90% of crimes get solved within 7 days of data retention.
    • Retention limits vary by jurisdiction: New Jersey state law mandates 5 years; Washington state and California have set maximum limits; New Hampshire enforces a 3-minute limit; New York state uses 21 days.
  • Democratic Control: City councils, mayors, and democratically elected state lawmakers hold final decision-making power regarding data retention periods and data sharing policies.
  • Vandalism & Activist Backlash:
    • Vandalizing cameras is a felony; arrests have occurred with suspects facing 15 to 20 years in jail.
    • Roughly 60 to 70 cities (out of 6,000, or about 1%) have deactivated or removed cameras, often driven by misinformation regarding facial recognition or fears of a permanent surveillance state.
    • Close to 20 cities have already turned their cameras back on after experiencing a rise in unsolved violent crimes.

Accountability, Police Abuse, and Audit Tools

  • The "Dirty Cop" Problem: Acknowledges public concern regarding police abuse of data bases (referencing state databases like California reporting 7,000 reported abuses last year).
  • Audit Assistance Tool: Introduced 4 months prior to the interview as a mandatory feature to detect abnormal search patterns (e.g., an officer repeatedly searching a specific target plate without adding it to a shared hot list).
  • Enforcement Outcomes: The tool successfully exposed and helped fire 9 Georgia police officers who abused the system.
  • Future Safeguards: Discussing potential multi-key authorization systems (requiring supervisor approval) for larger departments, weighed against the administrative burden for smaller 20-person police departments.

Emerging Technologies: Drones & AI

  • Drone Business Growth: Drones represent Flock's fastest-growing business unit; the LPR business accounts for just under 50% of forward-looking revenue.
  • How Drones Operate:
    • Housed in HVAC control boxes/docks on rooftops (police precincts or fire departments).
    • Deployed during 911 calls to arrive on scene ahead of officers (e.g., flying at 400 feet at 60 mph within 1 minute using 40x optical zoom).
    • Example given: Verified a suspect at a gas station was holding a lighter, not a gun, preventing a potentially fatal police confrontation.
  • Artificial Intelligence Stance:
    • Flock intentionally moves slowly with AI features to avoid the risks of hallucination or defining suspicion/predictive policing (e.g., Minority Report-style precrime).
    • Insists on maintaining a "human in the loop" for public safety decisions.

TL;DR

The White House strongly supports open-source AI as essential for U.S. technology leadership, while prioritizing regulatory clarity and a single national standard to help startups avoid a restrictive state-level patchwork. Michael Kratsios and Luther Lowe outline the federal strategy for science, technology, and AI at Startup School 2026.

Washington's AI Strategy & Open Source

  • Commitment to Open Source: The White House's national AI strategy (co-authored by Michael Kratsios, David Sacks, and Secretary Rubio in July 2025) explicitly commits to maintaining a vibrant ecosystem of both closed and open-source models.
  • Open Weights and Rumors: Speculative executive orders restricting open-source models are countered by active administration policy; Kratsios praised industry advocacy—including open letters organized by Y Combinator—for providing the direct feedback loop necessary for sound policymaking.
  • Federal-Private Dialogue: Government policies rely on real-time input from the startup community, tech ecosystems, and financial institutions to prevent decisions made in a Washington vacuum.

Regulating a Fast-Moving Technology

  • Avoiding EU-Style Red Lines: The White House explicitly avoids setting firm red lines or hard compute thresholds that fail to stand the test of time, citing the EU AI Act (passed before the invention of ChatGPT) as a cautionary tale of rigid regulation.
  • National Standards vs. State Patchworks: A key priority for President Trump is preempting state-level laws to establish a single national framework for AI, shielding resource-constrained startups from navigating conflicting state regulations.
  • Born-Free vs. Born-in-Captivity Tech: Regulation is categorized into "born-free" domains (like the early internet) which require preservation from over-regulation, and "born-in-captivity" domains (like commercial drones and supersonic flight) where targeted regulatory barriers and permit processes must be actively dismantled.

Washington Perspective vs. Silicon Valley Realities

  • Balancing Competing Concerns: Washington policymakers must simultaneously weigh the broader public perception of AI, data center expansion in local communities, healthcare applications, and labor market impacts alongside raw compute demand.
  • Supporting "Little Tech": The administration acknowledges the dominance of large tech players and utilizes traditional stakeholder mechanisms, Requests for Information (RFIs), and federal antitrust enforcement (such as ongoing DOJ and FTC cases against Google, Apple, Facebook, and Amazon) to ensure smaller startups retain a seat at the table.
  • Intellectual Property and Creators: While direct model outputs (e.g., generating unauthorized trademarked assets like Mickey Mouse) remain restricted by statute, the White House prefers to let market-driven revenue-sharing agreements and licensing deals mature organically rather than over-legislating the space prematurely.

The Inside View of Federal Policy & Science

  • Federated Decision-Making: Technology policy is distributed across multiple agencies (defense, health, energy, science); the White House acts as the central convening authority to align competing agency equities before policy is executed.
  • The "Science: A New Golden Age" Report: Released in early 2026, this report re-evaluates the post-WWII Vannevar Bush model, noting that R&D funding has inverted—with the private sector and philanthropy now driving roughly 70% of research compared to the federal government's 30%.
  • Autonomous Scientific Discovery: The report emphasizes that artificial intelligence will fundamentally transform material science, pharmaceuticals, and chemistry via autonomous cloud labs and robotic experimentation loops.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • AI in Science (Michael Kratsios): Scientists in material science, pharmaceuticals, and chemistry will conduct research dramatically differently because of artificial intelligence within 2 to 3 years.
  • Quantum Computing Goal (President Trump via Michael Kratsios): The Department of Energy is directed to build a scientifically relevant quantum computer by 2028.

TL;DR

Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed co-founded Oak Lab to build autonomous agents that learn continuously from direct experience rather than relying on static pre-training, synthetic data, or human curation. They argue that LLMs represent only about a quarter of intelligence and that achieving true general intelligence requires moving past current scaling paradigms through algorithmic innovations like continual backpropagation.

The Bitter Lesson and the Limitations of Current LLMs

  • The Essence of the Bitter Lesson: Rich Sutton explains that the core takeaway of his 2019 essay is to avoid wasting effort on embedding human knowledge, and instead focus purely on learning methods and search algorithms that scale with computation.
  • LLMs as Both Positive and Negative Examples: Rich Sutton views large language models as a positive triumph for compute scaling by digesting the internet, but a negative example when they hit the finite limit of human data and refuse to continue learning post-deployment.
  • The Scope of LLM Intelligence: Rich Sutton describes LLMs as an unanticipated, amazing scientific breakthrough in the skillful use of language, but stresses that they represent only roughly a quarter of total intelligence.

The Big World Hypothesis vs. Synthetic Data

  • The Rejection of Synthetic Data: Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed argue that synthetic data generation by frontier labs is "a big mistake" and a fundamental bottleneck.
  • Human Bottleneck in Simulation: Khurram Javed points out that generating synthetic data requires human domain experts to decide what is valid and build simulations, meaning the system is still bottlenecked by human knowledge rather than agent experience.
  • The Big World Perspective: Rich Sutton outlines the "big world hypothesis," stating that the world is infinitely more complex than any agent or simulator, forcing agents to constantly update severe approximations through continual learning from real-world interaction.

Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting via Continual Deep Learning

  • The Algorithmic Gap: Khurram Javed explains that current naive updates on a single stream of data destroy prior knowledge—a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting—which forces labs into batch-update workarounds like those used by coding assistants.
  • Continual Backpropagation: Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed state that catastrophic forgetting is entirely curable using algorithms like "continual backprop," which utilizes separate step sizes for every weight and continuously injects random unit initializations (generate-and-test in feature space).
  • The Local Minimum of Frontier Labs: Khurram Javed argues that frontier labs are trapped in a local minimum where their heavy investment in current products prevents them from taking the short-term performance hit required to pioneer a new continual learning paradigm.

Oak Lab's Research Agenda and Vision

  • The Alberta Plan Integration: Rich Sutton notes that Oak Lab's work builds on his 12-point Alberta Plan, where step 2 (continual deep learning) unlocks the ability to continually update world models and discover proper abstractions.
  • Hiring and Scaling Strategy: Khurram Javed states that Oak Lab is keeping its initial team extremely small—growing slowly to a handful or two or three people—to ensure ideological alignment around their distinct paradigm.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed claim that within 5 to 10 years, with Moore's Law scaling and proper energy-efficient algorithms, a unified continual-learning mind operating on a small footprint (comparable to 20 watts) will become entirely possible.
  • Rich Sutton and Khurram Javed state that in the next couple of years, Oak Lab aims to deploy a new generation of massively superior continual deep learning systems using continual backpropagation.

Ben Thompson and the host discuss the economics, geopolitics, and business models of the AI boom, highlighting capital duration mismatches, the commoditization of compute, and how hyperscalers like Amazon and Google are positioned to challenge Nvidia and legacy incumbents.

Geopolitical Realities & the AI Race

  • The Taiwan Bottleneck and TSMC Risk:
    • Ben Thompson argues that total U.S. AI dominance is dangerous because China's optimal game theory response to severe military superiority would be to blow up TSMC.
    • Moving semiconductor manufacturing entirely to the U.S. faces insurmountable cost and supply chain hurdles; Apple's partial diversification to India illustrates that companies only pay the astronomical "insurance policy" of moving out of China when forced.
  • The Current Global Equilibrium:
    • OpenAI and Anthropic are on the frontier, with Google, Grok, and Meta chasing.
    • Chinese labs (like GLM and Kimmy) are distilling models and staying 6 to 9 months behind, though inference costs for models like Kimmy remain significantly higher than commonly perceived.

Capital Mismatches, Bubbles, & Historical Paragons

  • The Railroad Analogy and Capital Exhaustion:
    • The current AI buildout echoes the 1870s railroad boom, which faced a severe duration mismatch: building infrastructure took decades, but the world ran out of short-term capital.
    • Even if a financial bubble or "blow up" occurs due to capital gaps, the underlying AI technology will persist and benefit humanity in the long run.
  • Google as the Modern Berkshire Hathaway:
    • Google’s recent equity issuance and high-margin search business parallel Berkshire Hathaway’s strategy of taking profits from high-margin, low-reinvestment businesses (like See's Candies) and deploying them into massive absolute-profit opportunities (like BNSF Railway).
    • AI requires cash-incinerating investments, but the absolute profits from addressing the entire TAM of white-collar work and robotics far outweigh Search.

Big Tech Strategies & Moats

  • Amazon's Deep Moat:
    • Amazon acts as its own first-best customer, building internal infrastructure (AWS, logistics, Graviton, Tranium) and scaling it until it can be successfully commercialized for external customers.
  • Apple's Hardware Focus:
    • Apple is insulated by owning customer access and physical device distribution.
    • Thompson argues that Apple is wise to stick to deterministic physical products rather than probabilistic AI models since they excel at hardware recall prevention and hardware-centric ecosystems.
  • Microsoft's Existential Middleware Play:
    • Microsoft's strategy mirrors IBM's 1990s playbook—acting as stable, dependable middleware that helps enterprises navigate AI without handing over their crown jewels.
    • However, usage-based pricing models ($100+/month user plans with token limits) risk breaking the predictable headcount-tethered revenue streams that made Microsoft successful.
  • Meta's Advertising Powerhouse:
    • Meta’s core advantage is its massive verification machine: the global ad marketplace, where billions of human clicks and AB tests validate generated text and images.
    • Thompson criticizes Meta management for failing to champion the societal positives of advertising and being overly apologetic about their ad-driven business model.

Compute, Commodities, & Nvidia's Positioning

  • The Commoditization of Compute and Intelligence:
    • Just as bandwidth and the internet became commodities with zero marginal cost for distribution, intelligence and compute are tracking toward commoditization.
  • Nvidia's Hidden Price Cuts and Ultimate Competitors:
    • Nvidia maintains high headline margins, but absorbs risk by backstopping neo-clouds and guaranteeing compute purchases to 2030, effectively executing hidden price cuts through balance sheet risk-shifting.
    • Nvidia's ultimate competitors are the hyperscalers (Google and Amazon) that possess superior costs of capital, build their own custom chips (TPUs and Tranium), and sell them as commodities.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Intel's Comeback via Scrutiny: Ben Thompson predicts that acute compute shortages and forgone revenues will force big tech companies to endure the pain of onboarding Intel and Samsung's logic foundries, ultimately saving Intel's foundry business.
  • External Tranium Adoption: Ben Thompson claims that Amazon will eventually sell its Tranium chips externally (following hints from Andy Jassy), giving them long-term leverage over AI companies.
