TL;DR

Annie Lamont, Co-Founder and Managing Partner of Oak HC/FT, shares insights from managing $14B across a 40+ year career, highlighting the convergence of AI with healthcare and fintech, and the growing existential threat posed by China capturing 50% of pharma's outside research dollars.

Oak HC/FT Fund Model & Strategy

  • Firm Evolution and Fund Size:
    • Started with a $500M fund to deploy quickly; recent funds have grown to $2B to meet larger capital requirements of modern companies.
    • Acts as a life-cycle investor, deploying check sizes ranging from $1M up to $100M from day one.
  • Investment Allocation:
    • Early-stage and seed investments traditionally oscillate between 20% and 40% of the fund ($400M to $800M).
    • Maintains the flexibility to invest up to 10% of the fund in common stocks (e.g., PIPEs in healthcare/biotech).
  • Venture Building & Repeat Founders:
    • Approximately 15% of the firm's work involves ideating and building companies from scratch (e.g., CareBridge, Main Street Health, Augur).
    • Actively backs repeat founders with high integrity, product/tech orientation, and deep market insights (such as Todd Park and Brad Smith).

AI Transformation in Healthcare & Fintech

  • Horizontal Platforms in Regulated Sectors:
    • Oak HC/FT targets horizontal platforms applied to healthcare and fintech, which together comprise 50% of the economy.
    • Deep sector expertise helps navigate the "smallest large market" (e.g., 100 key providers and 10 large payers).
  • Administrative Cost Reduction:
    • 25% to 30% of all healthcare costs are administrative, representing a primary target for AI-driven automation to lift burdens off clinicians.
  • Clinical and Diagnostic Improvements:
    • AI tools act as critical verification layers (e.g., assisting radiologists who misread 30% of images and allowing patients to leverage tools like ChatGPT).
    • Robotic surgery vision models are projected to surpass human sight limitations during procedures.

Life Sciences, Drug Discovery, & The China Threat

  • The Explosion of Life Sciences AI:
    • After a decade of tracking AI in biotech without viable developable products, the advent of specialized biological and chemical models (along with lab robotics) has radically accelerated drug design.
  • China's Research Dominance:
    • Pharma companies now spend 50% of their outside research dollars in China—a dramatic shift over the last 4 years.
    • China matches or exceeds U.S. university output in fundamental patents, creating an existential threat to the U.S. pharmaceutical and biotech ecosystem amid stagnant NIH funding.
  • Chai Discovery's Roadmap:
    • Backed by Oak HC/FT last year, Chai Discovery achieves 100x higher lab productivity in drug design.
    • Roadmap sequence: Antibodies (already nailed) -> Peptides (making material progress) -> Small molecules.

Portfolio Spotlights: Devoted Health, Haluminate, and Augur

  • Devoted Health:
    • Started 10 years ago by Todd Park, the Medicare Advantage health plan has tripled in size over the last year, halved its operating ratio, and dramatically increased EBITDA.
    • Integrates an AI frontend, the Devoted Medical Group primary care network, and proprietary payer technology platforms.
  • Haluminate:
    • A reinforcement learning environment creating agents that build financial and economic models, mimic investment analyst workflows, and simulate entire corporate ecosystems (e.g., real estate brokers).
    • Scaled from zero to 60 in 6 months.
  • Augur:
    • Founded by Dave Clark (former Amazon supply chain and e-commerce head with 22 years of experience), backed by a $100M upfront commitment from Oak HC/FT.
    • Provides real-time supply chain analytics and operational execution agents for Fortune 500 clients, currently scaling in its second year.

TL;DR

Ken Griffin announced a historic $3 billion philanthropic commitment to Carnegie Mellon University—the largest individual gift in higher education history—funding a $1 billion investment in Pittsburgh (including naming the Kenneth C. Griffin School of Computer Science) and a $2 billion expansion to establish CMU Miami.

The Historic $3 Billion Commitment & Allocation

  • Total Investment: $3 billion commitment by Ken Griffin (Founder and CEO of Citadel), recognized as the largest individual philanthropic gift in the history of higher education.
  • Pittsburgh Campus ($1 Billion):
    • Kenneth C. Griffin School of Computer Science: Receives $500 million to advance computer science leadership.
    • University Priorities: The remaining $500 million supports highest institutional priorities, including faculty and student talent recruitment, financial accessibility, and multidisciplinary research.
  • CMU Miami Campus ($2 Billion):
    • Scale and Timeline: Located on over 35 acres in the heart of Miami (specifically Wynwood), designed to serve more than 3,500 undergraduate and graduate students at full scale by 2041.
    • Educational Model: Organized around societal challenge areas—such as human health, climate resiliency, energy, national security, cybersecurity, and advanced manufacturing—rather than traditional academic majors or departments.

Speakers & Participants

  • Farnam Jahanian: President of Carnegie Mellon University, co-architect of the partnership and campus expansion vision.
  • Ken Griffin: Founder and CEO of Citadel, philanthropist, and primary donor.
  • Josh Shapiro: Governor of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, highlighting CMU's historical legacy and economic impact on the state.
  • Corey O'Connor: Mayor of Pittsburgh, emphasizing the city's industrial roots and technological future.
  • Dave McCormick: U.S. Senator for Pennsylvania, providing video remarks on higher education and regional growth (*unable to attend in person due to a Senate floor vote*).
  • Meredith Meyer Grelli: Vice Provost for Entrepreneurship and Associate Vice President, serving as stage moderator for the fireside chat.
  • Additional Key Figures Mentioned: Cason Carter, Julia Quinn, and Alex Slocum (Citadel colleagues); Alex Laskey and Andre Dua (McKinsey team supporting the effort); David Coulter (CMU Board Chair); Jim Garrett (Provost); Marshall Hebert (Dean of the Griffin School of Computer Science).

CMU's Legacy of Innovation & Economic Impact

  • Historical Innovations: Alums and researchers created foundational technologies, including CAPTCHA tests, Kevlar fiber, and JavaScript code. Home to the world's first Robotics Institute (>50 years old), the first drama school (67 Tony Awards, plus numerous Emmys and Oscars), and the nation's first Computer Science College.
  • Pittsburgh Tech Ecosystem: Features 250 high-tech companies in the local ecosystem (including 100 in robotics and 100 in AI). Notable alumni/spin-off unicorns highlighted include GECKO, Bridge, Skill AI, and Duolingo.
  • Corporate R&D Footprint: Major global corporations maintaining high-tech and deep-tech operations in Pittsburgh due to CMU's ecosystem include Microsoft, Google, Caterpillar, Honeywell, and Bosch.

Philosophy & Higher Education Context

  • Addressing Skepticism: President Jahanian and Ken Griffin discussed headwinds facing higher education, emphasizing that bold institutional leadership requires rejecting caution ("hunkering down") in favor of solving hard problems.
  • The "Who" over "Where": Griffin emphasized that institutional excellence is driven by people rather than geography, noting the cultural and entrepreneurial parallels between Pittsburgh and Miami (both driven by grit, immigrant ambition, and hard work).
  • Cross-Disciplinary Integration: CMU Miami is explicitly designed not as a traditional branch campus or hub-and-spoke model, but as a deeply integrated extension where faculty and students collaborate across traditional academic boundaries.

TL;DR

Google DeepMind's SynthID watermarking technology—developed over eight years for digital media like text, images, and video—has now expanded into biology as "SynthID Bio" to trace the provenance of AI-generated protein sequences and 3D structures without impacting their biological function.

Core Concepts of AI Watermarking

  • Defining the Goal: According to Pushmeet Kohli, watermarking provides a sense of provenance and lets users verify whether content originated from a human, nature, or an AI system.
  • Key Requirements: Imperceptibility (invisible to users), robustness (survives attacks, crops, transformations, or noise), and scalability (easy to integrate and detect across systems).
  • Flaws of Traditional Classifiers: Declining Accuracy: Machine learning classifiers that look for traditional tells (like overused words) lose accuracy over time as generative AI models improve.
  • The Text Watermarking Mechanism: Optionality & Secret Keys: When a large language model generates text, it chooses from a distribution of valid words. A secret background key biases certain word choices, allowing detectors to identify AI generation through statistical patterns across a chain of words.
  • Limitations in Text: Short Snippets: Very short text inputs (e.g., "What is the capital of France? It is Paris") cannot be effectively watermarked due to a lack of variable options.
  • The Image/Video Watermarking Mechanism: Co-Trained Networks: A neural network modifies the unwatermarked image subtly to embed a signal. It is co-trained with a detector network and tested against an adversarial agent that crops, shrinks, rotates, or adds noise to ensure the watermark remains robust.

SynthID Bio and Biosecurity Applications

  • Dual Biological Offerings: Jeremy Ratcliff explains that SynthID Bio includes SynthID Bio Structure (predicting protein structures on top of AlphaFold 3 by shifting atom positions and angles) and SynthID Bio Sequence (introducing watermarks into amino acid strings).
  • The Biosecurity Threat: Bypassing Screening: Bad actors can use AI to design custom protein sequences that look completely different in sequence space from restricted pathogens in DNA synthesis screening databases, but still fold into harmful structures.
  • The Solution for DNA Synthesis: Provenance Confidence: When a DNA synthesis company receives an order, watermarking allows them to verify that a sequence originated from an AI system with built-in safety guardrails rather than an unknown source.
  • Protein MPNN Analogy: Sequence Generation: SynthID Bio Sequence builds on protein MPNN (a third-party tool that goes from structure to sequence), taking advantage of allowable swaps between similar amino acids (like leucine and isoleucine) that maintain identical structure and function.
  • Wet Lab Validation: Hit Rates: Laboratory tests on watermarked protein binders showed near-identical hit rates and quantitative binding measures compared to unwatermarked binders, proving functionality is preserved.

Industry Adoption and Future Integration

  • Ecosystem Expansion: SynthID is already utilized across the industry by companies like Nvidia and OpenAI, and detection is built directly into apps like Gemini.
  • Regulatory Alignment: Pushmeet Kohli notes that generative content watermarking has been brought into law within the EU, with regulators recognizing it as a scalable solution for provenance.
  • Open-Source Biology Strategy: SynthID Bio is being released as open-source code to catalyze conversations with model developers and DNA synthesis screening companies, allowing them to exchange keys and implement the technology without central coordination.

  • Cloud Computers over Local Control: OpenAI debated building local agents (like the *slash-goal* feature in Codex) versus cloud-embodied agents; they chose cloud-hosted computers with unique identities so agents can work autonomously without relying on the user's local hardware.
  • Embodiment and Unified Context: Giving agents identities (e.g., prefixed with user handles like `AE-dot`) makes them intuitive for humans to interact with across multiple surfaces (commute voice calls, Slack threads, and desktop apps) while maintaining a single, unified context.
  • Single Agent vs. Multiple Agents: OpenAI discovered that letting users create multiple agents with whimsical names caused cognitive overload in shared environments like Slack; most consumers will likely want just one primary agent that feels like an extension of themselves.

Spaces: Collaborative Workspaces for Humans and Agents

  • Bridging Messaging and Docs: While messaging works well for quick chats, long-form outputs require a dedicated interface. OpenAI built Space, a collaborative workspace designed for equal-weight human and agent collaboration.
  • Agentic Workflows: Users can continuously tag their Dot in a document to pull data, flesh out sections, check off completed tasks, or ping teammates.
  • HTML & Agent-First Design: Unlike traditional documents optimized strictly for human typing, Spaces leverage modern HTML and CSS to host live interactive charts, data visualizations, and clickable UI prototypes that agents can keep up to date.

Safety, Trust, and Permission Architecture

  • Conservative Rollout: To ensure Dots are the most capable and trustworthy agents, OpenAI initially rolled them out strictly to Pro, Business, and Enterprise tiers (their most AI-literate audiences) with highly conservative default behaviors.
  • Default Confirmation Policy: By default, Dots perform proactive research using connected tools but never take action without user confirmation (e.g., asking before moving a conflicting calendar meeting or purchasing an item).
  • Defense-in-Depth Architecture: Running on the Codex harness, the system uses GPT-6 Astra combined with Auto-Review—a secondary model acting as a police officer to monitor base-model behavior against user rules—alongside full sub-agent auditability logs.
  • Ecosystem and Web-Bot Standards: Rather than bypassing web platforms aggressively, OpenAI is taking an ecosystem-first approach, collaborating with partners like Cloudflare on `web-bot auth` so agents explicitly declare themselves as bots.

Pricing and the Future Roadmap

  • Flat Monthly Contractor Pricing: OpenAI is moving away from traditional strict rate limits for Dots, designing pricing more like a responsive human contractor. Users pay a flat monthly fee for 24/7 availability, with the agent intelligently managing its own compute costs (e.g., running heavy tasks overnight or switching to more cost-efficient models as limits approach). Dot usage is separate from main Codex limits.
  • Coexistence with ChatGPT: ChatGPT (with 1.2 billion weekly users) remains the consumer assistant for everyone; OpenAI plans to eventually merge the Work tab into Chat and bring Dot capabilities all the way down to free users over time.
  • Specialist Dots: While personal assistant Dots act as extensions of individuals, OpenAI is developing Specialist Dots centrally managed by IT and designed to accelerate entire enterprise teams or workflows.

TL;DR

Barrett Lyon and Joel de la Garza discuss the stagnation of internet protocol design, the rise of pervasive digital surveillance, and how Lyon’s new venture, Doxnet, aims to rebuild a private, peer-to-peer mesh network from the silicon up to protect users from trackers, corporate data-harvesting, and centralized intermediaries.

The State of the Internet and Modern Surveillance

  • Erosion of Original Internet Ideals: The internet's original architecture was built by free-speech maximalists on open, decentralized concepts, but it has shifted into a tightly controlled chokehold managed by a handful of entities.
  • Carrier-Grade NAT: Lyon identifies carrier-grade NAT as one of the biggest stains on the modern internet, actively destroying applications' ability to perform innovative point-to-point tasks.
  • Creeping Domestic Censorship: State-level implementation of know-your-customer (KYC) rules and age-verification laws are forcing networks to solve identity problems they were never designed to handle, resulting in massive accidental collateral blocks (such as a French piracy policy mistakenly blacking out a major carrier via Cisco Umbrella).
  • The Ubiquity of Ad Tracking: Beyond state surveillance, commercial app trackers (including telemetry metadata on platforms like WhatsApp and Apple) accumulate exhaustive behavioral profiles—recording coffee habits, wake-up times, income levels, and major life milestones—that can instantly be queried and synthesized by modern LLMs.

Doxnet: Rebuilding the Stack for Privacy

  • Origins of the Name: The project draws its name from Lyon's high school hacker handle ("Dox") and a high school enterprise (Doxnet) where he and a friend sold shell accounts for $5 a month to roughly 1,000 users.
  • Architecture & Infrastructure:
    • Full-Stack Ownership: Doxnet owns its hardware from software down to the wire, operating 26 sites globally with redundant routing.
    • AI-Driven Operations: The network runs entirely without traditional network administrators or sysadmins, managed locally via AI agents rather than third-party inference providers to eliminate insider threats.
    • Corporate Governance: Operations are split across a US-based entity and a Swiss company handling European operations to ensure rigorous regional privacy governance.
  • Mesh Network and P2P Capabilities:
    • Bypasses traditional server relays used by apps like WhatsApp, Signal, iMessage, and Telegram (which Lyon characterizes as deceptive server-routed architectures).
    • Enables direct, encrypted peer-to-peer communication, phone calls, video calls, and file transfers with no file size limits (successfully transmitting 20GB files across a private network point-to-point with post-quantum encryption).
    • Requires only a human-verification puzzle to issue a privacy token; accounts operate without names, phone numbers, email addresses, or permanent identifiers.

Critiques of Existing Privacy Tools

  • The Illusion of Free VPNs: Most consumer VPNs merely act as basic entry and exit points leased from third-party landlords. Lyon warns that "if it's free, you are the product," noting that many foreign-operated VPNs have opaque ownership structures and potential ties to intelligence-crime syndicates.
  • Historical Precedents: Lyon highlights law enforcement stings—such as the FBI building and marketing a secure messaging app exclusively to organized crime groups—to demonstrate the hidden dangers of trusting centralized "private" messaging tools.

Barrett Lyon’s Track Record in Internet Infrastructure

  • Prolexic Technologies: Founded after Lyon formalized his high school DDoS-mitigation techniques (presented at Sans and Google) to protect businesses, e-commerce sites, and gambling platforms from extortion and cybercrime. The cloud-scale DDoS defense service eventually became Akamai Prolexic.
