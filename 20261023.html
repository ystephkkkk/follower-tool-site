TL;DR: The investment narrative has shifted from equity earnings to fixed-income math, as surging debt yields for AI neo-cloud companies like CoreWeave and massive capital expenditure plans threaten data center economics. Meanwhile, major tech and industrial earnings presented a mixed picture—highlighted by Tesla's 0% revenue growth and Meta's heavy future infrastructure commitments—while the housing market remains locked due to high mortgage rates and soaring ownership costs.

French Sovereign Debt and Global Markets

  • French Debt Pressures: Steve Eisman highlights that France's debt-to-GDP ratio stands at 120%, triggering a 16 basis point increase in the premium on French 10-year bonds over Germany to 175 basis points—the highest level since the 2011 European sovereign bond crisis.
  • US vs. French Debt Advantage: While US debt-to-GDP is also at 120%, the US dollar serves as the world's reserve currency and the global financial system functions on Treasuries, whereas investors are not forced to hold French sovereign debt.

The Shift in the AI Narrative

  • Fixed Income vs. Equity Mindset: Steve Eisman notes that while equity investors focus on stories, fixed-income investors focus on math, and the AI narrative has now shifted heavily to the debt markets.
  • CoreWeave's Climbing Debt Yields: The cost of CoreWeave's debt climbed from 9% to 15%, negatively impacting the entire AI complex.
  • Unworkable Data Center Economics: Neo-cloud companies like CoreWeave and Nebius lack strong balance sheets; raising future debt at a 15% yield makes the economics of AI data centers prohibitive, transforming parts of tech from capital-light to capital-heavy.

Broad Earnings Review: Tech, Autos, and Aerospace

  • Meta (Strong Revenue, Massive Commitments): Reported revenue of $64.6B (up 26% year-over-year) and a beat on earnings per share, driven by a 14% increase in average price per ad and the early success of its agentic AI Muse. However, costs jumped 68%, and the company agreed to spend approximately $825B in future leases and contracts mostly for AI computing infrastructure while having no positive cash flow.
  • Tesla (Stalled Growth & Zero Revenue Increase): Total revenues grew 0%. Automotive revenue fell 1% to $20.5B, energy generation and storage revenue dropped 16% to $2.9B, and EPS fell 4% to 48 cents, while services revenue rose 33% to $4.6B.
  • Netflix (Entering X-Growth): Revenue met expectations with 11.8% growth to $11.8B, but Q4 guidance came in below street consensus as streaming matures and YouTube captures newer media formats like short-form video and podcasts. Netflix also executed layoffs and replaced its Chief Content and Chief Film Officers.
  • SpaceX (Impressive Results Despite Massive CapEx): Total revenue jumped 161% year-over-year, driven by three divisions:
    • Space (Rocket Division): Revenue reached $1.8B (vs. $962M last year), nearly breaking even.
    • Starlink (Connectivity): Revenue rose to $5.2B (vs. $4.3B), with profitability reaching $2.4B (vs. $1.7B).
    • Superintelligence (SI / AI Division): Revenue jumped to $7.6B (vs. $2.5B) with positive profitability of $431M (vs. a $1.4B loss).
    • CapEx and Merger Concerns: Total CapEx hit $29.6B (up 185% quarter-over-quarter). Steve Eisman warns of massive future CapEx plans—including a $100B budget for Starbase Louisiana, Grok models, and data centers—and criticizes Elon Musk's disclosed committee discussions to merge SpaceX with Tesla.
  • General Motors & GE Aerospace: Both beat revenue and EPS estimates yet saw stock declines; GM grew revenue by only 2.9% and narrowed rather than raised guidance, while GE Aerospace grew revenue 18%, EPS 36%, and total orders 49%, but missed free cash flow expectations at $2B.
  • Equifax: Revenue grew 10% and US mortgage revenue rose 15% amid regulatory pressure from FHFA head Bill Pulte on credit scoring, though Q4 EPS guidance missed consensus by 3%.
  • Telecoms (AT&T and Moody's): AT&T reported EPS of 65 cents (up 20%) and revenue up 5.3%, but T-Mobile, Verizon, and AT&T shares fell 2–6% after Elon Musk reaffirmed intentions to build a fourth major mobile operator (coining the term "telepocalypse"). Moody's missed EPS estimates at $4.14 (vs. $4.27 expected) due to a 1% ratings revenue decline from slower bond issuance.
  • Other Notable Results: IBM beat on revenue and EPS with 5% growth; Blackstone saw EPS fall 13% to $1.32 with $63B in inflows offset by real estate write-downs; Comcast beat on EPS via Peacock and media but lost 144,000 broadband subscribers; NVR missed consensus on EPS ($89.45, down 20%) and new orders (4,391 vs. 5,043 expected) due to high mortgage rates.

The Housing Market and Home Buying Outlook

  • Locked Existing Home Market: Mortgage rates exceed 7% compared to 3% to 5% pandemic-era refinancings, meaning a buyer's monthly payment parity would require home prices to fall by roughly half—an event prevented by employment stability, leading to multi-decade lows in existing home sales.
  • Unaffordability Factors: Surrounding costs including home insurance, property taxes, and land-development fees make housing unaffordable.
  • Housing as an Investment: Steve Eisman argues that the era of homeownership generating wealth is over, predicting that housing prices will likely decline in most parts of the country for the next several years and advising buyers to purchase only for lifestyle reasons under the assumption of breaking even upon resale.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Steve Eisman predicts that housing prices will go down in most parts of the country for the next several years.

TL;DR

Amazon CEO Andy Jassy explains how massive AI demand is driving the company's unprecedented $220 billion 2026 CapEx buildout, why lowering the cost of inference is critical for broad enterprise adoption, and how generative AI is fundamentally reshaping Amazon's internal development and product lineup.

Amazon's AI Infrastructure & Capacity Demand

  • Massive CapEx Scale: Amazon expects to spend roughly $220 billion in CapEx in 2026, a staggering sum that reflects the unprecedented pace of infrastructure expansion.
  • Sold-Out Capacity: Andy Jassy notes that 2026 infrastructure capacity is sold out, 2027 is largely sold out, and a good chunk of 2028 is already committed.
  • The Cash Cycle & Visibility: Andy Jassy explains that data centers require capital investment roughly two years in advance with a 30-year useful life, while server and networking gear are purchased just months in advance based on clear demand visibility with an average 5-year deal structure.
  • The Barbell Demand: Andy Jassy describes AI demand as a barbell, noting that while massive frontier model labs are buying up massive capacity, mainstream enterprise production apps are still waiting for lower inference costs to scale broadly.

Custom Silicon & Annapurna Labs

  • Acquisition Genesis: Andy Jassy highlights the 2015 acquisition of Annapurna Labs—initially intended to offload virtualization, networking, security, and storage onto NICs to free up 100% of EC2 servers for compute—as one of Amazon's best-ever acquisitions.
  • Graviton Success: Andy Jassy shares that 99% of Amazon's top 1,000 EC2 customers use Graviton CPUs, which deliver about 40% better price performance than comparable X86 processors.
  • Trainium & Inference Costs: Andy Jassy explains that Annapurna's Trainium AI accelerators provide 30% to 40% better price performance, aiming to drive down the cost of inference, which he believes is currently an order of magnitude too expensive for pervasive enterprise rewriting of CPU applications.

High-Profile Investments & Partnerships

  • Strategic Investments: Andy Jassy addresses Wall Street's skepticism over Amazon's large investments in Anthropic ($8 billion) and OpenAI, framing them as key partnerships with two of the most successful category-defining companies.
  • Ecosystem Integration: Andy Jassy notes that Anthropic runs extensively on Bedrock and builds on Trainium, while partnerships with OpenAI allow customers to run OpenAI models on Bedrock via a unique stateful runtime environment designed for agentic workflows.

AI-Driven Organizational & Product Transformation

  • Radical Productivity Gains: Andy Jassy cites the Bedrock team rebuilding their inference engine with just 6 engineers in 76 days (a task that previously would have taken 40 people a year), proving that AI enables drastically smaller, cross-functional teams.
  • Workforce Shift: Andy Jassy predicts that organizations will employ fewer humans for routine coding, customer service, business intelligence, and legal work, shifting toward smaller, highly agile teams starting from much more advanced stages.
  • Alexa & Core Reinvention: Andy Jassy explains that Alexa's brains are being completely rewired from scratch with generative AI, and similar deep reinventions are underway across Prime Video, retail, Fire TV, and music rather than relying on incremental updates.

Cybersecurity Realities & Future Skills

  • Immediate Cybersecurity Threats: Andy Jassy warns that while media focuses on fantastical sci-fi AI risks, the immediate danger is cybersecurity—models uncovering vast new vulnerabilities that require applications to deploy patches in minutes rather than months.
  • Education & Critical Thinking: Andy Jassy advises young people (including his own children) to embrace AI tools rather than fight them, but stresses that human judgment, ingenuity, critical reasoning, and strategic thinking remain essential for synthesis and invention.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Andy Jassy predicts that supply constraints for various data center and chip components will persist for several years.
  • Andy Jassy predicts that companies will have to rewrite almost all of their code to be agentically capable and capable of responding to security threats in minutes.
  • Andy Jassy predicts that organizations in the future will employ fewer humans doing traditional tasks like coding, legal work, and research, shifting to smaller cross-functional teams.

TL;DR

As the agentic web drives an exponential surge in AI-powered security vulnerabilities and shifts the attacker landscape toward automated high school actors, Cloudflare CTO Dane Knecht predicts that engineering teams will spend the next 18 months heavily prioritizing security and zero-trust infrastructure to patch 70 years of legacy software.

AI Security Landscape and the Evolving Threat Vector

  • Explosion of Vulnerabilities: Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVEs) and Log4J-scale incidents are shifting from annual occurrences to weekly events, driven by AI models and harnesses that do not sleep.
  • Democratization of Offense: Dane Knecht noted that recent high-profile vulnerabilities are not just the work of nation-state actors, but increasingly of high school students deploying agents in capture-the-flag contests and leveraging bug bounties.
  • The "Messy Middle" of the Web: Dane Knecht described the current internet as a messy middle where humans and agents ride the same rails, forcing websites into inefficient scraping and parsing cycles because agents have a thirst to explore the human-designed web.

Shifting Engineering Resources and Zero-Trust Infrastructure

  • Resource Realignment: Dane Knecht expects that over the next 18 months, the majority of engineering team time will be dedicated to security, with some team keynotes indicating over 50% of engineering time going toward securing the internet during this transition.
  • Zero-Trust Advantage: Organizations that transitioned away from castle-and-moat security toward Zero Trust over the past 5 to 10 years are in a significantly better position to secure devices and traffic flows.
  • Forcing Automation Through Crisis: Dane Knecht explained that Cloudflare plans to scale its internal red teams from one to four or five by the end of next year, forcing engineering teams to automate away vulnerabilities by pushing them out of crisis.

AI-Driven Workflows, Cloudflare OS, and Internal Experimentation

  • Cloudflare OS: Dane Knecht detailed Cloudflare's internal platform, which allows employees—particularly go-to-market and G&A teams—to safely deploy AI tools, build internal applications, and maintain data governance.
  • Bottom-Up Experimentation: Instead of hiring consulting firms to catalog workflows, Cloudflare fostered a bottom-up experimentation culture, utilizing intern energy and high-agency employees (including an early `agents@cloudflare.com` initiative powered by interns) to build hundreds of internal productivity tools.
  • Intelligent Tooling & Hiring: Dane Knecht emphasized that hiring at Cloudflare heavily prioritizes curiosity, drive, and empathy, noting that a lack of personal AI use at home is a major red flag.

The Agentic Web and New Economic Models

  • Cloudflare Monetization Gateway: Dane Knecht highlighted Cloudflare's new gateway designed to facilitate microtransactions for content creators, providing wallets for agents and stablecoin rails to handle millions of transactions per second.
  • Project Signals: Dane Knecht mentioned a collaboration with OpenAI called Project Signals, which allows agents to proactively discover new content rather than repeatedly crawling and paying for infrastructure across the entire internet.
  • Reviving HTTP 402: Dane Knecht expressed excitement that agents are finally pushing the internet toward solving the 30-year-old HTTP 402 payment protocol challenge, creating a new economic model that can increase the number of content creators.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Dane Knecht predicts that over the next 18 months, the majority of a team's time will be spent on security, after which software productivity will rise significantly as resources return to standard development.
  • Greg Brockman (cited by Sarah Guo) predicted that by 2027, people will spend as much money on tokens for security as they do for engineering.
  • Dane Knecht predicts a world in 18 months where new software productivity increases dramatically once security resources have surveyed and patched legacy bugs.
  • Dane Knecht claims that over time, cyber security models and tooling *all have to be open* because malicious actors will gain access to them regardless, and closing them excludes the mass population that needs defense.

TL;DR

NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang delivered the GTC Berlin 2026 keynote, announcing a $10 billion European AI investment over the last year, the commercial rollout of the Vera Rubin architecture, the DSX Pod turnkey AI factory container, and OpenShell for secure AI agent runtimes. The keynote also introduced breakthrough edge-to-cloud hardware, including the RTX Spark PC (1 petaflop) and DGX Station (20 petaflops) powered by the N1X chip, alongside multi-modal agent toolkits and physical AI robotics reference platforms.

Europe's AI Economy and Industrial Evolution

  • Longstanding Regional Roots: Jensen Huang highlighted NVIDIA's 33-year history in Europe, noting that NV1, NV2, and NV3 chips were built in Europe, with early manufacturing partners in France (SGS Thomson, Croll) and Malta, and early workstation partners like Elsa (Germany) and Audi (2005).
  • Massive Financial Commitment: Jensen Huang reported that NVIDIA has invested nearly $10 billion across Europe in the last year alone into startups, major partnerships, and cloud infrastructure, bringing total European partnerships to over 12,000.
  • The Five-Layer AI Cake: Jensen Huang described the AI economy as a manufacturing framework composed of five distinct layers: Energy, Advanced Computers, AI Factories (generating token intelligence), Models, and End Applications/Startups.

Vera Rubin Architecture and Full-Stack Hardware

  • Generational Performance Leaps: Jensen Huang stated that through "extreme co-design" across chips, systems, software, and algorithms, NVIDIA has improved AI performance by 1 million times over the last 10 years, dramatically outpacing Moore's Law.
  • Vera Rubin Production Specs: Jensen Huang introduced the Vera Rubin architecture in full production, noting it delivers 30x greater performance than Blackwell in just two years, featuring 100% liquid-cooled nodes that assemble in under five minutes.
  • Networking and Silicon Photonics: Jensen Huang highlighted Spectrum-X, featuring Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) built with TSMC using CoWoS technology, yielding a 512-port 200 Gbps Ethernet switch with direct-to-chip laser integration.
  • DSX AI Factories & DSX Pods: Jensen Huang announced the NVIDIA DSX full-stack reference architecture and the DSX Pod—a containerized, factory-built AI factory integrating 7-rack Vera Rubin NVL72 units, 17 kilometers of Spectrum-X cabling, dry closed-loop coolers, and 800 VDC power centers, reducing on-site deployment from months to days.

AI Agents and Secure Runtimes

  • The Five Elements of an Agent: Jensen Huang outlined the core components required for autonomous AI agents: the Model (perception, reasoning, planning), the Harness (exoskeleton orchestrating memory and tools), the Sandbox (isolation layer), Knowledge Foundation (structured and unstructured data access), and Tools (CUDA-X accelerated solvers).
  • OpenShell Secure Runtime: Jensen Huang introduced OpenShell, an open-source (Apache 2.0) runtime that enforces system-call and network isolation for agents, paired with NVIDIA Sentry running on BlueField-4 for hardware-isolated trust domains that can cut compromised agent connections in milliseconds.
  • Enterprise Ecosystem Adoption: Jensen Huang noted widespread industry backing for OpenShell, including a major partnership with Anthropic to build cloud OpenShell agents.

Personal AI: RTX Spark and Workstations

  • RTX Spark PC Line: Jensen Huang unveiled the RTX Spark, a new line of desktops, laptops, and workstations developed over four years in partnership with Microsoft and powered by the N1X chip, delivering 1 petaflop of compute and 128 GB of memory in a portable form factor.
  • DGX Station Windows Workstations: Jensen Huang announced the upcoming availability (late this year) of the DGX Station running Windows, packing 20 petaflops and 754 GB of memory to run professional design software like CATIA, Autodesk, Adobe, Cadence, and Synopsys.

    • Focuses on converging unique societal-level problems with rare individual talents to build things no one else can construct.
    • Management challenge: Acknowledges that accepting and harnessing superior, highly creative talent induces psychological friction and institutional pain, but insists this tension is necessary to produce breakthrough technology.

Enterprise AI, Foundry, and Sovereignty

  • Core Technological Offerings:
    • Foundry: Integrates massive, disparate enterprise data sets.
    • Ontology: Acts as an application platform running ideas and operationalizing AI models across the enterprise.
    • Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs): Serve as the critical transfer mechanism on the front lines, bridging the gap between cutting-edge technology and entrenched institutional clients.
  • Sovereign AI Architecture:
    • Allows organizations to power models they create and completely own, enabling them to white-label products or selectively share capabilities.
    • Impregnates tribal knowledge into the enterprise on a 1-to-1 basis, preventing institutional brain drain.
  • CEO Frustration and IP Protection:
    • Karp states CEOs are "livid" because they are paying for tokens that generate zero value while simultaneously leaking proprietary IP to competitors.
    • Sovereign AI restores control over true costs (combining cash price and IP loss) and actual value generation.
  • High-Stakes National Security Deployments:
    • Palantir software drives critical anti-terror operations in Europe and plays a substantial, highly-secured role supporting Ukraine and institutional transformations within the U.S. Department of Defense (such as Project Maven).

The Political and Wealth Challenges of the AI Era

  • Critique of Wealth Taxes:
    • Argues that extremist solutions like wealth taxes would crush startups, protect incumbents like large tech giants, and fail to solve the underlying political tension.
    • Surveillance State Expansion: Warns that assessing net worth down to granular real-time asset levels would require an intrusive surveillance apparatus, acting as a massive breach of civil liberties and individual privacy (e.g., exposing personal and financial transactions).

The U.S. vs. China AI Geopolitical Race

TL;DR

Launch videos in 2026 no longer require expensive $20,000 commercial productions; instead, founders should treat every product demo as a mini-story and consistently ship raw, process-driven content on algorithmic social platforms.

The Modern Launch Playbook: Mini-Stories Over Major Events

  • Shift from Single Events to Continuous Shipping: Founders should abandon the outdated meta of coordinating a single, high-stakes launch (like a TechCrunch article or costly agency video).
    • Farza Majeed notes that a simple, authentic video saying "hi" and showing a product performs far better than a $20,000 produced New York Times video.
    • Success comes from treating launches as "mini-stories" and constant demos rather than high-risk rocket launches.
  • Demo-Driven Development: Work backwards from a demo at every stage of product creation.
    • If a feature sounds good on paper as a video hook before building begins, it is worth creating.
    • The internal excitement of shouting *"Yo, check this out!"* to a co-founder is the exact raw energy that should be packaged for the internet.

The Content Creation Process: Scripting and Hook Mechanics

  • Religiously Scripting Off-the-Cuff Videos: Despite appearing natural, high-performing videos are meticulously planned in advance.
    • Farza writes a script days ahead, memorizes and visualizes lines in the shower or on the way to work, and uses text editors like *Freewrite* (freewrite.io) to refine the copy.
    • The core rule is evaluating the text against oneself: *Did I stop scrolling to read this sentence?*
  • The First 10 Seconds & Visual Hooks: Setup should comprise only 10-20% of a one-minute video.
    • Start with a clear hook defining who you are, what you are doing, and why it matters.
    • Utilize recognizable visual anchors in the first frame (such as *Xcode* for developers) and zoom in immediately to capture attention while users are scrolling.
  • Embracing the "Cringe" Phase: Getting past initial discomfort requires sheer repetition.
    • Farza admits that many produced videos initially felt terrible or "cringe" to his team before going viral (e.g., a video he thought was awful hit 3 million views on Twitter).
    • Founders must push through publishing imperfect content because audiences forget quickly and the reps build necessary taste and skill.

Channel Selection and Conversion Realities

  • Prioritizing Platforms by Down-Funnel Conversion: Vanity metrics (like total views) matter far less than actual user downloads and retention.
    • Twitter/X: Historically yields massive view counts (e.g., 3 million views) but features the lowest conversion rates for actual app downloads (less than 1%).
    • YouTube: Generates lower initial views (e.g., 20,000 views) but acts as a long-term library with extremely high conversion rates (roughly 20% download the app).
    • Instagram: Drives the majority of traffic (around 70%) for Farza's current company, *Hey Clicky*.
  • Targeting Niche Audiences on General Platforms: Mass-market algorithmic feeds can successfully capture B2B or specialized buyers if directly addressed.
    • By directly calling out specific personas (e.g., "Are you the founder of a design agency?"), Farza generated 50 targeted design agency leads through TikTok videos averaging 4,000 views.

Lean Production Stack and Team Structure

  • Essential Hardware and Software: High-end production is completely optional, but audio quality is non-negotiable.
    • *Screen Studio* ($30) is used for smooth screen and cursor recordings.
    • An iPhone selfie camera is sufficient for native, authentic updates.
    • For cinematic quality, Farza uses a *Sony FX3 / FX30* paired with a *DJI mic*.
    • Audio Warning: Clean audio is mandatory; viewers will tolerate 720p video in a basement, but poor audio causes immediate drop-off. Never use built-in MacBook or AirPod microphones.
  • Early Team Composition: Hiring for creative leverage early on frees technical founders to build.
    • Farza's first hires at *Hey Clicky* and *Buildspace* prioritized a filmmaker alongside engineers.
    • A dedicated filmmaker handles the execution (sets, shots, motion graphics, and editing), allowing the founder to focus on product, hiring, and growth.

Market Structure & The "Dark Pool" of Compute

  • Unlit Market Dynamics: Most physical compute trades in blocks through long, bilateral commercial agreements—incorporating MSAs, SLAs, and hardware-specific order forms—rather than on widely adopted public exchanges.
  • OTC Channels: Tenured compute capacity for fixed terms and specific Total Contract Values (TCVs) is negotiated in private neo-chat channels, including Slack messages, X messages, Telegram group chats, and OTC venues.
  • Pricing Skew: Negotiations center heavily on the chip SKU and base board (e.g., H100, H200, B300) alongside memory types (VRAM, CPU memory, additional storage), delivery dates, and precise workload specifications.

Macroeconomics, Jevons Paradox, & Upstream Costs

  • Decoupling of Token and Compute Prices: According to a16z data referenced by the speakers, token and inference costs have fallen sharply while GPU compute prices have remained robust.
  • Jevons Paradox in Action: Cheaper tokens unlock vastly higher usage; teams consume significantly more tokens on the same budget, driving up underlying demand for compute.
  • Upstream Supply Chain: Bringing a usable GPU hour online requires the convergence of chips, power, buildings, grid connections, and massive capital.
  • IEA Electricity Projections: The International Energy Agency’s April update projects global data center electricity consumption to jump from 485 terawatt-hours in 2025 to 950 terawatt-hours in 2030.

The Middle-Market Financing Gap

  • The Hyperscaler Advantage: Hyperscalers, frontier labs, and large neo-clouds leverage strong equity, debt access, and large balance sheets to secure multi-year commitments (such as OpenAI's 10-year deals).
  • The Mid-Size Squeeze: Startups and Series B companies cannot easily underwrite $200M+ compute contracts over three years without eating an entire funding round.
  • Prepayment Hurdles: CoreWeave public filings show median prepayments of 15% to 25% upfront on cluster contracts; on a $100 million TCV cluster, a startup must put down $15 million to $25 million upfront, severely restricting operational runway.
  • Strategic Workarounds: Growing companies must weigh turning away customer demand, bridging shortfalls via spot/on-demand pricing, or financing the prepayment component against the opportunity cost of building and operating their own full stack.

Operating Models & Procurement Options

  • Build vs. Rent Spectrum: Companies can build and operate the full stack (handling power, permits, assembly, and management), own servers in a colocation facility with partner operations, or buy off-take rental capacity.
  • Control vs. Cost Trade-offs: Some businesses require root access, confidential compute, and topology customization, making full-stack ownership worth the premium despite rental alternatives.
  • Venture Capital Portco Impacts: VC investors increasingly focus on compute procurement because payment terms directly dictate portfolio company runway.

Market Participants, Brokers, & Policy

  • Ecosystem Roles: The market relies on price discovery providers (indexes and benchmarks for future derivative contracts), physical matching venues (decentralized marketplaces, bare-metal resellers, and neo-clouds), bilateral brokers, and specialist equipment lenders.
  • Bespoke Deal Complexity: Every deal features custom bills of materials, delivery lead times, and variable creditworthiness factors, making non-fungibility a major hurdle.
  • Export Controls & Open Weights: U.S. policy heavily shapes the market; in January, the U.S. Commerce Department moved qualifying H200-class license reviews for China and Macau to case-by-case review with conditions.
  • U.S. AI Action Plan: The plan explicitly encourages open-source and open-weight AI while recommending financial market improvements so startups and academics can access large-scale computing power.
