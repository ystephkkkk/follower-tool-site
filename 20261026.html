Nick Kokonas reveals how he mastered multiple fields—from options trading and building the world-class restaurant Alinea to creating the software company Tock—by viewing every industry through the lens of time-scale arbitrage, intense daily execution, and adjacent-industry theft.

Trading Floor Origins & The Art of Arbitrage

  • The Reality of High-Stakes Trading:
    • Capital Scarcity: Nick Kokonas started trading with just $60,000 ($20,000 of his own money and $40,000 from a backer), which he describes as drastically undercapitalized for the floor.
    • The 1-Year Goal: Kokonas set a hard target to be trading on a badge within one year of taking a $5.25/hour runner job at the Chicago Mercantile Exchange, hitting his goal almost to the day.
    • Day 1 Trial by Fire: On his first day in the pits, the strict rule was that he had to complete at least one trade in a zero-sum, hostile, and chaotic physical environment to break the psychological seal.
  • Bypassing the 30-Second Gap:
    • The Time-Scale Arb: Recognizing that a trade execution process took 30 to 40 seconds via paper tickets passed between New York and Chicago, Kokonas sought to bridge the gap directly.
    • Regulatory Scrutiny: By meticulously reading the voluminous CFTC rules—which lawyers and board members rarely read in full—he discovered that direct communication between identical firm tax IDs was not explicitly barred.
    • Union Workarounds: When the Merc blocked dedicated fiber-optic line permits to protect broker group interests, Kokonas bribed union cable workers with cases of Jameson to run the line.
    • Peak Revenue: The direct telecommunications link generated instant, zero-risk arbitrage profits, scaling to over $100 million in trading revenue at its peak.

Restaurant Innovation & Alinea's Blueprint

  • Open-Sourcing a Masterpiece:
    • Building in Public: Before launching Alinea, Kokonas created the "Alinea Project" forum thread on eGullet, open-sourcing the business plan, name choices, and menu development.
    • Marketing Leverage: The public documentation generated hundreds of thousands of views and early demand, securing heavy press coverage from the *New York Times*, *Wall Street Journal*, and *Gourmet Magazine* before opening day.
    • The Michelin Goal: The explicit target was to build the best restaurant in America and make it so exceptional that the Michelin Guide (which had not yet expanded to the U.S.) would look bad for omitting it.
  • Operations, Margins, and Scale:
    • The Universal Restaurant Calculator: Kokonas built a single-tab spreadsheet with roughly 80 cells to track every possible cost input, realizing that the low barrier to entry causes most restaurants to fail due to poor business fundamentals.
    • Financial Success: Unlike the popular narrative that restaurants only net 2-3%, Alinea scaled to roughly $20 million in annual revenue with 18% to 20% profit margins, ultimately selling in 2023 for an 8x return on initial investment (totaling roughly 27x to 28x overall with quarterly distributions).

Software & The Tock Revolution

  • Solving the Host Stand:
    • The KYC Problem: Kokonas founded Tock out of frustration with OpenTable, which acted as an intermediary and left restaurants completely blind to who was walking through their doors (Know Your Customer).
    • Dynamic Pricing: Tock introduced variable pricing for time-slotted businesses, matching the reality that a Tuesday 9:00 PM table has fundamentally different demand than a Saturday 7:00 PM table.
  • The M&A Playbook:
    • Diligence Control: When selling Tock during COVID-19, Kokonas, along with co-founder Jeff Kaplan and CFO Steve Bernaki, converted an 896-question diligence list into a Google Doc and color-coded it.
    • Bypassing Intermediaries: By refusing to act as passive passengers and completing the rigorous data request in just three days, they saved millions in advisory fees while keeping the deal on track to close before the buyer's Q1 S1 filing deadline.

Stealing from Adjacencies: Magic & Theater

  • Borrowing from Magicians:
    • Attention Control: Instead of reading standard hospitality books, Kokonas studied magic books by authors like Nate Stanforth and Darren Brown to understand how magicians handle attention, sight lines, and audience positioning.
    • Service Positioning: Applying magical principles, Alinea servers were trained to stand *alongside* the diner rather than directly across from them, creating a collaborative "same team" dynamic.
  • The "Strange House" Venture:
    • Immersive Illusion: Kokonas partnered with magician Nate Stanforth and game designer Alistair Lee to build *Strange House*—a 90,000-square-foot purpose-built experiential venue beneath Wall Street.
    • Human Connection: The project aims to combat societal isolation by leveraging theatrical staging, psychological illusion, and Disney-style room throughput to evoke childlike wonder and foster deep human connection among strangers.

TL;DR

Despite persistent inflation, a massive $40 trillion federal deficit, and an aging investor base, the U.S. dollar retains its status as the global financial anchor because there are no viable currency alternatives.

The Fed, Inflation, and Rates

  • Inflation Persistence:
    • On October 14, inflation came in at 3.7%, exceeding market expectations of 3.6% and creating pressure on the Federal Reserve.
    • While temporary oil shocks are typically "looked through," the Fed is deeply concerned that delaying action could unmoor longer-term inflation expectations.
  • Federal Reserve Dynamics:
    • Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh faces an unenviable political and policy bind, caught between a potential majority committee vote for rate hikes and political pressure from Donald Trump.
    • Benn Steil supports the Fed's elimination of formal forward guidance, noting that empirical evidence shows the Fed is systematically worse at forecasting than blue-chip private sector forecasters.
    • Steil defends the Fed's large balance sheet of $6.5 to $7 trillion, stating it does not hinder the Fed's ability to control inflation using interest on reserves.

Fiscal Policy, Scott Bessent, and the Deficit

  • Bessent's Operation Twist:
    • Treasury official Scott Bessent launched an operation to sell short-term Treasuries and buy long-term Treasuries to the tune of $6 billion to lower long-term rates.
    • The market quickly dismissed the move, calculating that $6 billion represents just 0.016% of the $40 trillion national debt.
  • The U.S. Deficit & Crowding Out:
    • The U.S. federal deficit currently runs at about 6% of GDP annually, totaling $40 trillion.
    • The trajectory is concerning due to a crowding-out effect: Treasury is competing globally for capital against booming AI infrastructure investments, pushing interest rates upward.
    • Congress and the executive branch currently lack the serious political will to cut spending or address entitlement reform.

The Global Dollar Standard & The France Sovereign Debt Crisis

  • The Exorbitant Privilege:
    • Historically, 76% of the market for U.S. debt consisted of "rate-insensitive investors" (mostly central banks holding Treasuries as a reflex).
    • That figure has dropped to 43%, meaning the U.S. must increasingly attract rate-sensitive investors, creating an accelerationist effect on interest rate rises as deficits grow.
  • The Lack of Alternatives:
    • The U.S. dollar remains dominant simply as the "winner of an ugly dog contest"; no major fiat alternative exists.
    • Central banks (particularly China and Russia) have increasingly diversified into gold and repatriated reserves to protect themselves against aggressive U.S. dollar-based financial sanctions (such as those applied to Iran).
  • France's Vulnerability:
    • France faces an even uglier fiscal position than the U.S. with large deficits and zero political will for reform (e.g., Marine Le Pen's proposal to lower the retirement age from 62 to 60).
    • Unlike U.S. Treasuries, no bank or sovereign wealth fund globally holds French sovereign bonds on reflex; French debt is treated strictly like corporate risk.

Historical Counterfactuals: Henry Wallace, Bretton Woods, and the Cold War

  • The Henry Wallace Counterfactual:
    • In Henry Steil's book *The World That Wasn't*, he explores the counterfactual where Henry Wallace (FDR's third-term Vice President) succeeded a failing Roosevelt instead of Harry Truman.
    • Wallace, despite not being an ideological spy, was a Soviet fellow traveler who praised Soviet economic planning and would have opposed the Truman Doctrine, the Marshall Plan, the creation of West Germany, and NATO.
  • Bretton Woods & Harry Dexter White:
    • The 1944 Bretton Woods conference established the IMF, the World Bank, and the dollar-based international financial system.
    • The U.S. architect of the system, Treasury official Harry Dexter White, was later identified in FBI files (via 18 intercepted Soviet intelligence cables) as a Soviet asset who actively protected other agents and undermined U.S. policy (such as giving currency plates to the Soviets, which cost the U.S. Treasury about $8 billion in today's dollars).
  • The Marshall Plan:
    • Following WWII, the IMF and World Bank were largely mothballed through the 1950s, making the Marshall Plan the primary vehicle for rehabilitating war-torn Europe and countering Soviet expansionism.

TL;DR

Regna AI is a new sovereign AI joint venture between Palantir and Ondas designed to provide mission-specific, post-trained open-weights models for defense, intelligence, and edge operations while safeguarding sensitive data.

Origins and Vision of Regna AI

  • Founding Context:
    • Formed after six months of close collaboration between Ondas and Palantir, tackling mission autonomy capabilities like *Sky Weaver*.
    • The name *Regna* translates to "Sovereign Kingdom," reflecting the commitment to delivering sovereign AI solutions for defense and government customers.
  • The Sovereign AI Thesis:
    • Inspired by Palantir CEO Dr. Alex Karp’s vision for sovereign AI across industries.
    • Focuses on post-training models for specific missions (such as intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance, or ISR) to compound operational "alpha"—measured in lives saved rather than commercial metrics.
  • Spin-Out Structure:
    • Initially bootstrapped by Ondas with Ondas serving as the first customer.
    • Structured to operate as an independent company serving the broader defense ecosystem rather than just a proprietary internal tool.

Technical Architecture & Edge Constraints

  • Rejection of General-Purpose AI:
    • Rather than relying on cloud-hosted frontier models designed for general tasks, Regna AI builds specialized, fine-tuned models tailored to specific operational constraints.
  • Edge Deployment:
    • Models are engineered to run on limited form factors and compute footprints found on edge platforms, including stratospheric balloons, long-endurance UAVs, and ground robotics.
  • Disconnected Operations:
    • Designed for environments with limited bandwidth and compute, enabling distributed inferencing directly on platforms while maintaining situational awareness.

Human-in-the-Loop Validation and Trust

  • Forward-Deployed Engineering Model:
    • Utilizes Palantir-style Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) working shoulder-to-shoulder with customers to map data inputs directly to operational decisions.
  • "Mini-Labs" for Customers:
    • Commanders maintain continuous control over model training, reviewing end-to-end data pipelines and recommended actions.
    • Operators can iteratively accept or reject outputs, ensuring high trust before deploying models to the battlefield or simulated exercises.

Complex Multi-Domain Use Cases

  • Stratospheric Balloon Routing:
    • Managing infinitely complex operations for stratospheric balloons—including dynamic weather, wind patterns, exclusion zones, and inclement weather routing.
  • Multi-Domain Orchestration:
    • Connecting assets across the stratosphere, air, space, ground, and surface domains to distill infinite data streams into actionable commander decisions.
  • Building on Existing Frameworks:
    • Leverages prior operational learnings from *Sky Weaver*, mission control, and orbit systems to shape the architecture of the new models.

TL;DR

Simile is a $2B AI startup building foundation models of human behavior through digital twins, backed by $300M in total funding from Greenoaks, Index Ventures, Fei-Fei Li, and Andrej Karpathy. By combining large language models with agentic architectures and partnerships with over 40 million real people globally, the company’s technology has demonstrated 85%+ accuracy in predicting human behavior and is currently used by enterprises like CVS, Wealthfront, Deloitte, and Banco Itaú to simulate decisions before real-world deployment.

Origins & Research Evolution: From "Smallville" to Foundation Models

  • Academic Origins: The company evolved from *Smallville*, a 2023 research experiment led at Stanford by Joon Sung Park and co-founders Michael Bernstein and Percy Liang.
  • The Smallville Experiment:
    • A game town (resembling Pokemon or Sims) populated by 25 autonomous AI agents powered by GPT-3/3.5 paired with an agentic architecture featuring memory, planning, and reflection.
    • Agents performed daily routines, organized a Valentine's Day party, and formed relationships with zero pre-programmed scripts, demonstrating emergent behavior.
  • Core Breakthrough: Proved that foundational LLMs could be prompted to generate novel, human-like behaviors and interact within simulated communities.

Technology, Methodology & Validation

  • Individual and Environmental Modeling: Simula models individuals first (learning base traits like risk tolerance and aesthetics) and then places them into simulated environments to observe chaotic, emergent societal interactions.
  • The "Gap" in Human Behavior:
    • Surveys show normal distributions (what people *say* they will do, e.g., going to the gym).
    • Actual behavior follows an exponential curve driven by activation energy and "optionality" (opportunity costs and available alternatives).
  • Validation & Accuracy:
    • Early testing relied on human evaluators assessing agent believability ("illusion of life").
    • Scaled to a representative sample of 1,000 U.S. participants using voice-to-voice AI interviews to build digital twins, predicting actual behavior with 85%+ accuracy after a two-week blind test.
  • Data Quality Controls: Partnered with Gallup (holding a panel of hundreds of thousands built over decades) and implemented attention checks (arithmetic, recall) and sparse polling intervals to prevent respondent fatigue and gaming.
  • Informed Consent: Excludes underage populations and unconsenting individuals; otherwise aims for a complete representative snapshot of society, capturing both positive and negative human tendencies.

Business Model, Products & Enterprise Use Cases

  • Platform Offering: Delivered via a SaaS web interface where enterprise users define target populations and ask unlimited questions to receive instant simulations with an accompanying confidence score (0-100 range probability, akin to a scientific p-value).
  • Vertical-Specific Agent Banks: Maintains pre-built simulated panels across key verticals, supplemented by custom data collection reaching tens to hundreds of thousands of real people weekly (with a 24-hour turnaround for new panels).
  • Key Enterprise Customers:
    • Retailers: CVS (optimizing store layout, messaging, and decor).
    • Financial Institutions: Wealthfront, Deloitte, and Banco Itaú (Brazil) for testing policies and communicating corporate values.
    • Other Verticals: Entertainment, CPG, and automotive companies.
  • High-Stakes Use Cases: Concept testing, conjoint analysis, max-diff analysis, messaging optimization for electric vehicles, internal HR retention modeling, and recruitment strategy.

Marvin Schwaibold of Shopify and Aaron Epstein of Y Combinator review how AI tooling is shifting product design toward a full-stack, editor-driven workflow, and evaluate motion and interactivity across five viewer-submitted startup websites.

Shopify's AI Product Studio & Workflow Evolution

  • Shift to AI-Driven Workflows: Marvin Schwaibold explains that while design processes remain iterative, tooling has moved from static aesthetic layouts in Figma to terminal-based AI agents (like Codex and Cloud Code) that rapidly produce real, testable demos.
  • The Designer as Editor: Marvin Schwaibold notes that AI handles the heavy lifting of code and execution complexity, placing humans in the driving seat to curate, quality-control, and steer outputs based on what looks and feels right.
  • Shopify's Dual-Track Initiative: Marvin Schwaibold describes his team's work over the past year touching core surfaces, split into two areas:
    • Admin Experience: Redesigning and polishing interfaces for web and the mobile app (the "green app").
    • Sidekick: Building AI infrastructure that allows merchants to manage and automate their stores conversationally via chat.
  • Prototyping for Buy-In: Marvin Schwaibold emphasizes that tangible prototypes built with AI spread through an organization like wildfire and secure founder/executive alignment much faster than traditional PRD documents.

Independent Studio Roots & The "Primal" System

  • Molly Studio Background: Marvin Schwaibold highlights his time running the Brooklyn design studio Molly alongside partner J Tal, working with major clients like Collins, Apple, and Google with a tight team of two to three concurrent clients.
  • The Primal Content System: Marvin Schwaibold explains this structured methodology used to digest heavy client information into exploratory website flows where content is progressively disclosed rather than demanding attention.
  • Collins Bookshelf Craft: Marvin Schwaibold details a hand-crafted bookshelf component built with engineer Jesper featuring organic, level-up bounce animations designed as an "Easter egg" to signal agency-level dedication and craft to potential clients.
  • Joining Shopify: Marvin Schwaibold shares that Tobi Lutke’s mandate—stating that the future needs to be designed and requiring designers to paint a picture—inspired him to bring his studio into Shopify for massive-scale impact.

Grass Shader & DialKit Prototyping

  • Weekend Grass Shader Project: Marvin Schwaibold details a personal project (available on GitHub via MarvinSchwaibold/grass-shader) converting real grass video footage into a web shader with 217,000 individual grass blade variations.
  • Iterative AI Prompting: Marvin Schwaibold describes feeding video, screenshots, and topographical height maps into ChatGPT and Codex agents over multiple prompts to correct underlying terrain geometry and lighting.
  • DialKit Integration: Marvin Schwaibold explains how Josh introduced DialKit (run via `npm run` from the website) to add binary toggles and variable sliders, exposing underlying structures to instantly spot errors and tweak values like grass length and wind movement.
  • Carousel Iteration: Marvin Schwaibold shares that a portfolio carousel inspired by Mike Matas took 91 prompts to get right, utilizing a side parameter panel to dial in physical properties like "pull boost."

Viewer Website Reviews (Motion & Interactivity)

  • Nstack: Marvin Schwaibold and Aaron Epstein praise the retro, whimsical desktop-emulator feel and dropping users straight into the product, but criticize visual overload, dense sidebars, and competing CTA buttons that violate the rule of clear visual hierarchy.
  • SkillSync: Marvin Schwaibold and Aaron Epstein note that while dark mode and dark aesthetic are clean, the page is a "mile wide and an inch deep," burying key information behind unclicked sidebar tabs and utilizing superfluous animations that do not aid user comprehension.
  • Instaplay: Aaron Epstein and Marvin Schwaibold highlight the instant engagement of interactive prompt-to-game capabilities, but critique the immediate paywall/sign-up friction and hidden navigation paths that obscure the primary goal of creating games.
  • Netlify (Mentlify): Marvin Schwaibold and Aaron Epstein praise the clean, trustworthy presentation, reductive typography, strong social proof (Microsoft, Anthropic, Coinbase), and breathable, well-spaced chapter-based scroll flow.
  • Nullify: Aaron Epstein and Marvin Schwaibold commend the high-quality instructional videos, but criticize the competing headline placeholders, difficult click targets on carousels, and the use of sluggish scroll-jacking that hinders native user scroll speed.
  • Instinct: Aaron Epstein and Marvin Schwaibold praise the immersive 3D/video elements and brave serif font choices for space tech, but recommend instantly triggering hover states and adopting Blossom Carousel (from the open-source Blossom Carousel GitHub repo) for smoother trackpad scrolling.
