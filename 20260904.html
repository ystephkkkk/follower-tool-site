TL;DR

Former Vanguard CEO Bill McNab discusses his 30-year career scaling the firm to over $5 trillion, his transition to board governance at giants like IBM and UnitedHealth, and his active role advising fintech startups and venture capital firms like Venrock.

## Vanguard Legacy and Leadership Principles

  • Early Career Influence: Bill McNab joined Vanguard in 1986 after a stint in New York banking, drawn in by the firm's contrarian values after an initial meeting where Jack Bogle discussed crossing $15 billion under management while lecturing for an hour and a half on asset management reform.
  • The Power of We vs. I: Rooted in his experience coaching rowing at Dartmouth and teaching Latin at Haverford School, McNab applied the collective drive of a rowing team to corporate leadership, heavily influenced by former CEO Jack Brennan.
  • Math and Psychology of Indexing: While the late 1990s market scandals pushed investors toward index funds psychologically, the overwhelming long-term mathematical reality—that index funds outperformed 90% of active equities over any rolling period exceeding a decade—fueled Vanguard's explosive growth to $15 trillion today.
  • Financial Crisis Strategy (2008–2009): Stepping into the CEO role in August 2008 just as Lehman Brothers collapsed, McNab and his board guaranteed no redundancies or layoffs for staff, instead doubling down on customer service and operational flexibility to keep employees and clients calm.
  • Post-Crisis Pivots: Vanguard went "all in" on ETFs, expanded its independent advisor channel, and partnered with behavioral finance experts Shlomo Benartzi and Dick Richard Thaler to pioneer automated 401(k) enrollment, contribution escalation, and default target-date funds.
  • Succession Philosophy: Reflecting on leadership tenure, McNab noted Jack Brennan's wisdom that after 10 to 12 years, people stop questioning a CEO, making it healthy to step down and let a new generation innovate; McNab retired as CEO at age 60.

## Board Governance at IBM and UnitedHealth

  • Core Governance Framework: McNab co-wrote a book on governance with Ram Charan and Dennis Carey, emphasizing that boards must focus on three core pillars: Talent and Culture, Strategy, and Risk, while avoiding operational interference.
  • IBM Transformation: Serving on IBM’s board under CEO Arvind Krishna, McNab highlights the company's strategic agility in pivoting toward hybrid cloud and AI, balancing the shareholder perspective with deep technical evaluation.
  • UnitedHealth Crisis Management: Following unexpected externalities—including the murder of a CEO and subsequent leadership changes—former CEO Steve Hemsley returned, demonstrating the necessity for boards to maintain extreme strategic agility and probe management effectively.
  • Public vs. Mutual Dynamics: Shifting from the mutual structure of Vanguard (where there is no stock price or outside shareholder pressure) to public boards required adapting to quarterly guidance and earnings calls, though McNab advocates for companies to emphasize multi-year 5-to-10-year aspirations.

## Startup Mentorship and Fintech Innovation

  • Venture Capital Pivot: McNab serves as a senior advisor to Venrock (working closely with partner Nick Bierme) to immerse himself in private markets and understand cutting-edge technology ecosystems.
  • Altruist: Invested in the custodian platform alongside venture backers, praising founder Jason Wank for using an engineering mindset to challenge legacy margin structures and build a future advisor platform.
  • Vanilla: An estate planning software startup founded by Steve Oshins that codifies complex high-net-worth estate strategies into automated, advisor-facing software.
  • Finny: A startup co-founded by Finny that utilizes AI and structural success-based pricing models to help wealth management firms match prospects with advisors based on optimal fit.
  • Other Startups:
    • Moment: Founded by ex-Citadel executives, reinventing fixed income and fractional bond trading.
    • Sillow: A litigation-focused firm bridging legal talent with engineering to target non-correlated litigation and class-action outcomes.

## The Future of Financial Advice

  • The Essential Human Element: While digital platforms and robo-advisors (such as Vanguard's digital advisor or Betterment) handle basic allocations, McNab strongly believes that human advisors are critical during volatility to talk clients off the ledge and prevent self-harming behavior.
  • Technology as an Enabler: AI and modern software (such as automated note-taking and client data tracking) will enable advisors to triple their client capacity—from 100 to 300 clients—while providing a significantly more personalized experience.
  • The Dangers of Leverage and Day Trading: McNab expresses serious concern over the return of retail day trading, single-day options, prediction markets, and hidden leverage within the bond market and hyperscaler infrastructure spending.

## Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Advisor Capacity Growth: McNab predicts that with incoming AI and fintech tools, individual financial advisors will scale their practice capacity from an industry norm of roughly 100 clients up to 300 clients more effectively. — Bill McNab

AMD's Computing & AI Vision

  • Evolution of Computing:
    • Computing has progressed from desks to pockets, and is now putting intelligence everywhere.
    • AI is described as "the new electricity," shifting computing from executing commands to actively understanding user intent and collaborating as a partner.
  • Global and Everyday Impact:
    • Space Exploration: AMD technology powers systems inside the Orion spacecraft for the Artemis II mission.
    • Healthcare: Powers intuitive robotic-assisted surgery systems (translating a 3mm hand movement into 1mm while filtering tremors) and cancer detection algorithms in Finland's LUMI supercomputer.
    • Entertainment: Powers EPYC processors and Threadripper for cloud platforms, PlayStation 5 Pro (Spider-Man 2), and game development pipelines.
  • The Agentic AI Shift:
    • AI has evolved from conversational models to reasoning systems, and finally to agentic AI (capable of planning, using tools, checking work, and operating continuously in 2025).
    • Productivity Multiplier: A single user orchestrating an agentic team can dramatically scale output, but this creates a massive compute and token consumption demand.
    • Token Growth Projections: Monthly global AI token processing jumped from 0.7 quadrillion to 1.7 quadrillion in one year, with estimates reaching 120 quadrillion AI tokens per month by 2030.
    • Economic Pressure: 93% of companies are exceeding their AI budgets, highlighting the need for efficient local compute to avoid unsustainable cloud token costs (e.g., high-volume cloud models can cost nearly €100,000 per user annually).

Next-Generation AI PCs & Hardware

  • The Pillars of Personal AI: Local compute, privacy and control, and personal context.
  • Gorgon Processors & Strix Halo:
    • Efficiency Gains: Newer, smaller models match or beat larger cloud counterparts (e.g., Qwen 3.5 with 9 billion parameters beating older models; open-source Laguna S2.1 beating cloud SANA 5 on software engineering benchmarks).
    • Strix Halo Specs: Features 120 gigabytes of unified memory, enabling local execution of models up to 200 billion parameters directly on a desktop or laptop.
    • Local vs. Cloud Economics: Eliminates per-token cloud charges, allowing users to run parallel agents locally (up to 10 simultaneous local agents on Strix Halo).
  • Gorgon Halo & Sunday (HP ZBook):
    • Gorgon Halo Specs: Increased unified memory from 128 to 192 gigabytes, running 200 to 300 billion parameter models locally (such as GLM 5.3 Flash outperforming cloud Fable 5 benchmarks).
    • Hardware Launches: Lenovo introduced the ThinkCentre X in Berlin powered by Gorgon Halo. HP announced Sunday, a next-gen ZBook built for personal AI featuring 190 gigabytes of unified memory, capable of rendering real-time 3D worlds from a single prompt locally.
  • Personal Context Layer:
    • Beyond applications, models, and compute, personal AI introduces a Context Layer that understands user intent, history, and goals to deliver tailored outcomes.

TL;DR

The U.S. economy's heavy reliance on massive AI capital expenditures—which account for roughly half of projected 2026 GDP growth—makes it vulnerable, with OpenAI acting as a critical Achilles' heel whose potential failure could trigger an immediate recession.

Macroeconomic Headwinds, Interest Rates, and AI Debt

  • Rising Interest Rates:
    • The 10-year Treasury yield neared 4.8%, bringing the market closer to a correction point driven by inflation fears and the massive supply of AI debt being issued.
    • Treasury Secretary Besson's recent attempt to lower long-term rates appears to have failed.
    • While higher rates pressure debt-issuing companies, they benefit consumers who save in banks or money market funds.
  • Economic Dependence on AI Capex:
    • Hyperscalers are spending roughly $700 billion in capex this year and even more next year.
    • This AI spending accounts for about 50% of the projected 2% US GDP growth for 2026.

The AI Ecosystem: Hyperscalers, Circular Financing, and Nvidia

  • Hyperscaler Cash Flow Transformation:
    • Major tech companies have pivoted from throwing off excess cash to spending hundreds of billions on AI capex, depleting cash flows and forcing some to raise debt and equity.
  • Circular Financing:
    • Led largely by Nvidia through loans and equity investments (such as deals with Perplexity and Poolside), funds are often recycled back into purchasing Nvidia chips.
  • Nvidia's Customer Concentration:
    • Nvidia's July 2026 quarter revenue grew over 100% year-over-year (accelerating from 85% in the April 2026 quarter).
    • However, Note 7 of Nvidia's 10-Q shows that 5 direct customers account for 22%, 14%, 13%, 11%, and 10% (totaling 70%) of its accounts receivable balance, tying its fortunes directly to hyperscalers.

OpenAI as the Systemic Achilles' Heel

  • Reliance on OpenAI & Anthropic:
    • Wall Street reports suggest 70% of hyperscaler AI revenue—and 25% to 35% of total hyperscaler cloud revenue—comes from Anthropic and OpenAI.
    • Oracle has a $600 billion backlog, with 50% coming from OpenAI alone; OpenAI also accounts for over 20% of CoreWeave's contracted order book.
  • OpenAI Departures:
    • August 2026: Chief Revenue Officer Denise Dresser and former COO Brad Lightcap (after 8 years) resigned.
    • March 2026: Head of Robotics and Consumer Hardware Caitlyn Kalinowski resigned over governance concerns regarding a Pentagon agreement.
    • April 2026: Chief Marketing Officer Kate Rouch and Science Research Overseer Kevin Wild departed.
  • Tepid Financials and Losses:
    • June Quarter Financials: OpenAI generated $6.7 billion in revenue (up only 18% from the March quarter) while costs surged to $12.3 billion (up $3 billion in three months, against a $1 billion revenue increase).
    • Strategic Misstep: OpenAI initially focused on the B2C market with underpriced or free products before playing catch-up in the B2B market against Anthropic.
    • Capital Burn: According to Ed Zitron, OpenAI must raise $100 billion per year for the next several years to survive.
    • Advertising Metric Ambiguity: OpenAI announced an annualized revenue run rate (ARR) of $1 billion for its advertising business, but missed its earlier 2026 projection of $2.4 billion in ad revenue.

Ramifications of an OpenAI Failure

  • Direct Impact on Oracle:
    • Oracle's credit rating is barely above junk at BBB- (S&P).
    • When Oracle previously reported an RPO explosion to $455 billion, its stock jumped from $230 to $330, but plummeted after analysts highlighted its OpenAI exposure; today, Oracle trades around $145 (down over 50% from its peak). An OpenAI failure would jeopardize Oracle immediately.
  • Broader Economic and Sector Impacts:
    • Hyperscalers & Tech: Amazon, Google, Microsoft, Oracle, and Nvidia would experience spending cutbacks, slowing cloud revenue and correcting tech stocks.
    • Financials: Investment banks and large banks at peak valuations would weaken due to declining AI financing needs.
    • Industrials: Power, electrification, and automation beneficiaries (e.g., GE Vernova, Quanta, Eaton, Rockwell) would decline.
  • Reallocation to Safety Sectors:
    • Investors are expected to shift capital toward safety sectors (healthcare, consumer staples) and subsectors (such as property and casualty insurance).
    • Highlighted Safety ETFs: LVHD (Franklin US Low Volatility High Dividend Index ETF), SPLV (Invesco S&P 500 Low Volatility ETF), and KBWP (Invesco KBW Property and Casualty Insurance ETF).

Legal and Regulatory News: Meta Settlement

  • State AG Settlement:
    • Meta settled a California-based lawsuit brought by state attorneys general regarding algorithm harm to young adults and children for over $17 billion.
    • Conduct Changes: Meta agreed to alter its algorithms and business practices regarding children and teens.
  • Legal Expert Perspective (Professor Ben Zaperski):
    • The settlement puts pressure on other defendants like TikTok and Snap.
    • Meta avoided larger verdicts, state-level regulations, and established a "line in the sand" protecting it from future individual tort claims by individuals attempting to sue years later.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Ed Zitron (via Steve Eisman): OpenAI will have to raise $100 billion per year for the next several years to survive.

TL;DR

Driven by coding agents, falling costs, and rapidly closing capability gaps with frontier labs, AI model consumption is experiencing a massive shift toward open-weight models, highlighted by a 150x increase in token usage on Ollama Cloud since the start of the year. Jeffrey Morgan, co-founder and CEO of Ollama, shares insights on this industry-wide transformation, the evolving balance between local and cloud-hosted open models, and the journey behind building a foundational tool used by 9 million developers and 85% of the Fortune 500.

State of Open Models & Enterprise Adoption

  • Massive Token Explosion:
    • Ollama Cloud Growth: Token usage has surged 150x since the start of the year (Jeff Morgan).
    • Individual Usage Inflection Points: Average weekly token usage per developer saw a major run-up at the start of the year driven by coding agents (Kimmy, GLM, MiniMax), followed by an explosive wave in April driven by OpenClaw and the Hermes agent project, which allowed non-developers to automate complex multi-step workflows (Jeff Morgan).
  • Enterprise Shift:
    • AT&T Benchmark: AT&T has already shifted 40% of their token consumption to open models, largely utilizing US and European models while evaluating Chinese origin models (Jeff Morgan).
    • Geographic Trends: Cloud token consumption on Ollama Cloud is predominantly driven by Chinese-origin models accessed globally, with the US and Germany standing out as major access hubs (Jeff Morgan). Local models show a strong, even split between US, European, and Chinese-origin models (Jeff Morgan).
  • Primary Drivers Beyond Cost:
    • Control & Customization: While cost is the immediate pain point that opens the door, businesses view open models as a way to gain better control over AI and customize it for their unique use cases (Jeff Morgan).
    • Security Testing: Open models serve as a critical advantage for security testing and pentesting because closed frontier models typically refuse to execute malicious-looking tasks (Jeff Morgan).

The Infrastructure & Ecosystem Stack

  • The OS for AI Models:
    • Unified Runtime: Ollama acts as a glue layer or operating system, packaging inference engines, hardware stacks (Nvidia, Apple Silicon, AMD, Intel), and application harnesses into a consistent day-zero experience (Jeff Morgan).
    • Day-Zero Fire Drills: Model release cycles are accelerating drastically (e.g., DeepSeek Flash releasing three iterations over the summer); coordination typically happens within the final 24 hours before public release to ensure inference speed, accuracy, and tool-calling spec compliance (Jeff Morgan).
  • The Rise of Best-of-Breed Layers:
    • Unbundling the Stack: Moving away from closed walled gardens, the open ecosystem is unbundling into best-of-breed services handling knowledge/context, coordination (sub-agents), and execution/sandboxes (Jeff Morgan).
    • Stateful vs. Stateless: Stateful elements like storage, security credentials, and safety memory will remain outside the model layer due to rapid model training cadences (Jeff Morgan).
  • Hardware & Local Workstations:
    • Next-Gen Personal Desktops: Hardware advancements like Nvidia's DGX Spark (featuring 128 GB of unified memory) and Apple Silicon (via the MLX project) allow developers to run 20B to 120B parameter models locally at high speeds (Jeff Morgan).
    • Hybrid Execution Model: Enterprises are adopting a hybrid approach where simpler, low-latency document processing runs locally for free on existing hardware, while heavy coding agents route to cloud models (Jeff Morgan).

Economics, Flash Models, and Task Composition

  • The Era of "Unlimited" Tokens:
    • Ultra-Low-Cost Workhorses: A new class of models (like DeepSeek Flash and GPT Luna) provides ultra-low cost per task and per token, enabling widespread team adoption and high-volume token consumption without strict rationing (Jeff Morgan).
    • Task Composition Over God Models: Rather than relying on a single "god model," developers are successfully chaining cheaper flash models together via orchestration layers to handle complex workflows cost-effectively (Jeff Morgan & Panel).
  • Future Budget Balance:
    • Token Volume vs. Spend: While open models will handle 80% to 90% of a business's total token volume, budget allocation will be split, with the hardest reasoning tasks reserved for expensive frontier labs and the vast majority of line-item grunt work handled by cheap open models (Jeff Morgan).

The Ollama Origin Story & Lessons Learned

  • Pivoting in the Wilderness:
    • Early Missteps: Founded by former Docker engineers (Jeff Morgan and co-founder Michael), the company spent its first two years (including post-YC Winter 2021 and Series A funding from Benchmark in 2022) overengineering ideas like Kubernetes SSO and developer security tools before finding product-market fit with Ollama in July 2023 (Jeff Morgan).
    • The Zero-to-One Moment: The pivot clicked instantly when running local Llama models revealed a genuine, highly resonant developer pain point, leading to rapid viral adoption and 100,000+ GitHub stars (Jeff Morgan).
  • Monetization & Enterprise Expansion:
    • The Docker Parallel: Much like Docker, Ollama spent years cultivating a massive open-source developer base before introducing monetization via Ollama Cloud earlier this year (Jeff Morgan).
    • Frictionless Adoption: Because open models are free and can be run anywhere without IT permission, hobbyist adoption translated seamlessly into widespread Fortune 500 enterprise usage (Jeff Morgan).

World Labs co-founders unveiled Atlas, a next-generation world model that unifies pixel generation and 3D reconstruction through "new view prediction," allowing users to generate novel perspectives, 3D spaces, and simulations from sparse input cameras.

Atlas Capabilities and Technical Architecture

  • New View Prediction: Functions as a foundational primitive where given views or scene descriptions, the model predicts what the environment looks like from any position in space and time.
  • Multimodal Native Input: Processes text, images, videos, 3D camera poses, and depth maps natively from the pre-training phase.
  • Joint Reconstruction and Generation: Combines computer vision subfields by using generative capacity to fill in gaps (unseen pixels) while using explicit 3D camera poses for high-precision reconstruction.
  • Transition from Marble: Evolves past the previous *Marble* model by bypassing Gaussian splat bottlenecks and bifurcating output modalities earlier in a unified architecture.

Spatial Intelligence and Practical Applications

  • Bullet Time and Creative Work: Enables complex Matrix-style "bullet time" shots using as few as three consumer iPhone videos instead of hundreds of studio cameras.
  • Industrial and Architectural Design: Reduces the arduous, labor-intensive process of mapping verbal feedback or sketches into 3D software by allowing users to bypass traditional dense capture loops (a 50x to 100x reduction in required source images).
  • Robotics and Simulation:
    • Addresses the critical bottleneck of robotics data by bridging the real-to-sim and sim-to-real gap.
    • Allows neural simulators trained on diverse data to act as simulation beds—and potentially planners—for robotic policies like industrial cabling.

Roadmap and Future Directions

  • Dynamics and 4D Video: Leverages pre-training data that already contains latent dynamics (such as water waves and moving cars), with future iterations scaling temporal movement to enable full 4D walk-around videos.
  • Editability and Control: Focuses on industrial-strength multi-turn editing, allowing users to control scene layout, object identities, and time without degrading visual output quality.
  • AI Completeness: Establishes "new view prediction" as an AI-complete primitive equivalent to next-token prediction, mirroring how biological evolution tied the development of eyes and movement to spatial understanding.
