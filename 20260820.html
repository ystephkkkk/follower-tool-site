TL;DR

Max Hodak, co-founder and CEO of Science Corporation, discusses the company's mission to treat the brain as a computational system—highlighting the commercial approval of their PRIMA retinal implant in Europe and their long-term vision of achieving substrate independence. Sarah Guo hosts the discussion, exploring how bridging neuroscience with AI representations can revolutionize medicine and reduce the fragility of the human condition.

Science Corporation & the PRIMA Retinal Implant

  • Company Mission: Science Corporation is a medical device company focused on using a differentiated understanding of the brain to drive large effect sizes in medicine.
  • The PRIMA Product:
    • A tiny chip implanted under the retina in the back of the eye for patients blinded by the loss of light-sensitive cells (such as macular degeneration, retinitis pigmentosa, and Stargardt's disease).
    • Patients wear glasses equipped with a laser projector that projects an image onto the implant, stimulating the retina directly to bypass dead rods and cones and send visual signals to the brain.
  • Regulatory Milestone: In July (2 years post-acquisition of the French company Pixium), Science received CE designation marketing approval in Europe to commercially sell PRIMA, with first sales scheduled for the coming weeks.
  • Clinical Results: Clinical trials demonstrated patients filling in Sudoku/crossword puzzles and reading books, providing proof-of-concept for restoring form vision.

The Brain as a Computer & BCI Taxonomy

  • Computational Framework: Hodak views the brain and the universe literally as computers, arguing that matter can be arranged to solve computational problems over time.
  • BCI Landscape & Spectrum:
    • BCI is a broad category akin to "pharma," containing vastly different hypotheses and product types rather than a single bet.
    • Low-impact/High-volume: Products like silent speech or "brain keyboards" (speech-to-text, inner monologue capture) act primarily as hand substitutes or input devices, though humans may already be bottlenecked by language and cognitive limits (~10 bits per second).
    • High-impact/Deep integration: Restoring sensory modalities (vision, hearing, balance, motor control) redraws the border around the brain and moves toward the "Matrix" level of intervention.

Science's Pipeline & Three-Pronged Strategy

  • Core Pipeline Elements:

1. Vision: Retinal prostheses and in-house gene therapies (next-gen going to human trials next year).

2. Biohybrid Neural Interfaces: Research involving engrafting living neurons that grow in and form new biological connections.

3. Perfusion Medicine (Vessel Program): A program targeting other medical areas like organ perfusion.

  • Targeting Major Killers: Hodak argues that support organs (heart, pancreas, liver, lungs) exist to keep brain activity going, and believes perfusion and BCI programs will target major causes of death like cardiovascular disease and brain-metastasized cancer.

AI Models and Neuroscience Parallels

  • Platonic Representation Hypothesis: Internal mathematical objects and concept representations inside large AI models exhibit geometries very similar to those found in neuroscience.
  • Practical Alignment: Science uses these parallels constructively to achieve alignments between animal brain neural recordings and AI model internal representations, signaling that AI is capturing true underlying data manifolds of intelligence.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Gene Therapy Trials: Science's indigenously developed state-of-the-art gene therapy will go to human clinical trials next year (stated by Max Hodak).
  • Next-Gen Retinal Prosthesis: The next version of the PRIMA retinal prosthesis is currently in animal studies and is targeted for human trials next year, expanding the addressable patient population from hundreds of thousands to millions (stated by Max Hodak).

TL;DR

Borderless founders—entrepreneurs who combine the local insights and networks of their home markets with the speed, talent, and capital of Silicon Valley—are increasingly building the next generation of global companies, accelerated by AI and powerful international diaspora networks.

The Origins and Evolution of International Investing at a16z

  • Initial Focus on Latin America:
    • Angela Strange and Gabriel Vasquez trace a16z's international investing back to early fintech investments in Latin America, starting with a check into Santiago Suarez (CEO of Audi, which now serves a quarter of the Colombian population).
    • The strategy originated from a WhatsApp group created by Vasquez, which brought together unicorn founders from Brazil, Colombia, and Mexico who were not previously connected, leading to shared deal flow and angel investments.
  • The AI Acceleration:
    • The rise of AI shifted the dynamic from a16z traveling to local regions (Brazil, Colombia) to founders actively wanting to integrate into the Bay Area faster.
    • While AI democratizes the ability to build from anywhere, it simultaneously concentrates the fastest-moving epicenter in the Bay Area.
  • The "Borderless Dinners" and Diaspora Networks:
    • a16z organized dinners mapping out successful founders from Latin America (such as Guillermo Rauch from Vercel and Pedro Franceschi from Brex) to mentor newcomers.
    • Early attendees of these groups (e.g., Victor Cardenas from Slash, Oreos from Factories) have now graduated into unicorn founders and speakers themselves.
    • International diasporas function similarly to powerful college alumni networks, driven by a deep desire to pay it forward to the next generation of founders.

Defining the Borderless Founder

  • Inclusive Definition: Anyone trying to build a global company internationally, whether they start building primarily in their home market or land directly in the US with global ambitions.
  • The "Chip on the Shoulder" Mindset:
    • Borderless founders often come from overlooked countries (e.g., Gabriel Vasquez growing up in El Salvador) or small rural towns, driving a desire to prove they can achieve greatness regardless of birthplace.
    • They contrast with traditional US founders who rely on "everything as a service" at every stack layer (e.g., fraud-as-a-stack, KYC-as-a-stack); international founders in regions like Latin America often have to build entire infrastructure stacks locally.
  • Geographic Expansion: Beyond Latin America, a16z spends significant time in the UK, Sweden, Israel, and Germany.

Native Advantages of Borderless Founders

  • Talent & Diaspora Networks:
    • Borderless founders map top engineering and business talent from their home countries living in Silicon Valley.
    • Companies like Taco in Brazil are actively recruiting Brazilian talent back from the US to solve local bureaucratic challenges using AI.
    • Founders leverage non-obvious talent pools, such as regional scholarship programs and robotics competitions, rather than just top-tier university brands.
  • Brand & Government Validation:
    • Polish founders of 11 Labs became national representatives in the "AI Olympics," securing direct investment and initiatives from the Polish government.
    • Swedish government support gave early-stage validation to startups like Lovable and Gora, opening doors to large enterprises.
    • A strong local brand acts as a beacon to attract top international talent back to home markets (e.g., recruiting credit experts from Capital One to move to Bogota for Audi).
  • Customer Base & Design Partnerships:
    • Early design partnerships with major conglomerates (e.g., Factor Labs working with the Slim family's conglomerate in Latin America) convert into multi-million dollar contracts and expand into broader enterprise workflows.
    • Conversely, top Silicon Valley AI companies like Cognition found early go-to-market traction and high revenue shares by selling into hungry enterprise markets in Brazil.

Ecosystem Building, Repeat Founders, and Silicon Valley Integration

  • Ecosystem Playbooks & Local Luminaries:
    • a16z identifies "local luminaries"—respected local operators (e.g., Fredrik Hjelm of Voi in Sweden) who angel-invest and connect younger builders to the global stage.
    • Global scout programs recruit rising stars at international companies to foster local density.
  • Repeat Founders:
    • First-generation unicorn founders who achieved $1B–$5B outcomes are returning with deeper lessons to build even larger companies (e.g., Fredrik Hjelm's new company, pip.com).
    • These repeat founders provide elite "CEO school" experience for young, ambitious engineers working under them for a few years.
  • Tactical Soft-Landing Support:
    • Visas: a16z helps founders navigate the stressful US visa process, including investing in native visa platforms like Extraordinary.
    • Hacker Houses & Campuses: a16z sponsored Palo House (originated by students from Germany's CDTM program met at Slush in Finland), which hosted 1,200 people over 4 months, acting as a mini-college liberal arts incubator for international tech talent.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Global Venture Returns Share (Gabriel Vasquez): The share of venture capital returns coming from Silicon Valley companies will shift from roughly 10% toward 20% to 30%, bolstered by global borderless companies with dual-country footprints (headquarters in the US, engineering in home markets).
  • Continued Growth of International Investing (Angela Strange): International investing trends will continue to scale; out of recent a16z apps, 40% of investments were in international founders (split evenly between US-based and foreign-based).
