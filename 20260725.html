TL;DR

Eric Landau, Co-CEO of Encord, explains how his company evolved from a computer vision tool into the data layer for physical AI, emphasizing the compounding nature of product-market fit and the importance of riding the emotional rollercoaster of startup life.

Background & Origin

  • Academic & Professional Trajectory:
    • Particle Physics & Quant Career: Eric Landau spent a decade as a high-frequency trading quant following a background in big data particle physics.
    • The "Bitter Lesson": Early AI relied on heavy feature engineering and domain expertise (mirroring his early career in physics and quant trading), whereas modern AI relies strictly on scaling data and compute.
  • The Decision to Quit:
    • Left a lucrative quant career during COVID at the exact moment his trading desk experienced its most profitable month ever (including the day oil traded negative).
    • Spent early days fighting Python dependencies on a couch, experiencing immediate contrast with the existential dread of his previous career.

Product-Market Fit & Market Evolution

  • The "Desert" Years (2021–Release):
    • Spent two years building before achieving breakout adoption, driven by long-term conviction in AI as a generational paradigm shift.
    • ChatGPT Catalyst: ChatGPT didn't create an overnight binary switch for Encord, but it made the market receptive to conversations about AI infrastructure.
  • Compounding PMF:
    • PMF was not a single launch event, but a slow, daily compounding process where the market moved toward their continuously improving product.
    • Milestone of realization: Closing sales via Gong without knowing what the customer's company actually did.
  • Data Scale & Operations:
    • Deals with multiple petabytes of multimodal data (video streams, sensor data, audio, language).
    • Processes more data than was used to train GPT-4.
    • Maintains a Bay Area operations facility equipped as a "film set" for robot data collection, alongside headquarters in London.

Pivot to Physical AI

  • The 80% Economy:
    • Started in vision because it was the hardest modality, anticipating multimodal systems.
    • Shifted focus as the market moved toward physical AI, noting that 80% of economic activity involves manipulating or moving things in the real world.
  • Key Use Cases:
    • Autonomous driving (nearing mass-scale production).
    • Robotics (humanoids, consumer robotics, manufacturing), with robotics insiders predicting robots will outnumber humans in 5 to 10 years.
    • Quirky applications include facial recognition for cows to track health.
  • Product Stack:
    • Beyond data collection for pre-training, Encord provides data management, curation ("finding a million needles in a billion haystacks"), annotation/enrichment, and evaluations.

Team Building & Sales Evolution

  • Building the Sales Org:
    • Built entirely through trial and error, requiring three successive sales teams before finding what worked.
    • Highlighted the danger of ignoring YC advice (e.g., keeping a bad hire for 9 months due to a lack of a single forced reason to fire them).
  • Geographic Strategy:
    • Co-founder is in San Francisco; Eric is based in London.
    • Strategy is to be where important people are (customers and talent, then investors). Most customers are in the US/Bay Area, while London offers a comparative advantage for AI and engineering talent with less intense competition.
    • Competes against scaled players and companies like Mercor, welcoming competition to drive company improvement.

TL;DR: Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced major partnerships in South Korea, including a $500 billion business arrangement with SK Group and a $1 billion investment in AI cloud leader Naver, while emphasizing the critical role of open-source AI models in global cybersecurity and innovation.

South Korea Partnerships and Infrastructure Investment

  • SK Group Partnership: $500 Billion Business Agreement: Nvidia and SK Group entered into a comprehensive partnership spanning memory consumption, purchases, and AI supercomputer sales as SK scales toward gigawatts of AI factories.
    • Memory Supply & Roadmap: Nvidia and SK Hynix are collaborating across a continuous memory roadmap, spanning HBM2, HBM3, 3E, 4, 4E, and beyond, with Jensen Huang noting that Nvidia faces memory and supply chain constraints.
    • SK Telecom AI Cloud: SK Telecom is building an AI cloud infrastructure intended to reach up to two gigawatts in the near future, utilizing Nvidia AI supercomputers.
  • Naver Investment: $1 Billion Capital Commitment: Nvidia is investing $1 billion in Naver, Korea's leading AI cloud provider, to support their expansion of a 200-megawatt AI data center in Korea and subsequent global scaling.

The Future Semiconductor Landscape and Supply Constraints

  • Computers for Computers: Paradigm Shift: Huang noted that the semiconductor industry is moving beyond building computers solely for people. With the rise of 100 billion AI agents and billions of robots utilizing computers, the chip industry will need to expand to be 10 times larger over the next decade.
  • Infrastructure Bottlenecks: Growth Limitations: Nvidia is currently constrained in HBM memories, LPDDR memories, land, power, and construction workers. Huang stated that while the industry has the capacity to double infrastructure each year, growing much faster than that will be difficult due to physical limitations of land and power.

Global AI Approaches: US vs. China

  • Strategic Differences: Tokens vs. Efficiency: Referencing conversations with SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won, Huang noted that China focuses heavily on lowering the dollar per token, whereas the United States focuses on token quality.
  • Talent Pool: Researcher Manufacturing: Huang highlighted that China produces more AI researchers annually than likely the rest of the world combined, and that brilliant researchers across both nations will continue to find answers despite different resources, constraints, and conditions.

Open vs. Closed AI Models and Cybersecurity

  • The Case for Open Models: Essential for Control and Security: Huang, alongside peers like Satya Nadella, recently signed a letter defending open-source AI models, asserting they are vital for cybersecurity, innovation, startup ecosystems, and company/national sovereignty.
  • Cost and Utility Realities: Closed vs. Open Pricing: Huang explained that closed models are often cheaper because users do not need to train, fine-tune, or host them. However, open models are necessary when organizations require strict control, specialized adaptation, or protection of proprietary corporate intelligence ("alpha").
  • Distributed Self-Defense: Mitigating Single Points of Failure: Huang cited the example of Hugging Face utilizing an open model (GLM 5.2) to quickly identify and patch vulnerabilities after an incident, demonstrating that open capabilities provide crucial distributed self-defense rather than relying solely on single points of failure in closed models.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Semiconductor Industry Growth: Jensen Huang predicts the semiconductor industry will likely have to grow to be 10 times larger than it is today over the next decade or so to support future demand from billions of robots and 100 billion AI agents.
  • Industry Growth Rate: Huang claims the global AI infrastructure industry has the ability to double each year, but will have a hard time growing much faster due to physical constraints in land, power, and construction.
  • SK Telecom Infrastructure: SK Telecom intends to build up to two gigawatts of AI cloud infrastructure in the near future.
  • Naver Capacity Expansion: Naver is scaling up its AI cloud infrastructure in Korea up to 200 megawatts while expanding its footprint globally.
  • Nvidia-SK Business Volume: Nvidia and SK Group will transact over $500 billion of business with each other over time, including purchasing memory and scaling gigawatt-level AI factories.
  • Nvidia Product Scale: Nvidia plans to build a trillion dollars worth of VERA Rubin systems, requiring massive system memory procurement from partners like SK Hynix.

TL;DR

AI is rapidly reducing the cost and complexity of writing software, meaning the traditional "hard bit" of building pure software is gone; founders must now focus on distribution, hard regulatory/physical barriers, and deep customer engagement. At Y Combinator, partners stress that durable startups rely on obsessing over building something people want, launching early and often, and maintaining strong co-founder relationships rather than overly relying on AI or solo execution.

The Economics and Reality of AI Development

  • Cost Trends: Intelligence Is Getting 10x Cheaper: The cost of intelligence drops by roughly a factor of 10 every year for the same level of capability, making token costs a non-issue over a 6 to 12-month horizon.
    • YC provides accepted founders with $1M in free tokens, and student founders with $25,000 in credits through partner labs and hyperscalers.
  • Engineering Impact: Models Surpass Humans in Coding: There is essentially no programming task today where a human is more cost-effective or efficient than the absolute best available AI model.
  • The Death of Pure Software: Low-Moat Software is Dead: Products like Calendly, DocuSign, or basic SaaS wrappers lack durability because replication has become trivial.
    • Startups must identify their "hard bit"—such as brutal B2B sales, complex hardware, or regulatory hurdles like obtaining a banking license.

Startup Ecosystems and Hubs

  • Startup Hubs: SF Remains the Global Epicenter: While regional hubs exist (like London or Paris), San Francisco remains the primary global hub attracting the most ambitious people and biggest visions.
    • Replicating a community from scratch is extremely difficult; founders should instead join an existing high-culture startup or move to an established hub.
  • Ecosystem Origins: The GoCardless Family Tree: Founded in 2011, GoCardless and a handful of other companies formed the kernel of the London startup ecosystem, later spawning dozens of major companies including TransferWise and Monzo.

Founders, Teams, and Co-Founder Dynamics

  • The Solo Founder Myth: Co-Founders Provide Emotional Resilience: While a one-person billion-dollar company is theoretically possible, the marginal benefit of adding a co-founder far outweighs the coordination cost.
    • Solo founders face higher statistical failure rates and often suffer severe crises of confidence without a partner to pull them out of the gutter.
  • Co-Founder Selection Criteria: Prioritize Integrity and Resilience: Look for the smartest, most determined, highest-integrity person you have worked with—someone who shares your values and whose absence on a competing team would scare you.
    • Technical co-founders should not waste time hunting for a "business" co-founder; business skills are easily learned, and technical alignment is more valuable.
  • High Agency: Building Agency as a Muscle: High agency is the core belief that your actions produce tangible outcomes; it is developed by tackling progressively harder side projects and seeing them through to execution.

Research vs. Sales and the Startup Playbook

  • The Build Trap: Launch Early and Often: Founders suffer from loss aversion and prefer building or researching over talking to customers because rejection is terrifying.
    • Successful startups treat their business as an empirical hypothesis-testing exercise, launching early and constantly cycling between building and talking to users.
  • YC Success Patterns: Overwhelming Bottlenecks: Top-ranking startups set ambitious goals, reassess every two weeks, and relentlessly throw all their focus at their single biggest bottleneck.
  • Pivoting in the AI Era: Hypothesis Testing vs. Getting Sad: Pivoting should be driven by hard empirical data showing your core hypothesis is wrong, rather than running out of enthusiasm because sales are hard.

AI-Native Operations and the Future of B2B

  • AI Integration: Start with Narrow Loops: To build an AI-native company, make internal information queryable (via tools like Obsidian or Gbrain) and automate hyper-specific, narrow processes (such as automated sales follow-up drafts).
  • What Not to Automate: Never Outsource Customers or Co-Founders: Founders must retain direct exposure to customer conversations and maintain authentic communication with their co-founders.
  • Model Routing: Automatic Model Intermediation: Within roughly 12 months, queries will be automatically routed behind the scenes to the optimal intelligence tier without human intervention or concern over specific underlying models.

World Models vs. Large Language Models (LLMs)

  • The LLM Limitation:
    • Text-Based Proxies: LLMs learn entirely from human-written books and text on the internet—like someone born in a room who reads books for centuries without ever going outside.
    • Missing Traits: Result in experts with zero direct experience of the real world, lacking common sense or the ability to handle unfamiliar situations.
  • The World Model Approach:
    • Direct Learning: Models learn directly from the real world—video, audio, sensory data, and physical interaction—starting from scratch like a baby without language.
    • Data & Modalities: Utilizes massive amounts of video data (the easiest to source) and touch/sensory data gathered via robotics.
  • LLM vs. World Model Division:
    • LLM Strengths: Unbeatable for language-first, discrete, low-dimensional sequence problems (mathematics, programming, text).
    • World Model Strengths: Vastly superior for high-dimensional, noisy, long-horizon problems.
  • Flaws of Current Approaches:
    • Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models: Described by LeBrun as a "very bad hack"—using an LLM as a hammer for everything, resulting in inaccurate, slow, and expensive systems ($100 of compute per 24 hours for a home robot).

  • Founding Team & Structure:

    • Geographic Setup: Four primary hubs located in Paris, New York, Montreal, and Singapore (deliberately omitting San Francisco to ensure high commitment from early hires).
  • Record-Breaking Seed Round:
    • Funding Amount: Raised a massive $1.2 billion seed round in January.
    • The Real Cost: LeBrun noted to his team that dilution is not the true cost of $1.2 billion, but rather the expectations created in the outside world if nothing is visibly produced for two years.
  • Current Bottlenecks:
    • Securing compute (GPUs) remains difficult even with abundant capital, alongside hiring top-tier talent who understand world models over traditional LLMs.

Advice for Founders & Ambition

  • Founder Strengths: Founders strong in go-to-market and sales should wait for mature markets, whereas engineering-led founders excel at taking very early tech and building initial products.
  • The Ambition Framework:
    • Narrow Problem, Large Vision: Founders should pick an extremely narrow problem (one thing for one industry or set of customers) while holding an extremely large vision for the space.
    • European Ambition: European founders often think too small and avoid taking risks; big ambitions require risking failure to achieve breakthrough results.
