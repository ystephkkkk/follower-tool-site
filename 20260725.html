TL;DR

Sam Altman discusses how the explosive progress of AI is reshaping startups, the extreme chaos of scaling OpenAI, and his vision for abundant intelligence, decentralized liberty, and infrastructure bottlenecks.

Startups and the AI Era

  • Speed and Competitiveness: A 10-week-old startup today can achieve what previously took a year of work, driven entirely by AI and small, agile teams.
  • The Temptation of Easy Wins: Most startups focus on building vertical AI agents for enterprise, which works but misses the opportunity to tackle bigger, crazier challenges using new tools.
  • Trusting Exponentials: Founders must internalize the exponential nature of model progress and start working on things that require cheaper or smarter models coming in two to four years.
  • Advantage of Chaos: Startups have their biggest edge when the ground is shifting, costs are dropping, and cycle times are shrinking.

Scaling OpenAI and Infrastructure Bottlenecks

  • Key Bottlenecks: Altman identifies transistors followed by electrons as the primary bottlenecks to unabated AI scaling.
  • Core Suppliers: Keeping suppliers on OpenAI's timeline requires intense communication, showing them upcoming research, and aligning incentives by getting them to believe in the mission.
  • Compute Investments: Altman admits he badly undershot on early compute investments due to getting "psyched out by the financial markets."
  • Supply Chain Focus: OpenAI brought chip design and model design together, though electron production remains more of a commodity; Altman emphasizes the need for data centers that can build more data centers.
  • Project Pivots: OpenAI has repeatedly killed major projects—shutting down robotics when GPT-3 succeeded, and setting aside SORA and the browser to prioritize coding agents.

Leadership, Chaos, and Decision-Making

  • Operating in Chaos: Dealing with high-stress environments and chaos cannot be taught; it is only learnable through repeated exposure and emotional resilience.
  • Embracing Bad Days: Rather than viewing the opposite of a bad experience as a good one, Altman views it as no experience, allowing him to be grateful for difficult days.
  • Raising Ambition: Average corporate employees and young founders often suffer from catastrophically low ambition due to a lifetime of following rules; this can be broken through small, repeated wins.
  • Getting on Planes: Altman strongly advocates for getting on planes in marginal situations, noting he undertook a 35-day tour across 28 countries when the world was melting down post-GPT-4 launch.

The Singularity, Liberty, and the Future

  • AI Authoritarianism vs. Liberty: The critical fight of the current moment is whether society will head toward AI authoritarianism (a single machine god controlled by a few) or liberty, putting powerful tools in the hands of everyday people.
  • The Invention of the Joint Stock Corporation: Altman considers the joint-stock company the most important invention of the Industrial Revolution for aligning incentives and pooling capital.
  • Ceaseless Human Drive: Altman does not expect AI to usher in a permanent four-hour work week; evolutionary biology and human ambition mean people will simply find new, busier ways to create, compete, and be useful.
  • Evolutionary Biology: Betting against evolutionary biology is a bad bet; primal human desires—such as cooking together, quests, and adventure—will persist.

TL;DR

AI is rapidly reducing the cost and complexity of writing software, meaning the traditional "hard bit" of building pure software is gone; founders must now focus on distribution, hard regulatory/physical barriers, and deep customer engagement. At Y Combinator, partners stress that durable startups rely on obsessing over building something people want, launching early and often, and maintaining strong co-founder relationships rather than overly relying on AI or solo execution.

The Economics and Reality of AI Development

  • Cost Trends: Intelligence Is Getting 10x Cheaper: The cost of intelligence drops by roughly a factor of 10 every year for the same level of capability, making token costs a non-issue over a 6 to 12-month horizon.
    • YC provides accepted founders with $1M in free tokens, and student founders with $25,000 in credits through partner labs and hyperscalers.
  • Engineering Impact: Models Surpass Humans in Coding: There is essentially no programming task today where a human is more cost-effective or efficient than the absolute best available AI model.
  • The Death of Pure Software: Low-Moat Software is Dead: Products like Calendly, DocuSign, or basic SaaS wrappers lack durability because replication has become trivial.
    • Startups must identify their "hard bit"—such as brutal B2B sales, complex hardware, or regulatory hurdles like obtaining a banking license.

Startup Ecosystems and Hubs

  • Startup Hubs: SF Remains the Global Epicenter: While regional hubs exist (like London or Paris), San Francisco remains the primary global hub attracting the most ambitious people and biggest visions.
    • Replicating a community from scratch is extremely difficult; founders should instead join an existing high-culture startup or move to an established hub.
  • Ecosystem Origins: The GoCardless Family Tree: Founded in 2011, GoCardless and a handful of other companies formed the kernel of the London startup ecosystem, later spawning dozens of major companies including TransferWise and Monzo.

Founders, Teams, and Co-Founder Dynamics

  • The Solo Founder Myth: Co-Founders Provide Emotional Resilience: While a one-person billion-dollar company is theoretically possible, the marginal benefit of adding a co-founder far outweighs the coordination cost.
    • Solo founders face higher statistical failure rates and often suffer severe crises of confidence without a partner to pull them out of the gutter.
  • Co-Founder Selection Criteria: Prioritize Integrity and Resilience: Look for the smartest, most determined, highest-integrity person you have worked with—someone who shares your values and whose absence on a competing team would scare you.
    • Technical co-founders should not waste time hunting for a "business" co-founder; business skills are easily learned, and technical alignment is more valuable.
  • High Agency: Building Agency as a Muscle: High agency is the core belief that your actions produce tangible outcomes; it is developed by tackling progressively harder side projects and seeing them through to execution.

Research vs. Sales and the Startup Playbook

  • The Build Trap: Launch Early and Often: Founders suffer from loss aversion and prefer building or researching over talking to customers because rejection is terrifying.
    • Successful startups treat their business as an empirical hypothesis-testing exercise, launching early and constantly cycling between building and talking to users.
  • YC Success Patterns: Overwhelming Bottlenecks: Top-ranking startups set ambitious goals, reassess every two weeks, and relentlessly throw all their focus at their single biggest bottleneck.
  • Pivoting in the AI Era: Hypothesis Testing vs. Getting Sad: Pivoting should be driven by hard empirical data showing your core hypothesis is wrong, rather than running out of enthusiasm because sales are hard.

AI-Native Operations and the Future of B2B

  • AI Integration: Start with Narrow Loops: To build an AI-native company, make internal information queryable (via tools like Obsidian or Gbrain) and automate hyper-specific, narrow processes (such as automated sales follow-up drafts).
  • What Not to Automate: Never Outsource Customers or Co-Founders: Founders must retain direct exposure to customer conversations and maintain authentic communication with their co-founders.
  • Model Routing: Automatic Model Intermediation: Within roughly 12 months, queries will be automatically routed behind the scenes to the optimal intelligence tier without human intervention or concern over specific underlying models.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Cost Reductions: The cost of intelligence will continue a 10x cost reduction per year for the same level of capability — Speakers (Y Combinator Partners).
  • Model Routing and Abstraction: Within approximately 12 months, AI model selection will be entirely automated behind the scenes, routing queries to the appropriate intelligence tier without user intervention — Y Combinator Partners.

World Models vs. Large Language Models (LLMs)

  • The LLM Limitation:
    • Text-Based Proxies: LLMs learn entirely from human-written books and text on the internet—like someone born in a room who reads books for centuries without ever going outside.
    • Missing Traits: Result in experts with zero direct experience of the real world, lacking common sense or the ability to handle unfamiliar situations.
  • The World Model Approach:
    • Direct Learning: Models learn directly from the real world—video, audio, sensory data, and physical interaction—starting from scratch like a baby without language.
    • Data & Modalities: Utilizes massive amounts of video data (the easiest to source) and touch/sensory data gathered via robotics.
  • LLM vs. World Model Division:
    • LLM Strengths: Unbeatable for language-first, discrete, low-dimensional sequence problems (mathematics, programming, text).
    • World Model Strengths: Vastly superior for high-dimensional, noisy, long-horizon problems.
  • Flaws of Current Approaches:
    • Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models: Described by LeBrun as a "very bad hack"—using an LLM as a hammer for everything, resulting in inaccurate, slow, and expensive systems ($100 of compute per 24 hours for a home robot).

  • Founding Team & Structure:
    • Partnership: Founded alongside Yann LeCun (Executive Chairman), who provides the scientific vision and research direction.
    • Geographic Setup: Four primary hubs located in Paris, New York, Montreal, and Singapore (deliberately omitting San Francisco to ensure high commitment from early hires).
  • Record-Breaking Seed Round:
    • Funding Amount: Raised a massive $1.2 billion seed round in January.
    • The Real Cost: LeBrun noted to his team that dilution is not the true cost of $1.2 billion, but rather the expectations created in the outside world if nothing is visibly produced for two years.
  • Current Bottlenecks:
    • Securing compute (GPUs) remains difficult even with abundant capital, alongside hiring top-tier talent who understand world models over traditional LLMs.

Advice for Founders & Ambition

  • Founder Strengths: Founders strong in go-to-market and sales should wait for mature markets, whereas engineering-led founders excel at taking very early tech and building initial products.
  • The Ambition Framework:
    • Narrow Problem, Large Vision: Founders should pick an extremely narrow problem (one thing for one industry or set of customers) while holding an extremely large vision for the space.
    • European Ambition: European founders often think too small and avoid taking risks; big ambitions require risking failure to achieve breakthrough results.
