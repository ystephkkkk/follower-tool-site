TL;DR

Datadog CEO Olivier Pomel reflects on building a multi-product cloud monitoring giant from New York, emphasizing the power of bottom-up adoption, obsessive customer feedback loops, and the urgent necessity of moving faster on hiring and firing.

Background & Origin Story

  • Roots in New York: Graduated from Centrale in 1999, did an internship at IBM Research in upstate New York, and stayed through the dot-com boom, bust, and September 11th.
  • The Educational Software Precursor: Worked with co-founder Alexis at an educational software startup that grew to roughly 800 people, where Olivier led dev and Alexis led tech ops.
  • The DevOps Insight: Experienced firsthand the friction between developers and operations teams; realized bringing DevOps together into one platform was the missing puzzle piece.
  • The YC Rejection: Applied to Y Combinator and was rejected by Paul Graham (received a rejection email, and later an accidental invite retraction); framed the rejection email as motivation.
  • Painful Early Fundraising: Angel round was super painful; New York VCs didn't understand the competitive landscape, and Valley VCs thought it was a "form of mental impairment" to start an infrastructure company outside the Bay Area.

Product Strategy & Cloud Tailwinds

  • Riding the Cloud Explosion: Started in 2010 when companies dismissed AWS as a "toy"; targeting small, modern, cloud-first companies proved to be the ultimate tailwind.
  • Category Naming: Shifted initial descriptions from a vague "data platform for DevOps collaboration" to Infrastructure Monitoring, which immediately resonated with buyers and bosses.
  • Portfolio Expansion: Grew to 20–25 products, driven by observing customer extensions, internal workflows, and forward-looking "strategic" bets.
  • The AI Shift: The company serves the top 10 AI companies in the world alongside traditional enterprises (banks, train companies); building observability for agents and baking intelligence directly into the data plane.

Culture, Leadership, and Staying Grounded

  • No Written Culture Document: Refuses to put core values on the wall, believing culture flows directly from the top through everyday actions, hiring, and firing.
  • Reading Raw Feedback: Continually samples actual support requests, sales transcripts, customer feedback, and employee survey comments rather than relying on polished executive summaries.
  • Challenging the Chain of Command: Randomly drops questions into Slack or email threads regarding low-level complaints, forcing management to stay dialed into ground-level realities.
  • Public Markets Reality: Went public on March 2019; the lock-up expiration coincided with the COVID lockdown day market crash, causing the stock to drop roughly 65% (from a $10B valuation down to ~$4B) before rebounding.

AI Impact & Engineering Transformation

  • No-Code Directive: Co-founder Alexis told the engineering team that within two quarters, they won't be writing code anymore.
  • Hack Project Evidence: Over the holidays in December, excellent developers independently rebuilt whole systems in a couple of days that would have previously required a team of six over six months.
  • Organizational Agility: Shifted the organization to take more shots, move faster, and accept being wrong more often because smaller teams can now explore ideas previously requiring massive headcount.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • AI-Driven Code Generation: "In two quarters, we're not writing any, any code anymore" — Olivier Pomel (citing his co-founder's directive on the inversion of software development).

Next-generation surgical robots are shifting toward data-centric, Ethernet-based architectures that use AI foundational models, real-time edge compute, and agentic workflows to improve surgical efficiency and enable advanced autonomy.

State-of-the-Art vs. Data-Centric Architectures

  • Current Surgical Robotic Limitations:
    • Today's systems rely heavily on Level 1 autonomy (teleoperated robots) and closed, proprietary cabling/protocols between components.
    • Distributed setups create massive complexity and maintenance overhead when moving between product generations.
  • The Data-Centric Shift:
    • Modeled after autonomous vehicles and next-gen humanoids, future robots use a centralized data-center-on-wheels approach over unified Ethernet.
    • Unified I/O & RDMA: The Holoscan sensor bridge (free and open-source FPGA IP) converts signals (CSI MIPI, SPI, I2C, GPIO) to Ethernet, enabling Remote Direct Memory Access (RDMA) straight to GPU memory.
    • Low-Latency Performance: Achieves speed-of-light implementations down to 1.2ms from sensor input to output controller (compared to ~50ms in legacy systems).

Edge Computing and Compute Requirements

  • IGX Thor Platform: High-end edge compute (IGX Thor T7000) delivers over 5,000 Teraflops of FP4 for inference, 128GB of memory, and 400 Gbps of Ethernet bandwidth (double the previous generation's 700 Gbps, with 800 Gbps targeted next).
  • 3-Computer Solution Workflow: Integrates training, simulation, and deployment into a continuous flywheel that uses the same software brain in both digital twins and physical systems.

Datasets and Next-Generation AI Models

  • Open-H Embodiment Coalition:
    • Built with luminary partners (including Axel Krieger at JHU and Nasir Navab at TUM), growing from 3 to 35 partner organizations across industry, academia, and healthcare.
    • Features 8 robotic embodiments spanning simulation, table-top, ex-vivo, animal, and clinical data (e.g., CMR Surgical donated nearly 500 hours of kinematics and video data).
    • Hosted on Hugging Face as a 4-terabyte dataset, achieving over 1,000 downloads in its first few days.
  • Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Models:
    • *Classical VLAs:* Built on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and fine-tuned on task data (e.g., JHU's SutureBot) to perform end-to-end tasks like suturing.
    • *World Foundation Model VLAs:* Newer architectures use generative World Foundation Models (like those from Semaphor Surgical) for kinematics and video generation, outperforming classical VLAs.
  • Cosmos-H Surgical Simulator:
    • Open-sourced model driven by robot kinematics and ground-truth frames to simulate 8 different embodiments.
    • Runs at 12 frames per second on a single RTX Pro 6000 GPU (with real-time distillation underway).
  • Real-Time Telesurgery Tokenizers: Neural tokenizers encode surgical video into latent space, rivaling traditional JPEG compression quality.

Smart Hospitals and Agentic Systems

  • Operating Room Economics & Pressures:
    • OR time costs $46 per minute in the US across over 50,000 ORs.

TL;DR

OpenAI introduced the GPT-5.6 family (Sol, Terra, and Luna) and shifted the developer focus from "token-maxing" (measuring progress by AI volume) to "value-maxxing" (measuring outcomes and cost-performance). 1. Shift from Token-Maxing to Value-Maxxing

  • The Token-Maxing Trap:
    • Defined by measuring progress through raw AI volume (tokens burned, prompts sent, concurrent agents managed).
    • Led to companies burning through annual AI budgets in months and enterprises throttling spend.
  • The Value-Maxxing Philosophy:
    • Core Definition: Measuring progress by what AI accomplishes—outcomes, time saved, and artifact quality.
    • Strategic Spending: Spending more tokens is justified if it drives proportional value (e.g., higher reasoning levels for quality, faster time-to-completion for migrations, scaling proven workflows, or using LLMs-as-a-judge for risk management).

2. GPT-5.6 Model Family & Codex Best Practices (Charlie Guo - DevEx, OpenAI)

  • Model Tiering:
    • Sol: Flagship frontier model for complex coding and professional tasks.
    • Terra: Balanced daily driver for workloads balancing intelligence, cost, and latency.
    • Luna: High-volume workloads prioritizing low cost and fast latency over deep intelligence.
  • Codex Optimization Tips:
    • Reasoning Level Rule of Thumb: Start with Sol on Medium rather than defaulting to extra-high/Max for routine day-to-day work.
    • Speed vs. Tokens: Turn on Fast mode to run outputs at 1.5x speed (consuming limits faster to save human time).
    • Permissions & Memory: Use auto-approval (avoiding full-access security risks while bypassing per-action permission fatigue) and Chronicle (screen recording memory for repeating tasks).
    • Prompt Dieting: Audit `AGENTS.md` and skill instructions to remove outdated, verbose constraints and save input tokens.

3. API Harness Features for Cost-Performance (Charlie Guo & Team)

  • Programmatic Tool Calling:
    • Gives the model a JavaScript sandbox to write and execute code, call tools, and run computations.
    • Savings: Removes math/logic from the model's chain of thought, saving 24% fewer input tokens and cutting an entire model round-trip.
  • Prompt Caching & Persistence:
    • Placement Strategy: Append dynamic variables (like the current date) to the *end* of prompts so the large static prefix remains cacheable.

    • Persistent Reasoning: Preserves reasoning terms across turns, boosting capability and cache efficiency.
  • Context Compaction:
    • Triggering auto-compaction on long conversation histories yielded 82% fewer input tokens (dropping from 24,000 to just over 4,000 tokens on a test query).

Data Center Evolution: From Copper to Optics

  • The Copper Wall: As speeds and bandwidths increase inside data centers, the reach and capability of copper wiring decreases due to electrical resistance and heat.
  • The Physics Advantage of Light: Optics eliminates resistance, allowing data to transmit over longer distances with far less loss compared to electrical signaling.
  • The Four-Stage Data Center Framework:
    • Scale Across (Kilometers): The historic internet backbone connecting data centers; now operating at full bandwidth over kilometers to support massive inferencing models.
    • Scale Out (Meters): Connecting compute racks to switch banks using pluggable optical transceivers (roughly 10× the optical lanes of scale across).
    • Scale Up (Inside the Rack / Centimeters to Meters): Replacing internal rack copper links with optics to handle 1.6 terabits per second speeds; represents a 100× market expansion relative to scale across.
    • Scale In (Millimeters / On-Chip & PCB): Future transition to replace copper traces on printed circuit boards and disaggregate high-bandwidth memory from compute using optical interconnects (representing a 1,000× market scale).

Optical Component Supply Chain & Manufacturing Challenges

  • Scale of Production Shift: The optical industry historically served telecom customers (AT&T, Verizon) requiring hundreds of lasers; AI data centers now demand hundreds of millions of lasers.
  • Indium Phosphide Fab Constraints:
