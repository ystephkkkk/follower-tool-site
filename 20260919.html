Secretary of War Pete Hegseth visited Saronic Technologies in Austin, Texas, on September 18, 2026, to praise the defense tech company's rapid scaling of autonomous maritime vessels and to highlight the Department of War's broader push to break through Pentagon bureaucracy by prioritizing commercial speed, non-traditional innovators, and domestic manufacturing.

Saronic Technologies: Scale, Facilities, and Economic Impact

  • Austin Headquarters & Manufacturing:
    • Scaled to 1,200 Texas employees operating 540,000 square feet across Austin, with maritime trials conducted in Galveston.
    • Projected to generate over $260 billion in economic impact for Texas.
  • Port of Brownsville (Port Alpha):
    • Represents a $3 billion investment.
    • Creates up to 10,000 high-paying jobs and $160 billion in regional impact for Cameron County.
    • Delivers 150,000 gross tons of new shipbuilding capacity, which will double domestic shipbuilding.
  • Louisiana Expansion:
    • Involves a $300+ million investment to modernize the historic Gulf Craft Shipyard in Franklin and establish a new engineering hub in New Orleans.
    • Creates over 1,500 high-paying maritime careers combining historic craft with robotics.
  • Supply Chain Footprint:
    • The Corsair USV is manufactured entirely with American materials and hands sourced through a network of 94 American suppliers across 27 American states.

Combat Deployments & Technological Milestones

  • First Combat Search and Rescue (CSAR):
    • In June, two U.S. Army AG-64 Apache pilots went down in contested waters near Oman.
    • Saronic's 24-foot autonomous Corsair USV (built in Austin) navigated forward and safely rescued the two pilots, marking the first known combat search and rescue mission executed by an autonomous vessel in American history.
  • First Sea Drone Combat Strike:
    • In July, CENTCOM employed the Corsair to strike a submarine and ship maintenance facility at the Bandar-e Abbas naval base in the Persian Gulf, marking the first operational employment of sea drones in U.S. military combat operations and degrading Iran's naval capabilities.
  • Vessel Portfolio & Production Speed:
    • Delivered over 300 combat-proven Corsairs.
    • Rapidly fielded the 52-foot Mirage and scaled blue-water reach with the 180-foot Marauder, all built in under a year.

Department of War Strategy & Acquisition Reform

  • War on Pentagon Bureaucracy:
    • Secretary Pete Hegseth and Chief Technology Officer Emil Michael are working to dismantle the legacy defense oligarchy, open up building access to fast runners, and move from bureaucracy to business.
  • Three Department Pillars:
    • Reviving the warrior ethos (focusing strictly on lethality over social engineering/DEI).
    • Rebuilding the military via generational funding investments.
    • Re-establishing deterrence through decisive hard power and sinking narco-terrorist boats rather than catch-and-release.
  • Procurement Shifts:
    • Providing larger, longer, and more predictable contracts to companies that deliver at pace and scale while investing in workers instead of stock buybacks.

Workforce & Talent Pipeline

  • Veteran Leadership & Integration:
    • Founded and operated by Navy SEAL veteran and CEO Dino Mavrakis (eight combat tours) and Marine veteran Rob Layman.
    • Leverages the Department's SkillBridge program and Service Academies to create a premier talent pipeline.
  • Industrial Workforce Transition:
    • Actively transitioning skilled civilian American welders and machinists from the automotive and aerospace industries into cutting-edge naval shipbuilding.

Bill Gurley examines historical catastrophes (like the 737 MAX, Fukushima, and Challenger) to demonstrate how society successfully investigates failures through independent searchers and root-cause analysis—and contrasts this with the systemic failure to uncover the origins of COVID-19.

Catastrophic Investigations & The CAPA Framework

  • The Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) Framework: A five-step scientific process used in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, and aerospace (Detect, Investigate, Confirm root cause, Correct, Prevent) where skipping root-cause analysis makes prevention impossible.
  • Surfside, Florida Condo Collapse (June 2021):
    • Casualties & Cost: 98 deaths.
    • Investigation: Four parallel investigations, including NIST, Alan Kilsheimer, a Florida grand jury, 17 consolidated victim lawsuits, and the *Miami Herald* (staffing 37 people, winning a Pulitzer Prize).
    • Outcome: Led to major rule changes in Florida and coastal states to prevent future collapses.
  • Boeing 737 MAX Crashes (Oct 2018 / Mar 2019):
    • Root Cause: A fuel-efficient engine was attached forward on an older airframe, causing an imbalance; secret software called MCAS pushed the plane down during sensor malfunctions.
    • Investigation: Six search groups included US Senate/House commissions, the DOJ, Indonesia's KNKT, *Seattle Times* reporter Dominic Gates (Pulitzer winner), 57 whistleblowers, and the pilots' union (who recorded meetings and filed FOIA requests).
    • Blockers: Boeing and the FAA acted as blockers, initially blaming pilots and refusing document requests; 45 days after the first crash, the plane was still not grounded, leading to a preventable second crash.
  • Hurricane Katrina (2005):
    • Casualties & Cost: 1,800 deaths, 400,000 displaced, $125 billion in damage.
    • Root Cause: Levees washed out from underneath during a Category 3 hurricane due to man-made engineering errors by the US Army Corps of Engineers.
    • Investigation: House/Senate commissions, Pulitzer-winning journalists (Mark Schleifstein and John McQuaid), NSF-backed UC professors, and an LSU team.
    • Blockers: The Army Corps of Engineers attempted to block investigations and encapsulated the American Society of Civil Engineers (ASCE) after paying them $1 million.
    • Outcome: Led to 90 recommendations and $15 billion in sea walls that have held for 20 years.
  • Fukushima Nuclear Disaster (2011):
    • Casualties & Cost: 2,200 deaths, 154,000 displaced, $200 billion in cleanup.
    • Root Cause: A seawall of 5.7 meters was overtopped by tsunamis (internal reports showed expected tsunamis of 10-15 meters were buried).
    • Investigation: Led by Dr. Kyoshi Kurokawa heading Japan's first-ever private parliamentary commission with zero institutional ties to regulators or the power company.
  • Space Shuttle Challenger Disaster (January 1986):
    • Root Cause: O-rings on solid rocket boosters failed in cold weather.
    • Searcher: Caltech physicist Richard Feynman served on the Rogers Commission, cleared his schedule for six months, conducted independent investigations, and famously demonstrated the O-ring failure with ice water on live television.

COVID-19 Origins & The Absence of Investigation

  • Scale of Impact: 15 million deaths, $45 trillion in global cost, and severe learning loss for children—making COVID-19 3,000 times larger in cost and death toll than the combined five historical catastrophes ($349B, 5,000 deaths).
