TL;DR

Circle reported a strong Q2 2026, driven by 19% year-over-year growth in USDC circulation to $73.3 billion, a record 70% share of stablecoin transaction volume in June, and the advancement of major infrastructure milestones including the launch of the Circle National Trust Bank and the upcoming ARC mainnet.

Financial Results & Business Performance

  • Circulation & Minting Metrics:
    • USDC Circulation: Ended Q2 2026 at $73.3 billion, up 19% year-over-year, while average circulation hit an all-time high of $76.5 billion.
    • Minting & Redemption: Daily minting and redemption averaged $1.9 billion in Q2 (up 105% year-over-year), with total quarterly mint/redeem volume reaching $170 billion.
    • Platform Holdings: USDC held within Circle's platform infrastructure grew 106% year-over-year to $12.4 billion (17% of total circulation). Coinbase held 30% and Hyperliquid accounted for approximately 6% of circulation at quarter end.
  • Revenue & Profitability:
    • Total Revenue & Reserve Income: Reached $71 million, up 7% year-over-year, supported by circulation growth and other revenue, though partially offset by a lower reserve return rate of 3.48% (down 66 bps year-on-year due to SOFR declines).
    • Other Revenue: Reached $34 million (up 1.4x year-over-year), driven by blockchain partnerships, though down $8 million sequentially due to moderating blockchain revenue and prioritization of ARC.
    • Margins: Revenue less distribution cost margin was 41.2% (up 3 percentage points year-over-year). Adjusted EBITDAR grew 8% year-over-year to $143 million with a 50% margin.
  • Other Digital Asset Growth:
    • EURC: Grew 2.2x year-over-year, maintaining its position as the largest digital euro.
    • USYC: Grew 10x year-over-year to over $3 billion in assets, remaining the world's largest tokenized money market fund.

Network Expansion & Strategic Partnerships

  • Coinbase Partnership Renewal: The strategic agreement with Coinbase has been renewed on its existing terms, ensuring USDC remains central across all Coinbase products.
  • Regulatory Milestones: Circle received its OCC National Trust Bank Charter and an additional limited purpose trust charter from the state of New York, establishing the Circle National Trust as an infrastructure bank for the internet financial system.
  • ARC Network Launch & Partnerships:
    • Mainet Launch: Set to launch on September 16, 2026; testnet has processed over half a billion transactions across ~3 million wallets with near-perfect uptime, backed by a private mainet of over 100 active partners.
    • DTCC Collaboration: Partnering to bring tokenized securities to ARC, starting with DTC-custodied assets and expanding to repo, collateral mobility, securities lending, and corporate actions.
    • BlackRock Integration: BlackRock plans to deploy BUIDL on ARC with native USDC integration to enable institutional fund management within a single onchain environment.
  • Circle Payments Network (CPN):
    • Volume Growth: Reached nearly $15 billion in annualized total payment volume (TPV) on a trailing 30-day basis at the end of Q2; further accelerated to $23 billion as of July 31st (130% growth since the last earnings report).
    • Adoption: Financial institution enrollment grew nearly 30% quarter-over-quarter to 175 institutions, expanding CPN's reach to more than 58 countries.

Market Trends & Trading Infrastructure

  • Derivatives & Real-World Assets (RWAs): On major venues like Binance and Hyperliquid, USDC's share of open interest collateral reached 40%. Notably, tokenized real-world assets reached nearly 75% of Hyperliquid's traded volume as of late Q2.
  • Prediction Markets: Spot volume on Polymarket (a USDC distribution partner) grew over 8x year-over-year, with open interest growing more than 4x year-over-year.

Agentic Finance & Corporate AI Transformation

  • Ecosystem Traction: The USDC network and payment protocols like X42 handle 99.3% of agentic payments, with over 900 paid services available in Circle's agent marketplace. Cloudflare recently added agentic wallets with X42 and USDC support.
  • Internal AI Operations: 86% of Circle employees are weekly active users of AI tools; employees shipped over 1,100 AI apps in 2026 (mostly non-technical builders), driving software development velocity up several hundred percent.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Through-Cycle Growth Framework: Circle believes a 40% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) over several years through cycle is achievable, aligning with third-party projections for the 2030 stablecoin market ($1 trillion to $4 trillion, or 27% to 77% CAGRs) — Jeremy Fox-Geen
  • Other Revenue Guidance: Raised the full-year other revenue guidance range to $310 million to $330 million (up from $150–$170 million), driven by ARC pre-sale revenues — Jeremy Fox-Geen
  • ARC Milestone Recognition: Circle expects to achieve approximately 75% of ARC product milestones and recognize associated revenue in 2026, including $180 million from the $242 million ARC token pre-sale held in Q2 — Jeremy Fox-Geen
  • RLDC Margin Outlook: Raised the full-year revenue less distribution cost (RLDC) margin outlook range to 41.7% to 43.7% (up from 38% to 40%), reflecting ARC revenue contributions — Jeremy Fox-Geen
  • Adjusted Operating Expenses: Reaffirmed the full-year guidance range of $570 million to $585 million, while noting expectations to land at the higher end of the range due to continued platform investments — Jeremy Fox-Geen
  • CPN Monetization: Monetization of the Circle Payments Network (CPN) will begin in the second half of 2026 — Jeremy Allaire

TL;DR

Starcloud is building space-based data centers to bypass terrestrial energy and regulatory constraints, achieving a billion-dollar valuation just 17 months after YC demo day. Founded by Philip Johnston, the company successfully trained the first large language model in space in November 2025 and has filed with the FCC to deploy 88,000 satellites.

Origin Story & Strategic Pivot

  • Founding Background: Philip Johnston founded Starcloud on January 1, 2024, after recognizing plummeting launch costs during a 2023 visit to Starbase Texas where SpaceX was building gigafactories to produce up to three Starships per day.
  • Initial Concept: The company initially launched as *Lumin orbit* in January 2024 to pursue space-based solar power, inspired by Isaac Asimov and 1960s sci-fi concepts.
  • The Pivot to Data Centers: After discovering that space-based solar power loses 95% of energy during transmission from space to Earth, Johnston and the team recalculated break-even costs. While space-based solar required a drop to $50 per kilo, data centers made economic sense at a break-even launch cost of $500 per kilo, prompting an immediate pivot.
  • Rejected Concepts: The team evaluated and rejected asteroid mining, space manufacturing, and space hotels because they require expensive and complex re-entry processes, whereas data centers do not.

Starcloud-1 Mission & Engineering Breakthroughs

  • The Launch Strategy: On January 2, 2024 (the day after founding), Starcloud booked the first available SpaceX rideshare launch as a forcing function, 18 to 21 months out, before knowing exactly what payload they would fly.
  • Starcloud-1 Metrics: Launched in November 2025, the mission cost $2 million total (compared to prime contractor quotes of $75M to $100M). The team of 12 traveled to Florida to watch it lift off at midnight.
  • Hardware on Orbit: Starcloud-1 carried five GPUs (three from Nvidia, two from ARM), including an Nvidia H100.
  • Thermal Management Innovation: To solve the extreme power density and heat of the H100 in a vacuum, Starcloud submerged all components, power delivery systems, and memory in a phase change material for immersion cooling.
  • Pre-Launch Crunch: Facing a tight deadline without time to reserve a proper vacuum thermal chamber, co-founder Ezra built a makeshift thermal cycle test using ice baths and hair dryers/hot air guns at 5:00 AM on launch day.
  • On-Orbit Commissioning: Following deployment (featured on a 5-story screen by Jensen Huang at GTC 2025), the satellite required 12 hours for first contact and a grueling 3-day troubleshooting cycle to resolve a repeating restart loop triggered by one of 20 failure modes.
  • First Space Milestones: Starcloud successfully trained the first large language model in space, ran the first version of Gemini, fine-tuned a model on orbit, and performed high-power inference on satellite imagery.

Roadmap & Constellation Plans

  • Starcloud-2 (Next 20 Satellites): A 10-kilowatt spacecraft equipped with three SpaceX Starlink laser terminals for high bandwidth and low latency. It will fly a Bitcoin mining ASIC, more H100s, Blackwell chips, and AWS Outposts hardware (on-premises server blades for local VC2 instances, tailored for military customers).
  • Starcloud-3 and Beyond: A 200-kilowatt, 3-ton spacecraft measuring about 6 meters long. Fifty of these can fit inside a single Starship, generating approximately 10 megawatts of new compute capacity per flight.
  • FCC Filing: Starcloud has filed with the FCC for a constellation of 88,000 satellites, projected to provide roughly 20 gigawatts of new compute capacity.

Engineering Challenges & Hardware Trade-Offs

  • Thermal Dissipation: Split 50/50 with radiation, the primary engineering hurdle is heat rejection in a vacuum. Starcloud designed a deployable liquid-cooled radiator that is 10 times less mass per watt and 500 times less cost per watt than the International Space Station's radiator.
  • Radiation Hardening: Rather than using expensive space-graded components, Starcloud uses off-the-shelf automotive-style parts. They conduct rigorous heavy-ion testing at Brookhaven National Laboratory and high-velocity proton testing at the Knoxville cyclotron to expose hardware to 5-year mission doses in 24 hours.
  • DC-to-DC Architecture: Solar panels generate direct current (DC) immediately, eliminating energy losses from traditional AC-to-DC conversions.

Fundraising & Market Dynamics

  • Initial Rejection: The seed round in late 2023 sought $2 million at a $10 million post-money valuation on a SAFE. It took three months, and roughly 100 VCs rejected the idea as too sci-fi. After YC Demo Day, they faced at least 20 more rejections before securing their first check.
  • Benchmark Partnership: Starcloud became Benchmark's first-ever space investment. Chathan led the round after conducting deep technical due diligence on the engineering team. The round faced complications mid-process when SpaceX privately indicated they were exploring similar concepts, triggering conflict-of-interest policies for multiple VCs.
  • The Hard Tech Shift: Johnston notes a rapid public market sentiment shift against traditional SaaS moats (accelerated by tools like Claude code), which quickly translated into investor appetite for deep tech and hard tech.
  • Hiring Rigor: Despite raising significant capital (including a large round following their March announcement), the company maintains a tight-knit engineering team of only 20 engineers and one commercial lead from the Space Force (formerly working on Golden Dome), deliberately moving slowly and selectively on hires.

TL;DR

Radiant is developing portable, factory-built nuclear microreactors called Kaleidos to provide clean, reliable, and accessible power for remote communities, data centers, national security needs, and space exploration. By shifting from on-site construction to mass production in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, the company aims to eliminate the high costs and logistical complexities that have historically stalled the nuclear industry.

The State of Energy and the Nuclear Industry

  • Aging and Strained Grid:
    • The current U.S. power grid was largely built in the 1960s and 1970s and is increasingly showing its age.
    • High Outage Rate: Approximately 45% of Americans have experienced at least one power outage in the past year, which can be catastrophic for businesses.
    • Energy Deficit Risk: Society currently runs near the median of required power, leaving many states dipping below energy needs during peak times and stifling innovation.
  • Historical Decline of Nuclear:
    • Lost Momentum: The U.S. built 52 nuclear reactors at Idaho National Laboratory over a 21-year span, but the last one was completed in 1977, and none have been built there since.
    • Regulatory Overburden: Nuclear energy was heavily sidelined by environmental misinformation and a mounting stack of regulations, effectively pricing the technology out of business.
    • On-Site Construction Flaws: Traditional nuclear projects require massive holes dug in the ground, causing unpredictable costs, timeline overruns, and permanent radiological designation for the site.

The Kaleidos Microreactor Product & Factory Model

  • The Kaleidos Design:
    • Size and Portability: A transportable microreactor that fits on the back of an 18-wheeler and can be moved by land, air, and sea.
    • Diesel Generator Analogy: Comparable in size to a diesel generator, but delivers many times more power and operates for 5 years without needing refueling.
    • Daisy-Chaining Capability: Four Kaleidos units can be linked together to power a small city or a very large data center.
  • Frictionless Customer Experience:
    • Apple-Like Simplicity: Built to eliminate customer friction by handling all burdens—including construction, interconnects, and waste management.
    • Return-to-Factory Refueling: After a 5-year operational window, the unit is shipped back to Radiant’s factory in Oak Ridge, Tennessee, for refueling, ensuring customers never have to deal with waste on site.
  • Factory-Built Advantage:
    • Cost Control: Moving from construction projects to factory mass production removes on-site uncertainties and shifts financial risk into the manufacturing process, turning reactors into predictable commodities.
    • Target Scale: The long-term vision is to produce one nuclear reactor per week off a standard production line.

Origins, National Security, and Expanding Use Cases

  • Space Exploration Roots:
    • SpaceX Heritage: Founder Doug Bernauer worked at SpaceX for about 12 years on the Mars challenge to make life multilanetary.
    • The Energy Bottleneck: Realizing that solar power on Mars was insufficient to make fuel and return via Starship, Bernauer determined that nuclear power was the only viable solution, leading him to found Radiant.
  • National Security & Energy Independence:
    • Avoiding Foreign Dependencies: Countries relying on adversaries for energy have strings attached that can cause entire nations to shut down during conflicts.
    • Rapid Deployment: Microreactors can be deployed rapidly alongside AI infrastructure, drones, or military operations where grid power is unavailable.
  • Novel Use Cases:
    • Providing power in boxes that fit in a few parking spaces unlocks entirely new applications, such as high-altitude camps on Mount Everest or cities on the bottom of the ocean.

Upcoming Milestones and Prototype Testing

  • Idaho National Laboratory Partnership:
    • Historic Location: Radiant is taking its unit to a dome structure built in the 1950s at Idaho National Laboratory that originally housed the Experimental Breeder Reactor and was slated for teardown.
    • Competitive Selection: Radiant was selected through a competitive process as the first company to test in this repurposed facility.
    • Testing Plan: Since 2020, the company has targeted 2026 to bring the system to the dome, go critical, reach temperatures over 700°C, and run continuously for 150 hours at full power.
  • Regulatory Support: Executive orders signed by the President in May of the prior year accelerated the entire process, supporting Radiant in the race to turn on the world's first commercial microreactor.
