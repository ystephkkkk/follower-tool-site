TL;DR

The bond market is flashing red with 10-year Treasury yields hitting a 19-year high above 5.1%, driven by stubborn inflation, massive national debt, and heavy capital demand from AI investments. Meanwhile, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East have driven Brent crude above $103 per barrel, and AI data center provider SB Energy has delayed its $50 billion IPO due to weak investor demand.

Market Vitals and Bond Market Pressures

  • Market Indices & Yield Spikes: Major indices declined as US treasuries sold off, with the 10-year Treasury yield rising above 5.1% (a 19-year high), the 30-year yield returning to 2004 levels, and the 5-year yield breaching 5%.
  • Federal Reserve Rate Hike Odds: On Cali, the odds of another rate hike next month rose to 66%, and the odds of a third hike later in the year rose to 34%.
  • Treasury Intervention: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessant executed a $6 billion long-dated bond buyback program with another $6 billion operation scheduled, which Howard Marks criticized as a cosmetic fix unable to solve structurally unsound economic problems.
  • Historical Interest Rates (Howard Marks): Today's 5.3% to 5.4% 30-year bond yields are historically low rather than high, with the recent era of zero interest rates (2009–2021) representing an aberration.

Drivers of Rising Bond Yields & Deficits

  • Stubborn Inflation: Inflation has consistently remained above the Federal Reserve's 2% target for the last five years, creating a need for purchasing power protection.
  • National Debt & Deficits (Howard Marks): The US national debt has reached $40 trillion, running an annual deficit approaching $2 trillion during a period of economic prosperity and low unemployment.
  • Competing Capital Demand: AI is drawing hundreds of billions of dollars from the debt market, actively competing with the US Treasury for capital.
  • International Comparison: Japan has run massive debts for a long time without acute financial market failures, but US fiscal profligacy ("golden credit card" behavior) risks long-term currency debasement and a demand for higher risk compensation from foreign investors.

Geopolitical Tensions & Energy Markets

  • UN General Assembly Standoffs: President Trump stated he could annihilate the Islamic Republic quickly, while Iran's President Masud Peskian asserted they would never bow their head or bend at the knee.
  • Middle East Energy Impact: The Strait of Hormuz remains closed and US blockades continue against Iranian ports, pushing Brent crude above $103 per barrel (up over 40% since the war began).
  • Global Food and Energy Vulnerability (Daniel Baer): The combination of the Iran war (driving up diesel and transport costs) and paralyzed Black Sea wheat exports from Russia and Ukraine creates a "double whammy" threatening millions with high food prices or food shortages.
  • Midterm Elections Impact (Daniel Baer): Political pressures surrounding the US midterms influence bargaining incentives, with adversaries and partners closely watching the vulnerability of an American president.

SB Energy IPO Delay

  • Valuation & Banker Struggles: AI data center provider SB Energy delayed its IPO this month after bankers struggled to find buyers at the target $50 billion valuation (making it purportedly more valuable than PayPal, Honda, or Chipotle).
  • Operational Reality: The company currently has zero data centers in operation; less than 10% of its planned data centers are under construction, while over 90% have not broken ground.
  • Revenue Backlog Discrepancies: While claiming a revenue backlog of over $400 billion, only $1 billion is expected within the next 2 years, over 80% is projected past 8 years from now, and some portions extend past 20 years.
  • Shrinking Core Business: SB Energy's existing revenue primarily stems from its solar business, which saw revenues fall more than 8% last year.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Interest Rate Trajectory: Interest rates will probably not go down much, if at all, in the coming year or two (*Howard Marks*).
  • Energy Cost Projections: Even if the Strait of Hormuz were fully reopened, added insurance costs and tolling mechanisms mean energy prices and crude oil are unlikely to return to pre-war levels in the medium term (*Daniel Baer*).

TL;DR

IFS CEO Mark Moffat and Bank of America Managing Director Thomas Köhrer discussed how industrial AI is transforming asset-heavy, mission-critical sectors like manufacturing, aerospace, and utilities, emphasizing that true deployment requires a balance of probabilistic AI reasoning and deterministic safety standards where failure is not an option.

Speakers & Roles

  • Mark Moffat — CEO, IFS (Industrial software executive with a 28-year background spanning 24 years in tech services and 4 years at IFS)
  • Thomas Köhrer — Managing Director, Bank of America (Panel moderator and discussion lead)

IFS Company Profile & Strategy

  • Asset-Heavy Focus: IFS has a 40-year history supporting capital-intensive, asset-heavy industries across the asset and service lifecycle, including utilities, manufacturing, aerospace, defense, service-based industries, mining, oil, and gas.
  • Scale & Retention: The company operates with a 96% Gross Retention Rate (GRR) and supports 100% of commercial aviation flights in the US, carrying 800 million passengers annually.
  • Forward Deployed Engineering: Pre-sales teams and developers spend extensive time on-site in warehouses and field ride-alongs to identify pinch points, leakage, and cost optimization opportunities in complex industrial value chains.

Industrial AI vs. Enterprise AI

  • Penetration Gap: While software engineering, legal, finance, and customer contact centers have heavily benefited from frontier models, industrial value chains remain relatively less penetrated.
  • Deterministic vs. Probabilistic: Due to strict health, safety, and operational standards (e.g., jet engine maintenance requiring 99.99999% reliability), industrial AI must bridge probabilistic AI thinking with deterministic execution.

AI Progress & Global Competition

  • Moralizing Technology: Thiel criticizes the tendency to moralize technology rather than viewing it functionally, arguing that tools are neutral and that societies focus too much on good-versus-evil debates while tolerating 99% incompetence.

Politics, Democracy & Polarization

Company Overview & Structure

    • Aligns the duration of capital with sustained operational commitments and end-to-end transformation.
