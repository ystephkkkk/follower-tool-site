TL;DR

AI and automation will ultimately grow 100x larger than the internet, completely disrupting human-computer interaction and physical labor over the next five years. Brett Adcock—founder of Figure.ai, Hark, and Cover—shares insights on building frontier companies in humanoid robotics and digital AI-human symbiosis.

The State of Humanoid Robotics & Figure.ai

  • The Core Thesis: Brett Adcock notes that Figure.ai is building humanoid robots as a single piece of hardware capable of doing everything in the world, addressing the massive global labor shortage and high turnover rates in industrial and logistics settings.
  • Product Milestones:
    • Figure recently manufactured its 1,000th EVT robot for Figure 3.
    • Robots are actively deployed with commercial customers like BMW, handling logistics and package sorting.
    • A recent live stream demonstrated a Figure robot sorting packages off a conveyor belt at human speeds (2.9 seconds per package) for 200 hours straight.
  • Why Humanoids over Quadruped Robots: Adcock argues that humanoid robots are only about 3x to 4x harder to build than four-legged dog robots, but offer a million times higher TAM (Total Addressable Market) and ROI.
  • The Home Robotics Frontier: While commercial logistics provides near-term revenue, Adcock spends 3 to 4 hours daily, 7 days a week, working on the harder problem of making robots operate autonomously in new domestic environments they have never seen before (e.g., folding laundry in varying lighting and table heights).

Hark and the Post-iPhone Era of AI Devices

  • The Digital Symbiosis Thesis: Alongside physical robotics, Adcock founded Hark to build digital AI-human symbiosis—acting as a "Jarvis from Iron Man" style superhuman assistant with near-perfect memory, multi-modal capabilities, and autonomous computer use.
  • Solving Computer Use: Most AI agents rely on APIs or MCPs, but only 1 in 1,000 websites have APIs. Hark solved general-purpose computer use by spinning up a virtual computer sandbox for every agent, allowing them to look at a screen and use a mouse and keyboard autonomously via a custom reinforcement learning process.
  • Reinventing Hardware: Hark is designing next-generation AI devices to replace the iPhone and MacBook, which Adcock calls "complete rubbish for AI."
    • The team features a hardware design leader who worked on the last several generations of iPhones and MacBooks.
    • Adcock dismisses Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses as "horrible" products that fail to operate independently without an iPhone tether.
  • Launch Timeline: Hark will launch traditional browser and mobile app versions in about a month, with sci-fi-level custom hardware currently being tested in the lab.

The Talent Wars & Meta's Aggressive Hiring

  • Extremely Selective Engineering Bar: At Figure, mechanical engineering candidates must pass rigorous design case studies (building actuators, gearboxes, and bearings from scratch). Out of 10 case studies a week for six months, Figure hired zero candidates.
  • Meta Buying the AI Race: Meta has been aggressively paying top dollar to monopolize the 20 to 30 people in California who know how to build advanced AI pre-training models.
    • Senior AI infra engineers are receiving offers from Meta worth $36 million in RSUs over four years of guaranteed cash.
    • Junior engineers in their late 20s are commanding total compensation packages between $750k and $2 million per year.
  • Adcock's Take on Meta's Strategy: While Meta's mercenaries are purely money-driven, Adcock respects the execution and admits he would have done the same if he were Mark Zuckerberg to buy into the AI race. However, he prefers a smaller, mission-driven team of 20 to 30 people.

Cover: Re-engineering K-12 School Safety

  • Pivoting the Tech Stack: Cover aims to stop school shootings by deploying full-scale weapon-detection scanning systems. Adcock pivoted the entire technology stack a year ago to design custom silicon chips in Europe, reducing the price by 90% and making it vastly more scalable.
  • Leadership: The company is led by a chief engineer from NASA's JPL.
  • Timeline: The team is building out the full-scale system in October 2024 and aims to bring it up before the end of the year, though initial beta testing in a K-12 school might slip by a quarter due to a 6-month silicon fabrication lead time.

Founder Psychology & Operating Frameworks

  • The Three-Bucket Life Strategy: Adcock divides life into three buckets: Work, Family (three young kids), and Social/Travel. To achieve an A+ in work and family, he completely zeroed out the social bucket, turning down even 10-day visits from college roommates.
  • Rock Bottom as a Baseline: During his first startup, *Veterary* (2012–2017), Adcock was severely broke, took out $50k–$100k in personal loans, and operated in negative bank balances before selling the company for $110 million. At Archer, he took out a second mortgage on his house and funded Figure using falling stock lockups.
  • Handling Lows: His mental framework for extreme stress is to build a punch list and take things "day by day," echoing the mindset used when training for ultramarathons (focusing on a tiny distance ahead before considering quitting).
  • The Advantage of Hard Things: Adcock believes working on hard things is easier than easy things because there is less competition, higher investor appetite for binary payouts, and they attract top-tier overachievers.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • General Robotics / Unsupervised Tasks: Humanoid robots will perform unsupervised multi-day tasks in homes they have never seen before, driven entirely by neural networks going straight from pixels to torques — Brett Adcock (Stated as "on track" for 2024 with 4 months remaining).
  • Human-Level Speech Turing Test: By 2027, an AI system will be able to take a phone call and successfully pass a full human Turing test with speech, making it impossible to tell whether you are talking to a human or a robot — Brett Adcock.
  • School Safety Standoff Detection: A full scanning system capable of detecting weapons from a 20-foot standoff will be built and beta-tested in a K-12 school — Brett Adcock (Targeted for 2026, though initial system rollouts begin late 2024).

The AI Boom & Its Potential Achilles' Heel

  • Nvidia's New Financing Deal: Nvidia is partnering with six large asset managers on a $500 billion financing push to treat compute infrastructure like commercial real estate toll roads or borrowable assets (likely involving securitizations). This could improve hyperscaler free cash flow and boost revenue for participating banks and private credit companies.
  • The LLM Dependency Risk (The Achilles' Heel):
    • While hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google) appear financially secure on the surface, their AI revenue is heavily concentrated: 70% of hyperscaler AI revenue and 25% to 35% of total cloud revenue comes from Anthropic and OpenAI.
    • Oracle has a $600 billion backlog, with half originating from OpenAI alone.
    • Both Anthropic and OpenAI currently lose billions and rely on raising capital to survive.
  • Competitive Pressures on LLMs:
    • There are virtually no moats around LLMs, as users switch models constantly.
    • Chinese LLMs are open weight models that are much cheaper, and enterprises are using them increasingly. Overcharging for tokens will be difficult if Chinese AI is "good enough," creating the potential for a destructive price war.
    • Should Anthropic and OpenAI fail, hyperscaler margins will compress, growth will slow, and the entire AI chain could go into reverse.

TL;DR

Driven by its Axon 2 recommendation engine, AppLovin transformed from a struggling post-IPO company into a $100B+ ad tech powerhouse. Co-Founder & CEO Adam Foroughi and CTO Giovanni Ge explain how they achieved this with a remarkably lean engineering team and an AI-first approach.

Company Origin and Lean Philosophy

  • Early Profitability Roots: Adam Foroughi notes that AppLovin started with around 10 people and stayed very lean because early capital was difficult to raise, forcing the company to be profitable from the beginning.
  • Individual Contributor Culture: Adam Foroughi shares that up until CTO Giovanni Ge joined, early lead engineer Basil wrote roughly 60% of the company's code himself, establishing a culture driven by individual contributors rather than heavy meetings, management frameworks, or standard HR processes.
  • Lean Headcount Stability: Adam Foroughi states that the core engineering organization has remained around 100 people and total company headcount around 400 for years, even through periods of massive business expansion.

Re-Architecting the Ad Engine: Axon 2

  • Overcoming Outdated Infrastructure: Adam Foroughi explains that AppLovin was a $5.5B company in late 2022 relying on the outdated Axon 1 model, prompting them to recruit researcher Giovanni Ge to architect Axon 2 just as the stock dropped 30% on earnings day.
  • Semantic Embeddings vs. Trees: Giovanni Ge details how Axon 2 replaced legacy tree-based models (hundreds of thousands of rigid if-else branches that failed to capture dynamic real-world relationships) with learnable semantic embedding tables and deep neural networks.
  • GPU Hardware Optimization: Giovanni Ge notes that neural networks utilize standard Generalized Matrix Multiplications (GEMM), which are highly optimized for modern GPU architecture compared to selection trees, making Axon 2 both more accurate and cheaper to run.
  • The Immersive Ad Funnel: Adam Foroughi highlights that mobile gaming ads function like 60-second interactive mini-games, generating massive engagement data from a user base of roughly 1 billion people before closing the loop at the conversion funnel.

Scaling Beyond Gaming into E-commerce

  • Vertical Expansion: Adam Foroughi and Giovanni Ge explain that a year after scaling Axon 2 in gaming, they realized the core neural architecture was general enough to handle other verticals, successfully launching into e-commerce ads.
  • The Napkin Design Session: Giovanni Ge recounts how CTO Basil and senior leaders sketched the e-commerce data flow engine on a restaurant napkin in Vegas, building the first prototype before returning to San Francisco, resulting in positive ROAS within months.
  • Product-Less Engineering: Adam Foroughi emphasizes that AppLovin operates without a traditional product management organization; instead, competent engineers understand business problems, write their own architecture, and rely on business teams solely for advertiser relationships.

AI Integration and Engineering Management

  • Engineers on Top of AI: Giovanni Ge states his philosophy that engineers should not sit next to AI, but rather *on top of* AI, taking human accountability for code while letting AI handle syntax, completion, and generation.
  • Junior Talent Acceleration: Giovanni Ge and Adam Foroughi criticize the tech industry practice of handholding junior engineers with low-level tasks, noting that AppLovin allows new hires to push code and run live tests within their first week.
  • The Value of Taste: Giovanni Ge argues that as AI commoditizes building, taste—knowing what to build and what *not* to build—becomes the ultimate differentiator, attributing Axon's success largely to what they deliberately decided not to do.

Market Recovery and Future Vision

  • Surviving a 92% Drop: Adam Foroughi details how AppLovin survived a 92% stock decline post-IPO by keeping headcount low to avoid excessive stock-based dilution, halting investor relations, generating strong cash flow, and aggressively buying back their own shares.
  • Evaluating Future Expansion: Adam Foroughi outlines potential 3-to-10-year R&D horizons, such as connected TV and open web video placements, where discovery-based advertising can replicate their success outside of mobile gaming.
  • Path to a Trillion-Dollar Valuation: Adam Foroughi calculates that reaching a trillion-dollar valuation requires scaling free cash flow past $30 billion annually, necessitating continuous expansion beyond gaming into broader consumer and e-commerce markets.

Predictions & Forward Calls

  • Giovanni Ge predicts that while AI currently assists with coding and questioning, human engineers will remain essential for long-horizon planning and making critical architectural decisions for the foreseeable future.
  • Adam Foroughi projects a 5-plus year time horizon for AppLovin to expand its discovery-based advertising model into new spaces like connected TV and open web video placements.
  • Adam Foroughi estimates that to achieve a trillion-dollar valuation, AppLovin must scale its free cash flow to over $30 billion annually by expanding its addressable market beyond mobile gaming into broader consumer verticals.

Susan Kare shares the design history behind the original Apple Macintosh icons, typefaces, and UI elements, detailing lessons learned from Steve Jobs, Paul Rand, and the original Mac team about embracing constraints, iterating constantly, and making ideas meaningful and memorable.

1. Background & Joining Apple

  • Pre-Apple Life: Susan Kare was an art history PhD and artist welding life-sized razorback hogs for a museum in Arkansas before joining Apple.
  • The Recruitment: High school friend and Apple system software programmer Andy Hertzfeld brought Kare in for an interview to design small symbols and typefaces for the new computer.
  • The Interview Prep: Brought typography books and bitmap graph paper drawings (including a pun with a boot and paper) to the interview; that initial interview notebook is now in the permanent collection of MoMA.

2. Designing the Original Macintosh Interface

  • Core Philosophy: Aimed to build a computer anyone could use without a manual, making the technology friendly and understandable.
  • System Constraints: Worked within strict 16x16 pixel black-and-white grids for early icons, proving that creativity thrives within constraints.
  • Typography:
    • Chicago: Designed the 9-pixel cap-height system font using horizontals, verticals, and 45-degree angles to replace jaggy screen text; later used for the first iPod.
    • New York & Geneva: Created to replace working names "Paoli" and "Winwood" after Steve Jobs demanded world-class city names.
    • Ransom: A ransom-note style font inspired by newspaper clippings.
  • Iconographic Mistakes & Lessons:
    • The Bomb Icon: Created a Bugs Bunny-style bomb for total system failure, assuming nobody would ever see it; a user famously called Apple asking if their computer was going to explode.
    • The Print Icon: Featured too much product detail (paper holes); Kare noted it would have had a longer lifespan with simpler, less product-specific design.
  • The Command Key: After Steve Jobs rejected an "Apple farm" of menu shortcuts, Kare found a four-leaf clover/highway cloverleaf symbol in a book representing a "feature" (derived from the 1200 AD Borgholm Castle), which became the Mac command key.

3. Post-Apple Work & Client Projects

  • General Magic: Designed the logo and a literal desktop metaphor with hallways, doors, and text drawers.
  • Facebook Virtual Gifts: Ran a 4-year program selling digital drawings for $1; discovered that "cute trumped everything" (teddy bears, heart shapes, and especially penguins) compared to expensive luxury items.
  • Other Notable Projects: Designed pixelated Amtrak route maps for *The National*, puzzle logos for The Magic Puzzle Company, game assets for *Pikmin Bloom* and *Peridot*, and retro-looking badges for Ledger crypto wallets.

4. Design Advice from Mentors & Collaborators

  • Paul Rand:
    • Advised: *"Always focus on making things meaningful and memorable."*
    • Charged $100,000 for the NeXT logo, demanding: *"You will do one logo, and you will like it."*
    • Advocated uniting the product name with a visual that reinforces it rather than relying on expensive abstract symbols.
  • Steve Jobs:
    • Advised showing multiple options rather than just one so he could evaluate and still pick a direction.
    • Warned not to tell people something is easy because it makes them feel dumb if they struggle; frame it as hard so they feel smart when they figure it out.
    • Put up goals that inspired Steve Caps and Susan Kare to sew a pirate flag with a striped apple eyepatch and fly it from the roof.
  • Alan Kay (via Bud Tribble):
    • Credited with the tenet on user interfaces: *"You could make those fonts because you didn't know you couldn't."*

TL;DR

Travis Kalanick, alongside a16z partners Ben Horowitz and Erik Torenberg, launched Atoms—a company building "industrial AI" systems of software, sensors, robotics, and AI to automate trillion-dollar physical industries like mining, food production, and manufacturing.

Atoms & Industrial AI: Digitizing the Physical World

  • Bits to Atoms Vision: Kalanick draws a direct parallel between computing resources and physical automation, mapping the CPU, storage, and network of the digital world to manufacturing, real estate, and transport/logistics in the physical world.
  • Atoms Food: Aiming to build infrastructure for better food, with automated robotic manufacturing targeted between $6 and $8 per meal, alongside robotic couriers ("autonomous burritos") and dedicated industrial real estate.
  • Atoms Mining: Developing autonomous mining machines (such as 2 million-pound autonomous vehicles) to make operations safer while offering gold mine CEOs 20% more gold per year.
  • Transport ("Wheelbase for Robots"): Building specialized industrial robotics and autonomy platforms for multi-hundred-billion-dollar markets spanning food delivery, parcel delivery, personal autonomy, trucking, and off-road mining.

Uber Reflections & Cultural Evolution

  • The Lyft Acquisition: Kalanick defended the decision not to acquire Lyft, citing a profound cultural mismatch despite spending roughly $1 billion a year fighting them in the market.
  • The Best Idea Wins: Reflecting on Uber’s original culture of meritocracy and "toe-stepping," Kalanick emphasized that teams must constructively fight for the best ideas rather than settle for mediocre, politically expedient ones.
  • Founding Evolution: Kalanick noted he operates a few inches further from the line of survival than he did during his early days at Uber, having transitioned from a fear-of-failure mindset to falling in love with building anew.

Parallels to the Second Industrial Revolution

  • Confronting the Final Boss: Kalanick compared current pushback against technological progress to the Second Industrial Revolution, noting historical precedents like the violent resistance faced by Carnegie and Frick in steel, and Edison's fear-campaign against Westinghouse's alternating current.
  • The Shift in Media: Kalanick contrasted the hostile media landscape of Uber's peak era with today's decentralized media environment (podcasts, X/Twitter), which he credited with allowing builders to communicate directly without legacy media interference.
