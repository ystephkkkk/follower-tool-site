TL;DR

Krishna Guha and Steve Eisman break down the rise of the 10-year Treasury yield above 5%, attributing it to a combination of AI debt issuance crowding out Treasuries, war-driven oil prices, a massive 6% GDP deficit, and Kevin Warsh’s removal of Fed forward guidance guardrails.

Kevin Warsh's Shift in Fed Policy

  • New Leadership Approach:
    • Supply-Side Ideology: Krishna Guha notes that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh views himself as a supply-side change agent, moving away from the 20-year consensus of demand-side New Keynesian economics championed by his predecessors (Ben Bernanke, Janet Yellen, and Jay Powell).
    • Anti-QE Stance: Warsh believes quantitative easing (QE) was massively overdone and bloated the central bank balance sheet, arguing that QE should be restricted strictly to market seizure emergencies like the Global Financial Crisis or the pandemic.
  • Balance Sheet Reduction Plans:
    • Current Scale: The Fed's balance sheet sits between $6.5 trillion and $7 trillion, down from a peak of $9 trillion.
    • Tactical Caution: Warsh has established an external expert task force on the balance sheet, but Guha emphasizes he must proceed carefully to avoid triggering a spike in long-term interest rates by dumping bonds prematurely.

The Perfect Storm Driving the 10-Year Yield to 5%+

  • Competing Borrowers & AI Boom:
    • Hyper-Issuance: A surge of debt issuance by AI hyperscalers is competing directly with U.S. Treasuries.
    • Structural Shift: Bond investors are pricing in a multi-year AI investment boom that has transitioned the global economy from a world of excess savings to one of high investment demand chasing a limited pool of capital, alongside a federal deficit running at 6% of GDP (within a $40 trillion market).
  • Energy Shocks:
    • Refined Products: While Brent and WTI crude fluctuate, refined products like diesel and jet fuel have surged drastically—with diesel prices essentially doubling in a short window—creating a lasting energy cost impulse.
  • Removal of Guardrails:
    • Death of Forward Guidance: Warsh has largely abandoned broad strategic communication and the Fed's "reaction function playbook," returning the central bank toward early-Greenspan opacity. Guha argues this removal of market guardrails has left bond investors to navigate uncertainty alone, causing yields to spike.

Scott Bessent's Bond Buyback Program

  • Operation Twist Execution:
    • Scale Disconnect: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent initiated a buyback program initially set at $4 billion per operation, later scaled to $6 billion, aimed at retiring long-term high-yield bonds while financing them by issuing short-dated Treasury bills.
    • Butter Knife to a Gunfight: Given the $40 trillion Treasury market size, Guha characterizes the initiative as bringing a butter knife to a gunfight, noting that Bessent ultimately backed off after biting off more than he could chew.
  • Credibility & Regulatory Shifts:
    • Loss of Market Confidence: Intervening in the debt market made the U.S. government look like a struggling emerging market, damaging credibility.
    • Regulatory Reforms: The administration and the Fed are exploring reforms to Liquidity Coverage Ratios (LCR) and banking regulations to encourage major banks to hold fewer Fed reserves and more Treasury debt.

Economic Strains & The Rubicon for Rates

  • Two-Speed Economy:
    • AI Resilience: AI investment is largely interest-rate insensitive because projected returns (e.g., 500% to 10,000%) dwarf 6% to 9% borrowing costs. High-end consumer wealth effects driven by AI stock gains are currently propping up consumer spending.
    • Traditional Strain: Ground zero for rate pain is the housing market, where 3D-impact mortgage rates sit around 7%, while mom-and-pop "old economy" businesses face severe crowding out.
  • The Stock Market Threshold: Guha warns that while a 5-handle on the 10-year yield strains non-AI sectors, the ultimate breaking point to watch is whether the stock market can withstand current yields, as a sell-off would trigger a devastating negative wealth effect for consumers.

Historical Rating of Fed Chairmen

  • Alan Greenspan:
    • Macro Management (9-10/10): Highly praised for macroeconomy handling.
    • Regulation (3-4/10): Heavily faulted for deregulation and the philosophy that markets are self-equilibrating, planting seeds for the Global Financial Crisis.
  • Ben Bernanke:
    • Crisis Intervention (High Mark): Credited with saving the global economy from a second Great Depression through aggressive financial system stabilization and pioneering QE/forward guidance tools that were later reused during COVID-19.
  • Janet Yellen:
    • Labor Recovery (Very Good Record): Praised for recognizing deep structural issues post-GFC and keeping rates low longer to foster a fuller labor market recovery, though she did not face a moment of maximum peril.
  • Jay Powell:
    • COVID & Inflation Catch-up (Extraordinary Credit): Credited with preventing a 2020 financial collapse via aggressive private-sector backstops and achieving a rare soft landing by bringing inflation down without crushing employment, despite initial delays in recognizing post-pandemic inflation driven by trade wars and Middle East conflicts.

TL;DR

Reflection has raised $4.6 billion from investors like Nvidia, Sequoia, and Lightspeed, and is releasing Beam—a 500-billion total parameter (23-billion active) open-weight model trained completely from scratch—to establish a Western open frontier in AI. Co-founded by CEO Misha Laskin (former Gemini reward modeling lead) and CTO Ioannis Antonoglou (former AlphaGo/AlphaZero creator and Gemini RLHF lead), the company aims to provide enterprises and sovereign entities with proprietary control over their AI infrastructure and token economics.

1. Market Shift: From Rental to Ownership

  • The Real Estate Analogy for AI: Misha Laskin compares the 3-year history of the commercial AI market to a rental real estate market where users "rent intelligence" via closed models. As startups scale into mature enterprises, demand has shifted toward ownership and control, which can only be achieved via open models.
  • Token Consumption Flip: Misha Laskin notes that token consumption on platforms like Vercel and OpenRouter flipped over a 6-month period from approximately 70% closed / 30% open to roughly 70% open / 30% closed.
  • Capability Maturity: Ioannis Antonoglou points out that open models have kept pace with closed models, closing the distance between the open and closed frontiers as the market matures.
  • Geopolitical Motivation in China: Misha Laskin attributes the earlier dominance of Chinese labs in open-weights to a concentrated, geopolitically motivated push to kickstart an open-source ecosystem, catalyzed by the unexpectedly strong release of DeepSeek V3.

2. Beam: Architecture, Efficiency, and Training

  • Model Specs: Ioannis Antonoglou and Misha Laskin describe Beam as a 500-billion total parameter model with 23 billion active parameters, built as a mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture.
  • Key Capabilities: Misha Laskin highlights that Beam is built as a workhorse model for enterprises and the public sector, excelling specifically in coding and agentic benchmarks.
  • Reasoning Efficiency: Ioannis Antonoglou states that Beam achieves 3x to 4x greater reasoning and token efficiency compared to open frontier models like GLM 5.3 and closed models like GPT-6 Luna, measuring performance by how many tokens or compute are required to solve complex tasks.
  • Training from Scratch: Ioannis Antonoglou emphasizes that Beam was built entirely from scratch in the West, avoiding distillation to ensure complete ownership of pre-training dynamics, router stability, and data ingestion.
  • Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning: Ioannis Antonoglou and Misha Laskin reveal that Beam’s training involved one of the largest openly documented RL runs in open source, utilizing roughly 10,000 H300 GPUs running continuously for weeks.

3. Safety, Alignment, and Openness

TL;DR

Consumer AI is heavily dominated by a high-spending power-user economy, where only about 4.5% of US consumers pay for subscriptions, while massive white space remains in social, shopping, and entertainment.

7th Edition of a16z’s Top 100 Consumer AI Apps

  • Traffic Plateaus vs. New Spenders:
    • Fewer Newcomers: Only 11 new products appeared across web and mobile lists combined—the lowest in any prior edition.
    • Spend Data Disconnect: Of the 50 products that ranked for consumer card spend (via Yipit data), 29 did not appear on either traffic list.
  • The Power-User Spend Economy:
    • Low Paid Penetration: Roughly 4.5% of US consumers pay for an AI product subscription (about double from a year ago).
    • Concentrated Dollars: The top 10% of paying users drive over half of total revenue, and the top 1% spend $93/month personally. The median spender puts $25/month toward AI.
    • Top Categories for Spend: Developer tools, productivity tools, and creative tools (e.g., n8n, Granola, Higsfield, Manis) are heavily over-represented among top spenders.

Personal Agents and Prosumer Assistants

  • The Shift from Search to Execution: AI has evolved from a tool for faster search/productivity into agents that actually get things done.
  • Early Pioneers and New Entrants:
    • OpenClaw: Pioneered local, open-source agent setups (with users buying Mac Minis to run local data), but traffic has since declined following team acquisition/pivots.
    • Instinct: Announced 100,000 users growing 10% day-over-day; 40% of users connected a credit card in the first 3 weeks, spending over $1,000 on average in month one.
    • Muse: Reached ~500,000 downloads and 250,000 active users in its first 12 days (available only in US and Canada). Compared to Threads (which hit ~16 million downloads in its first 22 days in the same markets), Muse sits around 5 million over a similar period due to higher server costs and a more personal nature.
  • Adoption Friction & Privacy: True mainstream adoption is still nascent. Intimate personal data sharing creates tension regarding privacy, security, and trust. Furthermore, power users still heavily use assistants for coding and technical automation (driving high server costs in the hundreds/thousands per month), whereas mainstream consumers face a "blank box" problem without clear use cases.

The Evolution of Business Models: Beyond Subscriptions

  • The Subscription Inversion: Around 85% of the web list monetizes via subscriptions and 62% via credits/tokens, with only 13% using ads or transaction fees. This is an unnatural inversion compared to the historical consumer internet.
  • OpenAI’s Advertising Milestone:
    • OpenAI reached an annualized run rate of $1 billion in advertising (as of August data, with 1.2 billion weekly active users).
    • This scale was achieved much faster than traditional ads products due to massive density and rich user context enabling high-intent targeting.
  • Verticalized Ad Success: Products like Open Evidence have captured 50–60% density of all US physicians, proving that targeted verticalized audiences can monetize effectively via advertising.

Lab Trajectories: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini

  • Usage & Spend Hierarchy:
    • ChatGPT: Dominates globally, sitting roughly 6x ahead of Claude and 2x ahead of Gemini on web traffic, and holding ~3x more paid consumer subscribers in the US than Gemini and Claude combined.
    • Claude: Surpassed Gemini in number of US paid subscribers, driven by successful product launches (like Claude artifact/design) and strong press momentum. Anthropic maintains a strict "no ads" policy and counts ~7.5% of its subscribers on the $100+/month max plan (compared to ~1% for ChatGPT and Gemini).
    • Gemini: Benefits from the natural distribution of Google's massive install base, though trailing Claude in paid consumer subscriptions.

Creative Tools & Vertical Infrastructure

  • Audio and Music Specialists: Labs have largely avoided audio due to IP headaches, allowing pure-play startups like Suno and 11 Labs to run away with the top traffic and spend ranks.
  • Image & Video Dynamics:
    • OpenAI and Google have captured casual consumer traffic with advanced image/video generation.
    • However, power users still favor Midjourney for its distinct aesthetic taste.
    • Video generation remains a complex frontier where Chinese companies training on unconstrained data have gained an early edge.
  • The Software Experience Layer: Startups like Granola, Whisper Flow, and Superhum succeed by building frictionless interfaces that improve automatically as underlying model capabilities get better, avoiding the innovator's dilemma faced by tech giants.

Consumer White Space (The Unbuilt Frontier)

  • Time-Saving vs. Time-Spending: Most consumer AI tools focus on *saving* time (productivity), whereas the largest internet companies thrive on helping people *spend* time (entertainment, social, connection).
  • Open Categories: Major web 2 categories remain largely untouched by native AI startups, including dating, recruiting, social AI, gaming, home buying, retail, and marketplaces.
