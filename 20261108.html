TL;DR

AI data centers emerged as a dominant, bipartisan issue in the 2026 U.S. midterm elections, with widespread public opposition signaling that political resistance is becoming a permanent constraint on AI infrastructure.

Election Outcomes & The Data Center Backlash

  • Midterm Landslide: Democrats won control of both congressional chambers in a midterm election shaped by voter discontent over the economy and the war in Iran.
  • Bipartisan Data Center Opposition: Data centers became the most common topic in campaign ads; opposition was not partisan, with local ballot measures opposing data centers passing almost everywhere nationwide.
  • Texas Bellwether: Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governorship—making her the first Democrat in the office since 1990—after pledging a moratorium on data center construction in a state accounting for over 25% of planned U.S. capacity by 2030 (following Republican incumbent Greg Abbott’s earlier September permit moratorium).
  • Parallels to Globalization: Unlike factory offshoring where Rust Belt workers had no say, building AI data centers requires local permits and zoning, giving citizens direct leverage to express disapproval of the technology.

Macro Economic Context & The SaaS Reality

  • Stripe Partnership Insight: Stripe processes the equivalent of 1.7% of global GDP and partnered with Stratechery to provide macroeconomic data.
  • SaaS Revenue Growth: Despite "SaaS-pocalypse" headlines that wiped nearly $1 trillion from enterprise software valuations, Stripe’s SaaS index (tracking 72,000 non-AI SaaS businesses) shows year-over-year revenue growth accelerating to over 30%, with the fastest growth in the youngest SaaS companies.

Shifts in AI Constraints & Infrastructure Ecosystem

  • Evolution of Constraints: The primary bottleneck for AI dispersion has shifted sequentially from compute to memory, then power, and now definitively to political opposition, threatening prolonged scarcity.
  • Neo Clouds vs. Hyperscalers: Neo clouds face heightened credit concerns and difficulties securing permits without the political sway of hyperscalers, who can repurpose existing sites and moderate spending under the cover of political opposition.
  • Nvidia’s Mixed Position: Nvidia faces short-term risks from a weakened neo-cloud customer base, but CEO Jensen Huang’s focus on tokens-per-megawatt efficiency makes Blackwell chips indispensable in a power-constrained world.
  • China's Advantage: While constrained by U.S. export controls on leading-edge chips, China benefits from domestic power generation capacity, using power-heavy supercomputers like Huawei's Cloud Matrix 384 to offset chip disadvantages.

Long-Term Industry Implications

  • Space-Based Data Centers: Persistent terrestrial zoning opposition increases the long-term viability and necessity of placing data racks in space to meet limitless compute demand.
  • Frontier Labs Relief: Public opposition effectively paces the R&D spending race between frontier labs like Anthropic, improving their financial outlook as inference margins remain high.
  • Apple & Startup Impacts: Apple gains a strategic advantage as edge inference and memory crunch relief become paramount, while startups suffer from reduced access to cheap compute and slowed ecosystem maturation.
  • Human Adaptation: Permanently higher marginal costs for AI compute may prevent the hyper-cheap Web 2.0 free-service wave, but it also gives humanity more breathing room to adapt to economic shifts driven by artificial intelligence.

TL;DR

Driven by collapsing sequencing costs and massive genomic data sets, AI is transforming from sequence prediction to modeling cell states and few-shot biological design. Experts across bioinformatics and generative modeling detail how personalized agentic labs, virtual cells, and DNA language models are accelerating drug discovery and cancer treatment.

1. Cancer as a Data Problem and Personalized Research Labs

  • Alfredo Gonzalez (Co-founder, Amanta; UCLA PhD):
    • Exploding Data Availability: Sequencing costs have plummeted to where getting a genome sequenced is cheaper than a Bay Area dinner, and cancer sequencing is widely covered by insurance (Medicare/Medicaid).
    • From Cohort Averages to N-of-1: The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) 10 years ago forced researchers to look at limited cohort averages; today, each patient provides an individual 20,000-gene data set (along with RNA expression, copy numbers, and alternative splicing) to mine directly.
    • Agentic Biology Pipelines: Amanta replaces traditional 30-40 person bioinformatics research teams with agentic workflows that mine patient data in real time, pulling in fresh discoveries overnight to optimize treatments.
    • Case Study Success: In rare brain tumors and patients who failed multiple lines of toxic chemotherapy, dynamic re-analysis of tumor evolution and pathway activation uncovered alternative, effective therapies that standard hospital protocols missed.

2. Virtual Cells and Autonomous AI Research

  • Yusuf Roohani (Head of Science, Entos AI; Stanford PhD / Arc Institute):
    • The Virtual Cell Vision: Moving beyond molecular sequence models (like AlphaFold and ESM) to model biological systems at cellular, tissue, and organism levels to bridge the gap in clinical efficacy.
    • Universal Cell Embeddings: Published in *Nature* (early 2025), a model trained on 35 million cells using gene expression matrices learned cellular representations reflecting Waddington's developmental landscape without explicit supervision.
    • Scaling Bottlenecks: Mining NIH transcriptomic data via agents scaled datasets to 500 million+ cells, but revealed massive overrepresentation of immune cells (specifically T-cells), highlighting the limits of static data collection.
    • AI-Guided Experimentation (Locus): Shifting from linear data collection to an active discovery loop optimized for molecular design and property prediction, outperforming standard coding agents by treating AI research primitives as shared engineering problems.

3. DNA Foundation Models and Decoding Genomic Language

  • Garyk Brixi (Stanford PhD / Evo 2 & Minerva Project Lead):
    • Trillion-Scale Pre-training: Evo 2 was trained on 9 trillion base pairs (down-sampled to one representative per species and heavily augmented around functional annotations) using a 7B and 40B dense parameter hybrid attention-convolution architecture.
    • Decision Model Capabilities: By predicting next-token nucleotides, Evo 2 acts as a decision model; its variant likelihood scores match or beat supervised human-clinical models on gene risk benchmarks like BRCA1 without human-specific training data.
    • Mechanistic Interpretability: Sparse autoencoders reveal that Evo 2 internally learns complex biological concepts—such as phage-associated features, CRISPR arrays, and prophages—solely from next-token nucleotide prediction.
    • Minerva & Co-evolution: A new bidirectional model (Minerva) resolves sequence co-evolution limitations, enabling rapid genome scanning that recently uncovered hundreds of novel non-coding RNAs and validated a new UG27 reverse transcriptase in bacteria.

4. Few-Shot Prompting and In-Context Learning for Bio Design

  • Divya Nori (Researcher, Hazy Lab / MIT):
    • Emergent In-Context Learning (ICL): Without task-specific fine-tuning, Evo 2 demonstrates ICL capabilities via next-token prediction, allowing researchers to prompt models with a handful of examples (4 to 64) to steer biological design.
    • Cross-Scale Applications: Successfully applied from small 100-nucleotide proteins (such as rare land modulins binding rare earth metals) up to 100,000-nucleotide whole bacteriophage genomes and CRISPR-Cas9 systems.
    • Wet-Lab Validation: Using reinforcement learning (GRPO with predictive reward models) to post-train Evo 2, researchers designed a novel cell-type specific enhancer that was successfully validated in vivo, showing specific fluorescence activity in the mouse brain.
